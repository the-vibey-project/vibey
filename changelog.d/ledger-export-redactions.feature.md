* **The public ledger export publishes `_redactions`:** every published kind keeps the record of what was redacted in its payload -- a list of `{path, class}` entries, the JSON Pointer to the redacted field and why -- without the kind having to name it, so a reader can tell that a field was redacted, where and why instead of meeting a bare `[REDACTED]`. An entry never carries a value, a hash of one or its length: any other field is withheld and counted, and so is an entry that is not a pointer and a class of the usual shape. The design question the issue left open is settled as the first option it listed: where a segment of a path is itself private (the last one of a `private-detail-key` entry, whose key *is* the private detail, or any segment that is an address or a path) it is published as `[private]` and counted as a replaced path. `[private]` rather than `~private~`, because a `~` starts an escape in an RFC 6901 pointer. A record with nothing redacted publishes no `_redactions` field. A rule set withholds it explicitly with `publish_redactions=False` (the billing projection does, and says why beside `BILLING_ALLOWLIST`); the new flag and `private_segment_token` are part of the rules' fingerprint. The producer of `_redactions` (#637) is not in the tree yet, so this is tested against fixture payloads; the end-to-end check waits on it. Part of #1723.

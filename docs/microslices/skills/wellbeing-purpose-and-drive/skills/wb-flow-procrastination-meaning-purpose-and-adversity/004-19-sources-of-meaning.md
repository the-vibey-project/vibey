## §19. Sources of Meaning

**⚠️ What people actually report** when asked: ⚠️ **relationships and family first, by a wide
margin, then work and vocation, then faith and worldview, then contribution and service,
creation and craft, learning and understanding, and nature and beauty.**
**⚠️ Frankl's account** — ⚠️ **meaning through creative work, through experience and love, and
through the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering — remains influential and is a
phenomenological rather than an empirical claim.** ⚠️ **Note that it emerged from an extreme
context and its author was explicit that it was not a scientific study.**
**⚠️ Religion and worldview** are worth stating plainly: ⚠️ **the epidemiological literature
consistently associates religious participation with wellbeing outcomes, with the usual
confounds (⚠️ social connection, health behaviours, selection effects) and genuine debate
about how much survives them.** ⚠️ **The COHERENCE function (§18) — a framework that answers
why things happen and what one is for — is a plausible mechanism independent of any question
about truth.**
**⚠️ Generativity** — ⚠️ **contributing to what outlasts you — appears across traditions and
in developmental psychology (Erikson) as a durable source.**
**⚠️ Meaning is more often FOUND than constructed** — ⚠️ **people generally locate it in
what they are already committed to rather than deriving it from first principles.**

## §14. ⚠️ Habits and Behaviour Change

**⚠️ Habits are context-cued automatic responses** — ⚠️ **which means the leverage is in the
CONTEXT, not in the intention.**
**⚠️ Implementation intentions** — ⚠️ **"when situation X arises, I will do Y" — have a solid
meta-analytic base and are among the more reliable tools here, because they pre-decide and
remove the moment of choice.**
**⚠️ Friction is the underrated lever**: ⚠️ **changing the number of steps required to start
or to stop something does more than motivation typically does, and it does not deplete.**
**⚠️ Habit formation time**: ⚠️ **the "21 days" claim has no basis; the study usually cited
found a median around 66 days with an enormous range (roughly 18 to 254), which is a very
different message.**
**⚠️ Identity-based framing** — ⚠️ **"I am someone who..." — is popular and rests on thinner
evidence than the popular treatments imply; ⚠️ it maps onto SDT's integration (§11), which is
where its plausibility comes from.**
**⚠️ The transtheoretical (stages of change) model** is widely taught and ⚠️ **has been
substantially criticized on evidence, which is worth knowing if you encounter it as
settled.**

## §13. ⚠️ Goals

**⚠️ Goal-setting theory** (Locke and Latham) is ⚠️ **among the better-supported findings in
applied psychology: specific, difficult goals outperform vague or easy ones, given
commitment, feedback and ability.**
**⚠️ The conditions matter and are usually dropped**: ⚠️ **the effect depends on the task
being reasonably straightforward, on the person accepting the goal, and on feedback being
available.** ⚠️ **For complex, novel tasks, LEARNING goals outperform performance goals.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — goals have documented SIDE EFFECTS that the popular literature omits.**
> ⚠️ **Narrow specific targets can crowd out unmeasured dimensions, encourage unethical
> behaviour to hit the number, reduce intrinsic motivation, and induce excessive
> risk-taking when someone is just short of a threshold.** **⚠️ This is Goodhart's law
> operating on a person.**

**⚠️ Approach versus avoidance goals**: ⚠️ **framing toward what you want rather than away
from what you fear is associated with better outcomes and persistence.**
**⚠️ Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (WOOP)** has ⚠️ **reasonable evidence
and is more effective than visualizing success alone — ⚠️ indeed, pure positive fantasizing
about outcomes is associated with LOWER achievement, which is one of the more useful
counterintuitive findings that has held up.**

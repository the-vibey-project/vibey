## §12. ⚠️ Intrinsic Motivation and Rewards

**⚠️ The overjustification effect**: ⚠️ **rewarding an activity someone already enjoys can
reduce their subsequent intrinsic motivation for it.** ⚠️ **The classic demonstration is
children rewarded for drawing who then drew less freely.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — this is real and it is routinely overstated into "rewards don't work," which
> is false.** ⚠️ **The meta-analytic picture is narrower: the undermining effect is strongest
> for EXPECTED, TANGIBLE rewards CONTINGENT ON MERE ENGAGEMENT in an already-interesting
> task.** **⚠️ Unexpected rewards, verbal praise, and rewards for genuine performance
> quality show little or no undermining — and rewards clearly work for tasks nobody finds
> interesting.**

**⚠️ The practical translation**: ⚠️ **pay people well and fairly, then avoid making the pay
salient in moment-to-moment work** — ⚠️ **the finding that money is a poor motivator applies
above a threshold of feeling fairly paid, not below it.**
**⚠️ Autonomy-supportive versus controlling framing** of the same incentive changes its
effect (§11).
**⚠️ The corollary for personal use**: ⚠️ **gamifying something you already love carries a
real risk, and turning a hobby into an income source is the everyday version of this
effect.**

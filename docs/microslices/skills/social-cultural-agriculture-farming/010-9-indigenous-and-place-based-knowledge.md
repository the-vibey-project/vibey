## 9. Indigenous and place-based knowledge

Many Indigenous and local communities understand agriculture as part of a larger territory that includes water, animals, ancestors, seasonal cycles, obligations, and collective identity. Knowledge may be transmitted through practice, story, ceremony, language, observation, and stewardship rather than separated into technical categories.

Collaboration must respect consent, data sovereignty, intellectual property, sacred knowledge, benefit-sharing, and the right not to disclose sensitive information. “Combining traditional and scientific knowledge” should not mean extracting local knowledge and relabeling it without authority or credit. Research should be co-designed where possible, return useful results, and recognize community governance.

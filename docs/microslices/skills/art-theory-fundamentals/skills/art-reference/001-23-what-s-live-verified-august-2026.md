## §23. What's Live — verified August 2026

### 23.1 ⚠️ AI, authorship and copyright
**⚠️ One question is now settled in the US and the more interesting ones are wide open.**

**⚠️ Settled: purely AI-generated work cannot be copyrighted in the US.**
- **The saga: Stephen Thaler applied in 2018 to register "A Recent Entrance to Paradise,"
  created by his AI system, listing the machine as author.** **The Copyright Office
  refused; a federal district court upheld that in 2023, holding human authorship a
  "bedrock requirement"; the DC Circuit affirmed in 2025** — ⚠️ **Judge Millett writing
  that the Copyright Act of 1976 requires eligible work to be authored in the first
  instance by a human being.**
- **⚠️ On 2 March 2026 the Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*
  (No. 25-449)**, **leaving the DC Circuit ruling standing.** **Human authorship is a
  requirement of US copyright.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the bar for AI-ASSISTED work is much higher than most people assume, and
> this is where the practical exposure sits.** ⚠️ **The Copyright Office has rejected
> claims from artists who argued their Midjourney prompting constituted sufficient
> authorship — including, in one reported case, a work refined through more than 600
> prompts.** **In the Kashtanova graphic novel decision, the Office allowed copyright over
> the human-authored text and the arrangement but EXCLUDED the AI-generated images.**
> ⚠️ **Prompting alone, however elaborate, has not been accepted as authorship.** **What
> can qualify: creative selection and arrangement of AI output, and substantial human
> modification of it.**
> **⚠️ The commercial consequence is real**: **if a delivered image can't be copyrighted,
> a client can't own it exclusively, and competitors may reproduce it.**

**⚠️ Wide open — the training-data question.** **Class actions filed against Stability AI,
Midjourney, DeviantArt and others in 2023–24 remain in active litigation as of 2026**, with
**three unresolved questions: whether training on copyrighted images is fair use;
whether outputs resembling specific works are derivative; and what remedy artists have.**
⚠️ **Related cases have gone against AI developers on narrower facts — in *Thomson Reuters
v. Ross Intelligence* a court found training on Westlaw headnotes was not fair use, though
that involved a non-generative search tool and is on appeal.**
**⚠️ And it is not globally harmonized**: **the US relies on case-by-case fair use;
Japan has legislated broad training exceptions; the EU provides opt-out mechanisms under
the DSM Directive; and the UK offers limited protection for "computer-generated works"
that the US does not.**
**⚠️ The Copyright Office has explicitly said the current bar reflects the current state
of the technology and could change if AI systems evolve to give users sufficient control
over expressive elements.** **This is not a permanent settlement.**

**⚠️ The theoretical questions this raises are older than the technology**, and §11–§12 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`,
§18 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` and §20 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` are where they live: **if authorship requires intention, what is a prompt?**
**If photography's evidentiary force is indexical (§18 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world`), what is an image caused by a
model rather than by light?** ⚠️ **Duchamp's readymade already established that selection
can be an artistic act — which is precisely the argument prompt-based artists make, and
precisely the argument the Copyright Office has so far declined.**

### 23.2 ⚠️ The art market — a recovery that mostly isn't one
**⚠️ The Art Basel and UBS Global Art Market Report 2026 (Clare McAndrew, Arts Economics)
covers 2025**, and **the headline and the substance point in different directions.**

**The headline**: **global sales rose 4% to an estimated $59.6 billion in 2025** — **the
first growth since 2022, after two years of contraction.** **Dealer sales up 2% to $34.8bn;
public auction up 9% to $20.7bn; auction-house private sales down 4% to just under
$4.2bn.** **Transaction volume ~41.5 million, up 2%.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the aggregate obscures more than it reveals, and one critic's framing is
> worth repeating: in a market this structurally unequal, averaging IS obscuring.**
> ⚠️ **Nine of the year's top ten highest-priced lots sold in a SINGLE WEEK in November
> 2025, almost all at Christie's and Sotheby's in New York.** **US sales of works above
> $10 million rose roughly 40%, and the high end restored its share of total auction value
> to 54% after 2024's contraction — meaning the middle and lower ends LOST share.**
> ⚠️ **"The market grew" and "most participants had a harder year" are both true.**

**⚠️ Context that reframes the headline further:**
- **⚠️ The market remains below its 2022 post-pandemic peak of $68.1 billion, and is
  smaller than a decade ago — down 7% in value since 2015.**
- **⚠️ Profitability fell for many even as sales rose**: **38% of dealers and 40% of
  mid-tier auction houses reported lower profits**, **under pressure from fair
  participation costs, travel, regulatory compliance and overheads.**
- **⚠️ Smaller dealers were a genuine bright spot** — **those with turnover under $250,000
  reported 17% sales growth** — **so the picture is barbell-shaped rather than uniformly
  top-heavy.**
- **Geography: US 44% of global value, UK 18%, China 14% (continuing to contract), France
  8% and now the largest EU market.** **The top three account for 76%.**
- **⚠️ Online sales fell back to 15% of total value** — **the digital channel losing
  momentum as transactions returned to in-person** — **while art fairs rose to 35% of
  dealer sales from 31%.**

**⚠️ Sourcing caveat worth stating**: **this report is co-published by an art fair and a
bank with an art-advisory business, which is not a reason to dismiss it — it's the most
comprehensive dataset available and is compiled by an independent economist — but it is a
reason to read its framing critically.** **The underlying figures are widely reported and
consistent; the "cautious optimism" framing is the interested part.**

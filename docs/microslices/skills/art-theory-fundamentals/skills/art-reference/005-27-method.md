## §27. Method

**§1–§22 → `art-claims-elements-colour-and-composition`, `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`, `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon`, `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` rest on stable material** — **perceptual and craft knowledge, the philosophical
tradition from Plato to contemporary analytic aesthetics, art-historical periodization,
and interpretive method** — sourced from §25. ⚠️ **None of it needed verification; the
oldest material here is 2,400 years old and the newest philosophical position is decades
settled.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **AI authorship/copyright** and **the art
market** — ⚠️ **the only two areas where a static answer would now be wrong.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §2–§7 → `art-claims-elements-colour-and-composition`, which is the part I'd defend most firmly: ⚠️ **the
value-over-colour claim, the RYB debunk, and the golden-ratio and rule-of-thirds
corrections are well established and are among the most widely mistaught things in art
education.** **The chroma-subsampling connection in §3 → `art-claims-elements-colour-and-composition` is a genuine mechanistic
explanation, not an analogy.**

**High** in §8–§13 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`'s exposition of the philosophical positions. ⚠️ **I've been careful to
present these as arguments with live objections rather than as results** — **§1 → `art-claims-elements-colour-and-composition`'s whole
purpose is to make that distinction available** — **and where I've indicated a position is
more defensible (moderate moralism in §13 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`, cluster accounts in §12 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`, moderate
intentionalism in §20 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world`), I've said so as a judgement rather than a fact.**

⚠️ **§16 → `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon` and §19 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` were written to give both the strong version of institutional critique
and the genuine methodological cautions against its excesses**, **because both are argued
seriously inside the discipline and a reader is poorly served by a version that only
contains one.**

**High** in §23.1's legal facts — ⚠️ **the Thaler chronology (2018 application, 2023
district court, 2025 DC Circuit, cert denied 2 March 2026) is confirmed across law-firm
publications, the Constitution Center, and CNBC** — **and I've been careful to separate
what IS settled (human authorship required) from what is not (training-data fair use,
where the class actions remain in active litigation).** ⚠️ **The Copyright Office's own
statement that the bar reflects current technology and could shift is important and
usually omitted.**

**High** in §23.2's figures, which come from a single well-identified source — **the Art
Basel/UBS report by Arts Economics — and are consistently reported across many
outlets.** ⚠️ **I've flagged the sourcing interest explicitly: co-published by an art fair
and a bank with an art advisory arm.** **The figures are reliable; the "cautious optimism"
framing is the interested part, which is why I foregrounded the concentration finding —
nine of the top ten lots in one November week — over the 4% headline.** **That's an
interpretive emphasis and I'd defend it, but you should know it's mine.**

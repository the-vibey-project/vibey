## §5. Composition

```
BALANCE      symmetrical, asymmetrical, radial. ⚠️ Visual weight ≠ physical size
             — a small dark or saturated shape can balance a large pale one
EMPHASIS     ⚠️ focal point via value contrast, edge, isolation, convergence, colour
UNITY / VARIETY   ⚠️ the fundamental tension. Too much unity is dull; too much
             variety disintegrates
RHYTHM       repetition with variation; intervals
MOVEMENT     ⚠️ how the eye travels. Leading lines, implied direction, gaze
SCALE / PROPORTION   relations to each other and to the viewer
HIERARCHY    ⚠️ what reads first, second, third
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the rule of thirds and the golden ratio are not what they're presented
> as.** ⚠️ **The rule of thirds is a 19th-century compositional convenience with no
> perceptual basis; it works mainly because it stops beginners centring everything.**
> **The golden ratio's supposed ubiquity in historic art is largely retrospective
> pattern-matching** — ⚠️ **claims about the Parthenon and the Mona Lisa require choosing
> which points to measure between, and the measurements rarely survive scrutiny.**
> **Neither is worthless as a starting device. Both are false as laws.**

**⚠️ What is better supported**: **the picture plane's edges are active** (**shapes relate
to the frame, and tangents — where an edge just touches another — create ambiguity and
read as errors**); **closure and grouping follow Gestalt principles** (**proximity,
similarity, continuity, closure, figure-ground**), ⚠️ **which ARE grounded in perception
and are the most defensible "rules" in composition**; **and the eye is drawn to faces,
contrast, and points of convergence involuntarily.**

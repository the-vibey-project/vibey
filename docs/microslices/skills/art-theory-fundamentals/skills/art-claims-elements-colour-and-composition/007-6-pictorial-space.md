## §6. Pictorial Space

```
LINEAR PERSPECTIVE  ⚠️ 1/2/3-point. A CONVENTION formalized in the Italian
   Renaissance (Brunelleschi, Alberti) — ⚠️ geometrically correct for a single
   fixed eye, which is not how anyone looks at anything
ATMOSPHERIC   ⚠️ distance reduces contrast and saturation, shifts toward the
   sky's hue. Physically real (scattering) and reliably convincing
OVERLAP, SCALE, POSITION, TEXTURE GRADIENT, FOCUS   ⚠️ overlap is the strongest
   and simplest depth cue there is
```
**⚠️ Alternative spatial systems are not failed perspective.** **Isometric/axonometric
(much East Asian painting), hierarchical scale (Egyptian, medieval — size denotes
importance), multiple viewpoints (Cubism), and flat pictorial space are DIFFERENT
CONVENTIONS with different purposes.** ⚠️ **Reading them as "not yet having discovered
perspective" is a Renaissance-progressivist error, and one that art history has spent a
century correcting** (§16 → `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon`).
**⚠️ The picture plane is always flat**, **and much of modern art's programme was insisting
on that flatness rather than disguising it** (§15 → `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon`).

## §9. Beauty and the Sublime

**⚠️ The classical position (Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas)**: **beauty is objective —
a property of the object consisting in something like proportion, integrity, harmony and
radiance.** ⚠️ **Aquinas's formulation — *integritas, consonantia, claritas* — is
compact and still worth thinking with.** **Beauty on this view is linked to being and to
truth, not merely to pleasure.**

**⚠️ The turn to the subject (18th century)** — **beauty relocated from object to
response.**
**⚠️ Kant is the pivotal figure and worth understanding properly**, because he tried to
hold both:
- **⚠️ The judgement of beauty is DISINTERESTED** — **not about whether you want, need or
  can use the thing.**
- **⚠️ It claims UNIVERSALITY without a concept**: **when you call something beautiful you
  are not merely reporting a preference — you speak as though others ought to agree** —
  **yet you cannot produce a rule that proves it.**
- **⚠️ "Purposiveness without purpose"** — **it appears designed for a end you cannot
  specify.**
- **Free vs dependent beauty**; **and genius as the capacity to produce exemplary work
  without following rules.**

**⚠️ The sublime is a separate category and a genuinely useful one**: **Burke and Kant on
the overwhelming — vastness, power, terror held at a safe distance** — ⚠️ **pleasure mixed
with a kind of fear, and NOT a species of beauty.** **It's what the beautiful cannot
account for: storms, mountains, the enormous, the terrible.** **Much Romantic and some
contemporary work is aiming at the sublime, not the beautiful, and judging it by beauty's
standards misses the point.**

**⚠️ Modern and contemporary art's relationship to beauty is complicated and worth stating
carefully.** **Much 20th-century avant-garde practice deliberately rejected beauty as
bourgeois, decorative, or complicit** — ⚠️ **and a strand of recent criticism (Scarry,
Nehamas, and others) has argued the rejection was overdone and that beauty deserves
rehabilitation as a serious category.** **This is a live argument, not a settled
progression.**

## §10. Taste and Judgement

**⚠️ Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste" is the best short treatment of the problem, and
his answer is neither relativist nor dogmatic.** **The puzzle: taste appears personal,
yet we do not actually treat all judgements as equal** — ⚠️ **anyone claiming a mediocre
hack equals Shakespeare is not expressing a preference we respect but making an error.**
**Hume's solution is the QUALIFIED JUDGE: delicacy of sentiment, practice, comparison,
freedom from prejudice, and good sense.** ⚠️ **The standard is the converging verdict of
such judges over time — which is a sociological answer to a philosophical question, and
whether that's satisfying is exactly the debate.**

**⚠️ Kant's antinomy of taste** states the difficulty cleanly: **judgements of taste
cannot be based on concepts (or we could prove them by argument), yet they must be based
on something (or we couldn't meaningfully disagree).**
**⚠️ Bourdieu's sociological challenge is the strongest modern objection**: **taste is
socially produced and functions as class distinction** — **"aesthetic disposition" is
acquired through upbringing and education, and disinterested contemplation is itself a
class-marked stance available to those with the leisure for it** (see a sociology
reference §17). ⚠️ **This does not by itself refute Kant — showing how a judgement is
acquired doesn't show it's wrong — but it is a serious pressure on any claim that taste is
purely a faculty.**

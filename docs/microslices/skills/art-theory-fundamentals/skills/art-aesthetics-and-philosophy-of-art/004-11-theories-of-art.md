## §11. Theories of Art

```
MIMETIC        ⚠️ art as imitation/representation. Plato (suspicious — twice
               removed from the Forms), Aristotle (favourable — catharsis).
               ⚠️ Dominant for two millennia; broken by abstraction
EXPRESSION     ⚠️ art as the expression/transmission of emotion. Tolstoy,
               Collingwood, Croce. ⚠️ Problem: much great art expresses nothing
               in particular, and much emotional outpouring isn't art
FORMALISM      ⚠️ value lies in FORM, not subject. Bell's "significant form,"
               Fry, later Greenberg. ⚠️ Problem: "significant form" is defined
               circularly as the form that produces aesthetic emotion
INSTITUTIONAL  ⚠️ Dickie — art is what the artworld confers the status on.
               Handles Duchamp; ⚠️ criticized as circular and as ceding the
               question to whoever holds institutional power
HISTORICAL     ⚠️ Danto, Levinson — something is art in virtue of its relation
               to prior art and an interpretive context. ⚠️ Danto's insight:
               Warhol's Brillo Box is visually indiscernible from a Brillo box,
               so whatever makes it art is NOT VISIBLE
AESTHETIC      ⚠️ art as what's made for aesthetic experience. Beardsley
CLUSTER        ⚠️ Gaut, Weitz — no necessary and sufficient conditions; a
               family-resemblance cluster of typical features
```
**⚠️ The honest position most philosophers hold now**: **no single definition works, the
cluster/family-resemblance approach handles the cases best, and the failure of definition
is informative rather than embarrassing** (§12).

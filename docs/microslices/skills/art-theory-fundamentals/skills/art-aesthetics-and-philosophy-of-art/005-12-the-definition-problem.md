## §12. ⚠️ The Definition Problem

**⚠️ The interesting fact about "what is art?" is that every proposed definition is either
too narrow or too broad, and this has been true for a century.**
- **Mimetic definitions exclude abstraction.**
- **Expression definitions exclude a great deal of formal, conceptual and decorative work
  — and include a tantrum.**
- **Formalist definitions exclude conceptual art and can't ground "significant."**
- **Institutional definitions include anything a gallery shows and can't explain why the
  institution's judgement should count.**

**⚠️ Weitz's argument, following Wittgenstein**: **art is an OPEN CONCEPT.** **Its
conditions of application are perpetually revisable because novelty and boundary-pushing
are internal to the practice.** ⚠️ **Any definition tight enough to be informative will be
falsified by the next thing artists do — deliberately, because falsifying it is part of
what artists do.**
**⚠️ The practically useful reframe**: **stop asking "is this art?" and ask "what does
calling it art do?"** — **it invokes a mode of attention, a set of institutions, a market,
a critical tradition and a history.** ⚠️ **"Is it art?" is almost always a proxy for "is it
any good?" or "should this be here?", and those are the questions actually in dispute
(§1 → `art-claims-elements-colour-and-composition`).**

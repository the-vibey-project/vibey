## §10 Responding to common objections

Four objections, answered in turn. Three of the four are weaker than their reputation; the shape of
each answer is different, so learn the shape rather than a script.

| Objection | The move that answers it | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| The Bible has contradictions | Ask for the specific one; engage the passage | Claiming there are no difficulties |
| Religion is a crutch for weak people | Name the genetic fallacy | Arguing only about whether believers are weak |
| All religions lead to God | Pluralism is itself a claim needing defense | Treating a counter-claim as a refutation |
| Science has disproven religion | Scope — empirical method cannot settle a metaphysical question | Overclaiming what the historical examples show |

### "How can you believe the Bible when it has contradictions?"

**Ask what specific contradiction they have in mind.** Most people who raise this cannot name one —
they have heard the claim but not examined it. If they can, engage that passage. Most alleged
contradictions resolve by attention to context, genre, authorial intent, or harmonization of
complementary accounts; the field that addresses this systematically is textual criticism and
harmonization, and the scholarly literature is substantial.

**Do not claim there are no difficulties. Do claim that none are fatal** to the text's reliability or
to any Christian doctrine. That distinction is the whole answer, and it is an honest one only if you
hold both halves. The manuscript and dating evidence behind the second half is §8 →
`ministry-apologetics-resurrection-and-scripture`.

### "Religion is a crutch for weak people"

Even if it were, that would not make it false — **a crutch is the appropriate response to a broken
leg**. The questions are whether the leg is actually broken (the human condition includes genuine
brokenness) and whether the crutch actually works (Christianity has produced transformation across
cultures and centuries).

The deeper response is structural: this is a **genetic fallacy**. Explaining why someone holds a
belief does not address whether the belief is true. People may believe in God for psychological
reasons and God may still exist. Note that the fallacy cuts both ways and an honest use of it says
so — a psychological account of *un*belief is equally irrelevant to whether God exists, so do not
answer this objection by producing one.

### "All religions lead to God"

This claim — religious pluralism — **is itself a religious claim that needs defense**, not a
self-evident truth. The major religions make mutually exclusive claims about the nature of ultimate
reality (one personal God, many gods, no God, an impersonal absolute), the human condition (sin,
ignorance, desire, illusion), and the solution (grace, effort, enlightenment, liberation). They
cannot all be true in the same sense. The question is which, if any, is true — not whether they all
lead to the same place. Christ's own claims were explicitly exclusive: *"I am the way, the truth, and
the life. No one comes to the Father except through me"* (John 14:6).

Be honest about what this accomplishes. It shifts a burden; it does not close the question. The
developed pluralist position does not deny the surface contradictions — it argues the traditions are
culturally conditioned responses to one ultimate reality that none of them describes literally. The
reference does not engage that version, and an objector who holds it will not recognise their
position in the answer above.

### "Science has disproven religion"

Science addresses the natural world through empirical observation and testable hypotheses. **It
cannot address whether a transcendent God exists, because that is a metaphysical question outside
the scope of scientific method.** Science can settle specific claims — the age of the earth,
evolutionary mechanisms — but not whether there is a God who created the natural order that science
studies. Many of history's greatest scientists (Newton, Kepler, Mendel, Boyle, Faraday) were devout
Christians who saw science as the study of God's creation.

Two honest caveats. The boundary claim is itself philosophically contested — from one side by theists
who take fine-tuning (§6 → `ministry-apologetics-arguments-for-god`) to be empirical support for
design, from the other by naturalists who hold that any claim with empirical consequences is testable.
And the list of devout scientists establishes **compatibility**, historically demonstrated; it is a
biographical argument, and does not show the beliefs are true.

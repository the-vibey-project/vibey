## §24 Further reading

The reference's own list, grouped by its five parts, with the reason it gives for each work — except the
final church-planting entry, which the source lists without one. Nothing has been added to the reason
column.

### Evangelism

| Work | Why the source lists it |
|---|---|
| Rebecca Manley Pippert, *Out of the Saltshaker and Into the World* | The classic relational evangelism guide. |
| Tim Chester, *Evangelism and the Sovereignty of God* — *(attribution as the source gives it; the widely known book of this title is attributed elsewhere, so verify the author before citing)* | How God's sovereignty frees us from manipulative techniques. |
| Jerram Barrs, *The Heart of Evangelism* | Evangelism rooted in God's character. |
| The Lausanne Movement, *The Cape Town Commitment* | The global consensus document on evangelism and mission. |

The second entry is the one that connects to the load-bearing part of Part I: the whole of §5 →
`ministry-evangelism-ethics` is the argument that a genuine gospel invitation survives disclosure and an
engineered one does not. Its title, reason line **and attribution** are reproduced exactly as the source
gives them — and the attribution is the one thing on this list the pack cannot stand behind, because the
widely known book of that title is attributed elsewhere. Substituting an author would be supplying a name
the source did not give; check it yourself before you cite it. This is the only group whose reason lines
name a **failure mode** rather than a topic.

### Apologetics

| Work | Why the source lists it |
|---|---|
| William Lane Craig, *Reasonable Faith* | The comprehensive reference for classical/evidential apologetics. |
| N.T. Wright, *The Resurrection of the Son of God* | The definitive historical study of the resurrection. |
| Alvin Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil* | The free will defense against the logical problem of evil. |
| Tim Keller, *The Reason for God* | Accessible apologetics for skeptical urban audiences. |
| Paul Copan, *True for You, But Not for Me* | Responding to relativist objections. |

Craig and Plantinga are the two authors whose arguments the body actually runs: Craig's modal version of
the ontological argument at §6 → `ministry-apologetics-arguments-for-god`, and Plantinga's Free Will
Defense against the logical problem of evil at §9 →
`ministry-apologetics-evil-objections-and-worldview`. Craig, Habermas and Geisler are also named at §11
as the classical/evidential school, one of the three methods compared there.

> **EVERY AUTHOR ON THIS LIST ARGUES FOR THE POSITION**
> Two honest observations about the apologetics group, neither of which is a criticism of any book on
> it. First, "definitive" for the Wright volume is **the source's evaluation**, not a scholarly
> consensus — it is a long, contested historical monograph, and its central argument is exactly what
> critics of the minimal-facts approach dispute. Second, the list contains **no critic**: no reply to
> Craig, no evidential-problem-of-evil case, no historical-Jesus work arguing the other way. A reader
> who wants to know how strong these arguments are, rather than how to present them, will need sources
> this list does not supply. That matters more here than anywhere else in the reference, because §11
> makes listening to the actual objection the most important apologetic skill — and you cannot answer an
> objection you have only met in summary.

| Work | Why the source lists it |
|---|---|
| Tim Keller, *Preaching: Communicating Faith in an Age of Skepticism* | Preaching to the modern mind and heart. |
| Haddon Robinson, *Biblical Preaching* | The standard text on expository sermon development. |
| Andy Stanley, *Communicating for a Change* | Sermon structure and audience engagement. |
| Bryan Chapell, *Christ-Centered Preaching* | Sermon structure with redemptive focus. |
| Charles Spurgeon, *Lectures to My Students* | The classic pastoral manual. |

Three of the five are about **structure**, which is the emphasis of §12 → `ministry-preaching`: structure
exists for the listener, who cannot re-read, so signposting and repetition that would feel excessive on
the page are not optional.

### Teaching

| Work | Why the source lists it |
|---|---|
| James Wilhoit and Leland Ryken, *Effective Bible Teaching* | Teaching Scripture effectively in small groups and classrooms. |
| Greg Ogden, *Discipleship Essentials* | A structured curriculum for one-on-one or small group discipleship. |
| Colin Marshall and Tony Payne, *The Trellis and the Vine* | Shifting from programs to people in ministry. |

> **THE EMPIRICAL CLAIMS HAVE NO SOURCES ON THIS LIST**
> Parts I and IV lean on claims from persuasion research and learning science — listening first produces
> durable change (§1), the curse of knowledge (§13), learning is a change in long-term memory and
> working memory is tiny (§16), roughly three seconds of wait time improves answer quality (§16),
> performance during a lesson is not learning (§18), onboarding is the growth constraint (§21). **No
> work on this reading list is a primary source for any of them.** These three titles are ministry
> books, not cognitive-science ones. Treat the empirical claims as the reference states them — scoped,
> directional, and not traceable through this list — and go to the pedagogy and communication
> literature directly if you need to know how strong any one of them is. The claims themselves, with
> the scope the reference gives them, are at §16 and §18 → `ministry-teaching-and-discipleship`, §1 →
> `ministry-evangelism-persuasion-and-listening`, §13 → `ministry-preaching` and §21 →
> `ministry-church-planting`.

### Church planting

| Work | Why the source lists it |
|---|---|
| Ed Stetzer and Daniel Im, *Planting Missional Churches* | The comprehensive church planting guide. |
| Aubrey Malphurs, *Planting Growing Churches for the 21st Century* | Practical strategy and process. |
| Alan Hirsch, *The Forgotten Ways* | Missional DNA and apostolic movement. |
| The community-building and organizing literature broadly — Robert Putnam on social capital, Saul Alinsky on organizing (*adapted with appropriate theological discernment* — the source's own qualifier, in its own words), and the small-groups literature | *(No reason line given; the source names only the works.)* |

The last row is the only entry in the whole list that is a **body of literature rather than a book**, the
only one carrying a caveat the source wrote itself, and the only one the source gives **no reason line
for** — it names the works and stops. Reading it as the general body of work behind Part V's
community-building claims is this pack's inference from where those claims sit, not a line the source
wrote. The caveat is the source's, and it is doing real work: Alinsky's organizing tradition supplies the
mechanics behind §21 → `ministry-church-planting` — named roles, explicit norms, bounded asks, the
tyranny of structurelessness — while arriving from a tradition with its own ends. Take the mechanism,
check the end.

## How to read a pointer

Every glossary entry ends with a pointer of the form **§N → `sibling-skill`**. `§N` is the section that
does the work — the argument the term belongs to, the objection it faces, the practice it names — not a
longer restatement of the definition. The skill name is the file to open.

| Sections | Skill | What lives there |
|---|---|---|
| §1–§2 | `ministry-evangelism-persuasion-and-listening` | Evangelism as a communication task, what the persuasion evidence supports, trust, listening, earning the right to be heard |
| §3–§4 | `ministry-evangelism-methods-and-presentations` | Six approaches compared, the integrated approach, the four gospel presentations and their limitations |
| §5 | `ministry-evangelism-ethics` | The disclosure test, persuasion vs manipulation vs coercion, power asymmetry, the hard cases |
| §6 | `ministry-apologetics-arguments-for-god` | What apologetics is, why the case is cumulative, the cosmological, teleological, moral and ontological arguments |
| §7–§8 | `ministry-apologetics-resurrection-and-scripture` | Minimal facts, the five alternative explanations, manuscripts, dating, internal evidence |
| §9–§11 | `ministry-apologetics-evil-objections-and-worldview` | The logical and evidential problems of evil, the theodicies, four objections answered, worldview engagement, the three methods |
| §12–§15 | `ministry-preaching` | Sermon structure and patterns, expository method, the curse of knowledge, delivery and anxiety, the weekly rhythm |
| §16–§18 | `ministry-teaching-and-discipleship` | Small groups, questioning, the four-stage discipleship process, spiritual disciplines, what the learning evidence supports |

## What this section is not saying

- **Not a rule against emotion, or against asking for a decision.** The source's objections are to
  *manufactured* pressure and *false* urgency. The adjectives are carrying the argument; strip them
  and you have misread the section.
- **Not a claim that ethical constraint costs you effectiveness.** The persuasion material points the
  other way — listening first produces durable change, and more forceful assertion persuades less
  than is assumed (§1–§2 → `ministry-evangelism-persuasion-and-listening`).
- **Not a theory that conversion is an outcome technique produces.** The reference is explicit
  elsewhere that conversion is ultimately the work of the Holy Spirit rather than the persuader
  (§6 → `ministry-apologetics-arguments-for-god`). That is a theological claim of the tradition this
  reference is written from, not an empirical finding, and it is what makes manufactured pressure
  incoherent on the tradition's own terms as well as unethical.

> **THE DURABILITY CLAIM IS THE SOURCE'S, AND IT IS NOT QUANTIFIED**
>
> "It produces decisions that do not last" and "it stops working if the target sees the mechanism"
> are empirical claims. The source offers no study, no figure and no effect size for either, and
> neither should you — the directional claim is what is on offer. The same applies to the persuasion
> findings this section leans on: they are stated directionally in §1 and are worth no more than
> that when quoted.

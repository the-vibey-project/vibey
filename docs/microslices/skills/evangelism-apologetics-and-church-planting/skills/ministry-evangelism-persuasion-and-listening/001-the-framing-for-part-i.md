## The framing for Part I

Evangelism is **the communication of the gospel message with the intent to invite someone to follow
Jesus Christ**. It is a privilege and a responsibility — and it is also a communication task. That
last clause is what makes this part of the reference possible: because evangelism is communication,
the evidence on what persuades, what builds trust, and what constitutes ethical influence applies
to it directly.

That evidence points consistently at **substance over technique**: relevant content, genuine
engagement with the other person's actual position, and conversation rather than broadcast. The
source is candid that this is both reassuring and challenging — **reassuring because you do not need
a system, challenging because you need genuine relationship**, and relationship cannot be scheduled
into a campaign.

The ethics of that influence are not an appendix to this. They are §5 → `ministry-evangelism-ethics`,
and the disclosure test there governs everything in this skill: a technique that only works because
the other person cannot see it is not on the table, however effective it is.

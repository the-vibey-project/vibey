## §19 Church planting models

| Model | Description | Strengths | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apostolic/Pioneering | Planter starts from zero with no existing group; builds from new converts and transfers | Reaches the unchurched directly; high contextual relevance; entrepreneurial | Slow; high risk; requires exceptional leadership and resilience |
| Daughter/Satellite | An existing church sends a group and a planter to start a new congregation | Shared resources; existing support; proven model; lower risk | Can reproduce the parent church's limitations; less contextual innovation |
| Multi-site | One church expands to multiple locations with shared leadership and brand | Leverages strong teaching; reaches multiple areas; efficient use of gifted communicators | Campus pastors can be under-developed; brand-driven; centralization risk |
| House/Simple church | Small groups meet in homes; no dedicated building; lay-led or bi-vocational | Low cost; intimate community; reproducible; flexible | Limited reach without multiplication; quality control; doctrinal drift risk |
| Team-based | A team of 3–6 leaders plants together rather than a solo planter | Shared load; complementary gifts; built-in community; resilience | Requires team cohesion; slower initial decisions; potential for conflict |
| Bi-vocational | Planter works a secular job while planting; the church supports itself partially | Financially sustainable; engaged with the secular world; models workplace Christianity | Time constraints; burnout risk; slower growth |

The limitations column is not decoration: each is a real cost that surfaces later, usually in the
sustaining phase (§21), and choosing a model is choosing which of those costs you pay. The models are
not mutually exclusive either — team-based and bi-vocational describe how leadership is constituted
and funded, and either can combine with a pioneering or daughter start.

> **THE SOURCE DOES NOT RANK THESE, AND NEITHER SHOULD YOU**
>
> No survival or success figure is attached to any model here, and none should be supplied from
> elsewhere without saying where it came from — comparative outcome data is thin, self-reported and
> confounded by who chooses which model in which context. Match the model to the context, the sending
> relationship and the gifts actually present.

## §7. ⚠️ Identification — and a Hard Limit

> **⚠️ STOP. Never eat a plant or fungus identified from any text description, including
> this one. This is not liability boilerplate.**
> ⚠️ **The lethal lookalikes are genuinely difficult**: **water hemlock and poison hemlock
> resemble edible members of the carrot family, and water hemlock is among the most toxic
> plants in North America.** **Death cap and destroying angel mushrooms resemble edible
> species and cause liver failure with a symptom-free window that makes people think
> they're fine.** ⚠️ **Pokeweed, nightshades and many others are commonly misjudged.**
> **⚠️ What is actually required**: **multiple keyed features verified against a regional
> dichotomous key, cross-checked with a second independent source, ideally confirmed in
> person by someone experienced with that species in your region.** **Photo-ID apps are
> useful for narrowing and are NOT sufficient for consumption decisions.**

**⚠️ Taxonomy basics worth knowing**: **binomial nomenclature (Genus species), the
hierarchy, and ⚠️ why common names are unreliable — "cedar" means different genera in
different regions, and one plant may have a dozen local names.** **Use Latin names when
precision matters.**
**Dichotomous keys, herbarium specimens, and ⚠️ the fact that vegetative features are far
less reliable than floral ones** — **which is why "I identified it from the leaves" is
weak evidence.**

## §33. Method

**§1–§27 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`, `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`, `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting` rest on stable material** — **plant physiology, soil science, ruminant nutrition,
population ecology, and long-standing safety practice** — sourced from §31. ⚠️ **None of
it needed verification; RuBisCO, Liebig's law and the rumen have not changed.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **H5N1 in dairy cattle** and **chronic wasting
disease** — ⚠️ **the two live situations in this domain that actually change what a
producer or hunter does this season.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, §17 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare` and §22 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`, which are the mechanistic cores. ⚠️ **"You
feed the rumen microbes, not the cow" and "overgrazing is a function of time, not animal
numbers" are the two ideas that most change how someone manages livestock**, **and both
follow from mechanism rather than from tradition.**

**High** in §24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`'s antlerless-harvest point, which I'd defend firmly — ⚠️ **it is
well-established management science and it remains one of the most resisted
recommendations in the field.**

**High** in §28.1's facts, which come substantially from primary agency sources — **USDA
APHIS, FDA, CDC and a CDC *Emerging Infectious Diseases* paper.** ⚠️ **The most
misunderstood item is the 36% retail milk figure: it indicated widespread undetected herd
infection, and it is viral RNA, not infectious virus.** **I've stated that explicitly
because the number circulates without the distinction.** ⚠️ **The waterfowl point is also
counterintuitive and well-sourced — USDA says genomic and epidemiological data do not
support direct migratory-bird seeding of premises, which redirects attention to equipment
and people, where control is actually possible.**

⚠️ **§28.2 contains a numerical disagreement I've flagged rather than resolved.** **CDC
says 36 states (January 2026); a state agency says 37 states plus provinces and four other
countries (July 2026); another 2026 source says 33 states for free-ranging cervids.**
**The spread is explained largely by captive-versus-wild counting and by
detection-versus-establishment, and I've directed the reader to their own state agency.**

⚠️ **The most important item in §28.2 is not a number**: **available CWD tests are not food
safety tests, and results read "no prions detected" rather than "safe."** **That is
Virginia DWR's own framing and it is the thing hunters most often get wrong.** ⚠️ **I've
presented the human-risk question as genuinely unresolved — no human case has ever been
reported, and every public health body still recommends avoiding exposure — and included
the 2026 interspecies-transmission lab study with the authors' own caution that it does
not confirm human risk.** **Overstating and dismissing are both errors here.**

⚠️ **Two places where I deliberately declined to be authoritative**: **§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science` (plant ID for
consumption) and §25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting` (hunting regulations).** **In both cases a confident text answer is
the failure mode, and the correct output is a pointer to a regional key or a state
agency.**

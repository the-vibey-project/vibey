## §27. ⚠️ Field Care and Game Meat Safety

**⚠️ Meat quality is decided in the first two hours, and the enemy is heat.**
```
1. ⚠️ FIELD DRESS PROMPTLY. Body cavity heat is what spoils meat
2. ⚠️ Avoid puncturing the digestive tract; if you do, trim generously
   and rinse — GI contents are the main contamination source
3. ⚠️ COOL FAST. Get the carcass below ~40°F/4°C as quickly as possible.
   ⚠️ In warm weather this may mean quartering and icing within hours,
   NOT hanging
4. AGING requires CONTROLLED refrigeration — ⚠️ "hanging in the shed" in
   50°F+ weather is spoilage, not aging
5. ⚠️ Keep it clean, dry and airflowing; a game bag keeps flies off
```
**⚠️ Personal safety while handling**: **wear gloves — ⚠️ brucellosis, tularemia (rabbits
especially) and leptospirosis are real occupational risks of field dressing.** ⚠️ **Do not
handle or consume an animal that appeared sick or behaved abnormally; report it to your
wildlife agency instead** (§28.2 → `agri-reference`).
**⚠️ Cook game to safe internal temperatures** — **whole-muscle venison to at least
145°F/63°C with rest, ground to 160°F/71°C, and wild birds to 165°F/74°C.**
⚠️ **Trichinella remains a genuine risk in bear and wild pig** — **those must be cooked
thoroughly, and freezing does NOT reliably kill the species found in wild game.**
**⚠️ Lead fragments**: **lead bullets fragment extensively and fragments spread well beyond
the wound channel** — ⚠️ **radiographic studies have found fragments distant from the
visible damage.** **Trim generously around wound channels; ⚠️ many hunters switch to
copper or other non-lead projectiles, which also removes the documented lead-poisoning
risk to scavenging raptors and condors.**

## §12 Hebrew Bible textual criticism

### §12.1 Different problem, different witnesses

The Hebrew Bible situation differs sharply from the NT: the medieval Masoretic manuscripts are remarkably uniform (the Masoretes' counting systems worked), but they are late (10th–11th century), and earlier evidence shows the text was once **plural**.

The witnesses:

- **Masoretic Text (MT)** — the Tiberian manuscripts (Aleppo, Leningrad, etc.).
- **Dead Sea Scrolls** — biblical manuscripts from Qumran and other Judean Desert sites (3rd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE). Some closely match the later MT (proto-MT); others align with the Septuagint's Vorlage or the Samaritan Pentateuch; others are independent. **Emanuel Tov** and **Eugene Ulrich** have been central to describing this pluriformity; Ulrich describes "multiple literary editions" of some books.
- **Samaritan Pentateuch** — a separate Hebrew tradition of the Torah in Samaritan script; around 6,000 differences from MT, most trivial, but it agrees with the LXX against MT in a large number of places and contains some deliberate sectarian changes (e.g., Mount Gerizim).
- **Ancient translations:** Septuagint (the most important), Aramaic Targums, Syriac Peshitta, Latin Vulgate.

### §12.2 Striking Hebrew Bible variants

| Passage | Variants | Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deuteronomy 32:8** | MT: "according to the number of the **sons of Israel**"; 4QDeut^j and LXX: "**sons of God**" | Most scholars see the scroll reading as earlier, reflecting a divine-council worldview; ESV and NRSVue follow it **\[majority\]** |
| **1 Samuel 10:27–11:1** | 4QSam^a contains a paragraph about Nahash the Ammonite gouging out right eyes, absent from MT; Josephus knew it | The NRSV (1989) added it to its main text |
| **1 Samuel 17:4** | Goliath's height: MT "six cubits and a span" (\~9'9"); 4QSam^a and LXX "four cubits and a span" (\~6'9") | A reminder that "the Bible says" depends on which text |
| **Jeremiah** | LXX (and 4QJer^b,d) about one-eighth shorter than MT, different chapter order | Evidence of two literary editions |
| **Psalm 145** | An acrostic missing its *nun* line in MT; present in 11QPs^a and LXX | A textbook example of a lost line recovered |
| **Psalm 22:16 \[17\]** | MT "like a lion (*ka'ari*) my hands and feet"; LXX and some Hebrew witnesses "they dug/pierced" | Famous for its Jewish–Christian polemical history |

### §12.3 Three editorial philosophies for the Hebrew Bible **\[as of 2026\]**

| Project | Base | Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Biblia Hebraica Quinta (BHQ)** | Leningrad Codex | **Diplomatic** — print one real manuscript; put variants and editorial judgments in the apparatus and commentary |
| **Hebrew University Bible Project (HUBP)** | Aleppo Codex | **Diplomatic**, with a far fuller multi-apparatus documentation |
| **The Hebrew Bible: A Critical Edition (HBCE)** (formerly the Oxford Hebrew Bible), led by Ronald Hendel | Reconstructed | **Eclectic** — print the reconstructed earliest inferable text with each choice justified; defended in Hendel's *Steps to a New Edition of the Hebrew Bible* (2016) |

The critique of the eclectic approach is that it prints a text that "never existed"; the critique of the diplomatic approach is that it privileges one medieval manuscript. Some propose a third option (synoptic editions presenting parallel text forms). **\[contested\]**

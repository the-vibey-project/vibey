## §34 Case 3 — Luke 2:1–2 (the census "when Quirinius was governor of Syria")

1. **Textual criticism:** The text is stable — this is not a manuscript problem.
2. **Translation:** Most versions read "the **first** registration, when Quirinius was governor." A minority suggest *prōtē* can mean "**before**" ("before Quirinius was governor"), a rendering noted in some footnotes. **\[minority\]**
3. **History:** Josephus dates Quirinius' census of Judea to **6 CE**, after Archelaus was deposed; Matthew and Luke place Jesus' birth under Herod the Great, who died in **4 BCE** on the usual reckoning (1 BCE on a minority reckoning). Proposed resolutions include an earlier governorship or special role for Quirinius, a registration begun earlier and completed later, the alternative translation above, or simply that Luke erred. **\[contested\]**
4. **Archaeology/epigraphy:** Egyptian papyri document household-registration censuses and return-to-residence requirements in Roman Egypt; a fragmentary Tivoli inscription (*Lapis Tiburtinus*) mentioning a man who governed Syria twice has been claimed for Quirinius, but the name is not preserved. Coins and inscriptions help fix Herod's reign and Roman governors.
5. **Lesson:** Some problems are historical, not textual or linguistic; each discipline has to state what it can and cannot settle.

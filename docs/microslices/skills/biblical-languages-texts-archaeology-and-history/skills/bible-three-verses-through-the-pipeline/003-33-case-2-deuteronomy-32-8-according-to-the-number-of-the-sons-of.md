## §33 Case 2 — Deuteronomy 32:8 ("according to the number of the sons of ...")

1. **Textual criticism:** The Masoretic Text reads "sons of **Israel**"; a Qumran manuscript (4QDeut^j) reads "sons of **God**" (*bene 'elohim*), and the Septuagint tradition reflects a similar reading ("angels/sons of God"). By the principle of the harder reading, scholars widely judge "sons of God" earlier — a later scribe more plausibly changed it to avoid implications of a pantheon than the reverse. **\[majority\]**
2. **Archaeology:** Texts from **Ugarit** (Ras Shamra, discovered in 1928; 14th–13th century BCE) describe the high god El presiding over a council of divine "sons" — providing the ancient Near Eastern conceptual background.
3. **History of religion:** The verse fits the divine-council imagery also seen in Psalm 82; it is central to scholarship on the development of Israelite monotheism.
4. **Translation:** ESV and NRSVue follow "sons of God"; NIV and some others keep "sons of Israel" with a footnote.
5. **Lesson:** The Dead Sea Scrolls did not just confirm the Masoretic Text; in places they **changed** which reading scholars print.

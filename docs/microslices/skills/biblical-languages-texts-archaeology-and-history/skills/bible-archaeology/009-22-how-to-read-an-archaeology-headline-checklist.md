## §22 How to read an archaeology headline (checklist)

1. **Is it peer-reviewed?** Press releases, museum announcements, and ministry websites often precede (or replace) publication.
2. **Who funded and who is interpreting?** Not disqualifying, but relevant.
3. **Is the object from a controlled excavation, or the market?** Unprovenanced = suspect (Part VI — §29–§30 → `bible-forgeries-looting-and-provenance`).
4. **What is the dating basis** — stratigraphy, pottery, radiocarbon (which samples? how modeled?), palaeography?
5. **Is the biblical link a discovery or an interpretation?** "Evidence of King X" usually means "a find consistent with a period in which the Bible places X."
6. **What do other specialists say?** Look for responses in *BASOR*, *Tel Aviv*, *IEJ*, *Levant*, *Radiocarbon*, *NEA*.
7. **Beware of both hype directions:** "Archaeology proves the Bible!" and "Archaeology disproves the Bible!" are both headline genres, not scholarly conclusions.

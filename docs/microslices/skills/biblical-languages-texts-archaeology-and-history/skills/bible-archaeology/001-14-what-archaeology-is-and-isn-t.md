## §14 What archaeology is (and isn't)

Archaeology is the study of the human past through **material remains** — architecture, artifacts, bones, seeds, soils, residues, landscapes — and the **contexts** in which they are found. Context is everything: an object's scientific value comes mainly from knowing exactly where it lay, what was above and below it, and what was found with it. A looted object on the market has lost most of that information permanently.

**What archaeology does not do well:** "prove" or "disprove" the Bible directly. Material remains rarely speak about specific individuals, and they almost never speak about theology. What archaeology does superbly is reconstruct **settlement patterns, economies, technology, diet, trade, destruction events, religious practice, literacy, and chronology** — the world in which biblical texts were written and which they describe. Inscriptions are the exception that can name names.

## §18 The science toolbox

- **Zooarchaeology:** animal bones reveal diet and economy. The near-absence of **pig bones** at Iron Age highland sites is a much-discussed possible marker of Israelite identity (vs Philistine sites with pig) **\[contested as an ethnic marker\]**.
- **Archaeobotany:** seeds, pollen, phytoliths — agriculture, climate, and datable short-lived samples.
- **Residue analysis:** organic traces in vessels (wine, oils, incense, cannabis and frankincense residues on the Tel Arad shrine altars published in 2020).
- **Ancient DNA:** a 2019 study of skeletons from Ashkelon showed an early Iron Age influx of **European-related ancestry** among the Philistines, diluted within a few centuries — matching textual/material hints of Aegean origins.
- **Isotopes** (strontium, oxygen): mobility and origin of individuals and animals.
- **Petrography and chemical sourcing:** where clay, stone, or metal came from.
- **Archaeometallurgy:** the Faynan (Jordan) and Timna (Israel) copper districts, excavated by Thomas Levy and Erez Ben-Yosef, revealed large-scale 10th-century BCE copper production by largely **nomadic** societies — the basis of Ben-Yosef's argument (2019) that archaeology has an "**architectural bias**" that underestimates non-sedentary polities (with implications for Edom and early Israel). **\[contested\]**
- **Microarchaeology:** microscopic sediment analysis (e.g., Weizmann Institute) to identify floors, fires, dung, and site-formation processes invisible to the eye.

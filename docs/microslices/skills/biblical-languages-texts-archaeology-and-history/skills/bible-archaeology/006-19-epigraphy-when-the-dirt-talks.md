## §19 Epigraphy: when the dirt talks

Inscriptions are where archaeology and the biblical text meet most directly. A starter list:

| Inscription | Date | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Merneptah Stele** | c. 1208 BCE | First known mention of "**Israel**" (as a people in Canaan) |
| **Mesha Stele** (Moabite Stone) | c. 840 BCE | Moabite king's victory over Israel (cf. 2 Kings 3); names Omri, YHWH; possibly "house of David" (reading disputed) |
| **Tel Dan Stele** | 9th c. BCE (found 1993–94) | Aramaic; an Aramean king (probably Hazael) claims to have killed a king of Israel and a king of the "**house of David**" (*bytdwd*) — the first widely accepted extra-biblical reference to David's dynasty **\[majority\]** |
| **Kurkh Monolith** | 853 BCE | Shalmaneser III names **Ahab** the Israelite at the Battle of Qarqar |
| **Black Obelisk** | c. 841 BCE | Shows **Jehu** (or his envoy) bowing to Shalmaneser III |
| **Siloam Inscription** | c. 700 BCE | Records the meeting of tunnelers in Hezekiah's tunnel (cf. 2 Kings 20:20) |
| **Sennacherib's Prism** / **Lachish Reliefs** | 701 BCE | Assyrian account of the siege of Judah; Hezekiah shut up "like a bird in a cage" |
| **Ketef Hinnom amulets** | c. 600 BCE | Silver scrolls with a version of the **Priestly Blessing** (Numbers 6:24–26) — the oldest known text overlapping with a biblical passage |
| **Lachish Letters**, **Arad Ostraca** | c. 600–587 BCE | Military correspondence on the eve of the Babylonian conquest; evidence for literacy in Judah's army |
| **Cyrus Cylinder** | 539 BCE | Policy of returning deported peoples and cults (cf. Ezra 1) — though it doesn't mention Jews |
| **Pilate Stone** (Caesarea) | c. 26–36 CE (found 1961) | Names Pontius Pilate as prefect of Judea |
| **Caiaphas ossuary** | 1st c. CE (found 1990) | Probably the family of the high priest |
| **Gallio Inscription** (Delphi) | c. 52 CE | Dates Gallio's proconsulship — the anchor for Paul's chronology (Acts 18:12) |

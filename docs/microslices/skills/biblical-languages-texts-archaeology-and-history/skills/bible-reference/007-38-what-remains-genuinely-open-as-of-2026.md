## §38 What remains genuinely open (as of 2026)

1. Whether biblical texts can be dated linguistically (Hebrew diachrony debate).
2. How the Biblical Hebrew verb encodes tense, aspect, and mood.
3. The limits of verbal aspect in Greek.
4. Whether the "initial text" of the ECM can be equated with the authorial text — and whether "original text" is a coherent target.
5. The final shape of NA29 and ECM (target 2030) — which changes to translation footnotes will follow.
6. Diplomatic vs eclectic editing of the Hebrew Bible.
7. The Iron Age I–IIA chronology and the nature of the 10th-century Judahite polity.
8. The emergence of Israel and the historical memory in the Exodus traditions.
9. The role of AI in field Bible translation — quality, ownership, and community acceptance.
10. The replacement for the criteria of authenticity in historical Jesus research.
11. The significance of new palaeographic dating tools like Enoch.

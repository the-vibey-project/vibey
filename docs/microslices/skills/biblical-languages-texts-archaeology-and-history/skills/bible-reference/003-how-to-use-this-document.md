## How to use this document

This is built as one connected system, not nine separate essays, because that is how the disciplines actually work. A translator cannot choose a word until a textual critic has decided which words are there; a textual critic weighs manuscripts that archaeologists dug up and palaeographers dated; historians use all of it and then argue about what it means. Part VII walks three real verses through the whole pipeline so you can see the gears mesh.

**Confidence markers used throughout**

| Marker | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **\[settled\]** | Broad consensus across confessional and secular scholarship |
| **\[majority\]** | The mainstream position, with a live minority dissent worth knowing |
| **\[contested\]** | Genuinely disputed among competent specialists; no consensus |
| **\[as of 2026\]** | A status fact likely to change (editions in progress, statistics, new finds) |

**Map of the document**

- **Part I — The languages:** Biblical Hebrew (with Aramaic), Biblical Greek (Septuagint and New Testament), Biblical Latin, Biblical German, Biblical English
- **Part II — How translation is actually done** (major-language committees and minority-language field translation)
- **Part III — Textual criticism** (New Testament and Hebrew Bible)
- **Part IV — Archaeology**
- **Part V — History and historians**
- **Part VI — The ethics layer:** forgeries, looting, provenance
- **Part VII — Three verses through the whole pipeline**
- **Part VIII — Learning paths, tools, bibliography, glossary, sources**

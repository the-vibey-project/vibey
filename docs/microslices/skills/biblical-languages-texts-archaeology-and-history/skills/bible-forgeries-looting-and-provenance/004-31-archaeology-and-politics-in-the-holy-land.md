## §31 Archaeology and politics in the Holy Land

- **Jerusalem:** The City of David excavations in Silwan are largely funded by the Elad foundation, which also promotes Jewish settlement in the neighborhood; critics (including some Israeli archaeologists) argue the archaeology is instrumentalized for politics; supporters point to the scientific yield. Both descriptions can be true at once.
- **West Bank excavations:** conducted under Israeli military administration (Staff Officer for Archaeology); international law questions arise over removal of finds and publication. Palestinian heritage authorities have their own programs and claims.
- **The Temple Mount Sifting Project:** sifting debris removed from the Temple Mount during 1999 construction work — a salvage effort born of a political conflict over the site.
- **Nationalism is not new:** Yadin's Masada excavations (1963–65) were intertwined with Israeli national narrative; European archaeology has similar histories. Good practice now emphasizes transparency, publication, local partnership, and community engagement.

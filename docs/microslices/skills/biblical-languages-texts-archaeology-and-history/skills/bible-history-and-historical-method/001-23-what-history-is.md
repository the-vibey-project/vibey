## §23 What history is

**The past** is everything that happened. **History** is the disciplined, evidence-based reconstruction and interpretation of the past — always partial, always revisable, always argued. Historians do not "report" the past; they make **inferences** from surviving traces (texts, objects, landscapes, bodies, data) to claims about what happened, why, and what it meant.

Three features distinguish history from mere chronicle:

1. **Evidence-dependence:** every claim should be traceable to sources, openly cited.
2. **Critical handling of sources:** no source is taken at face value; each is interrogated.
3. **Argument and explanation:** history explains causes, contexts, and meanings, and does so in public, contestable arguments.

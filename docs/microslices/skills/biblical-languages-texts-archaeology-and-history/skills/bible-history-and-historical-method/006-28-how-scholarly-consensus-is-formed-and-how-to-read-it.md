## §28 How scholarly consensus is formed — and how to read it

- **Peer review** in journals (*JBL*, *NTS*, *VT*, *JSOT*, *BASOR*, *IEJ*, *Tel Aviv*) and academic presses.
- **Professional societies:** Society of Biblical Literature (SBL), Society for New Testament Studies (SNTS), International Organization for the Study of the Old Testament (IOSOT), American Schools of Overseas Research / ASOR, Evangelical Theological Society (ETS), Catholic Biblical Association.
- **Institutional plurality:** secular universities, divinity schools, seminaries, and yeshivas. Each has pressures — confessional commitments in some, skeptical group-norms in others. The most reliable conclusions are the ones **shared across** these communities.
- **Reading a consensus claim responsibly:** ask *which* scholars (specialists in that exact subfield?), *how measured* (surveys are rare; "most scholars" is usually an impression), and *what arguments* support it. A consensus is strong evidence but not an argument by itself.

## Executive summary

**Debt consolidation is a payment-structure tool, not a debt-reduction tool, and it works only when income reliably covers the new payment.** Consolidation (a balance-transfer card, a personal loan, a home-secured loan, a 401(k) loan) replaces several debts with one new debt that must be repaid in full; only settlement and bankruptcy reduce what is owed. The best verified evidence shows a short-lived benefit: borrowers in a Federal Reserve study saw card utilization drop about 12 percentage points in the first quarter, but the gap shrank to about 1.7 points within a year and total debt ended up higher ([Dore and Mach, Fed 2019](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2019022pap.pdf)). Dave Ramsey's seven Baby Steps reject consolidation entirely (federal student loan consolidation, case by case, is the lone exception) in favor of a behavioral method, the debt snowball, that independent planners call a good motivational scaffold with shaky technical assumptions. Bankruptcy is the legal reset that Ramsey calls a last resort, and for South Carolina residents it is a mainstream option that the means test, state exemptions, and South Carolina's garnishment limits all shape. EntreLeadership, Ramsey's small-business arm, applies the same no-debt, cash-reserve logic to companies; its useful pieces for a one-person LLC are the money rules, not the team-management machinery.

The key takeaways, in the order they matter:

- **Cost math beats marketing.** On $30,000, a 12% loan over 60 months costs about $10,040 in interest, while the same rate over 84 months costs about $14,485; a lower payment from a longer term can cost more (self-computed amortization, shown in Part 1).
- **Secured consolidation changes who can collect.** Turning card debt into a HELOC, cash-out refinance, or title loan swaps lawsuit risk for foreclosure or repossession risk, and it can convert debt that South Carolina's exemption and garnishment rules make hard to collect into debt that is not.
- **A 401(k) loan becomes taxable if the job ends and the balance is not repaid or rolled over** by the tax filing deadline for that year ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-loans)).
- **South Carolina is unusually debtor-protective on wages and old debt.** State law bars garnishing earnings on consumer credit debts, the statute of limitations on contract debt is three years, and any part-payment on an old debt can revive it under section 15-3-120 ([SC Code 37-5-104](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c005.php); [SC Code Title 15, Ch. 3](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c003.php)).
- **The Chapter 7 means test looks backward six months and counts unemployment compensation but not Social Security**, so a recent income drop can matter a great deal; South Carolina's median for a one-person household is $64,808 for cases filed since April 1, 2026 ([USTP median income table](https://www.justice.gov/ust/eo/bapcpa/20260401/bci_data/median_income_table.htm); [Official Form 122A-1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/form_b122a-1.pdf)).
- **Ramsey's Step 1 is still $1,000 and Step 2 is smallest-to-largest regardless of rate, with tax debt placed first**, and his organization says to pause retirement saving, even the employer match, until the debt is gone ([Step 1](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-1); [Step 2](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-2)).
- **No head-to-head outcome study compares the Baby Steps, consolidation, and bankruptcy.** Every ranking of them, including any in this report, is reasoning from partial evidence.
- **EntreLeadership has no "Business Baby Steps," "EntreLeadership Pyramid," or "Six Steps."** What exists is a 15-chapter 2011 book, a Five Stages of Business model, and Six Drivers of business growth, plus a no-debt rule and a six-month business reserve.
- **For a solo LLC owner, state unemployment rules matter more than most management advice.** South Carolina's Department of Employment and Workforce (DEW) treats self-employment as reportable and potentially disqualifying, and unreported earnings are treated as fraud ([DEW](https://www.dew.sc.gov/individuals/how-unemployment-insurance-works)).

## South Carolina collection law decides how much leverage creditors actually have

This section is where the reader's state matters most. **Wages:** South Carolina's Consumer Protection Code provides that for debts from consumer credit sales, leases, loans, or rental-purchases the creditor "may not attach unpaid earnings" by garnishment, and SCDCA says garnishment is currently prohibited for the collection of most debts ([SC Code 37-5-104](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c005.php); [SCDCA FAQs](https://consumer.sc.gov/consumer-faqs/fair-debt-collection-faqs)). Whether this covers medical bills, non-consumer-credit judgments, child support, taxes, or federal student loans was not researched. Federal law elsewhere caps ordinary garnishment at the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or earnings above 30 times the minimum wage ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa)).

**Exempt income:** South Carolina's exemption statute protects the right to receive, or property traceable to, Social Security, **unemployment compensation**, veterans' and disability benefits, alimony and support, and pension payments from attachment, levy and sale ([SC Code 15-41-30](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c041.php)). Federal rules separately protect directly deposited Social Security, SSI, VA, railroad and federal retirement benefits in bank accounts, generally two months' worth, without the account holder having to claim an exemption; the federal list names Railroad Unemployment Insurance but not state unemployment, so state unemployment protection rests on the South Carolina statute ([31 CFR 212.6](https://ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-212/section-212.6); [CFPB](https://consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-can-i-find-an-attorney-who-specializes-in-elder-law-en-1981)). The popular label "judgment-proof" is not a statutory term. The honest synthesis is that someone living on protected income with no non-exempt assets is hard to collect from on card debt, but still faces lawsuits, judgment liens, collection calls, and credit damage.

**Old debt:** the South Carolina limitations period for a contract action is **three years** (section 15-3-530), and section 15-3-120 states that payment of any part of principal or interest is equivalent to a written promise, reviving the claim ([SC Code Title 15, Ch. 3](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c003.php); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-15/chapter-3/section-15-3-530)). So a token payment on a card more than three years past default can restart a full three years. Including a time-barred debt in a consolidation loan or plan might do the same (inference; no case on a DMP payment was found). A bankruptcy court applying South Carolina law held that merely listing a stale debt in schedules did not revive it ([D.S.C. Bankr. opinion](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-scb-3_15-bk-02896/pdf/USCOURTS-scb-3_15-bk-02896-0.pdf)). Federal Regulation F bars suing or threatening to sue on time-barred debt ([NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/limits-collection-time-barred-debt-and-new-fdcpa-rules)), presumes a violation above seven calls in seven days about a debt ([12 CFR 1006.14](https://ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1006/subpart-B/section-1006.14)), and requires a validation notice within five days of first contact; writing to dispute within 30 days stops collection until proof is sent ([SCDCA FAQs](https://consumer.sc.gov/consumer-faqs/fair-debt-collection-faqs)).

The crucial consolidation implication is that **a new secured loan creates a creditor whose remedies sit outside these protections.** Exempt-income rules do not block foreclosure on a home or repossession of a car. Consolidation also does not provide the automatic stay that bankruptcy does, and it does not stop collectors on debts you leave out.

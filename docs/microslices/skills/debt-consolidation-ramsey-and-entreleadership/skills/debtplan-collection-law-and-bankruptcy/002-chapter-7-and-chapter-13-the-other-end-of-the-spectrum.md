## Chapter 7 and Chapter 13: the other end of the spectrum

Chapter 7 liquidates non-exempt assets and discharges most unsecured debts; over 99% of individual cases not dismissed or converted end in discharge. The means test applies when current monthly income exceeds the state median; abuse is presumed if projected five-year disposable income meets thresholds tied to unsecured debt (25% of it or $10,275, whichever is greater, or $17,150, whichever is lower) ([US Courts Ch. 7](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics)). For cases filed since April 1, 2026, **South Carolina median family income is $64,808 (1 person), $83,761 (2), $95,672 (3), and $116,314 (4)** ([USTP](https://www.justice.gov/ust/eo/bapcpa/20260401/bci_data/median_income_table.htm); [USTP means testing](https://www.justice.gov/ust/means-testing)). "Current monthly income" is the average over the six full months before filing, from all sources; unemployment compensation is entered unless you contend it is a Social Security Act benefit (then listed separately), and Social Security benefits are excluded ([Official Form 122A-1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/form_b122a-1.pdf)). A recent income drop can therefore pull the figure below the median, though the outcome depends on facts and allowed expenses. Above the thresholds, a case may be challenged and the filer may be steered to Chapter 13.

**Exemptions:** South Carolina opted out of the federal exemption scheme ([SC Code 15-41-35](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c041.php)). The inflation-adjusted amounts effective July 1, 2024 were a **$76,125 homestead ($152,250 for multiple owners of one unit), $7,600 vehicle, $6,100 household goods, $1,525 jewelry, $7,600 liquid assets (for debtors not claiming a homestead), $2,275 tools of trade, and a $7,600 wildcard** ([SC Bankruptcy Court](https://www.scb.uscourts.gov/news/reminder-south-carolina-exemption-amount-adjustments)). The statute requires adjustment in even-numbered years, so a July 1, 2026 update exists but was not found; a figure of about $80,125 for the homestead surfaced on a non-official site and is unverified. Nolo reports a 730-day domicile requirement to use current South Carolina exemptions ([Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/south-carolina-bankruptcy-homestead-exemption.html)). Equity above the cap is at risk of trustee liquidation.

**Costs and timeline:** the US Courts list a $245 filing fee, $75 administrative fee, and $15 trustee surcharge for Chapter 7 (total $335), with up to four installments; South Carolina firms quote $338, a discrepancy the research could not reconcile (verify with the court). Attorney fees in South Carolina are quoted at roughly $1,100 to $3,000 for Chapter 7 by a lead-generation site, a weak source, and up to $4,500 for Chapter 13 ([Ascend](https://tryascend.com/bankruptcy/south-carolina/cost)). Pre-filing credit counseling from an agency on the Justice Department's approved list is required within 180 days before filing, and a debtor-education course precedes discharge ([USTP approved list](https://www.justice.gov/ust/list-credit-counseling-agencies-approved-pursuant-11-usc-111)). The meeting of creditors typically occurs 21 to 40 days after filing and discharge generally 60 to 90 days after that meeting. Items not discharged in Chapter 7 include support obligations, certain taxes, certain educational loans and government-guaranteed loans, and willful and malicious injury. Chapter 13 sets a three-to-five-year plan (three years below state median income, five above), with debt limits of $526,700 unsecured and $1,580,125 secured, a $235 filing fee plus $75 administrative fee, and unsecured creditors receiving at least what Chapter 7 would pay ([US Courts Ch. 13](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics)). Not researched: preference or fraud issues from paying selected creditors or taking a new loan shortly before filing, the effect on mortgages and cars, and reaffirmation. Those are exactly the questions for a South Carolina bankruptcy attorney.

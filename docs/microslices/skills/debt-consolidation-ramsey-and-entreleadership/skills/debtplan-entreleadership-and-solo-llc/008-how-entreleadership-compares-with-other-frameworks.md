## How EntreLeadership compares with other frameworks

| Framework | Built for | Core ideas | Fit for a solo LLC |
|---|---|---|---|
| EntreLeadership (Ramsey, 2011) | Small-business owners, first-time managers | Team ownership, no-debt ethos, six-month reserve, Five Stages | Money chapters fit; hiring and culture chapters premature |
| Profit First (Michalowicz) | Owner-operators, including solo | Sales minus Profit equals Expenses; allocate each deposit to Profit, Owner's Pay, Tax and OpEx accounts | Closest conceptual fit; the author's allocation percentages look internally inconsistent, so do not rely on them |
| The E-Myth Revisited (Gerber) | Founder-"technicians" | Entrepreneur, manager, technician roles; work on the business, not in it | Names the founder-as-worker trap |
| Traction / EOS (Wickman) | Leadership teams of small-to-mid firms | Vision/Traction Organizer, Rocks, Level 10 meeting, Scorecard | Heavy for one person (inference) |
| Good to Great / Built to Last (Collins) | Established large companies | Hedgehog concept, Level 5 leadership, BHAGs | Descriptive; Collins himself writes "Correlations, Not Causes" |

Scaling Up, High Output Management, Radical Candor and The Lean Startup are team- or product-oriented and add little for a one-person firm (inference). Sources: [Apple Books](https://books.apple.com/us/book/id441095781); [Penguin Random House, Profit First](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/549696/profit-first-by-mike-michalowicz/); [Mike Michalowicz](https://mikemichalowicz.com/profit-first/); [HarperCollins, E-Myth](https://www.harpercollins.com/products/the-e-myth-revisited-michael-e-gerber); [EOS](https://www.eosworldwide.com/eos-model); [Scaling Up](https://scalingup.com/); [Collins research](https://www.jimcollins.com/books/research.html); [PRH, High Output Management](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/72467/high-output-management-by-andrew-s-grove-former-chairman-and-ceo-of-intel/); [Macmillan, Radical Candor](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781250235374/radicalcandorfullyrevisedupdatededition/); [PRH, Lean Startup](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/210088/the-lean-startup-by-eric-ries/). All adoption counts (EOS, Scaling Up, Profit First) are self-reported by publishers, no independent critique was retrieved for several, and no head-to-head empirical comparison of frameworks exists. The Good to Great critiques (halo effect, later firm failures) rest on earlier Wikipedia-based notes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_to_Great)).

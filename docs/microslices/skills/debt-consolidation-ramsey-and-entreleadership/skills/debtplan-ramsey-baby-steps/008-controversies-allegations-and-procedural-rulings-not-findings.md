## Controversies: allegations and procedural rulings, not findings

The following are plaintiffs' allegations or procedural rulings; none is a finding of liability in the sources read, and none bears on whether the snowball arithmetic works.

**O'Connor v. The Lampo Group (M.D. Tenn.):** the plaintiff alleged she was fired after revealing a pregnancy while unmarried; the company said it enforced a "righteous living" conduct rule and disclosed in a March 2021 filing that it had fired at least eight other employees in five years for premarital sex ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/dave-ramsey-s-company-fires-employees-over-premarital-sex-court-n1262498)). On June 12, 2025 the judge allowed her religious-discrimination claim to proceed ([Religion News Service](https://religionnews.com/2025/06/18/dave-ramseys-company-loses-again-in-court-over-discrimination/)). **The case was dismissed by agreement on December 11, 2025 (Doc. 120)**; settlement terms were not disclosed in the docket sources, and a settlement is an inference ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/17372107/oconnor-v-the-lampo-group-llc/)).

**Amos v. Lampo Group (M.D. Tenn.):** a video editor alleged religious discrimination after his July 2020 firing; the company said the suit has "absolutely no merit" and that he was let go for performance. The district court dismissed in December 2023, and the Sixth Circuit reversed the discrimination claim on August 8, 2024 while affirming dismissal of a fraud claim ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/appeals-court-rules-against-dave-ramseys-company/)). Earlier reporting said trial was set for mid-July 2025. As of the docket, a November 10, 2026 trial was later set, and **Doc. 406 (September 18, 2026) cancelled the trial, pretrial conference and deadlines, to be rescheduled "as soon as feasible"**; the reason is not stated in the order read ([CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/61614266/amos-v-lampo-group-llc-the/); [Doc. 406](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.tnmd.88710/gov.uscourts.tnmd.88710.406.0.pdf)). A trial before 2027 appears unlikely, but no document says so.

**Patrick v. Ramsey (W.D. Wash., 2:23-cv-00630):** 17 former listeners allege Ramsey was paid up to $30 million (2015 to 2021) to endorse Timeshare Exit Team without adequate disclosure and seek over $150 million; Ramsey stopped advertising the firm in 2021 and his lawyers said he was not responsible for details of the relationship ([Religion News Service, 2023](https://religionnews.com/2023/06/01/dave-ramsey-sued-for-150-million-by-former-fans-who-followed-his-timeshare-exit-advice/)). The Ninth Circuit denied his bid to compel arbitration on November 12, 2025; a 60-day extension was granted September 29, 2026, the latest filing seen was October 7, 2026, and no class has been certified ([CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67290865/)).

Two other items are thinly sourced: a former employee's suit alleging she was pushed out after coming out as gay, where the settlement report is single-source (Wikipedia) while a 2021 Nashville Post article reported no resolution ([Nashville Post](https://www.nashvillepost.com/business/legal/former-ramsey-employee-says-she-was-fired-for-coming-out/article_484bdd21-20d7-58b0-bdb8-9045862c482f.html)); and Inc. magazine's removal of the company from its 2020 Best Workplaces list over the conduct policy ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/dave-ramseys-company-dropped-from-best-workplaces-list-by-inc-magazine/)). Several other circulating claims were searched for and not found in any source, including any EEOC action, so none is repeated here. A mortgage-industry author's claim that Ramsey is a "paid lead provider" for a partner lender is an interested party's opinion ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-pro-fact-checks-product-claims-by-dave-ramsey/)).

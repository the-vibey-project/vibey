## Steps 3 through 7 are less contested, with one major exception

**Step 3** is three to six months of essential expenses (three with a steady job, six with variable income), held liquid and uninvested, typically taking 6 to 12 months ([Step 3](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-3)). **Step 4** is 15% of gross household income, excluding the employer match, in tax-advantaged accounts; the order is match, then Roth IRA, then back to the 401(k) ("Match beats Roth beats traditional"), or for those without a match or who are self-employed, Roth IRA first, then SEP-IRA or solo 401(k). The funds are split "evenly" across four categories (growth and income, growth, aggressive growth, international); a 25%-each figure appears in a separate article, not on the Step 4 page ([Step 4](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-4); [Mutual funds](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/retirement/how-to-invest-in-mutual-funds)). **Step 5** covers college: an ESA up to $2,000 per year or a 529, with retirement ahead of college ([Step 5](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-5)). **Step 6** is extra principal-only payments on the mortgage, no cash-out refinance, no 401(k) withdrawals; an example shows a $300,000 mortgage at 5% over 15 years at about $2,373 per month ([Step 6](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-6)). The 25%-of-take-home and 15-year-fixed rules belong to the home-buying article, not the Step 6 page ([How much house](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/real-estate/how-much-house-can-i-afford)). **Step 7** treats 10% giving as a common starting point rather than a rule, and covers wills, trusts, and insurance ([Step 7](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-7)). Insurance is "not a Baby Step" but a protection for every step, and Ramsey recommends level term life insurance of 10 to 12 times income ([Insurance](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/insurance/insurance-and-the-baby-steps)), advice independent planners consider mainstream.

The SmartVestor/RamseyTrusted referral network is attached to Step 4. The program formerly called Endorsed Local Providers is now RamseyTrusted, with nearly 5,000 providers ([RamseyTrusted](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/retirement/how-does-elp-program-work)). Ramsey's own disclosure says SmartVestor Pros pay a $3,000 training fee, $400 per month, territory fees up to $1,050 per month, and, in select territories, up to 25% per year of gross fees from referred clients; Ramsey calls itself a "paid, non-client promoter," acknowledges a conflict of interest, and says it does not monitor Pros' advice ([SmartVestor disclosure](https://ramseysolutions.com/retirement/smartvestor-disclosure)). InvestmentNews reported that Missouri regulators found Lampo acted as an unregistered investment solicitor from 2010 to 2014 and in fall 2016 ordered $50,000 to an investor education fund plus $5,000 costs; Ramsey then restructured the program as advertising ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/rias/advisers-profit-through-association-with-dave-ramsey-but-not-by-as-much-as-in-the-past/71056)). The article's date was not shown in the fetch and its fee figures predate the current disclosure.

## Step 2: the snowball is smallest-to-largest, and tax debt jumps the line

Mechanics: list every debt except the mortgage, smallest balance to largest, ignoring interest rate; pay minimums on all but the smallest; throw everything extra at the smallest; when it is gone, roll its payment into the next. Covered debts include credit cards, student loans, auto loans, medical debt, personal loans, and IRS or back-tax debt. The page gives a typical timeline of 18 to 24 months for most families. The FAQ resolves an earlier ambiguity: **tax debt goes to the top of the snowball "regardless of the balance,"** so the accurate description is smallest-to-largest with tax debt first ([Step 2](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey-7-baby-steps/step-2)). Other stated rules: sell unneeded items and put non-retirement savings above $1,000 toward debt; cut up cards; avoid balance transfers and personal loans; pause 401(k) contributions "even the match" because debt at 7%, 15%, or 20% outweighs assumed 10% to 12% returns; treat retirement funds as untouchable except when facing bankruptcy or foreclosure; pause the snowball if the emergency fund is spent; and, for collections, call creditors, ask about hardship programs, and protect the Four Walls. The FAQ also concedes that the avalanche "saves you a little more" in interest.

### Ramsey's position on every consolidation tool is no, except one

Ramsey Solutions' debt-consolidation article (by Jade Warshaw, published June 2, 2023, updated February 28, 2025) answers "No" for consolidation loans (citing longer payoff, fees, often higher interest, and collateral risk), "No" for balance transfers (fees and rate spikes), and "No" for HELOCs, which it calls a trap. The lone "Maybe" is federal student loan consolidation, "the only form of debt consolidation we recommend," case by case, for example to swap variable for fixed rates; the company notes that it does not lower the rate (the weighted average is rounded up), may forfeit credit toward income-driven plans or PSLF, and is generally irreversible ([Debt consolidation](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/debt-consolidation-truth); [Student loan consolidation](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/should-i-consolidate-my-student-loans)). It says forgiveness and income-driven plans are not to be banked on. The article also says consolidation hurts credit and that the author does not "give a rip" about credit. Credit scores are called an "I love debt" score; the pages advise cutting up cards and say a mortgage is available through manual underwriting ([Truth about debt](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/the-truth-about-debt); [Living without a credit score](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/living-without-credit-score)). A different quoted phrase attributed to Ramsey, "I don't care about your credit score," was not found in a primary source and should not be treated as verified.

### On counseling, settlement, and bankruptcy, the primary text was thin

Per summaries, Ramsey calls credit counseling a "last-ditch effort" (DMPs run three to five years with fees, and nonprofit status is not automatic proof of trustworthiness), settlement "a scam," and bankruptcy a last resort that is "not an easy out," lasts roughly 7 to 10 years on a report, and does not erase student loans, child support, or generally IRS debt. His alternatives to bankruptcy are covering the Four Walls, selling items, a bare-bones budget, a second job, and a coach ([Credit counseling](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/credit-counseling); [Debt relief](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/what-is-debt-relief); [Avoid bankruptcy](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/how-to-avoid-bankruptcy)). In a 2022 column he rejected an attorney's advice to file over a $3,000 debt as bad advice ([Dave Says](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/company/newsroom/ramsey-syndication/2022-12-27)). The research did not retrieve primary text for those pages beyond summaries and snippets, so the details above should be checked live. His own history is relevant context: Ramsey's biography says the bankruptcy was **September 23, 1988**, after real-estate success built on debt, and not the early 1990s that circulates in some accounts; his testimony page says only that he and his wife filed bankruptcy ([Dave bio](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/dave-ramsey); [Testimony](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/dave-ramsey-personal-testimony)).

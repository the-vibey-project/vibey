## When income stops, Ramsey's pages say to protect the Four Walls and slow the plan

For people out of work or in hardship, Ramsey's pages tell readers to cover the Four Walls first, call creditors about hardship options, treat Baby Step 1 budgeting as the priority, and pause the snowball if the emergency fund must be used ([Budgeting after job loss](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/budgeting/budgeting-after-job-loss); [When an emergency exceeds your fund](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/saving/what-to-do-if-emergency-is-more-than-emergency-fund); [If you get laid off](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/career-advice/what-to-do-if-you-get-laid-off); [When you can't pay your bills](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/what-to-do-when-you-cant-pay-your-bills)). The fixed-income article keeps standard budgeting, says saving the first $1,000 may take longer, and names income level as the main obstacle; it does not address disability benefits or Social Security asset rules ([Fixed income](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/debt/debt-free-on-fixed-income)). No page was found on whether the steps formally change for someone out of work beyond this pause guidance. The July 2026 show segment reported by Yahoo (a caller proposing to cash out a 401(k) to clear $20,800) shows the same line: don't cash it out; stop contributing until debt-free ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/dave-ramsey-says-early-401-205108168.html)). That is a secondary report, not a transcript.

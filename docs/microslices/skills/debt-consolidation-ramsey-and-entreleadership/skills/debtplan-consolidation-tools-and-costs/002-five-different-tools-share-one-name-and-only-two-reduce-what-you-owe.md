## Five different tools share one name, and only two reduce what you owe

The Federal Trade Commission describes a debt consolidation loan as combining several debts into one loan with one monthly payment, notes that some versions use your home as collateral, and warns that lenders may charge "points" equal to 1% of the amount borrowed each ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-get-out-debt)). A cleaner taxonomy, assembled from the FTC, CFPB, and credit-bureau sources in the research, separates five approaches. Consolidation replaces several debts with one new credit product and repays everything in full. A debt management plan (DMP) keeps the original debts but has a nonprofit negotiate lower rates and fees, again repaying principal in full. Settlement pays less than owed, usually after payments stop. Bankruptcy discharges eligible debts through a court. Self-directed hardship negotiation with creditors sits alongside these. Only settlement and bankruptcy reduce principal; consolidation and DMPs reduce interest cost and simplify payments. (The research did not retrieve an authoritative definition separating "refinancing" from "consolidation"; the usual distinction is that refinancing replaces one loan and consolidation merges several.)

| Approach | Repays full principal? | Typical cost | Credit effect (sourced) | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Balance transfer card | Yes | 3% to 5% transfer fee | Hard inquiry; utilization can fall | Rate reverts to roughly 15% to 29% after 15 to 21 months |
| Personal loan | Yes | About 6% to 36% APR; online origination fees up to 12% | Moves revolving debt to installment | Re-running up cleared cards |
| Home equity, HELOC, cash-out refi | Yes | About 8.1% to 8.7% (home equity); about 6.75% to 7.375% plus points (cash-out refi) | Scores rose short-term in CFPB study | Foreclosure; refinancing the whole mortgage |
| 401(k) loan | Yes | Lost investment growth; tax if unpaid | None directly | Taxable "deemed distribution" on job loss |
| Nonprofit DMP | Yes | SC fee ceilings of $50 setup, up to $70 per month | Notation not scored negative, but card closures can raise utilization | Cannot cover secured debts; plan must be affordable |
| Settlement company | No (pays less) | 15% to 25% of enrolled debt | NFCC: drops "exceed 100 points in most cases" | Lawsuits, taxable forgiven debt |
| Chapter 7 / Chapter 13 | Chapter 7 discharges; Chapter 13 repays over 3 to 5 years | Court fees plus attorney | Report for up to 10 years (Chapter 7) | Non-exempt assets; some debts survive |

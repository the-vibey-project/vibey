## Student loans, medical debt, and tax debt follow their own rules

Federal student loans are in transition. A court order ended the SAVE plan on March 10, 2026; the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) launched July 1, 2026 with payments of 1% to 10% of earnings and forgiveness after 30 years; loans disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 can use only RAP or the tiered standard plan; PAYE and ICR are scheduled to end by July 1, 2028 ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/student-loans/learn/save-lawsuits); [Attorney Haskell](https://attorneyhaskell.com/blog/student-loan-repayment-changes-2026)). These come from secondary sources because studentaid.gov would not load. Consolidating can change which plans you can use (RAP excludes Parent PLUS and consolidation loans that repaid Parent PLUS debt), so check the servicer before consolidating. Private refinance rates run from just under 4% to roughly 11% to 14% depending on source ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/loans/student-loans/current-interest-rates/); [Credible](https://www.credible.com/refinance-student-loans)); refinancing federal loans into private ones forfeits federal protections, a point the research did not verify from a primary source.

Medical debt: the three credit bureaus voluntarily stopped reporting paid medical debts, those under a year old, and those under $500 in March 2022, and a judge vacated the CFPB's January 2025 rule to remove most medical debt from reports in July 2025; eleven states had their own laws as of June 2025 ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-08-29_IF12169_044474269126c7f04642e141e5ac7130f1beba16.html)). Tax debt has structured options: short-term plans up to 180 days, Simple Payment Plans for assessed balances of $50,000 or less, offers in compromise (not available during an open bankruptcy), and Currently Not Collectible status, which is temporary, with interest and penalties continuing ([IRS Topic 202](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc202)).

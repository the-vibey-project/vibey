## Credit scores: modest dents from consolidation, long shadows from default

myFICO publishes weights of payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%, and declines to publish point effects. A hard inquiry "can lower your score by a few points," counts for scoring for 12 months, and stays on the report two years. Closing a card is not helpful: in myFICO's example, closing an unused $3,000 line on $6,500 of total limits with $2,000 of balances lifts utilization from 30% to 57% ([myFICO weights](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score); [myFICO new credit](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/credit-scores/new-credit); [myFICO FAQ](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/faq/cards/impact-of-closing-credit-card-account)). Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, bankruptcy may stay up to 10 years and collections or adverse items up to 7; Experian reports Chapter 13 for seven years and Chapter 7 for ten ([15 U.S.C. 1681c](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=(title:15%20section:1681c%20edition:prelim)); [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/will-debt-relief-hurt-my-credit-score/)). A study of 90-plus-day delinquency found negative effects on scores, credit limits, homeownership and income lasting up to ten years ([arXiv 2306.00574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00574)). Time-to-recover figures were not sourced.

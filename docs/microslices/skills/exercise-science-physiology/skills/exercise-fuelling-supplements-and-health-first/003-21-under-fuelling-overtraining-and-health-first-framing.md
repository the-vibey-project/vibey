## §21. ⚠️ Under-Fuelling, Overtraining and Health-First Framing

> **⚠️ This section matters more than the optimization sections above it, and it is the
> one most often left out of technical treatments.**

**⚠️ RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)** — **the current framework, expanded from
the earlier female athlete triad because ⚠️ it affects men too.** **Chronic LOW ENERGY
AVAILABILITY — insufficient intake relative to training expenditure — produces
system-wide consequences:**
```
⚠️ Endocrine disruption (⚠️ menstrual dysfunction; suppressed testosterone in men)
⚠️ REDUCED BONE MINERAL DENSITY and stress fracture risk
⚠️ Impaired immune function · GI problems · cardiovascular effects
⚠️ Impaired training adaptation and performance — the thing being pursued
⚠️ Depressed mood, irritability, impaired concentration
```
⚠️ **It occurs at any body size and is NOT diagnosable by appearance.** **It can be
unintentional — simply not eating enough for a training load that increased.**
**⚠️ Overtraining exists on a continuum**: **functional overreaching (planned, recovers in
days, and is part of normal training) → non-functional overreaching (weeks to recover) →
overtraining syndrome (⚠️ months, and it is serious).** **Warning signs: performance
declining despite training, persistent fatigue, disturbed sleep, elevated resting heart
rate or altered HRV, mood change, recurrent illness or injury, loss of motivation.**
⚠️ **The remedy is rest and adequate fuelling, and the instinct — to train harder because
performance is dropping — makes it worse.**

> **⚠️ On disordered patterns, said plainly and without euphemism.** ⚠️ **Exercise and
> nutrition tracking can become compulsive**, **and the traits that make people good at
> training — discipline, tolerance for discomfort, willingness to override signals — are
> the same traits that make this progression hard to notice from inside.**
> **⚠️ Signs worth taking seriously**: **training through injury or illness; distress or
> guilt when a session is missed; exercise crowding out relationships, work or rest;
> rigid food rules that expand over time; rising volume paired with falling
> performance; a sense that the number — on a scale, a lift, a watch — determines
> self-worth.**
> ⚠️ **If several of those are familiar, that's worth raising with a doctor, a registered
> dietitian, or a therapist.** **These are common and treatable, and getting help earlier
> is straightforwardly better than later.** **⚠️ Nothing in the optimization sections above
> is worth acquiring an eating disorder or a stress fracture for, and I'd rather say that
> directly than bury it.**

**⚠️ The health-first framing that the epidemiology actually supports**: ⚠️ **the largest
health returns come from moving from SEDENTARY to SOME activity — the dose-response curve
is steepest at the low end** — **and additional volume beyond moderate levels yields
progressively smaller health returns.** **⚠️ Which means the person with the most to gain
is the one doing nothing, not the one optimizing their fifth weekly session.**

## §4 Force structure, logistics, doctrine, intelligence, and procurement

### Force structure and the permanent trilemma

Each domain does something the others cannot:

| Domain | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| Land | Holds and controls territory and people |
| Naval | Controls access and communication |
| Air | Provides reach and speed |
| Space and cyber | Provide the enablers everything else depends on |

The **permanent trilemma is readiness versus modernization versus force size**. Any budget must
trade among a ready force now, a modern force later, and a large force.

The **tooth-to-tail ratio is persistently misunderstood**. Support is not overhead; it is the thing
that makes combat power sustainable. Sustaining a deployment requires several units for each one
deployed, which is why **deployable strength is a fraction of total strength**.

### Logistics

> **AMATEURS TALK TACTICS; PROFESSIONALS TALK LOGISTICS.** This is a cliché because it keeps being
> demonstrated. Logistics covers fuel, ammunition, food and water, maintenance and spares, medical,
> transport, engineering, and the movement of all of it. **Consumption rates in high-intensity war
> exceed peacetime assumptions by large margins** — peacetime planning factors are a poor guide to
> what a real war consumes.

Three consequences follow, and each is routinely missed:

- **The culminating point is usually a logistics phenomenon.** Offensives stop when they outrun
  supply, not when they run out of courage. Clausewitz's concept and the judgement it demands are at
  §1 → `endeavour-military-theory-levels-of-war-and-deterrence`; the mechanism by which the
  culminating point actually arrives is this one.
- **The industrial base is part of logistics.** It is the ability to replace losses, and in a
  protracted war that matters more than the initial inventory.
- **The overwhelming majority of tonnage moves by sea**, which makes shipping a strategic asset that
  is rarely counted as one.

### Doctrine and institutional learning

Doctrine is shared professional understanding — how *this* military fights, so that units who have
never met can cooperate. **Its function is coordination as much as correctness** — a doctrine
that is merely correct but not shared buys nothing.

> **THE DOCTRINE PARADOX.** Doctrine that is too rigid prevents adaptation; doctrine that is too
> loose prevents coordination. Both failure modes are real.

**Realistic force-on-force training against a thinking opponent** is expensive and repeatedly shown
to be the highest-return investment in effectiveness.

**Lessons-learned systems are where militaries most often fail.** Identifying a lesson is easy.
Changing doctrine, training, procurement and organization to reflect it is slow and threatens
established interests — which is the actual reason lessons go unlearned, not any shortage of
insight. This is the third organizing idea of Part I in operation: **what matters is not who fields
a technology first but who can absorb, integrate and redistribute it fastest.** That is an
organizational capability, and it is much harder to buy than equipment.

### Intelligence

The intelligence cycle — **direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination** — is tidier
than practice.

| Discipline | Source |
|---|---|
| HUMINT | Human sources |
| SIGINT | Intercepted signals |
| IMINT / GEOINT | Imagery and geospatial data |
| MASINT | Measurement and signature data |
| OSINT | Openly available information |

**OSINT's importance has grown enormously** with commercial satellite imagery and social media —
material that was once the preserve of national systems is now purchasable or simply published.

The analytical failures are well studied and none of them are collection problems:

- **Mirror imaging** — assuming the adversary reasons as you do.
- **Confirmation bias** — weighting evidence by how well it fits the held view.
- **Politicization** — analysis bending toward what the customer wants.
- **Signal-to-noise** — the warning indicator was present and buried.

> **STRATEGIC SURPRISE IS USUALLY A FAILURE TO BELIEVE, NOT A FAILURE TO COLLECT.** In case after
> case the warning was collected and reported; it was discounted because it contradicted an existing
> assumption. The corollary is the deception finding: **the most effective deception reinforces what
> the target already expects** rather than trying to plant something new.

### Defence procurement

The structural problem is a market shape, not a personnel failure: **a monopsony buyer, few
suppliers, long development cycles, changing requirements, and political constituencies attached to
production.** That combination produces predictable cost growth and schedule slip, and it does so
across states and across decades.

- **Requirements creep is the dominant technical cause.** Each added requirement is individually
  reasonable; the accumulation is what produces unaffordable programmes.
- **The exquisite-versus-mass debate is the live one** — small numbers of highly capable, expensive
  platforms versus large numbers of cheaper, attritable ones.
- **Surge capacity is absent by construction.** Decades of low-rate production optimized for
  peacetime efficiency produced supply chains with no ability to expand.
- **The innovation mismatch** — procurement cycles measured in decades against technology cycles
  measured in months — **is arguably the central defence-management problem of the period.**

## §3. Weaving

```
WARP  ⚠️ lengthwise, under tension on the loom — stronger, more stable
WEFT (filling)  crosswise
⚠️ SELVEDGE  the self-finished lengthwise edges

BASIC WEAVES
⚠️ PLAIN    over-one-under-one. Most interlacings → firmest, most stable,
   least drape. Poplin, muslin, chiffon, canvas
⚠️ TWILL    diagonal float line. Fewer interlacings → more drape, better
   abrasion resistance, hides soil. ⚠️ Denim, gabardine, chino
⚠️ SATIN    long floats → maximum lustre and drape, ⚠️ poor abrasion
   resistance and snags easily. The floats ARE the weakness
DERIVATIVES  basketweave, rib, herringbone, jacquard (⚠️ patterned by
   individual warp control — the loom that inspired punched cards)
PILE  velvet, corduroy, terry — ⚠️ pile has DIRECTION (nap), which must
   run consistently through a garment or panels will read as different colours
```
**⚠️ The structural point**: ⚠️ **fewer interlacings per unit area = more drape, more
lustre, less stability, less abrasion resistance.** **That single tradeoff explains the
entire weave family.**

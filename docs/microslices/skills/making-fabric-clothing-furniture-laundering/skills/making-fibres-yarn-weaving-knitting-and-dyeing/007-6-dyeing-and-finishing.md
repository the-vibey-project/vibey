## §6. Dyeing and Finishing

```
⚠️ DYE CLASSES ARE FIBRE-SPECIFIC — this is the fact home dyers miss
  REACTIVE   ⚠️ cellulose. Forms a covalent bond — excellent wash-fastness
  DIRECT     cellulose, simple, ⚠️ poor wash-fastness
  ACID       ⚠️ protein (wool, silk) AND nylon. Needs acid + heat
  DISPERSE   ⚠️ polyester ONLY, and needs ~130°C under pressure —
     which is why you CANNOT reliably dye polyester at home
  VAT        ⚠️ indigo. Insoluble; reduced to dye, oxidizes on the fibre —
     which is why denim fades the way it does (the dye sits on the
     surface of the yarn, and abrasion removes it)
STAGE  fibre → yarn → piece → garment dyeing (⚠️ later = cheaper and
   more responsive to fashion, but less even)
```
**⚠️ Fastness types are separate properties**: **wash, light, crock (rubbing), perspiration.**
⚠️ **A dye can be excellent at one and poor at another** — **and "crocking" is why new dark
denim transfers onto upholstery.**
**Finishes**: **mercerization (⚠️ caustic treatment of cotton that increases lustre,
strength and dye uptake), sanforization (pre-shrinking), calendering, brushing/napping,
resin finishes for wrinkle resistance (⚠️ which reduce strength and abrasion resistance),
and water/stain repellents** (§27.2 → `making-reference`).

## §4. Knitting

**⚠️ Knits are LOOPS, not interlacings — which is why they stretch and wovens don't.**
```
⚠️ WEFT KNIT   one continuous yarn across. ⚠️ CAN RUN/LADDER when broken
   Jersey (⚠️ curls at the edges — a structural consequence of stitch
   asymmetry, not a defect), rib (stretches crosswise, doesn't curl),
   interlock (stable, both faces alike), purl
⚠️ WARP KNIT   many yarns lengthwise. ⚠️ Does NOT run. Tricot, raschel
GAUGE  needles per inch — finer gauge, finer fabric
```
**⚠️ Practical consequences**: ⚠️ **knits need stretch stitches or a serger (§11 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`) because a
straight lockstitch will SNAP when the fabric stretches; they generally don't need much
ease (§9 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`) since the fabric provides it; and they grow and recover rather than holding a
shape.**
**⚠️ Hand knitting and crochet** — ⚠️ **crochet uses substantially more yarn than knitting
for the same area and produces a denser, less elastic fabric.** **⚠️ Gauge swatching is not
optional: a half-stitch-per-inch error compounds across a garment into inches.**

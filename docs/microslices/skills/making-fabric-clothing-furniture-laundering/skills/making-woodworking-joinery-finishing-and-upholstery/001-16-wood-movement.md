## §16. ⚠️ Wood Movement

> **⚠️ THE single most important fact in woodworking, and the reason most amateur
> furniture eventually splits itself apart.**
> ⚠️ **Wood absorbs and releases moisture with ambient humidity, FOREVER — a hundred-year-old
> table still moves seasonally.** **⚠️ It moves substantially ACROSS the grain and
> essentially not at all ALONG it.**
> **⚠️ Tangential movement (roughly along the growth rings) is about TWICE the radial
> movement, which is why flatsawn boards cup and quartersawn boards stay flat.**
> **⚠️ A wide solid panel can move by a quarter of an inch or more across its width between
> summer and winter.**
> **⚠️ THEREFORE: never fix a solid wood panel rigidly on all four sides across its grain.**
> **It will either split itself or tear out its fastenings.**

```
⚠️ THE SOLUTIONS, all of which are the same idea
  FRAME AND PANEL  ⚠️ the panel FLOATS in a groove. The oldest and best
     solution to the problem, and why every traditional door looks like that
  BREADBOARD ENDS  attached with elongated holes or a single fixed pin
  ⚠️ TABLETOP FASTENERS  buttons, Z-clips or figure-8s that let the top
     slide relative to the base
  ELONGATED SCREW HOLES  wherever cross-grain fastening is unavoidable
  ⚠️ PLYWOOD / MDF  dimensionally stable — which is the real reason
     sheet goods dominate casework
```
**⚠️ Moisture content**: ⚠️ **acclimatize lumber to the space it will live in BEFORE
building** — **kiln-dried to roughly 6–8% for heated interiors, higher for outdoors.**
**A moisture meter is cheap and prevents expensive failures.**
**⚠️ Reading a board**: **grain direction for planing (⚠️ plane "downhill" or you tear
out), cup, bow, twist, crook; ⚠️ and reaction wood, which moves unpredictably and should
be rejected.**

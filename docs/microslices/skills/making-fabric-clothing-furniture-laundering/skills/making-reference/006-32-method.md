## §32. Method

**§1–§26 → `making-fibres-yarn-weaving-knitting-and-dyeing`, `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`, `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`, `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending` is craft and materials knowledge that has been stable for a long time** — **fibre
properties, weave structures, dye chemistry, grain, wood movement, joinery, and the
mechanics of soil removal.** ⚠️ **Sinner's circle dates to the 1950s and wood has always
moved; none of it needed verification.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **microfibre shedding and filter regulation**
and **PFAS restrictions in textiles and furniture** — ⚠️ **both chosen because they change
what you should DO, not merely what's true: how to wash synthetics, and what a
stain-repellent finish will and won't now do.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §1 → `making-fibres-yarn-weaving-knitting-and-dyeing`, §8 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction` and §16 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`, which are the load-bearing sections. ⚠️ **The
wet-strength inversion (§1 → `making-fibres-yarn-weaving-knitting-and-dyeing`), grain (§8 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`) and wood movement (§16 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`) are the three facts that
prevent the most expensive mistakes in this document** — **and §16 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery` in particular is the
reason most amateur furniture eventually destroys itself.** **§19 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`'s oily-rag warning is the
one genuine safety hazard here and is well documented.**

**High** in §22–§26 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`'s care mechanics, which are standard textile-science and conservation
practice.

**Moderate** on §27.1's magnitudes, and I've said so in the section rather than burying it.
⚠️ **Reported per-wash shedding figures span from about 100,000 to 12 million fibres across
sources, and the widely-quoted 700,000 is one point in that range rather than a
consensus.** ⚠️ **That spread is real and reflects the absence of a standard measurement
method — which is exactly what the IEC standards work reported to conclude by end-2026 is
for.** **The regulatory facts (France's AGEC mandate from 1 January 2025, the vetoed
California bill, the ESPR review) are solid; the effectiveness percentages come from a mix
of vendor, NGO and trade sources and should be treated as indicative.** ⚠️ **Note the
sourcing skew: several sources here sell filters, bags or detergent.**

**High** on §27.2's regulatory facts, which come from multiple law-firm briefings that
agree with each other on the substantive dates and thresholds: ⚠️ **California AB 1817 and
New York effective 1 January 2025, California's 100 ppm total organic fluorine dropping to
50 ppm on 1 January 2027, the outdoor-apparel labelling requirement and 2028 ban, and
REACH's PFHxA restriction from 10 October 2026.** ⚠️ **The state-by-state list is
fast-moving — one source reports nearly 100 new bills in 2026 alone — so I've dated
everything and would verify current status for any specific market.**
⚠️ **The performance consequence in §27.2's gotcha — that PFAS-free replacements repel
water well and oil poorly — is my synthesis from the chemistry rather than a single
citation, and it's the practically useful part: it means §24 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`'s grease-first stain protocol
matters more than it used to.**

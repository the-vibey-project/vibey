## §27. What's Live — verified August 2026

### 27.1 ⚠️ Microfibre shedding: measured, regulated, and the numbers vary wildly
**⚠️ Directly relevant to §22 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending` because it changes how you should wash synthetics.**

- **⚠️ France's AGEC law made it the first country to require microfibre filtration:
  all new washing machines sold in France from 1 January 2025 must include a certified
  microfibre capture solution.**
- **⚠️ The EU picture is in progress.** ⚠️ **A multi-stakeholder IEC group is developing
  standards to MEASURE fibre shedding and filter efficiency, reported to conclude by
  end-2026, with the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation for washing machines
  under review in parallel and microfibre filtration on the table.** **⚠️ Implementing
  texts were reported not yet finalised as of March 2026.** **⚠️ California passed a
  built-in-filter bill in 2023 that cleared both chambers and was vetoed.**
- **⚠️ Scale**: **synthetic textiles are reported as roughly 35% of primary microplastic
  released to marine environments (IUCN), and synthetics make up about 60% of clothing and
  70% of household textiles in the EU.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the shedding figures vary by more than an order of magnitude across
> sources, and you should treat any single number with suspicion.** ⚠️ **Reported ranges
> for one wash of a synthetic load run from ~100,000 to 12 million fibres**, **with one
> source giving 500,000–6,000,000 for a 6 kg synthetic load and the widely-quoted headline
> being 700,000.** **⚠️ That spread reflects genuinely different test conditions — load
> size, fabric type, cycle, machine — and the absence of a standard measurement method,
> which is precisely what the IEC work is trying to fix.** **⚠️ The DIRECTION is
> well-established; the magnitude is not.**

**⚠️ What actually reduces shedding, and it maps onto §22 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`'s levers:**
```
⚠️ FABRIC CHOICE   acrylic reportedly sheds ~5× polyester. ⚠️ Tighter yarns
   and denser constructions shed less — this is a DESIGN lever
⚠️ COLD WATER      reported 30–40% reduction (20–30°C)
⚠️ LESS AGITATION  gentler cycles, ⚠️ fuller loads (less friction per item),
   and washing synthetics LESS OFTEN
⚠️ FILTERS         reported 80–90% retention; ⚠️ in-drum bags (Guppyfriend)
   independently tested at roughly 54%
⚠️ LOWER DRYER HEAT  thermal stress increases shedding
```
**⚠️ Note the trade-off honestly**: ⚠️ **recycled polyester has a real carbon benefit and a
real shedding cost.** **⚠️ And for high-wash items — underwear, base layers, activewear —
switching to wool, cotton or linen eliminates the shedding from those items entirely,
which is the single highest-leverage change since those are the garments washed most.**

### 27.2 ⚠️ PFAS restrictions reached textiles and furniture
**⚠️ This is why stain- and water-repellent finishes (§6 → `making-fibres-yarn-weaving-knitting-and-dyeing`) are changing under you.**

- **⚠️ California AB 1817 and New York's apparel ban both took effect 1 January 2025.**
  ⚠️ **California covers "textile articles" broadly — clothing, accessories, bags,
  curtains, furniture, upholstery, bedding, towels, tablecloths — while New York's is
  limited to apparel.** ⚠️ **California exempts carpets and rugs and PPE.**
- **⚠️ The thresholds tighten**: **California prohibits regulated PFAS at 100 ppm total
  organic fluorine from 2025, ⚠️ dropping to 50 ppm on 1 January 2027.** ⚠️ **Outdoor
  apparel for severe wet conditions is delayed but must be labelled "Made with PFAS
  chemicals" from 2025, with the full ban from 2028.**
- **⚠️ The state patchwork is large and growing.** **Reported: Vermont from 1 January 2026;
  Maine's phased ban targeting apparel categories from 2026; Connecticut restricting most
  uses from 2026 with a full textile and apparel ban 2028; Rhode Island from 2027;
  Colorado's full outdoor-apparel restriction 2028.** ⚠️ **One mid-2026 analysis reports at
  least 18 states with class-based PFAS restrictions on consumer products and nearly 100
  new bills introduced in 2026 alone.**
- **⚠️ Furniture is explicitly in scope in several states** — **reported labelling and
  notification requirements for fabric treatments, textile furnishings and upholstered
  furniture beginning 1 July 2026 in at least one state, with a full ban from 1 January
  2028** — **and Colorado has adopted restrictions on indoor and outdoor furniture.**
- **⚠️ EU**: **REACH Annex XVII restricts PFHxA in apparel textiles from 10 October 2026.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the practical consequence for anyone making or buying, and it's not
> obvious.** ⚠️ **"Durable water repellent" finishes are being reformulated, and the PFAS-free
> replacements generally repel WATER well and repel OIL poorly.** **⚠️ That means the new
> generation of stain-resistant fabrics handles a spilled drink and does NOT handle greasy
> soil the way the old ones did** — **so §24 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`'s grease-first protocol matters more, not
> less.** ⚠️ **Also note this is now a litigation area, with reported spikes in plaintiff
> demand letters over alleged PFAS content, which is why brand claims are getting more
> carefully worded.**

**⚠️ Scope varies enormously state to state** — ⚠️ **California covers all textile articles,
New York only apparel, Maine and Vermont are broad but exclude certain outdoor and
vehicle textiles** — **so "PFAS-free" on a label means different things in different
markets.**

## §28. What's Live — checked August 2026

### 28.1 ⚠️ AI in negotiation and mediation
**⚠️ §22 → `negot-conflict-analysis-mediation-adr-workplace-and-ethics`'s process work being partially automated — with capability findings pointing in two
directions at once.**

- **⚠️ THE GAP BEING TARGETED IS REAL.** ⚠️ **A survey of over 1,000 mediators found 34%
  skip substantive pre-session communications in civil cases, rising to 61% in family cases
  — while the research consistently shows structured preparation improves joint gains and
  satisfaction** (§5 → `negot-framings-batna-zopa-interests-and-preparation`).
- **⚠️ WHAT AI PRE-MEDIATION ACHIEVED.** ⚠️ **A June 2026 study of a structured LLM
  pre-mediation pipeline reported outcomes "broadly comparable to human mediators" on
  short-term self-reported measures including trust in the mediator and confidence in
  reaching agreement, with 36% lower error on the preference-inference task.** ⚠️ **A second
  study found targeted prompt refinements reduced excessive affirmation patterns from 36.6%
  to 16.8%, matching human mediator baselines.**
- ⚠️ **The authors' own framing is modest: structured pipelines can provide "scalable,
  low-effort pre-mediation support" — and the single-party design mirrors how human
  mediators run pre-mediation today, enabling parallel deployment across parties.**
- **⚠️ AND A SEPARATE LINE OF WORK USES LLM AGENTS TO SIMULATE mediation for research**,
  ⚠️ **with one framework reporting patterns consistent with sociological theory including
  group polarization and surface-level consensus.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the same technology is markedly weaker at negotiating FOR you than at
> supporting a process, and the asymmetry matters.** ⚠️ **Stanford HAI reporting on
> agent-to-agent negotiation found weaker seller agents losing up to 14% in profit against
> equally-capable counterparts, with a researcher noting "we all tend to believe that LLM
> agents are really good nowadays, but they are not that trustworthy in a lot of
> high-stakes tasks" — and saying he would not trust an AI to negotiate his own car
> purchase.**
> ⚠️ **The advice reported was to use such tools "with extra caution" and as an information
> search tool rather than as a delegate — plus a call for transparency about AI use.**
> **⚠️ Separately, a 2026 study of LLM agents simulating legal dispute resolution found
> performance varies by role, with judge agents sometimes committing serious legal errors
> when interpreting clauses.**

**⚠️ The reasonable reading**: ⚠️ **AI is showing genuine promise in PREPARATION, structuring
and option generation — the §5 → `negot-framings-batna-zopa-interests-and-preparation` and §8 → `negot-anchoring-concessions-value-creation-and-closing` work — and remains unreliable in adversarial
value-claiming and in roles requiring legal judgement.** ⚠️ **The capability asymmetry
between parties is itself a fairness issue** (§19 → `negot-hard-tactics-multiparty-culture-power-and-remote`).
**⚠️ Sourcing note: these are recent arXiv preprints and conference papers plus one
university news item; the pre-mediation results are self-reported short-term measures on a
specific scenario and prompt set, which the authors state.** ⚠️ **Treat as promising early
evidence, not settled.**

### 28.2 ⚠️ What the first-offer evidence actually says
**⚠️ §6 → `negot-anchoring-concessions-value-creation-and-closing`'s advice, refined by the most comprehensive synthesis available — and the refinement
matters more than the headline.**

- **⚠️ THE OLD BASIS FOR THE ADVICE.** ⚠️ **A meta-analysis by Orr and Guthrie reported a
  correlation approaching 0.5 between first offers and negotiation outcomes — ⚠️ but it
  included only around 19 effects, which is a thin foundation for advice repeated as
  universally as this one has been.**
- **⚠️ THE 2025 SYNTHESIS.** ⚠️ **A comprehensive meta-analysis plus preregistered
  experiments (N = 2,121) — published as "The power and peril of first offers" — replicated
  BOTH the beneficial AND the detrimental meta-analytic effects.** ⚠️ **The title is the
  finding.**
- **⚠️ THE MECHANISMS DIFFER BY OUTCOME, which is the interesting part.** ⚠️ **Selective
  accessibility drove the effect of first-offer magnitude on counteroffers — the classic
  anchoring path.** ⚠️ **But ANGER drove the effects on IMPASSES and on subjective value.**
  ⚠️ **So the same aggressive offer that pulls the counteroffer up also raises the chance
  there is no deal and that the other side leaves unhappy.**
- **⚠️ COMPLEXITY IS THE KEY MODERATOR.** ⚠️ **As the number and type of issues increased,
  the effects of first offers became SMALLER and the mechanisms changed.** ⚠️ **First-offer
  advantage is largely a simple-negotiation phenomenon.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — there are documented conditions under which moving first HURTS, and they are
> exactly the conditions where people feel least certain.** ⚠️ **Research on information
> asymmetry found that for items where negotiators lack a reference value, WAITING for the
> other side's offer produced better results — replicated in subsequent work.**
> ⚠️ **Related findings: extreme first offers offend recipients and raise impasse rates; a
> "too-much-precision" effect where an overly precise opening price caused expert buyers to
> attribute LOWER competence to the seller and reduce their counteroffer; and one study
> finding first-mover economic gain accompanied by longer negotiations, more impasses and
> worse long-term relationships.**
> **⚠️ Note how neatly that last one instantiates §1 → `negot-framings-batna-zopa-interests-and-preparation`'s third organizing idea.**

**⚠️ The revised guidance**: ⚠️ **anchor first when the negotiation is simple, you are
well-informed about value, and the relationship is not the main asset.** ⚠️ **Let them go
first — or focus on issue expansion instead (§8 → `negot-anchoring-concessions-value-creation-and-closing`) — when you are poorly informed, when the
negotiation is genuinely multi-issue, or when impasse and relationship costs are
significant.**
**⚠️ Sourcing note: this rests on a peer-reviewed meta-analysis with preregistered
replications in an established journal, which is a strong evidence type.** ⚠️ **The general
caution from §11 → `negot-biases-emotion-fairness-and-trust` still applies — negotiation psychology has its share of effects that
replicated poorly, and the value here is in the MODERATORS rather than in any point
estimate.**

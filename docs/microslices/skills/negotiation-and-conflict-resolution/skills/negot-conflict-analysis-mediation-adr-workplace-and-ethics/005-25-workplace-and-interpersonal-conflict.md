## §25. ⚠️ Workplace and Interpersonal Conflict

**⚠️ Most workplace conflict is structural rather than personal** — ⚠️ **unclear roles,
conflicting incentives, resource scarcity, and ambiguous authority produce conflict that
gets attributed to personalities.** ⚠️ **The diagnostic question is whether a different
person in the same role would have the same conflict.**
**⚠️ Difficult conversations** (Stone, Patton and Heen's framing): ⚠️ **every one contains
three — WHAT HAPPENED (where both sides have different data and both contributed), FEELINGS
(usually unstated and driving everything), and IDENTITY (what this says about me, which is
why the conversation feels threatening).**
**⚠️ Practical moves**: ⚠️ **describe behaviour and impact rather than character; ⚠️ separate
intent from effect (⚠️ you know your intent and their effect, and never the reverse); ask
what they see; state your interest, not your position** (§4 → `negot-framings-batna-zopa-interests-and-preparation`).
**⚠️ Feedback** works better as ⚠️ **specific, timely, behavioural and forward-looking — and
the evidence on feedback effectiveness is more mixed than management training implies.**
**⚠️ When to escalate**: ⚠️ **safety, legal exposure, repeated bad faith, and genuine power
imbalance are situations where direct resolution is not appropriate and formal process
exists for a reason.**

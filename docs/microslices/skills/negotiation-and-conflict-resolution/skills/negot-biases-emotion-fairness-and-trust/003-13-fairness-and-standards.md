## §13. ⚠️ Fairness and Standards

**⚠️ People will reject profitable deals they consider unfair.** ⚠️ **Ultimatum game results
are robust across cultures: low offers are frequently rejected even at a cost to the
rejector — which means a purely economic model of the counterparty is wrong.**
**⚠️ Distributive fairness has competing norms**, ⚠️ **and this is why fairness arguments
persist: EQUALITY (equal shares), EQUITY (proportional to contribution), and NEED. ⚠️ Each
side sincerely favours the norm that benefits it** (§11).
**⚠️ PROCEDURAL fairness matters independently of outcome** — ⚠️ **people accept unfavourable
results markedly better when they had voice, were treated with respect, and understood the
process.** ⚠️ **This is one of the most robust findings in the field and it is nearly free
to apply.**
**⚠️ Objective standards** — ⚠️ **market rates, precedent, independent valuation, published
formulas — convert a contest of wills into a discussion of what applies, and give both sides
a face-saving rationale.**
**⚠️ Reciprocity** is the underlying engine of §7 → `negot-anchoring-concessions-value-creation-and-closing`, ⚠️ **and it is strong enough that
unreciprocated generosity reads as weakness rather than as goodwill.**

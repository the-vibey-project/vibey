## §11. ⚠️ Biases

```
⚠️ ⚠️ FIXED-PIE BIAS (§2)  ⚠️ assuming conflict where interests
   are compatible. ⚠️ The most costly one
⚠️ ⚠️ REACTIVE DEVALUATION  ⚠️ the SAME proposal is valued LOWER
   when it comes from the other side. ⚠️ Demonstrated
   experimentally, and it explains why proposals rejected at
   the table are accepted when a mediator floats them (§22)
⚠️ ANCHORING (§6) · ⚠️ and it affects the person who SET the
   anchor too
⚠️ ⚠️ OVERCONFIDENCE  ⚠️ in one's own case, in the likely
   outcome of litigation (§23), and in the accuracy of one's
   read of the other side
⚠️ ⚠️ SELF-SERVING FAIRNESS BIAS  ⚠️ people genuinely believe
   the fair outcome is the one that favours them. ⚠️ Not
   cynicism — an honest perceptual distortion, and it is why
   both sides in a dispute can be sincere
⚠️ LOSS AVERSION and FRAMING  ⚠️ the same terms framed as a
   loss from a reference point are resisted far more than when
   framed as a smaller gain — ⚠️ so the reference point is
   itself worth negotiating
⚠️ ⚠️ ESCALATION OF COMMITMENT  ⚠️ sunk costs driving continued
   investment in a failing negotiation or conflict (§21)
⚠️ ⚠️ AND THE HONEST CAVEAT  ⚠️ some negotiation and
   priming-adjacent findings have replicated poorly in the
   broader psychology literature. ⚠️ The effects above are
   among the better-supported, and specific effect SIZES
   should be held loosely (§28.2)
```

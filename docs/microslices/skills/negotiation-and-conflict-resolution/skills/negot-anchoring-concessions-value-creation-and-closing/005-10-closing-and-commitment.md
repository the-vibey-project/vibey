## §10. Closing and Commitment

**⚠️ Commitment devices** — ⚠️ **public statements, mandate limits, published policies —
work by making it credible that you CANNOT concede** (see a military reference §8 on the
same logic in deterrence). ⚠️ **The risk is that a commitment you cannot back away from
produces impasse.**
**⚠️ Deadlines** concentrate concessions: ⚠️ **a large share of movement in many negotiations
occurs near the deadline, which means the party with more time pressure is disadvantaged —
and time pressure is often asymmetric and concealed.**
**⚠️ Building a golden bridge** (§8's face point): ⚠️ **make it easy for them to say yes by
giving them a way to explain the outcome to their own side.**
**⚠️ Document precisely and promptly.** ⚠️ **Ambiguity that felt like flexibility at the
table becomes a dispute later — and the party that drafts has real influence over what
"agreed" means.**
**⚠️ Implementation is part of the negotiation**: ⚠️ **an agreement the other side cannot
actually perform is worse than no agreement, and checking feasibility is not a lack of
trust.**

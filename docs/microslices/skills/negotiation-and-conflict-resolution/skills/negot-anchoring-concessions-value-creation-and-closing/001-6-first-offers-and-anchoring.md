## §6. ⚠️ First Offers and Anchoring

**⚠️ The anchoring effect**: ⚠️ **an initial number pulls subsequent judgements toward it,
even when arbitrary — Tversky and Kahneman's finding, applied to negotiation as the claim
that whoever offers first sets the frame.**
**⚠️ The standard advice** is to make an ambitious first offer, ⚠️ **and it is right more
often than not — but the qualifications are substantial and §28.2 → `negot-reference` has the current
evidence.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "always make the first offer" is the most confidently repeated piece of
> negotiation advice and it is conditional.** ⚠️ **It holds best in simple, single-issue,
> distributive negotiations where you have good information about value.** **⚠️ It weakens
> as complexity rises, and it can REVERSE when you are poorly informed about what the item
> is worth — because moving first reveals your ignorance and forgoes the information their
> offer would have given you** (§28.2 → `negot-reference`).

**⚠️ Precision matters as well as magnitude**: ⚠️ **precise numbers signal informedness and
tend to draw smaller counter-adjustments — with the documented caveat of a "too-much-
precision" effect where excessive precision can reduce perceived competence.**
**⚠️ Justify the offer.** ⚠️ **An anchor with a rationale is far more effective than a number
alone, and an extreme offer with no rationale reads as bad faith and raises impasse risk.**
**⚠️ If they anchor first**: ⚠️ **do not counter immediately from their number. Name the
anchor, re-frame around standards or interests, and introduce your own reference point.**

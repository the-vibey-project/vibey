## §12. Social Psychology

**⚠️ This is the subfield the replication crisis hit hardest, and the classic studies need
individual assessment rather than blanket acceptance or blanket rejection.**

**⚠️ Survived, with qualification:**
- **Conformity (Asch)** — **replicates, with cultural and contextual variation.**
- **Obedience (Milgram)** — ⚠️ **the core finding replicates in modified ethical forms,
  but archival work showed the procedure was less standardized than reported, prods were
  improvised, and many participants expressed doubt the shocks were real.** **The
  headline is roughly right; the tidy story is not.**
- **Bystander effect** — **replicates, though ⚠️ field data on real emergencies shows
  intervention is more common than the lab literature suggests.**
- **Minimal group paradigm** — **robust. In-group favouritism arises from arbitrary
  categorization.**
- **Cognitive dissonance** — **broadly holds.**

**⚠️ Substantially damaged:**
- **⚠️ The Stanford Prison Experiment is not usable evidence.** **Tapes and interviews
  showed guards were coached toward brutality, at least one "breakdown" was reported as
  feigned, and it had no control condition.** **It is a demonstration, and a
  methodologically compromised one.**
- **⚠️ Social priming as a research programme** — **elderly-words-cause-slow-walking and
  its many descendants.** **Broad replication failure.**
- **⚠️ Stereotype threat** — **the phenomenon may exist; the large effects claimed, and
  their use to explain population-level achievement gaps, are not well supported by
  replication and show clear signs of publication bias.**
- **⚠️ The IAT** — **measures something, but has poor test-retest reliability for
  individual scoring and weak prediction of individual discriminatory behaviour.**
  ⚠️ **It should not be used as a personal diagnostic, which is precisely how it is
  usually deployed in training.**

**⚠️ The Fundamental Attribution Error is worth keeping** — **overattributing others'
behaviour to disposition and one's own to situation** — ⚠️ **though it's weaker in
collectivist cultures, which is itself a §24 → `psych-sociology-institutions-culture-and-the-weird-problem` lesson.**
**⚠️ Persuasion and influence** (Cialdini's principles) — **the effects are real and the
effect sizes in field settings are typically much smaller than the anecdotes suggest**,
and **several specific studies in that literature have replication problems.**

## §13. Clinical Psychology — and What Actually Works

**⚠️ Diagnosis is categorical by convention, not by nature.** **The DSM/ICD categories are
useful administrative and communicative tools built by committee**, ⚠️ **and they do not
carve nature at its joints** — **comorbidity is the norm rather than the exception,
boundaries with normality are fuzzy and shift between editions, and few categories have
identified biomarkers.** **Dimensional alternatives (HiTOP, RDoC) exist for this reason.**

**⚠️ What the outcome literature actually shows** — **and this is among the more
practically valuable knowledge in this document:**
- **⚠️ Psychotherapy works.** **Effect sizes are moderate and comparable to many medical
  interventions.**
- **⚠️ The DODO BIRD VERDICT: different bona fide therapies produce broadly similar
  outcomes for most common conditions.** **This has held up reasonably well across
  decades and is deeply unpopular with brand-name therapy advocates.**
- **⚠️ COMMON FACTORS explain much of the variance**: **the therapeutic alliance, the
  therapist, client expectancy, and a coherent rationale** — **often more than the
  specific technique.**
- **⚠️ Specific exceptions where technique DOES matter and matters a lot**: **exposure
  therapies for anxiety, OCD and phobias, where exposure-based approaches clearly
  outperform.** **This is the strongest technique-specific finding in the field.**
- **⚠️ Therapist effects are large.** **Which therapist you see predicts outcome better
  than which modality they practice.**
- **⚠️ Therapy has side effects.** **A minority of clients deteriorate, and this is
  under-discussed.**

**⚠️ The chemical imbalance framing of depression is not supported as stated.** **The
simple serotonin-deficiency account has been substantially undermined; antidepressants
have real average benefits that are modest and larger in severe depression, and the
mechanism is not the one in the marketing.** ⚠️ **This is not an argument against
medication — it is an argument against the explanation.**
**⚠️ Regression to the mean is the single most under-appreciated confound in clinical
work.** **People seek help when they are at their worst; they would improve somewhat
regardless.** **Any uncontrolled intervention will therefore look effective.**

> **⚠️ A note on the popular literature.** **Trauma and attachment have become
> enormously popular frames** — ⚠️ **and popular treatments frequently overstate what the
> evidence supports about mechanism, universality and physiological encoding.** **The
> underlying phenomena are real, PTSD is real and treatable, and the confident
> mechanistic stories layered on top often are not.**

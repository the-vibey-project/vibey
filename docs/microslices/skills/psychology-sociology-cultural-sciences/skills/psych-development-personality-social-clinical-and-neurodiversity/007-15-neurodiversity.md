## §15. Neurodiversity

**⚠️ Autism and ADHD are among the more strongly heritable psychiatric-classified
conditions, and both are dimensional rather than categorical** — **traits are continuously
distributed in the population and the diagnostic threshold is a clinical convention drawn
on a continuum.**
**⚠️ The medical and neurodiversity framings are both partially right and the argument
between them is often a category error**: **impairment is real and support needs are real;
so is the observation that much disability arises from environmental mismatch rather than
from the trait itself.** **The social model's core insight — that changing the environment
can reduce disability without changing the person — is well founded and applies
straightforwardly here.**
**⚠️ What the evidence supports:** **co-occurrence is the norm rather than the exception**
(autism and ADHD frequently co-occur, and both with anxiety and mood conditions);
**diagnosis in adulthood is increasingly common and reflects prior underdiagnosis rather
than an epidemic**; ⚠️ **presentation varies widely, and historical diagnostic criteria
were built on a narrow sample, which produced systematic underdiagnosis in girls, women,
and people who mask effectively.**
**⚠️ Executive function differences are central to the ADHD picture and often central to
the autistic one too**, and **support that targets environment and structure generally
outperforms support that targets willpower** — ⚠️ **which is the §11 motivation literature
applied honestly.**
**⚠️ Be sceptical of confident mechanistic accounts.** **The genetics are highly polygenic,
the neuroimaging literature is heterogeneous and has its own replication problems, and
popular single-cause explanations outrun the evidence considerably.**

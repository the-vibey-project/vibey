## §24. Cross-Cultural — the WEIRD Problem

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the single most important methodological finding about psychology as a
> body of knowledge.** **Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan showed that the overwhelming
> majority of psychological research samples come from Western, Educated, Industrialized,
> Rich, Democratic populations** — ⚠️ **and that these populations are frequent OUTLIERS
> on measures where cross-cultural data exists: visual illusions, spatial reasoning,
> fairness in economic games, individualism, analytic vs holistic cognition, moral
> reasoning.** **So "people do X" in most psychology papers means "American undergraduates
> do X," and the generalization to humanity is often unwarranted.**

**⚠️ Hofstede's dimensions — useful and heavily criticized.** **Individualism-collectivism,
power distance, uncertainty avoidance and the rest are the most-cited cultural framework
in business and management.** ⚠️ **The criticisms are serious: the original data came from
IBM employees in the 1960s-70s, nations are treated as culturally homogeneous units,
within-country variation exceeds between-country variation on several dimensions, and the
dimensions are often applied to individuals when they are country-level aggregates —
which is an ecological fallacy.** **Use them as a rough orienting vocabulary, not as
measurement.**
**⚠️ Better-supported cross-cultural findings**: **analytic vs holistic cognitive styles
(Nisbett)**, **tight vs loose cultures (Gelfand — strength of norms and tolerance of
deviance, with a plausible ecological-threat account)**, **and honour vs dignity vs face
cultures.**
**⚠️ Universals do exist**: **some emotional expressions (contested in scope, §8 → `psych-perception-memory-learning-cognition-and-emotion`),
incest avoidance, reciprocity norms, language, music, kinship systems, and religion in
some form.** **The interesting scientific work is at the boundary between the universal
and the variable, not at either pole.**
**Cultural evolution** (Boyd, Richerson, Henrich) — ⚠️ **culture as a genuine inheritance
system subject to selection, with cumulative culture and the ratchet effect as the human
distinctive.** **Henrich's argument that much adaptive cultural knowledge is opaque to its
users — that people follow practices that work without knowing why — is one of the more
striking ideas here.**

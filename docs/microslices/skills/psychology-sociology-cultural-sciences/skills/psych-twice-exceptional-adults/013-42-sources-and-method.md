## §42. Sources and Method

**Primary source.** A twelve-page literature synthesis on giftedness and twice-exceptionality
in adults with co-occurring autism and ADHD, covering definitional history, IQ validity under
adversity, neuroscience, the 2e intersection, overexcitabilities, perfectionism, impostor
feelings and burnout, interpersonal costs, societal context, and workplace and
relationships. **Citations above are given as the source gives them** (author, year, and
journal where it names one) and were not individually re-verified against the primary
papers; the source's framing around one assumed individual was removed, and this part is
written about 2e adults generally.

**Works the source cites** are each named inline where used — author, year, and journal
where the source names one — so §31–§40 double as the bibliography: the Terman and
Hollingworth studies; the frameworks of Renzulli, Sternberg, Gardner, Gagné and the
Columbus Group; Gross; the WISC-V GAI literature (Gilman, Peters, Silverman et al.; Cornoldi
et al.; Raiford et al.; NAGC); the imaging studies of §33; the camouflaging literature of
§35; Dabrowski; Webb; the perfectionism, impostor and burnout studies of §37; SMPY; the
Flynn-effect and reversal studies; Antonakis, House & Simonton; and the family, workplace
and relationship studies of §38 and §40.

**Wider literature added to harden the epistemics, marked where used:** Winkler & Voight
(2016, *Gifted Child Quarterly*) on overexcitabilities; Stricker et al. (2020, *Educational
Psychology Review*) on giftedness and perfectionism; meta-analyses of academic acceleration;
Sasson et al. (2017), Milton (2012) and Crompton et al. (2020) on first impressions and the
double empathy problem; cognitive epidemiology on intelligence and health; general
psychometrics on index scatter, measurement error, ceilings and regression to the mean; the
Javits appropriations record and Singapore's 2023 programme announcement.

**Method.** Each claim was sorted into research finding, small-sample result, clinical lore,
or the source's own inference, and labelled with the §1.3 → `psych-epistemic-situation-and-replication-reform`
gradient extended as in §30.1. ⚠️ **The tone is deliberately neutral**: much of the 2e
literature is written as advocacy, and advocacy is not evidence in either direction.
**Confidence**: high in the psychometric cautions (§31.1, §32) and in the separate-condition
findings drawn from §14–§15; moderate in the masking and burnout material; low in any
claim specific to adult 2e populations, because the studies mostly do not exist.

## §32. What IQ Scores Show — and Hide — Under Adversity

**⚠️ For a 2e adult the question is not "is IQ valid" (it is, at the population level, §14 →
`psych-development-personality-social-clinical-and-neurodiversity`) but "what does THIS score,
taken in THIS state, tell you".**

### 32.1 State effects on measured performance
- **Depression** depresses Performance IQ and Processing Speed more than Verbal IQ
  (Sackeim et al., 1992) **[MODERATE]**.
- **Chronic stress** impairs cognitive flexibility, working memory and attentional shifting,
  with sustained HPA-axis activation as the proposed pathway (Stenfors et al., 2013)
  **[MODERATE]** — the association is well supported; the mechanism less so.
- **Childhood PTSD** is associated with lower measured IQ, with deficits that appear to
  follow trauma rather than precede it (De Bellis et al., 2009) **[MODERATE]**.

### 32.2 Index scatter and the General Ability Index
- **In gifted children with ADHD or autism, Verbal Comprehension and Fluid Reasoning run
  well above Processing Speed** — in one 2026 sample (Gilman, Peters, Silverman et al.,
  N=390), mean VCI ≈132 against PSI ≈106, a gap of nearly 26 points **[MODERATE]** — a
  clinical sample, recently published, not yet independently replicated.
- **The General Ability Index (GAI)** omits Processing Speed and Working Memory. In the same
  study mean FSIQ was 126.6 and mean GAI 128.9. Among gifted children with ADHD, GAI–PSI
  discrepancies were roughly twice those of ADHD-only children (Cornoldi et al., 2023)
  **[MODERATE]**.
- **Practice has moved accordingly.** WISC-V Technical Report #5 (Raiford, Silverman, Gilman
  & Courville, 2019) set out an Expanded General Ability Index, and NAGC's 2018 position
  statement said the Full Scale score may disadvantage such students **[VERSIONED — a
  publisher report and an advocacy position, not an independent validation]**.

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the scatter is real and it is not a diagnostic signature.**
> **Large gaps between a person's highest and lowest index scores are common in the general
> norming samples too**, and they grow with overall ability. A 25-point spread is evidence
> that a composite hides structure — a sound reason to report index scores and the GAI —
> ⚠️ **not evidence of 2e status, and not a measurement of "true" ability.** Note also that
> the group-level FSIQ–GAI difference in the study above is about two points; the large
> discrepancies are at index level.

### 32.3 Claims in the source that outrun the data
- **"Masking and executive dysfunction can suppress measured IQ by 15–25 points"** —
  **[WEAK]**: no study is cited for the figure. State effects are real (§32.1); this
  magnitude for this population is unestablished.
- **"A score taken during burnout should be read as a floor, not a ceiling"** —
  **[INTERPRETATION]**. Reasonable caution about *one* direction of error, but ⚠️ **measurement
  error runs both ways**, and for very high scorers **regression to the mean** predicts that a
  retest will usually come out *lower*, not higher. The defensible version: **a score obtained
  in a depleted state is a lower-confidence estimate; note the state in the report, prefer
  index-level interpretation, and retest when circumstances change.**
- **"True" or "trait" IQ** as a fixed hidden number **[INTERPRETATION]** — a score is an
  estimate with an error band, under specific conditions; there is no directly observable
  true value to compare it with.

## §40. Families, Workplaces — and What the Evidence Supports Doing

### 40.1 Families
- **Parentification** of gifted children into problem-solver or regulator roles, with
  Hooper (2007) linking parentification generally to later complex-trauma symptoms
  **[CLINICAL LORE for gifted children]** — that literature is not about gifted samples.
  Sibling effects of the label (Colangelo & Brower, 1987) **[WEAK — old, small]**.
- **"But you're so smart"** — the assumption of effortless success that reads executive
  failure as unwillingness **[CLINICAL LORE]**: often reported, never measured.

### 40.2 Workplaces
- **Persson (2009, *Gifted and Talented International*)**, 287 Mensa members: highest job
  satisfaction among the self-employed or those with substantial autonomy; dissatisfaction
  from unchallenging tasks, unsuitable supervisors and colleague friction **[WEAK — Mensa
  sample]**.
- **Nauta and Corten (2002)** — a dual-perception chart: where the environment sees
  "arrogant" or "hard to manage", the employee experiences seeing the answer clearly and
  needing autonomy **[CLINICAL LORE — a practitioner framework]**.
- **A 2022 *Frontiers in Psychology* systematic review (40 studies)** — gifted adults have
  higher occupational status and income but report less enjoyment and meaning in work
  **[MODERATE]**.
- **The 2e inconsistency puzzle** — complex work done brilliantly, routine administration
  missed, the gap read as a moral failing **[CLINICAL LORE]**.

### 40.3 What the evidence supports doing
**⚠️ Ordered by the strength of what stands behind each item, not by appeal.**

1. **Assess the separate conditions properly** — by a clinician experienced with
   high-ability adults; ask for **index scores and the GAI**, not only a Full Scale IQ, with
   the testing **state** (sleep, depression, burnout) recorded (§32) **[ROBUST basis]**.
2. **Treat co-occurring depression and anxiety as such** — §13 →
   `psych-development-personality-social-clinical-and-neurodiversity`; "existential
   depression" or "positive disintegration" framings are not substitutes **[ROBUST]**.
3. **Change the environment before demanding more willpower** — structure, external cues,
   reduced sensory load, fewer task switches; §15's finding applied **[MODERATE–ROBUST]**.
4. **Seek autonomy and challenge at work; negotiate the routine load** **[WEAK–MODERATE]**.
5. **Seek peers who match on interest and neurotype** — supported by §38.2–§38.3; that peer
   access "transforms outcomes" for adults is **[CLINICAL LORE]**.
6. **Treat communication mismatch as two-way** — name directness as a style, check
   comprehension explicitly (double empathy) **[MODERATE]**.

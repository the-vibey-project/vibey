## §8. ⚠️ Deterrence

```
⚠️ ⚠️ DETERRENCE IS PSYCHOLOGICAL, NOT PHYSICAL. ⚠️ It exists in
   the mind of the opponent, which is why it can fail while
   capability is entirely adequate — and why you cannot verify
   that it is working
⚠️ THE TWO FORMS
   ⚠️ DETERRENCE BY PUNISHMENT  ⚠️ threaten unacceptable costs
   ⚠️ ⚠️ DETERRENCE BY DENIAL  ⚠️ convince the opponent the
      objective cannot be achieved. ⚠️ Generally the more
      credible of the two, because it does not require the
      defender to accept costs of its own
⚠️ ⚠️ THE THREE REQUIREMENTS — all must hold
   ⚠️ CAPABILITY  the means to do what you threaten
   ⚠️ ⚠️ CREDIBILITY  ⚠️ the opponent must believe you WOULD.
      ⚠️ This is the hard one, and it is why the credibility of
      extended deterrence — defending someone ELSE — is the
      central problem of alliance politics (§11)
   ⚠️ COMMUNICATION  they must understand what triggers it
⚠️ ⚠️ COMPELLENCE IS HARDER THAN DETERRENCE (Schelling).
   ⚠️ Deterrence asks someone NOT to act — they can comply
   invisibly and save face. ⚠️ Compellence asks them to act,
   visibly, which requires accepting humiliation. ⚠️ This
   asymmetry is underappreciated and explains many failed
   coercive campaigns
⚠️ THE STABILITY-INSTABILITY PARADOX  ⚠️ stability at the
   nuclear level can ENABLE conflict at lower levels, because
   both sides know it will not escalate
⚠️ ⚠️ THE MEASUREMENT PROBLEM  ⚠️ you cannot observe deterrence
   succeeding. ⚠️ Absence of attack is consistent with
   successful deterrence AND with the opponent never intending
   to attack — which makes deterrence claims nearly unfalsifiable
   and defence budgets correspondingly hard to evaluate
```

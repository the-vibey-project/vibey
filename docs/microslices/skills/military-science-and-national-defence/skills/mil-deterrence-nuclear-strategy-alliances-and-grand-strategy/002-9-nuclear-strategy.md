## §9. ⚠️ Nuclear Strategy

```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE FACT THAT ORGANIZES EVERYTHING: nuclear weapons made
   the costs of great-power war potentially unlimited while
   leaving the political disputes intact
⚠️ THE CONCEPTS
   ⚠️ FIRST and SECOND STRIKE · ⚠️ SURVIVABILITY as the key
      requirement — a force that cannot be destroyed
      preemptively removes the incentive to strike first
   ⚠️ ⚠️ THE TRIAD (land, sea, air) exists for survivability and
      redundancy, ⚠️ with ballistic missile submarines the most
      survivable leg and therefore the core of most postures
   ⚠️ MAD  mutual assured destruction as a CONDITION rather
      than a policy · counterforce vs countervalue targeting ·
      launch on warning and its risks
⚠️ ⚠️ EXTENDED DETERRENCE is the hard problem (§8)  ⚠️ will A
   risk its own cities for B? ⚠️ Forward deployment, nuclear
   sharing and alliance integration exist to make the answer
   credible — and the question never fully closes
⚠️ ⚠️ THE NEAR-MISS RECORD IS THE STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR
   CAUTION. ⚠️ Multiple documented incidents — false warnings,
   training tapes mistaken for attacks, satellite
   misinterpretation — where escalation was averted by
   individual human judgement. ⚠️ Deterrence "working" for
   decades is compatible with having been repeatedly lucky
⚠️ ARMS CONTROL  ⚠️ the treaty architecture built over decades
   has substantially eroded, with several major agreements
   lapsed or abandoned — a genuine and underreported change
⚠️ PROLIFERATION  ⚠️ the NPT bargain, and the observation that
   states seek weapons for security, prestige and domestic
   reasons in varying mixes
⚠️ ⚠️ NEW DESTABILIZERS  ⚠️ hypersonic systems compressing
   decision time, ⚠️ AI in early warning and decision support,
   ⚠️ cyber threats to nuclear command and control, and
   entanglement of conventional and nuclear systems
```

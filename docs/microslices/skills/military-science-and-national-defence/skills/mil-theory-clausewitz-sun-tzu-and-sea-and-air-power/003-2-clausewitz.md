## §2. ⚠️ Clausewitz

> **⚠️ The single most important body of theory here, and the most commonly misquoted.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ "WAR IS THE CONTINUATION OF POLICY BY OTHER MEANS"
   ⚠️ NOT a cynical observation. ⚠️ It is a NORMATIVE CLAIM:
   military force only makes sense as an instrument of a
   political purpose. ⚠️ War pursued for its own logic, or with
   objectives the military instrument cannot deliver, is the
   recurring source of catastrophe
⚠️ ⚠️ THE PARADOXICAL TRINITY — the most useful analytical tool
   ⚠️ 1. PRIMORDIAL VIOLENCE, hatred and enmity — associated
      with the PEOPLE
   ⚠️ 2. CHANCE and probability — the domain of the ARMY and
      its commander
   ⚠️ 3. RATIONAL SUBORDINATION to policy — the GOVERNMENT
   ⚠️ ⚠️ WAR IS UNSTABLE BETWEEN THESE THREE. ⚠️ A strategy that
   ignores any one fails — which is why wars lose domestic
   support, why plans dissolve on contact, and why purely
   political direction that ignores military reality produces
   disasters
⚠️ FRICTION  ⚠️ "everything in war is simple, but the simplest
   thing is difficult." ⚠️ The accumulation of small
   difficulties that separates the plan from the execution
⚠️ THE FOG OF WAR  decision under irreducible uncertainty
⚠️ CENTRE OF GRAVITY  ⚠️ the source of an opponent's strength
   and cohesion — ⚠️ frequently misapplied as "the important
   thing," when Clausewitz meant something more specific about
   what holds the whole together
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CULMINATING POINT  ⚠️ an offensive weakens as it
   advances — extending supply lines, absorbing losses, moving
   away from its base — until the defender becomes stronger.
   ⚠️ Recognizing this point is one of the hardest judgements
   in operational art
⚠️ ⚠️ AND HIS CLAIM THAT DEFENCE IS THE INHERENTLY STRONGER FORM
   of war, though with a negative object — you can preserve but
   not gain. ⚠️ This is directly relevant to §23
```

## §26. What's Live — checked August 2026

### 26.1 ⚠️ What Ukraine has demonstrated — and the argument about it
**⚠️ §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s institutional-learning problem made concrete, with a genuine analytical dispute
that is worth understanding rather than resolving.**

- **⚠️ THE SCALE OF THE SHIFT IS NOT DISPUTED.** ⚠️ **Australian Army research assesses
  tactical uncrewed aerial vehicles as accounting for 60–70% of Russian materiel attrition
  while inflicting approximately 80% of battlefield casualties and providing 86% of
  targeting data available to Ukrainian commanders** — ⚠️ **"performing at twice the combined
  lethality and intelligence utility of every other weapons system."** ⚠️ **NATO figures
  cited by CSIS attribute more than 65% of destroyed Russian tanks to Ukrainian drones.**
- **⚠️ THE ECONOMIC INVERSION IS THE STRATEGIC POINT** (§19 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **cheap systems destroying
  expensive ones at scale, described as a shift from "exquisite scarcity" to "intelligent
  mass" — large numbers of good-enough, networked, attritable systems.**
  ⚠️ **CEPA frames it as "mass is back — in the form of thousands of cheap drones."**
- **⚠️ THE PRODUCTION DIMENSION** (§14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`): ⚠️ **Ukraine reportedly planned 7 million
  military drones in 2026, up from 4 million produced the previous year — which is an
  industrial statistic, not a technological one.**
- **⚠️ CONTESTED AIR AND ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM.** ⚠️ **CSIS notes neither side achieved
  air superiority, contributing to the war's positional and attritional character — and that
  this "contrasts sharply with Western doctrines of air power that evolved from the Gulf
  War"** (§4 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`).
- **⚠️ COMMERCIAL SPACE AS A TACTICAL DEPENDENCY** (§20 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **IFRI assesses that commercial
  satellite communications, navigation and Earth observation now underpin daily operations —
  effectively a "space-enabled Internet of the Battlefield" — while creating vulnerabilities
  through single-provider dependence, electronic warfare exposure and geofencing risk.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — there is a serious argument that the obvious lesson is the wrong one, and it
> deserves weight.** ⚠️ **A Modern War Institute piece argues that drone proliferation has
> "revived and deepened, rather than diminished" reliance on attrition over manoeuvre,
> reaffirming artillery as decisive — and warns that a US Army optimizing for drones could
> lose its manoeuvre advantage.**
> ⚠️ **The deeper version comes from Breaking Defense: what Ukraine actually built was not a
> drone fleet but "an institution capable of absorbing new technology, adapting doctrine and
> returning improved capabilities to the front in weeks rather than years" — and the
> conclusion is that "any government can buy Ukraine's drones. The advantage will belong to
> those who build what Ukraine actually built: a system that learns."**
> **⚠️ That is §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement` and §1 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`'s third organizing idea, stated by a practitioner.**

**⚠️ And the institutional-learning failure is documented rather than alleged.** ⚠️ **The
Lowy Institute notes a 2024 investigation finding key US military study centres treating the
war as one topic among many — with the Marine Corps not having directly interviewed
Ukrainian personnel and the Navy unable to describe its approach — and that Congress
legislated a reporting requirement in the FY2025 NDAA.** ⚠️ **Lowy's own observation is the
sharpest: "that such a requirement needed to be legislated into existence is itself a
measure of the depth of the institutional failure."** ⚠️ **Progress is reported in 2026,
including a US Army force-wide effort to redevelop drone doctrine.**
**⚠️ Sourcing note: this draws on think tanks and military research centres across several
countries with differing institutional positions, which is why I have presented the dispute
rather than a verdict.** ⚠️ **Casualty and attrition percentages originate substantially in
Ukrainian claims and should be read as contested.**

### 26.2 ⚠️ European rearmament and the 5% pledge
**⚠️ §11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s burden-sharing dispute and §18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s industrial-base problem meeting a concrete
commitment.**

- **⚠️ WHAT WAS AGREED.** ⚠️ **At the June 2025 Hague Summit, NATO allies committed to
  investing 5% of GDP annually on defence by 2035, structured in two parts: at least 3.5% of
  GDP for core defence requirements and NATO Capability Targets, plus 1.5% for broader
  defence- and security-related areas.** ⚠️ **This more than doubles the 2% guideline set at
  Wales in 2014.**
- ⚠️ **National roadmaps were due by mid-2026, with a collective progress review scheduled
  for 2029 and the final deadline in 2035.** ⚠️ **The 2026 summit in Ankara was the first
  major test of whether roadmaps represent genuine investment.**
- **⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL CHANGES ARE REAL.** ⚠️ **Germany and Sweden rewrote debt rules to
  accommodate the increase — with Germany's constitutional "debt brake" amendment described
  as perhaps the most consequential structural change the commitment produced.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the spending figure is the least informative part, and several serious
> analysts say so.** ⚠️ **SIPRI's assessment is that the commitment "was primarily about
> sending political messages" while carrying significant fiscal and operational
> implications, and SIPRI researchers have warned specifically about "creative accounting"
> in how the 1.5% category is counted.**
> ⚠️ **DEFENCE COST INFLATION means nominal increases translate into smaller real capability
> gains than the numbers imply — the same arithmetic as an infrastructure reference §27.1.**
> ⚠️ **And a 2026 academic assessment argues that focusing narrowly on expenditure targets
> "risks obscuring more fundamental challenges," concluding that security depends "less on
> uniform spending benchmarks than on effective capabilities, societal resilience and
> coordinated contributions"** (§23 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`).

**⚠️ The industrial base is the binding constraint** (§18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`): ⚠️ **analysis points to
FRAGMENTATION across European producers causing "wasted investments, higher production
costs, the loss of economies of scale, strained supply chains," with underspending having
aged production lines and constrained surge capacity — and European defence firms described
as too small to compete globally.**
**⚠️ The political economy is the other constraint** (§11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, §24 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **high debt and social
spending are identified as the biggest impediments, with "guns or butter" trade-offs likely
to generate electoral grievance, and uneven threat perceptions across members risking wider
internal divides.**
**⚠️ Sourcing note: the pledge structure and dates come from NATO's own Hague Declaration
and topic pages — primary.** ⚠️ **The critical assessments come from SIPRI, academic
economists and think tanks spanning quite different policy positions, including
restraint-oriented ones; I have reported the criticisms because they converge from sources
that disagree about almost everything else.**

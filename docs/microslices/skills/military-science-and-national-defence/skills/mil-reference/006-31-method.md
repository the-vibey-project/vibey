## §31. Method

**§1–§25 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`, `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`, `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law` rests on an established academic and professional literature** — **Clausewitz and
the strategic canon, Schelling on deterrence and coercion, the levels of war, the logistics
literature, military innovation studies, civil-military relations theory, and international
humanitarian law.** ⚠️ **None needed verification; *On War* is from 1832 and Schelling's
work is sixty years old and still the reference.**

**⚠️ On scope: I have deliberately kept this at the strategic and institutional level.**
⚠️ **There is nothing operational, tactical or technical here — no targeting, no weapons
detail, no methods.** ⚠️ **That is not evasion: the questions that actually determine
whether a country is defended well are the ones in Parts II to IV, and they are exactly the
ones public discussion skips in favour of hardware.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **Ukraine's demonstrated lessons** and
**European rearmament** — ⚠️ **the first because §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s institutional-learning problem is
being tested in public and the answer is genuinely disputed, the second because §11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s
burden-sharing argument has become a concrete commitment with measurable milestones.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §5 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles` and §8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, which are the sections I'd most want read.
⚠️ **"Tactical success does not aggregate into strategic success" is the correction that
most improves anyone's reading of a war, in either direction — it explains both how a
militarily dominant power loses and why counting engagements tells you little.**
⚠️ **§8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s point that deterrence is unfalsifiable in principle is the one that most improves
scrutiny of defence budgets: absence of attack is consistent with successful deterrence and
with an opponent who was never going to attack, and no amount of spending resolves that
ambiguity.**
**⚠️ §25 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`'s proportionality definition is the correction I would most want carried into
public argument, because the casualty-comparison misuse is nearly universal and the actual
legal test is quite different.**

**Moderate-to-high** on §26.1. ⚠️ **The scale of the drone shift is corroborated across
research centres in several countries; the specific percentages originate substantially in
Ukrainian claims and I have marked them contested.**
⚠️ **What I have deliberately NOT done is resolve the analytical dispute — because a Modern
War Institute argument that drones have deepened attrition rather than replaced manoeuvre,
and the Breaking Defense argument that the transferable lesson is institutional rather than
technological, are both serious and neither is settled.** **⚠️ The documented
lessons-learned failure, legislated into an NDAA reporting requirement, is the most concrete
evidence in the section.**

**High** on §26.2's facts, which come from NATO's own declaration: ⚠️ **5% by 2035 split
3.5/1.5, roadmaps due mid-2026, review in 2029.**
⚠️ **The critical assessments come from SIPRI, academic economists and think tanks
spanning restraint-oriented and Atlanticist positions — and I have reported them because
they CONVERGE from sources that disagree about nearly everything else, which is the
strongest form of corroboration available on a politically contested subject.**
**⚠️ I have described the debates rather than taking positions on contested defence policy
questions, which are political judgements about interests and risk rather than technical
ones.**

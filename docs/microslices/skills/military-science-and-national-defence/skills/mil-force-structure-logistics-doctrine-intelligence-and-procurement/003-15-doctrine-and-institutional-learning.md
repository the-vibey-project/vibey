## §15. ⚠️ Doctrine and Institutional Learning

> **⚠️ §1 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`'s third organizing idea, and §26.1 → `mil-reference`'s central finding.**
```
⚠️ DOCTRINE IS SHARED PROFESSIONAL UNDERSTANDING — ⚠️ how this
   military fights, so that units who have never met can
   cooperate. ⚠️ Its function is COORDINATION as much as
   correctness
⚠️ ⚠️ THE DOCTRINE PARADOX  ⚠️ doctrine that is too rigid
   prevents adaptation; doctrine that is too loose prevents
   coordination. ⚠️ Both failure modes are observable
⚠️ TRAINING  ⚠️ realistic force-on-force training against a
   thinking opponent is expensive and repeatedly shown to be
   the highest-return investment in effectiveness
⚠️ ⚠️ LESSONS LEARNED SYSTEMS ARE WHERE MILITARIES MOST OFTEN
   FAIL. ⚠️ Identifying a lesson is easy; ⚠️ changing doctrine,
   training, procurement and organization to reflect it is
   slow and threatens established interests
⚠️ ⚠️ MILITARY INNOVATION RESEARCH consistently finds that
   PEACETIME innovation requires either external shock,
   civilian intervention, or a service seeking a new mission —
   ⚠️ and that organizations rarely innovate against their own
   dominant career paths and budget lines
⚠️ ⚠️ THE ADAPTATION SPEED PROBLEM  ⚠️ what matters is not who
   fields a technology first but who can absorb, integrate and
   redistribute it fastest (§26.1). ⚠️ That is an
   ORGANIZATIONAL capability and it is much harder to buy than
   equipment
```

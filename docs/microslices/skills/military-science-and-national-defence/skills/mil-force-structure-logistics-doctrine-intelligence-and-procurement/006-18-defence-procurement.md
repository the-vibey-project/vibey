## §18. ⚠️ Defence Procurement

```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM  ⚠️ a monopsony buyer, few
   suppliers, long development cycles, changing requirements,
   and political constituencies attached to production —
   ⚠️ which produces predictable cost growth and schedule slip
   (the same pattern as an infrastructure reference §20, for
   the same underlying reasons)
⚠️ ⚠️ REQUIREMENTS CREEP is the dominant technical cause.
   ⚠️ Each added requirement is individually reasonable and the
   accumulation is what produces unaffordable programmes
⚠️ ⚠️ THE EXQUISITE-VERSUS-MASS DEBATE is the live one
   ⚠️ Small numbers of highly capable, expensive platforms vs
   large numbers of cheaper, attritable ones
   ⚠️ ⚠️ Ukraine has pushed this argument hard toward mass —
   and §26.1 contains the caveat
⚠️ ⚠️ THE INDUSTRIAL BASE PROBLEM  ⚠️ decades of low-rate
   production optimized for peacetime efficiency produced
   supply chains with NO SURGE CAPACITY. ⚠️ Reconstituting it
   requires sustained, predictable orders — and the political
   system supplies neither reliably
⚠️ ⚠️ THE INNOVATION MISMATCH  ⚠️ procurement cycles measured in
   decades against technology cycles measured in months
   (§26.1). ⚠️ This is arguably the central defence-management
   problem of the period
⚠️ EXPORT CONTROLS and technology transfer (see a
   semiconductor reference §26) · offsets · the political
   economy of the defence industrial constituency
```

## §19. Unmanned Systems

**⚠️ The categories**: ⚠️ **from hand-launched reconnaissance quadcopters through loitering
munitions to large armed systems, plus uncrewed surface and underwater vessels and ground
vehicles.**
**⚠️ What they actually change**, at the level this file operates: ⚠️ **the cost of persistent
surveillance collapses; precision strike is no longer confined to well-funded states;
transparency of the battlefield increases enormously; and the exchange ratio between a cheap
attacker and an expensive target inverts** (§26.1 → `mil-reference`).
**⚠️ Counter-unmanned systems** are the corresponding growth area — ⚠️ **electronic warfare,
directed energy, guns, nets and interceptor drones — with the cost-per-engagement problem
central: using an expensive interceptor against a cheap threat loses the economic exchange
even when it works.**
**⚠️ Autonomy is a spectrum, not a switch**: ⚠️ **remote piloting, automated functions,
terminal guidance and full autonomy differ enormously, and public discussion collapses
them.** ⚠️ **Reporting consistently notes autonomy claims are overstated relative to fielded
reality.**
**⚠️ The autonomous weapons debate** (§25) is genuine and unresolved: ⚠️ **meaningful human
control, accountability for error, and whether a ban is verifiable.**

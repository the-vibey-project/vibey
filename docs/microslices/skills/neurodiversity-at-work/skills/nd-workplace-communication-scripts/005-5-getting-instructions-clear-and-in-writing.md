## 5. Getting instructions clear and in writing

### 5.1 Clarifying ambiguous or implicit instructions
- **General clarifier.** "To confirm scope before I start: the deliverable is [concrete artifact], the deadline is [date], and the success criterion is [measurable outcome]. Is that correct?"
- **When wording is vague.** "Can you give me a concrete example of what good looks like for this? I want to align on the bar before I invest time."
- **When priority is unclear.** "I have [A], [B], and [C] open. If you had to rank them this week, what is the order?"
- **When implicit context is suspected.** "Is there context about the [customer / stakeholder history / prior decisions] I should know before I proceed?"
- **When a request is conditional.** "Just to confirm: if [condition], you want [action], and if not, you want [other action]. Is that the logic?"
- **After a thumbs-up or one-word reply.** "Thanks. To make sure I read this correctly: does that mean approved as written, or approved with the changes we discussed [when]?"

### 5.2 Converting verbal instructions to written
- **In the moment.** "Let me echo this back so we are aligned: [restate]. I will send a short recap by email after this call. Please reply with corrections if I got anything wrong."
- **After the call.** "Recap of our [time] discussion. Decisions: [list]. Action items and owners: [list]. Deadlines: [list]. Open questions: [list]. Please reply 'confirmed' or with edits within [24/48 hours]."
- **Asking for instructions in writing** (mirrors JAN's Written Instructions accommodation, askjan.org/solutions/Written-Instructions.cfm). "I retain and execute on written instructions more reliably. Could you send the requirements for [task] in an email or [chat tool] message? I will use it as my source of truth and reduce the risk of rework."

### 5.3 Asking for context: why versus what/how
- **Asking for the why.** "Before I implement, can you give me the why behind this decision? It helps me make better calls downstream when edge cases come up."
- **Asking for the what/how.** "I have enough on the why. What I need now is the concrete what: the exact inputs, outputs, and acceptance criteria."
- **Asking for stakeholder context.** "Who is the audience for this, and what decision are they trying to make? That will shape how I write it up."

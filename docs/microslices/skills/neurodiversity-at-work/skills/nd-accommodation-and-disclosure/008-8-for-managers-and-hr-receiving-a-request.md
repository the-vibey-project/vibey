## 8. For managers and HR: receiving a request

- **Recognise it.** A request does not need the word ADA or the phrase reasonable accommodation. "I need a change at work because of a medical condition" is enough to start the process (US).
- **Respond promptly and in writing.** Name who will coordinate, and when.
- **Engage in the interactive process.** Discuss the specific adjustments proposed; if one will not work, offer an alternative rather than a flat no.
- **Ask only for what you need.** Reasonable documentation of the need, not full medical records, and not a diagnosis you do not require.
- **Keep it confidential.** Do not tell the team why someone has an adjustment.
- **Expect low cost.** Most of these adjustments cost nothing (§1.2); written instructions, agendas and focus time are good management for everyone.
- **Check effectiveness.** Set a review date; treat a not-effective notice (§6.5) as normal, not as a complaint.
- **Interview panels:** provide questions in writing when asked, keep formats structured, and assess the skill the role needs, not performance under unfamiliar social pressure.

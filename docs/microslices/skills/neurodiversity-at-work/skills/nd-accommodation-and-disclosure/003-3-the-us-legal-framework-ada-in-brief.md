## 3. The US legal framework (ADA) in brief

> **⚠️ US only, and not legal advice.** This summarises the general shape of US federal law. State and local law may add protections. Confirm with HR, an employment lawyer, or the EEOC before relying on it, especially in any adverse-action situation.

### 3.1 Who is covered
- **ADA Title I** (as broadened by the **ADA Amendments Act of 2008**, ADAAA) applies to private employers with **15 or more employees** and to state and local government employers.
- The **Rehabilitation Act** extends similar protections to **federal agencies and federal contractors**.
- Employers must provide **reasonable accommodations** during the application process and on the job, unless doing so would cause **undue hardship**.
- **Autism and ADHD** can qualify as disabilities when they substantially limit major life activities such as concentrating, thinking, communicating, learning or interacting with others. The post-2008 standard makes this threshold easier to meet than it once was. Whether a particular person is covered is an individual, fact-specific question.

### 3.2 How a request works
- **No magic words.** Per JAN's Dos and Don'ts of Disclosure (askjan.org/articles/Dos-and-Don-ts-of-Disclosure.cfm), you only have to let your employer know you need an adjustment or change at work for a reason related to a medical condition. You can use plain English and do not have to mention the ADA or say "reasonable accommodation". JAN's page on recognising requests (askjan.org/articles/Recognizing-an-Accommodation-Request-Under-the-ADA.cfm) makes the same point.
- **No diagnosis required up front.** A request can describe the functional limitation and the adjustment, without naming a condition.
- **The interactive process** is the legal term for the back-and-forth between you and the employer that settles which accommodations are put in place. Best practice is to **propose specific accommodations in writing**.
- **Documentation.** The employer may ask for **reasonable documentation** that you have a covered disability that needs accommodation. It cannot demand your full medical records, and should accept documentation from an appropriate health-care, rehabilitation or vocational professional.
- **Effectiveness is ongoing.** If an agreed accommodation does not work, you can ask to reopen the interactive process (template §6.5).

### 3.3 Disclosure timing and confidentiality
- The ADA does **not** require disclosure at any particular time. You can disclose during an application, after an interview invitation, after an offer, or only when an accommodation is needed on the job.
- **Pre-offer questions designed to draw out disability information are prohibited.**
- Disclosure to coworkers is rarely necessary, and the employer must keep medical information confidential; it is not theirs to share for you.

### 3.4 Deadlines if something goes wrong
- The window to file an ADA discrimination charge with the **EEOC** is generally **180 days**, extended to **300 days** where a state or local agency enforces an equivalent law. Check the current rule promptly; do not wait to see how things play out.

### 3.5 Outside the US
Other jurisdictions have their own frameworks and vocabulary. The UK's Equality Act 2010 framework uses the term "reasonable adjustments", and EU member states have their own national laws. Thresholds, procedures, documentation rules and deadlines all differ. Use a local official advice service or lawyer, and treat §3.1–§3.4 as US-only.

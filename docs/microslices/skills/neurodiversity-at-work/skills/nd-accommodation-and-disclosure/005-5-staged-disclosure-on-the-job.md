## 5. Staged disclosure on the job

A cautious default ladder. Move up a rung only when the rung below has not worked or your needs have grown.

| Stage | When | Action | Who learns what |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. No disclosure** | From day one | Use the everyday scripts and the How I Work one-pager (`nd-workplace-communication-scripts`) | Colleagues learn preferences only |
| **2. Soft disclosure** | Weeks 2–4 | Use §4.2 with your direct manager; ask in writing for the §2.3 starter set | Manager learns there is a medical reason, not what it is |
| **3. Formal interactive process** | Months 2–3, or when triggered | File the §6.1 written request with HR and your manager; reference JAN by URL | HR learns what documentation requires; manager learns the accommodations |
| **4. Ongoing** | Continuous | Check effectiveness; use §6.5 if an accommodation stops working | No new disclosure |

⚠️ The timings are a suggested pacing, not evidence-based thresholds.

### 5.1 Triggers to move from Stage 2 to Stage 3
Move to the formal process if any of these happen:
- **(a)** two reasonable informal requests are not honoured;
- **(b)** a conduct or performance concern is raised that connects to an unmet need;
- **(c)** burnout indicators (sleep disruption, masking feeling more costly, recovery time bleeding into the working week, reduced tolerance for sensory input) stay elevated for **more than four weeks**.

The formal interactive process is your protection. Under US law it does not require the word "ADA", but a written request creates a dated record.

### 5.2 Disclosing to whom
- **Manager:** a personal call. Many people disclose the need (Stage 2) without the label.
- **HR:** the usual recipient of documentation in a formal process.
- **Coworkers:** rarely necessary. Your preferences can be shared without any medical context.

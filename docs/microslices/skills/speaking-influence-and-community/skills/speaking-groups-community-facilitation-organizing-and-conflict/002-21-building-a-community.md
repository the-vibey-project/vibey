## §21. Building a Community

**⚠️ Start with a real purpose and real people** — ⚠️ **not a platform. The tooling question
is downstream and much less important than it feels.**
**⚠️ The founding problem is critical mass**: ⚠️ **an empty room is unattractive, so early
effort goes into a small core who show up reliably, and consistency beats scale.**
**⚠️ Rhythm matters more than content**: ⚠️ **a predictable, sustainable cadence — the same
time, reliably — builds a community faster than occasional excellent events.**
**⚠️ Onboarding is the growth constraint**: ⚠️ **most communities are unintentionally hostile
to newcomers through in-jokes, unexplained context, and nobody assigned to talk to the
person standing alone.** ⚠️ **A named greeter role is a small intervention with a large
effect.**
**⚠️ Explicit norms and a code of conduct**, ⚠️ **with the crucial part being that it is
ENFORCED — an unenforced code is worse than none, because it advertises safety that isn't
there** (§25).
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the founder bottleneck is the most common failure mode.** ⚠️ **A community
> where one person does everything cannot grow, cannot survive that person's absence, and
> quietly discourages others from taking initiative.** **⚠️ Delegating badly early beats
> delegating well later, and succession should be planned before it is needed.**

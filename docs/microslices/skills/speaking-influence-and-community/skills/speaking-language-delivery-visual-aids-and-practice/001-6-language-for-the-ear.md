## §6. Language for the Ear

**⚠️ Written prose and spoken prose are different crafts** (see a prose-craft reference for
the written side).
⚠️ **Shorter sentences; concrete over abstract; active voice; ⚠️ ONE idea per sentence,
because a listener cannot re-parse a nested clause.**
**⚠️ What works aloud**: ⚠️ **repetition (⚠️ which reads as clumsy and lands as emphasis),
parallel structure, deliberate pauses, and short sentences after long ones.**
**⚠️ Jargon** — ⚠️ **define it or drop it, and note that the curse of knowledge (§3 → `speaking-what-the-field-knows-anxiety-audience-and-structure`) makes
you a poor judge of which terms are jargon.**
**⚠️ Numbers need translating**: ⚠️ **an abstract quantity means nothing spoken aloud;
compare it to something the audience can picture.**
**⚠️ Read it aloud in draft.** ⚠️ **Anything you stumble over, the audience will stumble
over — and reading aloud is the single most reliable editing technique for spoken material.**

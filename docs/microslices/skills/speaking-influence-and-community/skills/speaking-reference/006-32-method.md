## §32. Method

**§1–§26 → `speaking-what-the-field-knows-anxiety-audience-and-structure`, `speaking-language-delivery-visual-aids-and-practice`, `speaking-listening-rapport-difficult-conversations-and-negotiation`, `speaking-persuasion-and-the-ethics-of-influence`, `speaking-groups-community-facilitation-organizing-and-conflict` draws on long-established craft and on the more durable parts of the research
literature** — **structure and comprehension, exposure for anxiety, the three-conversations
model, interests versus positions, the tyranny of structurelessness, and the facilitation
practices.** ⚠️ **I have tried throughout to mark which tier a claim sits in (§1 → `speaking-what-the-field-knows-anxiety-audience-and-structure`), because
this field's central problem is confident advice with no evidence behind it.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **the status of famous body-language claims**
and **the persuasion evidence** — ⚠️ **both chosen because they are places where widely
taught material is either wrong or has been overtaken, and where knowing the current state
changes what you should actually do.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §19 → `speaking-persuasion-and-the-ethics-of-influence` and §12 → `speaking-listening-rapport-difficult-conversations-and-negotiation`, which are the sections I'd most want read.
⚠️ **The disclosure test — would this still work if I told them exactly what I was doing? —
is the most useful single tool here, because it converts a vague ethical unease into a
question with an answer.** ⚠️ **And §12 → `speaking-listening-rapport-difficult-conversations-and-negotiation`'s finding that listening is not politeness but the
actual mechanism of durable persuasion is the thing that most changes how people behave once
they believe it.**
**⚠️ §24 → `speaking-groups-community-facilitation-organizing-and-conflict`'s specific bounded ask is the small practical technique with the largest effect on
community sustainability that I know of.**

**High** on §27.1, which is unusually well-documented: ⚠️ **Mehrabian's studies used 30 and
37 participants, single spoken words, and conflicting cues about attitudes — and Mehrabian
himself publicly rejected the general reading.** ⚠️ **The observation I'd most want carried
is the meta-one: I retrieved sources from June 2026 presenting 7-38-55 as fact alongside
sources documenting it as myth, which tells you the correction has not propagated and that
encountering the figure is diagnostic of the source.**
⚠️ **On power posing I have deliberately reported the split — perception effects hold,
hormonal claims did not replicate — rather than the flattened "it's debunked," because that
is what the evidence actually says.**

**Moderate** on §27.2, and deliberately calibrated. ⚠️ **The headline result — AI
out-persuading professional canvassers — is a June 2026 PREPRINT, and I have marked it as
such rather than treating it as established.** ⚠️ **What I hold more firmly, because it
appears across multiple independently published studies, is the set of mechanism findings:
conversation beats static messaging substantially, factual richness rather than
personalization is the primary lever, microtargeting effects are smaller and more contested
than the popular narrative implies, and attitude change and behaviour change can be
uncorrelated.**
⚠️ **That last one is the practically important warning for anyone doing advocacy work, and
it is the finding most likely to be ignored because it is inconvenient.**

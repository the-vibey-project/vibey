## Application to white papers and thought leadership

### The non-fiction narrative arc

**Ordinary World** (reader's current reality): establish the reader's specific problem in concrete, specific terms. Not abstract industry platitudes — the actual experience your reader is having day-to-day.

**Call to Adventure** (insight or data): introduce evidence that the ordinary world is no longer adequate. A new development, a new data point, a new threat. This is where urgency is established.

**Ordeal** (evidence that status quo fails): the crucial missing section in most white papers. Most go directly from problem to solution; they skip the recognition scene where the conventional approach is shown to fail in a specific, named way. This is the analog of the Belly of the Whale. Without it, the solution feels unearned. **The white paper should contain a section in which the reader's current approach is shown failing on its own terms before the new approach is introduced.**

**Elixir** (your approach): the methodology, framework, or insight. Arrives only after the reader has fully understood why the conventional approach fails.

**Return** (transformed reader who knows better): the reader, equipped with the Elixir, sees the world differently. They can now make better decisions, avoid the failure modes, achieve the outcome.

**For thought leadership**: the author may share a personal version of the journey to establish authority — demonstrating they have crossed the threshold themselves — then explicitly transfers the Hero role to the reader. The structural risk is the false Hero trap; the corrective is to make the author's journey instrumental to the reader's, not central in itself.

### Pitch structure as Hero's Journey

- **Ordinary World**: the problem as it exists today, in concrete terms
- **Call to Adventure**: why the problem matters now (urgency trigger)
- **Mentor's Gift**: your insight, framework, or solution
- **Ordeal**: evidence that other approaches have failed (why this is hard)
- **Elixir**: your specific approach and why it works
- **Return**: the world after your solution — measurable, specific, believable

### Product launch as Call to Adventure

The product launch frame: your users' world has changed (disruption), existing approaches are inadequate (Ordeal), your product is the new capability they need (Elixir). The customer is the Hero of this story; your product is the sword they pick up.

## Campbell's 17 stages: the psychological grammar

Campbell organizes under three headings borrowed from Arnold van Gennep's *Les rites de passage* (1909):

### Departure (Separation) — the old self ends

1. **Call to Adventure**: The unconscious is pressing for individuation; the hero's settled persona is no longer adequate. The hero's world is disturbed.
2. **Refusal of the Call**: The ego's defense of the status quo. Campbell: "Walled in boredom, hard work, or 'culture,' the subject loses the power of significant affirmative action and becomes a victim to be saved." A flat refusal ("I can't") is dramatically inert; a refusal articulated through familial duty or professional code creates psychological texture.
3. **Supernatural Aid / Meeting the Mentor**: Activation of the wise old man/woman archetype — an internal protective function projected outward onto a guide. A tangible token is transferred (a lightsaber, a map, a name) that anchors the hero when alone later.
4. **Crossing the First Threshold**: The act of committing irreversibly. Staged as a discrete scene with a clear physical or metaphorical boundary. The prose marks the transition with a change in voice, vocabulary, or rhythm.
5. **Belly of the Whale**: The symbolic death and rebirth — dissolution of the prior self that makes transformation possible. Frobenius described this as the hero entering the monster and emerging transformed.

### Initiation — the ordeal and the boon

6. **Road of Trials**: Externalizes the work of integrating Shadow material. The most common site of "saggy middle" collapse. Pace with the Fichtean approach — escalating crises, each revealing something the protagonist did not previously know.
7. **Meeting with the Goddess**: Encounter with unconditional love; the anima/animus. Not necessarily a female character — the archetype of complete acceptance.
8. **Woman as Temptress**: The seduction of regression — the temptation to abandon the quest for comfort. Two faces of the same encounter with the anima/animus.
9. **Atonement with the Father**: Confronting whatever holds ultimate authority and coming to "at-one-ment" — neither submitting nor rebelling but transcending the need for approval.
10. **Apotheosis**: Expanded consciousness. The moment of divine understanding.
11. **Ultimate Boon**: The prize — often symbolic rather than material. What the hero has been seeking.

### Return — the boon brought back

12. **Refusal of the Return**: The temptation to remain in the transformed state. The hero who has achieved enlightenment may not want to return to the ordinary world.
13. **Magic Flight**: Escape with the boon; sometimes the most dangerous part of the journey.
14. **Rescue from Without**: The integrated hero often cannot make it back alone — someone must reach in.
15. **Crossing the Return Threshold**: Returning to the ordinary world, which no longer fits the transformed hero.
16. **Master of Two Worlds**: Can move between material and spiritual without inflation.
17. **Freedom to Live**: Free from the fear of death, therefore free to live fully in the present.

**The most neglected stages**: Refusal of the Call (most writers underwrite this, losing the protagonist's flaw) and Return with the Elixir (most writers truncate this, losing the thematic payoff). Both must be fully realized.

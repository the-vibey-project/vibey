### 5.4 Scam-spotting checklist

1. **Check the sender's domain.** Real USPTO email ends in @uspto.gov. The USPTO says anything not in TSDR is not an official communication ([recognizing scams](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/protect/recognizing-common-scams)).
2. **No payment by phone, text, email, wire, app or gift card.** The USPTO says it never asks for payment that way; pay only inside your USPTO.gov account ([FTC alert, Sept 9, 2025](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/09/scammers-are-impersonating-united-states-patent-and-trademark-office)).
3. **Look-alike "registration" and "monitoring" notices.** Private solicitations resembling official bills for trademark registration, renewal, "recording" or "publication" are common. The USPTO keeps a catalog of examples ([examples](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/protect/examples-fraudulent-misleading-solicitations)). The USPTO has terminated more than 52,000 fraudulent applications tied to filing abuses, per the notes.
4. **Copyright Office look-alikes.** Registration happens only at copyright.gov; the Library of Congress blog (February 5, 2026) described one scam sender using an @copyrightgov.org address (Part 2, §2.9 in the sibling skill `ipfile-copyrights`, shows real examiner emails).
5. **Patent invention-promotion firms.** Promoters that offer evaluations, "marketing" or "licensing" for large upfront fees are the FTC's pattern case ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/invention-marketing-scams)). Federal law requires promoters to disclose their track record ([35 USC 297](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/35/297)). InventHelp has been the subject of FTC history and private suits ([settlement in Calhoun](https://truthinadvertising.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Calhoun-v-InventHelp-settlement-agreement.pdf)); the notes present these as allegations and settlements, not findings against any current company.
6. **Verify attorneys.** Search the USPTO OED roster ([OED](https://oedci.uspto.gov/OEDCI/practitionerSearchEntry)) and the SC Judicial Branch attorney search ([SC courts](https://www.sccourts.org/attorneys/)); the USPTO warns that attorney bar credentials have been hijacked ([notice](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2024/attorneys-check-see-if-your-bar-credentials-were-hijacked)).
7. **Never pay a "transfer" or "expedite" fee** to someone who contacted you first. Never give your USPTO.gov or ID.me credentials to a filer; they would own your account.
8. **Keep your email current and watch your spam folder.** Real USPTO and Copyright Office deadlines are driven by email ([USPTO](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-updates-and-announcements/dont-miss-important-e-mails-uspto)).

## §11. Endings

**⚠️ The ending is what the whole thing was for, and it retroactively determines the
meaning of everything before it.**
**⚠️ "Surprising yet inevitable" is the standard** — ⚠️ **and the inevitability comes from
SETUP, not from predictability.** **Whatever pays off must have been planted, or the
ending feels arbitrary; whatever is planted too obviously makes the ending feel
telegraphed.** **⚠️ Deus ex machina fails because the resolution doesn't come from the
protagonist's agency** (§3 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`).
**⚠️ The ending should answer the DRAMATIC QUESTION posed at the start** — **and the
answer can be "no" or "yes, but at a cost."**
**⚠️ Ambiguity is legitimate and is not the same as being unresolved**: ⚠️ **an ambiguous
ending answers the emotional question and leaves the factual one open.** **An unresolved
ending answers neither and reads as a failure of nerve.**
**⚠️ Denouement length is genre-dependent**: **⚠️ modern screen drama typically ends fast
after the climax; prose and epic fantasy tolerate much more.**

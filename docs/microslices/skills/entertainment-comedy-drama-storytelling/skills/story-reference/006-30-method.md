## §30. Method

**§1–§24 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`, `story-dialogue-point-of-view-theme-exposition-and-endings`, `story-comedy-jokes-improv-standup-and-timing`, `story-acting-directing-medium-and-industry` rest on craft tradition** — **Aristotle through the modern structure literature,
comedy theory, acting systems, and standard production practice** — sourced from §27.
⚠️ **None of it needed verification; *Poetics* is not going to move.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **the production contraction** and **AI in
film and television** — ⚠️ **the two things that materially changed the conditions under
which this craft is practised and paid for.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §1 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`, §5 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene` and §12 → `story-comedy-jokes-improv-standup-and-timing`, which are the load-bearing sections.
⚠️ **The "structure models are retrofitted descriptions" point is the one I'd defend
hardest** — **it's historically accurate, it explains the homogeneity complaint about
studio film, and it reframes the models from recipes into diagnostics, which is how
working writers actually use them.** **§5 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`'s scene diagnostic is the highest-yield practical
tool here.**

**High** in §12 → `story-comedy-jokes-improv-standup-and-timing`'s benign violation framing. ⚠️ **It's the most predictive available theory
of humour, it explains failures in both directions, and the psychological-distance
mechanism accounts for why the same joke lands differently by audience and by year** —
**which is also what makes §19 → `story-comedy-jokes-improv-standup-and-timing` tractable as a craft question rather than only a political
one.**

**High** in §25.1's figures, which come from Ampere Analysis, Luminate and the WGA's own
reporting and recur consistently across trade coverage. ⚠️ **The interpretive emphasis is
mine: the disappearance of the sub-$5M-per-episode tier matters more for the craft than
the headline order count, because that tier was where writers trained and where unusual
material got made.** **I've also included the counterweight — that ratings analysis suggests
volume fell rather than quality — because the declinist framing is easy and partly wrong.**

**High** in §25.2's contract terms, which come from SAG-AFTRA's own materials, Variety and
The Hollywood Reporter. ⚠️ **The comparison is the useful part: SAG-AFTRA secured
bargaining rights, an "articulable business reason" standard for scanning, and a
"significant additional value" test for synthetic performers, while the WGA's April 2026
deal reportedly secured notification without compensation for AI training.** **I've
included the internal criticism — no strike right over synthetics until 2030, and much
protection resting on future bargaining rather than present language — because it's a
substantive objection made by members, not an outside complaint.**

⚠️ **§19 → `story-comedy-jokes-improv-standup-and-timing` is deliberately structured as competing positions rather than a verdict.** **The
craft mechanics (target, distance, ambiguity) are stateable; the normative question isn't
mine to settle, and I've given each side its strongest version including the complication
that "punching up" presupposes an agreed hierarchy.**

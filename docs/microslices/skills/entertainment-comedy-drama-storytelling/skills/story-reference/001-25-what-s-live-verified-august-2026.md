## §25. What's Live — verified August 2026

### 25.1 ⚠️ The contraction: Peak TV ended and did not come back
**⚠️ This is the structural fact anyone entering or working in the industry needs, and it
is measured rather than vibes.**

- **⚠️ Scripted series orders by US distributors went from a peak of 759 in 2021 to roughly
  478–493 per year across 2023, 2024 and 2025** — ⚠️ **about a 36% decline, described as
  content commissioners finding "a new magic number for stability."** **FX's John Landgraf,
  who coined "Peak TV," dated its end to 2023 and noted the decline began before the
  strikes accelerated it.**
- **⚠️ The labour impact was severe and disproportionate.** **The WGA reported TV writer
  jobs falling 42% in the 2023–24 season — 1,319 positions — to 1,819 total**, ⚠️ **with
  declines of roughly 40–46% at every level from staff writer to co-executive producer.**
- **⚠️ The budget distribution barbelled, which matters more than the headline count.**
  **$20M+ per-episode budgets — once unheard of in television — became standard for
  franchise and genre series.** ⚠️ **Meanwhile sub-$5M-per-episode series fell from 82% of
  US scripted releases to under two-thirds by 2025, and shows under $1M per episode "have
  all but disappeared."**
- **⚠️ Geographic concentration shifted**: **40% of US scripted series released in Q1 2019
  filmed in Los Angeles, versus under 25% in Q1 2026** — **driven both by relocation and by
  the overall contraction.** ⚠️ **US production share showed signs of stabilizing in Q1
  2026 following California's expanded tax credit.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the craft consequence, which is the part that belongs in a storytelling
> reference.** ⚠️ **The middle disappeared.** **The lower-cost shows that filled slates
> during Peak TV were where writers learned, where unusual premises got made, and where
> steady between-seasons employment lived.** **⚠️ What remains is expensive franchise work
> and very little beneath it.**
> ⚠️ **One executive's description of the buyer environment: roughly four buyers for a
> given show, more hyperspecific parameters, and windows for a particular kind of material
> that "come and go in just weeks, or even days."** **⚠️ Consolidation compounds it — when
> the buyer is also the producer and the distributor, small strategy shifts propagate.**

**⚠️ Worth noting against the doom framing**: **an analysis of IMDb ratings suggests series
acclaim has continued rising** — ⚠️ **the drop in "buzzy" shows appears to stem from
production VOLUME rather than declining quality.** **And Ampere's analyst view is that the
cutback has bottomed, pending further consolidation.**

### 25.2 ⚠️ AI: the guardrails landed, and they differ sharply by guild
**⚠️ 2026 was the year the labour framework actually got written, and the union-by-union
divergence is the instructive part.**

- **⚠️ SAG-AFTRA ratified a four-year TV/Theatrical Agreement in June 2026** (**91.4% in
  favour, 19.3% turnout; effective 1 July 2026 through 30 June 2030**), **with over $1
  billion in new compensation and benefits funding and a pension plan merger.**
- **⚠️ The AI provisions are specific.** **Producers must have an "articulable business
  reason" to scan a performer; ⚠️ producers may not use synthetic performers without first
  bargaining with SAG-AFTRA, under a strict notice-and-bargaining schedule, with
  arbitration for damages on violation.** ⚠️ **Producers may use AI performers only where
  they bring "significant additional value" compared to a live actor.** **Voice work gained
  separate written-consent requirements, and the Interactive agreement now defines "voice
  actor" as a HUMAN performer.**
- **⚠️ The WGA's April 2026 deal was notably weaker on AI**: **studios agreed to notify the
  guild if they license writers' work for AI training and to hold consultation meetings,**
  ⚠️ **but with no requirement to pay writers when scripts are used to train models** —
  **reportedly trading AI enforcement for a $321M health plan infusion.** **⚠️ Compare the
  2023 WGA precedent that AI training on creative work is compensable; the 2026 outcome
  did not extend it.**
- **⚠️ The DGA's 2023 contract was the first to bar studios from delegating directors'
  creative work to generative AI**, **and its renewal was in negotiation with AI central.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — read the criticism as well as the terms, because it's substantive.**
> ⚠️ **Critics inside SAG-AFTRA argue studios still face little real constraint on AI
> performers**, **and note that the union gets notice and an opportunity to bargain but
> ⚠️ cannot strike over synthetic performers until 2030** — **a four-year term instead of
> the usual three, agreed at a moment when AI capability is moving fast.** **The AMPTP's
> stated priority across this cycle was securing longer "labour peace."**
> ⚠️ **Note also that much protection comes not from contract language but from the union
> reserving the ability to establish protections as actual use cases arise** — **which is
> a bet on future bargaining rather than a settled rule.**

**⚠️ Where AI is actually being used, as distinct from the discourse**: ⚠️ **VFX and
animation pipelines, previsualization, frame interpolation, editing assistance and
background generation** — **the unglamorous end.** **⚠️ Reported effects on employment are
material in VFX and animation and more modest elsewhere so far.**
**⚠️ Legislative activity is running in parallel**: **a New York law requiring advertisements
to be labelled when synthetics are used, a NY bill requiring disclaimers for AI in
published news, and over 16,000 signatures on a SAG-AFTRA letter urging passage of the
federal NO FAKES Act.**

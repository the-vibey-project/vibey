## §1. ⚠️ Rules, Heuristics and Descriptions

```
⚠️ CRAFT MECHANICS — reliable, teachable, near-objective in their effects
   ⚠️ Unclear stakes reduce tension. A scene with no opposition goes slack.
   Setups pay off better than surprises from nowhere. ⚠️ You can be WRONG here
⚠️ STRUCTURAL MODELS — descriptions retrofitted to successful work
   ⚠️ Three-act, hero's journey, Save the Cat beats. Useful for DIAGNOSIS.
   ⚠️ Dangerous as GENERATION — they produce competent, familiar, dead work
⚠️ TASTE — preference, not adjudicable
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the survivorship problem in screenwriting advice is severe and rarely
> acknowledged.** ⚠️ **Structure models were derived by examining films that got made and
> succeeded.** **They cannot tell you what would have worked and wasn't made, and they
> systematically encode the conventions of a particular era and market.**
> **⚠️ The honest use: write the thing, then use the model to find where it sags.**
> **The dishonest use: fill in the beat sheet and expect a story.** **⚠️ The second
> produces the specific deadness that people describe as "feels like every other
> movie" — because it structurally is.**

**⚠️ A second caution: most screenwriting gurus have modest produced credits.** ⚠️ **The
teaching industry selects for people who can articulate a system, which is a different
skill from making things.** **The exceptions — working writers who also teach — are worth
weighting more heavily** (§27 → `story-reference`).

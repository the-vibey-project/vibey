## §5. ⚠️ The Scene

**⚠️ The scene is the actual unit of craft, and most "structure problems" are scene
problems.**
```
⚠️ Every scene needs: someone who WANTS something specific IN THIS SCENE,
   an OBSTACLE, and a TURN — the state changes by the end
⚠️ VALUE CHARGE  the scene should move from + to − or − to + on some value
   (safety, love, hope, power). ⚠️ A scene that ends where it started is cut
⚠️ ENTER LATE, LEAVE EARLY  start as close to the turn as possible
⚠️ SUBTEXT  what characters say ≠ what they want (§7)
```
**⚠️ The diagnostic that fixes most flat scenes**: ⚠️ **ask what each character wants IN
THIS SCENE, specifically, and what they'll do to get it.** **If the answer is vague —
"to explain the plan," "to bond" — the scene will be flat regardless of the dialogue.**
**⚠️ Scene vs sequel** (Bickham/Swain): **scene = goal, conflict, disaster; sequel =
reaction, dilemma, decision.** ⚠️ **Prose needs the sequel beats; screen drama compresses
or externalizes them, which is one of the real differences between the forms** (§22 → `story-acting-directing-medium-and-industry`).

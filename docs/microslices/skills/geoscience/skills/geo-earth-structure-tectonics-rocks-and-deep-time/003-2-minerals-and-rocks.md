## §2. Minerals and Rocks

**A mineral**: naturally occurring, inorganic, crystalline, with a definite composition.
**⚠️ Silicates dominate — the silica tetrahedron (SiO₄⁴⁻) is the fundamental building
block**, and the polymerization degree (isolated → chains → sheets → frameworks) organizes
the whole classification.

```
IGNEOUS      from melt. ⚠️ Intrusive/plutonic = slow cooling = coarse grained (granite)
             Extrusive/volcanic = fast = fine grained (basalt)
             Composition: felsic (Si-rich, light) → mafic → ultramafic
SEDIMENTARY  ⚠️ clastic (grains), chemical (precipitated), organic (coal, some limestone)
             ⚠️ Only ~8% of crustal volume, but ~75% of surface exposure
METAMORPHIC  solid-state alteration by heat and pressure. Foliated (slate→schist→gneiss)
             or non-foliated (marble, quartzite)
```
**⚠️ How rock melts, and it's counterintuitive**: three mechanisms — **raising
temperature**, **decreasing pressure (decompression melting — mid-ocean ridges)**, and
**adding water (flux melting — subduction zones)**. ⚠️ **Adding water lowers the melting
point substantially, which is why subduction zones are volcanic despite being cold
downgoing slabs.**

**Bowen's reaction series** — the order minerals crystallize from a cooling melt, ⚠️ **and
running it backwards gives the order they weather: the last to crystallize (quartz) is the
most stable at the surface, which is why beaches are quartz sand** (§7 → `geo-surface-processes-soils-and-hydrology`).

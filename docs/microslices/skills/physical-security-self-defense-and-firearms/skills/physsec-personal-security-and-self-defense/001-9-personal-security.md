## §9 Personal security

**Awareness without paranoia.** Popular frameworks (e.g., "Cooper's color codes," the OODA loop) are useful **teaching heuristics**, not physiological laws. What's robust from cognitive psychology is humbling: **inattentional blindness and change blindness are real and large** — people miss obvious events when attending elsewhere, and eyewitness confidence is a poor indicator of accuracy. Practical takeaways:

- Phones down in transitional spaces (parking lots, doorways, ATMs, gas stations).
- Notice exits and **baselines** (what's normal here?) so anomalies stand out.
- Trust the "something's off" feeling enough to create distance — avoidance costs nothing.

**The "known person" majority.** Because most violence comes from acquaintances and partners:

- **Domestic violence / stalking safety planning** is a core personal-security skill: documentation, safe contacts, go-bag, changed routines, protective orders, trained advocates (National DV Hotline: 1-800-799-7233). The most dangerous period is often **around separation**. SC's self-defense statute explicitly presumes intent to harm when someone forcibly enters in violation of a protective order (§16). **\[Pack note: the source's pointer says §16; the South Carolina self-defense statute is §18 → `physsec-firearms-and-self-defense-law`, which states this presumption (§ 16-11-440(E)) as intent "to commit an unlawful act" rather than intent to harm — read §18 for the statute's own terms.\]**
- **Stalking/doxxing countermeasures**: data-broker opt-outs, separate mailing address (PO box/virtual mailbox), audit location-sharing on phones and apps, check for unknown Bluetooth trackers (both iOS and Android now alert on unknown AirTag-type trackers), lock down social media, and consider your state's address-confidentiality program if eligible.

**Vehicles.** Lock doors while driving in slow traffic; leave space to pull out when stopped behind a car; don't sit in parked cars absorbed in your phone; if carjacked at gunpoint, **give up the car** — property is replaceable.

**Rideshare / dating / meetups.** Verify vehicle and driver; share trip status; meet first in public; tell someone where you're going and when you'll check in. (The survival literature's single highest-value action — *tell someone your plan and when to raise the alarm* — applies here too.)

**Travel.** Research local crime patterns (the State Dept. travel advisories are a start), keep a low profile, carry a door wedge/portable lock for hotels, know hotel exits, split your money and cards.

**Children.** Teach "safe adults," body autonomy, and that secrets from parents are never required. For firearms: **don't rely on teaching kids not to touch guns** — controlled studies found children who'd received safety instruction frequently handled real guns anyway when they found one. Secure storage is the control that works (§14). **\[Pack note: the source's pointer says §14; storage is §15 → `physsec-firearms-mechanics-safety-and-carry`.\]**

**Executive-protection principles scale down to families:** advance planning, routine variation for high-risk people, low profile, a "cover and evacuate" mindset (not "fight"), and an established medical plan.

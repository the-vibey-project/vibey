## §16 Choosing, training, carrying

**Choosing a defensive handgun:** fit to your hand, controllable recoil, reliability (proven models), sights you can see (many now use red-dot optics), a trigger you can press without disturbing the sights, and a size you'll actually carry. Rent before buying. For home-only defense, a pistol-caliber carbine or a light shotgun/rifle offers easier hits but more complexity; trade-offs include maneuverability in hallways, over-penetration, and noise.

**Carry equipment:** a rigid holster that **fully covers the trigger guard** is non-negotiable; a sturdy belt; retention for open carry. Pocket carry only in a pocket holster. Never carry loose in a bag or waistband.

**Training pathway (suggested sequence):**

1. Basic safety and marksmanship (NRA Basic Pistol, state CWP course, or equivalent).
2. Defensive pistol fundamentals: drawing from concealment, malfunctions, reloading, shooting under time pressure.
3. **Judgment and law**: use-of-force law courses (e.g., Massad Ayoob's MAG-40, USCCA or state-specific classes); force-on-force scenario training with non-lethal marking rounds (the closest thing to reality).
4. **Medical**: Stop the Bleed or a trauma-care course.
5. Ongoing practice; competition (IDPA, USPSA) builds gun-handling under stress.

**Home defense plan (the counter-intuitive consensus):** gather family into the safe room, **call 911 and stay on the line**, hold a defensible position covering the door, and let police clear the house. "Clearing" your own house alone is extremely dangerous and legally complicated. Identify your target before shooting — family members come home unexpectedly, and tragedies happen.

**Police encounters while armed:** keep hands visible, follow instructions, disclose calmly if appropriate, don't reach for anything without permission. Under SC's 2024 permitless-carry act, merely carrying a weapon can't by itself be used as the basis to stop, search or detain someone, and the prior duty-to-inform provision for CWP holders was removed — but calm disclosure remains the widely recommended practice. **\[LAW-2026\]**

**After a defensive incident:** ensure the threat is over and you're safe → call 911 → give only essential facts (you were attacked, you're the victim, where the evidence is, witnesses) → request medical help → **do not give a detailed statement before speaking with an attorney** → expect your firearm to be taken as evidence → expect civil exposure even if criminally cleared. Self-defense "carry insurance"/legal-defense memberships exist; compare coverage, exclusions and whether funds flow before or after a verdict.

## §3. The State

**⚠️ Weber's definition remains the workhorse**: ⚠️ **an entity claiming a monopoly on the
legitimate use of physical force within a territory.** **⚠️ Note "claiming" and
"legitimate" — both are doing work, and many real states hold neither fully.**
**⚠️ Sovereignty**: ⚠️ **internal (supremacy within) and external (recognition without), and
the Westphalian model is a useful idealization that historians increasingly qualify.**
**⚠️ On state FORMATION, several accounts compete rather than one being settled:**
⚠️ **Tilly's "war made the state and the state made war" (⚠️ bellicist — extraction for
warfare built fiscal and administrative capacity); contractarian accounts; and accounts
emphasizing that the European path generalizes poorly, since post-colonial states inherited
borders and institutions without the same formation process.**
**⚠️ Failed, fragile and weak states** — ⚠️ **and the terminology is itself contested, with
critics arguing it measures deviation from a Western template rather than a functional
property.**

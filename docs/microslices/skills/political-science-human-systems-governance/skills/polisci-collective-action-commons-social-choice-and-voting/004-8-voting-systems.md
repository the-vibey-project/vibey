## §8. ⚠️ Voting Systems

**⚠️ There is no neutral voting system. Every rule embeds a judgement about what
representation means.**
```
⚠️ FPTP / plurality  ⚠️ simple, produces single-party majorities,
   strong local link. ⚠️ Disproportionate; wasted votes; rewards
   geographic concentration; ⚠️ can elect a legislature whose
   majority won a minority of votes
⚠️ TWO-ROUND  majority for the winner; ⚠️ costly, and can produce
   perverse first-round eliminations
⚠️ RANKED CHOICE / IRV / AV  ⚠️ eliminates the spoiler problem
   partially; ⚠️ NOT monotonic in all cases (⚠️ ranking a candidate
   HIGHER can in principle hurt them — a real theoretical defect)
⚠️ LIST PR  proportional, ⚠️ coalition government the norm,
   thresholds exclude small parties; ⚠️ weaker constituency link
⚠️ MMP  ⚠️ combines local members with a proportional correction
STV  proportional with ranked ballots in multi-member seats
APPROVAL and SCORE  ⚠️ escape Arrow's framing by not being
   ranked systems — which is why advocates favour them, and
   they face Gibbard-Satterthwaite regardless
```
**⚠️ DUVERGER'S LAW is among the more robust findings** (§1 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory`): ⚠️ **plurality single-member
districts TEND toward two-party competition, through the mechanical effect (seats punish
small parties) and the psychological effect (voters abandon them).** **⚠️ Stated as a
tendency, not a law — India and Canada are standing qualifications.**
**⚠️ Malapportionment and gerrymandering** — ⚠️ **and note that the requirement of
proportionality, compactness, competitiveness, and respect for communities of interest
CANNOT all be satisfied simultaneously, which is why redistricting is genuinely hard and
not merely corrupt.**

## §17. Public Opinion and Measurement

**⚠️ Polling relies on sampling theory that is sound and on response behaviour that
increasingly is not.** ⚠️ **Falling response rates mean modern polls depend heavily on
WEIGHTING and modelling assumptions, so two pollsters with the same raw data can publish
different numbers defensibly.**
**⚠️ Known problems**: ⚠️ **nonresponse bias (⚠️ and differential partisan nonresponse is a
live suspect in recent polling misses); question wording and order effects; social
desirability; and ⚠️ NON-ATTITUDES — respondents readily offer opinions on things they have
never considered, including fictitious legislation.**
**⚠️ The margin of error is a SAMPLING statistic only** — ⚠️ **it does not cover coverage
error, nonresponse, weighting choices or house effects, so the real uncertainty is wider
than the stated figure.**
**⚠️ Aggregation and forecasting** improve on single polls; ⚠️ **and probabilistic forecasts
are widely misread — a 30% outcome occurring is not a failed forecast.**
**⚠️ Deliberative polling and citizens' assemblies** measure something different: ⚠️ **what
opinion looks like AFTER information and deliberation, which often differs substantially
from raw polling.**

## §16. ⚠️ Voter Behaviour

```
⚠️ WHAT IS FAIRLY ROBUST
   ⚠️ PARTY IDENTIFICATION is a strong, stable predictor — and
      the Michigan school treats it as closer to a SOCIAL IDENTITY
      than a running tally of policy agreement
   ⚠️ INCUMBENCY ADVANTAGE in many systems
   ⚠️ ECONOMIC VOTING exists but is ⚠️ heavily filtered by partisan
      perception — partisans disagree about economic FACTS
   ⚠️ TURNOUT correlates with age, education and income; ⚠️ and
      compulsory voting raises it enormously where used
   ⚠️ Most voters have limited political knowledge (Converse's
      finding on unconstrained belief systems), and ⚠️ use
      HEURISTICS — party label, endorsements, retrospection
⚠️ WHAT IS CONTESTED
   ⚠️ The size of persuasion and campaign effects (⚠️ field
      experiments generally find them SMALL, which surprised
      practitioners) · media effects on vote choice · the
      relative weight of economic versus cultural grievance
⚠️ THE PARADOX OF VOTING  ⚠️ a single vote almost never decides an
   election, so instrumental rationality struggles to explain
   turnout at all. ⚠️ The leading answers are duty, expressive
   benefit and identity — which is itself telling about what
   voting IS
```
**⚠️ Retrospective voting (Fiorina)** is a defensible low-information strategy: ⚠️ **judge
outcomes rather than policies.** ⚠️ **Its known failure mode is that voters punish
incumbents for things outside their control — drought, shark attacks, the world economy —
which weakens the accountability story it was meant to support.**

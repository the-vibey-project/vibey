## §18. Polarization

**⚠️ The crucial distinction, because the two are frequently conflated:**
```
⚠️ IDEOLOGICAL POLARIZATION  ⚠️ how far apart people's POLICY
   positions are. ⚠️ Evidence for mass ideological divergence is
   comparatively modest in several democracies
⚠️ AFFECTIVE POLARIZATION  ⚠️ how much partisans DISLIKE and
   distrust the other side. ⚠️ This has risen markedly in a number
   of countries, and it is the better-evidenced trend
⚠️ ELITE POLARIZATION  ⚠️ legislators and parties have diverged
   more clearly than electorates in several cases — which raises
   the question of direction of causation
⚠️ SORTING vs POLARIZING  ⚠️ people may not have changed their
   views so much as ALIGNED their party with their other identities,
   producing sharper conflict without more extreme opinions
```
**⚠️ Proposed drivers, none established as dominant**: ⚠️ **media fragmentation and
selective exposure; social media (⚠️ where the evidence is genuinely mixed — several
large-scale studies find smaller effects than the public debate assumes); geographic and
social sorting; elite behaviour; and identity consolidation.**
**⚠️ The consequences that ARE reasonably documented**: ⚠️ **reduced willingness to compromise,
declining trust in institutions when they rule against one's side, and reduced social
interaction across party lines.**

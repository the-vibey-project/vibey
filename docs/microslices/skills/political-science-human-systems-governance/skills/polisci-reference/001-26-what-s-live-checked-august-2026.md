## §26. What's Live — checked August 2026

> **⚠️ I've paired these two deliberately. The first is the headline empirical claim about
> the state of democracy; the second is the live methodological dispute about whether that
> claim's evidence base can bear the weight.** ⚠️ **Reading either alone gives a misleading
> picture.**

### 26.1 ⚠️ What the democracy indices currently report
**⚠️ Reported as V-Dem's findings, attributed, because this is measurement output rather
than uncontested fact** (§26.2).

- **⚠️ The V-Dem Institute (University of Gothenburg) published its 10th Democracy Report
  in March 2026, titled "Unraveling The Democratic Era?"** ⚠️ **It draws on over 32 million
  data points covering 202 countries from 1789 to 2025, using more than 4,200 experts to
  code over 600 attributes.**
- **⚠️ The headline claim**: ⚠️ **global democracy for the average world citizen has
  regressed to 1978 levels, which the report characterizes as effectively erasing most
  gains of the "third wave of democratization."**
- **⚠️ The distribution**: ⚠️ **92 autocracies against 87 democracies; a reported 74% of the
  world's population (about 6 billion) living in autocracies, and only 7% (about 600
  million) in liberal democracies.**
- **⚠️ Direction of travel**: ⚠️ **44 countries identified as currently autocratizing (up
  from 42), home to a record 41% of the world's population, against 18 democratizing
  accounting for 5%.** ⚠️ **Newly identified democratizers include Botswana, Guatemala and
  Mauritius; the ten new autocratizers named include Croatia, Italy, Slovakia, Slovenia,
  the United Kingdom and the United States.**
- **⚠️ Of the 44 autocratizers, V-Dem states 28 were democracies when the decline began and
  15 have already broken down into autocracy.**
- **⚠️ Freedom of expression is identified as the most deteriorating right, declining in 44
  countries in 2025, with media censorship described as the most widespread tool of
  backsliding.**
- **⚠️ On the United States specifically, V-Dem reports it has lost its long-term
  classification as a liberal democracy, attributing this to concentration of powers in the
  presidency, and reports legislative constraints losing a third of their value in 2025.**
  ⚠️ **I report this as the index's finding and its stated reasoning, not as an independent
  verdict.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — indices disagree with each other, which is itself informative.**
> ⚠️ **The Bertelsmann Transformation Index's 2026 regional report described "no pronounced
> trend" over its review period, with developments pointing to "either stagnation or
> continuity, depending on perspective," and no country experiencing a regime shift in
> either direction.** **⚠️ It separately reported that 56% of states under review are
> governed autocratically and two-thirds of countries assessed since 2006 are now less
> democratic than 20 years ago.**
> **⚠️ Different projects, different concepts, different coding rules, different pictures.
> Cite the index, not "democracy."**

### 26.2 ⚠️ The live dispute: is measured backsliding real?
**⚠️ A genuine, published, unresolved exchange in the discipline — and exactly the kind of
thing that gets lost when index findings are reported as plain fact.**

- **⚠️ THE CHALLENGE.** ⚠️ **Little and Meng, published in PS: Political Science & Politics
  (2024), argue that most backsliding studies rest largely or entirely on SUBJECTIVE
  indicators depending on expert coder judgement.** ⚠️ **They survey OBJECTIVE indicators —
  such as incumbent performance in elections — and report finding "little evidence of
  global democratic decline during the past decade."**
- **⚠️ Their proposed explanation for the discrepancy**: ⚠️ **either coder bias, or leaders
  using more subtle undemocratic methods that only subjective judgement detects.** ⚠️ **They
  state the simplest explanation is changing coder bias — while explicitly conceding they
  "cannot rule out" that decline is occurring only in ways requiring subjective judgement,
  and framing their claim as that the prevailing narrative "is not justified by existing
  evidence."**
- **⚠️ THE RESPONSES, which are substantive.** ⚠️ **V-Dem-affiliated scholars (Knutsen,
  Marquardt, Lindberg and colleagues) argue that subjectivity pervades ALL measurement
  including seemingly objective indicators, and present theoretical and empirical tests
  which they argue show it is "highly unlikely" that time-varying expert bias drives the
  observed declines.** ⚠️ **They further argue Little and Meng's chosen measures are
  ill-suited to the question.**
- **⚠️ A separate response makes what I think is the sharpest point**: ⚠️ **objective
  indicators like incumbent electoral performance fail to track democratic quality WITHIN
  democracies, and so cannot test the cases where backsliding is best documented.**
  ⚠️ **The author's image is looking for keys under the streetlight — searching where the
  light is rather than where the decline is.**
- **⚠️ Work has continued in both directions.** ⚠️ **Event-based datasets (the Democratic
  Erosion Event Dataset) attempt to resolve the dispute with coded events rather than
  ratings, and a 2025 study tested LLMs as democracy coders — finding they largely align
  with human experts but deviate in consistent directions, with some models systematically
  pessimistic and others optimistic.** ⚠️ **That result is a caution about substituting one
  judgement-based coder for another rather than a solution.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the honest position, and I want to state it carefully because it is easy
> to misuse in either direction.**
> ⚠️ **This dispute does NOT establish that backsliding is fictional. The critics
> themselves decline to claim that, and the responses raise serious objections to their
> method.**
> ⚠️ **Nor does it leave the indices untouched: the point that expert-coded measures could
> in principle move with elite and media zeitgeist is legitimate, was made in a leading
> disciplinary journal, and has not been dismissed.**
> **⚠️ The defensible reading is that the DIRECTION is supported by multiple independent
> lines of evidence, the MAGNITUDE is genuinely uncertain, and precise index figures should
> carry wider error bars than their decimal places imply** (§1 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory`, §25 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`).
> **⚠️ Anyone citing "democracy has fallen to 1978 levels" as a hard fact, and anyone citing
> Little and Meng as proof the whole thing is manufactured, is overreading their source.**

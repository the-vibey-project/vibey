## §19. Regimes and Transitions

**⚠️ Regime typologies**: ⚠️ **liberal democracy, electoral democracy, COMPETITIVE
AUTHORITARIANISM (Levitsky & Way — real elections that are systematically unfair),
electoral authoritarianism, closed autocracy.** ⚠️ **The intermediate categories are where
most of the interesting cases and most of the measurement disputes live** (§26 → `polisci-reference`).
**⚠️ Democratization theories, competing rather than settled**: ⚠️ **modernization
(Lipset — wealth and democracy correlate; ⚠️ Przeworski's influential refinement is that
wealth may not CAUSE transitions but strongly predicts that democracies SURVIVE);
elite-pact transitions (O'Donnell & Schmitter); structural class accounts; and
international diffusion.**
**⚠️ Huntington's "waves"** framing remains a common organizing device.
**⚠️ BACKSLIDING has a characteristic modern form** that scholars broadly agree on
descriptively: ⚠️ **incremental and legal rather than a coup — executive aggrandizement,
capture of courts and electoral administration, pressure on media, and use of ordinary law
against opponents.** ⚠️ **Whether and how much this is happening in specific cases is
exactly what §26 → `polisci-reference` disputes.**
**⚠️ Authoritarian durability**: ⚠️ **the selectorate/winning coalition framework, and the
finding that party-based autocracies tend to outlast personalist ones.**

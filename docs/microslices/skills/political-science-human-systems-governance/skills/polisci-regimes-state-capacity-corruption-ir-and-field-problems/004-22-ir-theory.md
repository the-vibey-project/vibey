## §22. IR Theory

**⚠️ Presented as competing lenses, each capturing something real:**
```
⚠️ REALISM  anarchy (no world government) → self-help → security
   competition. ⚠️ Classical (human nature) vs NEOREALISM (structure,
   Waltz) vs offensive/defensive variants
   ⚠️ THE SECURITY DILEMMA is realism's best contribution: defensive
   measures are indistinguishable from offensive preparation, so
   both sides arm while both prefer peace — ⚠️ a collective action
   problem (§5) at the international level
⚠️ LIBERALISM  ⚠️ institutions, trade interdependence and domestic
   regime type change incentives. ⚠️ Institutions reduce
   transaction costs and provide information rather than
   overriding power
⚠️ CONSTRUCTIVISM  ⚠️ interests and identities are socially
   constructed, not given. "Anarchy is what states make of it"
   (Wendt). ⚠️ Explains normative change realism struggles with
MARXIST / world-systems · ⚠️ FEMINIST IR · ⚠️ POSTCOLONIAL IR
```
**⚠️ The DEMOCRATIC PEACE is among the more robust empirical regularities in IR**:
⚠️ **established democracies very rarely fight wars against each other.** ⚠️ **The
DISPUTE is over the mechanism — normative (shared norms of compromise) versus
institutional (accountability and audience costs) — and over definitional edge cases and
whether alliance structures during the Cold War confound it.**

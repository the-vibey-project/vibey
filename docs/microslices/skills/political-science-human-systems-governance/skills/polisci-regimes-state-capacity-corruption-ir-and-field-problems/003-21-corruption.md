## §21. Corruption

**⚠️ Definitions vary** — ⚠️ **"abuse of public office for private gain" is standard and
contested, because it presupposes a public/private boundary that differs across societies.**
**⚠️ Types**: **grand vs petty; ⚠️ state capture (rewriting the rules) versus
administrative corruption (bending them); and ⚠️ "legal corruption" — lobbying, revolving
doors and campaign finance arrangements that are lawful and function similarly.**
**⚠️ Measurement caution**: ⚠️ **the best-known indices are PERCEPTION-based, which means
they can move with news coverage rather than behaviour and may reflect the reporter's
expectations.** **⚠️ Experience-based surveys and audit studies are methodologically
stronger but narrower in coverage.** ⚠️ **This is the same subjective-measure problem as
§26 → `polisci-reference`.**
**⚠️ What the evidence supports as anti-corruption measures**: ⚠️ **audits with publicized
results, e-government reducing discretionary contact, procurement transparency, and civil
service pay and meritocratic recruitment.** ⚠️ **Anti-corruption agencies have a mixed
record and can be captured or used selectively against opponents.**

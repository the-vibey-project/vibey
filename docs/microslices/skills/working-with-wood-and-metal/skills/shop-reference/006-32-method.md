## §32. Method

**§1–§26 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`, `shop-woodworking-tools-sharpening-joinery-and-finishing`, `shop-metalwork-cutting-drilling-and-machining`, `shop-welding-forging-heat-treatment-and-shop-setup` rests on established craft practice and materials science** — **grain behaviour and
wood movement, sharpening geometry, the welding process families, heat treatment
metallurgy, and standard machine safety practice.** ⚠️ **None of it needed verification;
tempering colours and kickback mechanics have not changed.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **US table saw regulation** and **welding fume
exposure** — ⚠️ **both chosen because they are safety topics where the practical guidance
depends on facts that recently moved, and where hobbyists most commonly rely on outdated
or vibes-based information.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §2 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`, §7 → `shop-woodworking-tools-sharpening-joinery-and-finishing` and §9 → `shop-woodworking-tools-sharpening-joinery-and-finishing`, which are the sections I'd most want read.
⚠️ **"Sharp is safe" is the single most useful principle here and it is counterintuitive to
beginners.** ⚠️ **§9 → `shop-woodworking-tools-sharpening-joinery-and-finishing`'s kickback mechanism — specifically that using the fence and mitre
gauge together traps the offcut, and that the riving knife rather than the guard is the
effective control — prevents a specific, common, serious injury.** **And §2 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`'s point that
hobbyists reach for PPE (the weakest control) while ignoring extraction is the pattern
worth breaking.**

**High** on §27.1's chronology, which comes from the Federal Register, CPSC documents and
trade reporting: ⚠️ **the 3.5 mm/1 m/s performance criterion and its derivation from UL's
4 mm laceration threshold, the measured AIM performance figures, and the withdrawal
announced 20 August 2025.** ⚠️ **I've deliberately given BOTH the patent-monopoly objection
and the injury-prevention counter-argument, because this was never a clean safety-versus-
industry question — the Power Tool Institute source is an industry body and the Consumer
Reports source is an advocacy organization, and I've identified both inline rather than
presenting either as neutral.**

**High** on §27.2's regulatory numbers, which trace to OSHA's own standard and
interpretations: ⚠️ **the 5 µg/m³ PEL and 2.5 µg/m³ action level under 29 CFR 1910.1026,
IARC Group 1 classification, and OSHA's determination that engineering controls must be
the primary means.** ⚠️ **The epidemiological figures (558,000 exposed workers, +85% lung
cancer risk, doubled risk in stainless welders) come from industrial-hygiene vendors
citing research rather than from papers I read directly, and I've marked them as
reported.** ⚠️ **The "ten minutes on stainless overexposes you" quote is from a single
specialist in a vendor publication and I've explicitly flagged it as illustrative — the
order of magnitude is the useful part, not the number.**

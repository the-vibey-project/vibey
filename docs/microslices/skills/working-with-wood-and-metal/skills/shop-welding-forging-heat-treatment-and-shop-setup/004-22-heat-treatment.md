## §22. Heat Treatment

```
⚠️ ANNEALING  heat and cool SLOWLY → soft, machinable, stress-free
⚠️ NORMALIZING  heat and air cool → uniform grain
⚠️ HARDENING  ⚠️ heat above critical temperature, then QUENCH →
   very hard AND VERY BRITTLE. ⚠️ Unusable in this state
⚠️ TEMPERING  ⚠️ reheat the hardened part to a lower temperature →
   trades some hardness for toughness. ⚠️ NEVER skip this
⚠️ CASE HARDENING  hard surface, tough core
```
**⚠️ Only hardenable steels harden** — ⚠️ **mild steel will not, regardless of what you do
to it, because it lacks the carbon.**
**⚠️ Practical indicators**: ⚠️ **the magnet test (steel becomes non-magnetic at the
critical temperature — a genuinely useful shop trick), and TEMPER COLOURS on clean steel
(straw through to blue as temperature rises).**
**⚠️ Quench medium matters and is specified by the steel**: ⚠️ **water is fast and risks
cracking; oil is slower; air-hardening steels need neither.** **⚠️ Using the wrong quench
cracks the part.**
**⚠️ SAFETY**: ⚠️ **quench tanks flash and spit; oil quenching can ignite; never quench in a
sealed container.**

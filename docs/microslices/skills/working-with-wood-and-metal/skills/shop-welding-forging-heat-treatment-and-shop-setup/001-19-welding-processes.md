## §19. ⚠️ Welding Processes

```
⚠️ SMAW / "STICK"  ⚠️ flux-coated electrode. Cheap, portable,
   works outdoors and on dirty or rusty metal. ⚠️ Slag to chip,
   steeper learning curve
⚠️ GMAW / MIG  ⚠️ wire feed with shielding gas. ⚠️ Easiest to learn,
   fast, ⚠️ poor in wind (blows the shielding gas away)
   ⚠️ FCAW (flux-cored) works outdoors; more fume
⚠️ GTAW / TIG  ⚠️ non-consumable tungsten, separate filler.
   ⚠️ Most control and best quality; slowest and hardest.
   ⚠️ The only easy choice for thin material and aluminium
OXY-ACETYLENE  ⚠️ versatile (weld, braze, cut, heat), largely
   displaced for welding but still excellent for heating and bending
```
**⚠️ The fundamentals that determine weld quality**: ⚠️ **CLEAN metal (grind off mill scale,
rust, paint, oil — and NEVER weld galvanized without extraction); FIT-UP (a good weld
cannot fix a bad gap); joint preparation and bevelling on thicker material; correct
amperage; travel speed; and consistent arc length.**
**⚠️ Distortion is the thing beginners don't anticipate**: ⚠️ **welding shrinks as it cools
and pulls the work out of alignment.** **⚠️ Tack first, weld in a staggered sequence,
alternate sides, clamp, and expect movement.**
**⚠️ Common defects**: **porosity (contamination or lost shielding), undercut, lack of
fusion (⚠️ the dangerous one, because it can look fine), cracking, and excessive spatter.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — WELDING PPE IS NOT OPTIONAL AND THE HAZARDS EXTEND BEYOND THE WELDER.**
> ⚠️ **The arc emits intense UV: "arc eye" is a corneal burn whose symptoms appear hours
> later, and it burns EXPOSED SKIN like severe sunburn.** **⚠️ Bystanders need screening.**
> **⚠️ Correct shade lens, leathers, no synthetic clothing (it melts), boots, and
> ⚠️ RESPIRATORY PROTECTION AND EXTRACTION** (§27.2 → `shop-reference`).

## §9. ⚠️ The Table Saw

> **⚠️ Singled out because it causes more serious injuries than any other woodworking
> machine, and because the mechanism is specific and preventable** (§27.1 → `shop-reference`).
```
⚠️ KICKBACK — the dominant mechanism
   ⚠️ WHAT HAPPENS: the workpiece pinches the blade or contacts the
   REAR of the blade (which is rising and moving TOWARD you), and
   is thrown back at high speed — often taking the operator's hand
   into the blade with it
   ⚠️ CAUSES: no riving knife/splitter · using the FENCE AND MITRE
   GAUGE TOGETHER (⚠️ traps the offcut — a classic and serious
   error) · internal stress releasing as the cut opens · freehand
   cutting · misaligned fence · dull blade · standing in line with
   the cut
⚠️ THE CONTROLS THAT WORK
   ⚠️ RIVING KNIFE — ⚠️ the single most effective device; it moves
      with the blade and keeps the kerf open
   ⚠️ Blade height just above the stock
   ⚠️ Push stick past the blade; never freehand
   ⚠️ ⚠️ STAND OUT OF THE LINE OF FIRE
   ⚠️ Crosscut with a sled or mitre gauge, NEVER against the fence
   ⚠️ Support long or wide stock; use outfeed
   ⚠️ Blade guard on when the cut allows it
⚠️ ACTIVE INJURY MITIGATION (AIM) — ⚠️ flesh-detection systems that
   stop and drop the blade on contact. ⚠️ They address CONTACT and
   do NOT prevent kickback (§27.1)
```

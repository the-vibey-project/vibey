## §6. Hand Tools

```
⚠️ SAWS  rip (⚠️ teeth like chisels, cut along grain) vs crosscut
   (⚠️ teeth like knives, sever fibres). ⚠️ Western saws cut on the
   PUSH; Japanese saws cut on the PULL (thinner kerf, less force)
⚠️ PLANES  jack (rough), jointer (⚠️ long sole = flat), smoother
   (final surface), block (end grain), shoulder/rabbet (joinery)
   ⚠️ A plane is a jig for holding a blade at a fixed depth —
   the SOLE flattens, the iron cuts
⚠️ CHISELS  bench, mortise, paring. ⚠️ Bevel up or down changes
   the cut direction
⚠️ SCRAPERS  ⚠️ a burnished hook that cuts a real shaving —
   excellent on figured wood where planes tear out, and on finish prep
⚠️ BRACE and bits · spokeshaves · rasps and files · mallet
```
**⚠️ Hand tools remain genuinely competitive for**: ⚠️ **fitting joints (paring to a line),
small work, awkward geometry, quiet operation, and finish quality — a well-planed surface
is better than a sanded one and needs no abrasive.**
**⚠️ The workholding is half the skill**: ⚠️ **a bench with a good vice, dogs and a planing
stop does more for hand tool work than any individual tool.**

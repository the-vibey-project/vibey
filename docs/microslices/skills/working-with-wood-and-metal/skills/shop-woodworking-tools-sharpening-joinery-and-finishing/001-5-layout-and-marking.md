## §5. Layout and Marking

**⚠️ Accuracy is decided at layout, not at the cut.**
⚠️ **A MARKING KNIFE beats a pencil: it severs the fibres, giving a line you can register a
chisel in, and it's an order of magnitude more precise.**
**⚠️ Marking gauge, combination square, try square, sliding bevel, winding sticks (⚠️ for
seeing twist), and story sticks.**
**⚠️ The principles that prevent cumulative error:**
```
⚠️ WORK FROM A SINGLE REFERENCE FACE AND EDGE — mark them, and
   register every measurement from them
⚠️ ⚠️ TRANSFER rather than MEASURE wherever possible. Hold the piece
   against its mate and mark it. Numbers introduce error; direct
   transfer doesn't
⚠️ CUT TO THE LINE, and know which side of the line the kerf takes
⚠️ MARK YOUR WASTE so you cut the right side
⚠️ Check SQUARE with the square you'll use throughout — and check
   that the square is actually square
```

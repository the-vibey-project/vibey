## §8. Machines

**⚠️ Each machine has a characteristic hazard, and knowing it is most of using it safely.**
```
⚠️ TABLE SAW  ⚠️ kickback and blade contact — see §9
BANDSAW  ⚠️ the safest big machine (blade cuts DOWN into the table);
   ⚠️ blade drift, tension, and guide setup are the skills
MITRE SAW  ⚠️ climb-cut grab on wide stock; keep hands well clear
⚠️ ROUTER  ⚠️ feed direction matters enormously — ⚠️ CLIMB CUTTING
   (feeding with the rotation) pulls the tool and can snatch it out
   of your hands. Small bits spin very fast; large bits need slower speed
⚠️ JOINTER  ⚠️ hands over the cutterhead. Push blocks, always
THICKNESSER/PLANER  ⚠️ snipe, and ⚠️ NEVER reach into the outfeed
DRILL PRESS  ⚠️ clamp the work — a bit grabbing sheet metal or a
   small part turns it into a spinning blade
LATHE (wood)  ⚠️ catches, and ⚠️ face shield not just glasses
```
**⚠️ Blade and cutter selection** matters more than machine cost: ⚠️ **tooth count and grind
for the material and the cut, sharp carbide, and the right feed rate.**
**⚠️ Featherboards, push sticks, zero-clearance inserts and sleds** are ⚠️ **engineering
controls (§2 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`) and they improve accuracy as well as safety, which is the argument that
actually persuades people.**

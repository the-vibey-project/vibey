## §2. Attachment

**⚠️ Bowlby and Ainsworth's infant work is well established: infants form attachment
patterns with caregivers (secure, avoidant, ambivalent, later disorganized), measurable in
the Strange Situation, and these relate to caregiver sensitivity and responsiveness.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "adult attachment style" as a personality typology is a substantial
> extrapolation from infant research, and the popular version is much stronger than the
> evidence.** ⚠️ **Infant classifications predict adult outcomes far more weakly than
> popular treatments imply; attachment is relationship-specific rather than a fixed trait
> (⚠️ you can be differently attached to different people); and it changes over time —
> "earned security" is well documented.**
> **⚠️ The self-help version — that you have an attachment style that explains your
> relationships and dates back to infancy — outruns the science considerably.** **The
> underlying phenomena are real; the deterministic story is not.**

**⚠️ What IS well supported and practically useful**: **caregiver SENSITIVITY — accurately
reading and responding to a child's signals — predicts secure attachment, and it is
teachable.** ⚠️ **Interventions targeting sensitivity have among the better evidence bases
in parenting research** (§9 → `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`). **"Serve and return" interaction is the same idea in
developmental-science language** (§8 → `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`).

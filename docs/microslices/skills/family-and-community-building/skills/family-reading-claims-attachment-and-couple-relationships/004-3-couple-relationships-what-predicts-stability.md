## §3. Couple Relationships: What Predicts Stability

**⚠️ Gottman's observational work is the best-known and deserves a calibrated reading.**
**⚠️ The findings that hold up reasonably well:**
- **⚠️ The "Four Horsemen" — criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling — predict
  distress and dissolution, with CONTEMPT the strongest single predictor.** **This is
  robust enough to be useful.**
- **⚠️ Positive-to-negative interaction ratios during conflict differ markedly between
  stable and unstable couples.**
- **⚠️ Bids for connection and "turning toward" versus away.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the famous "94% accuracy predicting divorce" figure has been seriously
> criticized on methodological grounds.** ⚠️ **The core objection: the predictive models
> were fitted and evaluated on the same or closely related samples, which inflates accuracy
> — genuine out-of-sample prediction is much weaker.** **⚠️ The behavioural observations
> are valuable; the prediction claim is not what it appears.**

**⚠️ Other predictors with reasonable support**: **commitment (⚠️ distinct from
satisfaction, and it independently predicts stability), shared expectations, sexual
satisfaction, similar values, financial stress (⚠️ economic strain is one of the more
consistent predictors of relationship distress, which makes some "relationship problems"
downstream of material problems), and social network support for the relationship.**
**⚠️ The perennial null result worth knowing**: ⚠️ **similarity of personality predicts
relationship satisfaction much less than people expect**, **and "opposites attract" is
also unsupported.** **What matters more is how the pair handles difference.**

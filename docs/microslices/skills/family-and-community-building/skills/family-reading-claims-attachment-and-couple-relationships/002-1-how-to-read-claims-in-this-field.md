## §1. ⚠️ How to Read Claims in This Field

**⚠️ Four specific hazards, beyond the general replication problem:**

```
1. ⚠️ SELECTION vs CAUSATION — the dominant problem
   People who marry, attend religious services, join clubs or volunteer differ
   BEFORE they do those things: in income, health, conscientiousness, and
   social support. ⚠️ A raw association between the behaviour and the outcome
   is mostly uninterpretable

2. ⚠️ REVERSE CAUSATION
   Does low social connection cause depression, or does depression reduce
   social connection? ⚠️ Both, and disentangling them requires longitudinal
   or experimental designs most studies don't have

3. ⚠️ IDEOLOGICAL LOADING
   Family structure, parenting, gender roles and religion are politically
   charged. ⚠️ Researchers, funders and advocacy organizations on ALL sides
   have positions, and the same dataset gets reported very differently

4. ⚠️ THE SELF-HELP GAP
   ⚠️ The most popular relationship and parenting frameworks are often the
   least rigorously validated. Popularity selects for memorability and
   intuitive appeal, which anti-correlate with nuance
```
**⚠️ What good evidence looks like here**: **longitudinal designs following people over
time; sibling and twin comparisons (⚠️ which control for shared family background — see
§10 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`); natural experiments and policy discontinuities; randomized trials where ethically
possible (⚠️ they exist for parenting and community programs more than people assume);
and preregistered replications.**
**⚠️ And the honest caveat that applies throughout**: ⚠️ **population-level findings do not
determine individual cases.** **"Children of X do worse on average" is a statement about
distributions with enormous overlap, and it tells you almost nothing about any particular
child.**

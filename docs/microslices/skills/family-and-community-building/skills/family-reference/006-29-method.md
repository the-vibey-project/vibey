## §29. Method

**§1–§23 → `family-reading-claims-attachment-and-couple-relationships`, `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`, `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`, `family-social-capital-community-and-program-evaluation` rest on established material** — **attachment research, couple observation
studies, behaviour genetics, resilience science, social capital theory, collective
efficacy, and program evaluation methodology** — sourced from §27. ⚠️ **None of it needed
verification.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **the loneliness evidence base** and **US
social connection trends** — ⚠️ **the two areas where current data and current caveats both
matter.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §1 → `family-reading-claims-attachment-and-couple-relationships`, §10 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving` and §14 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`, and §1 → `family-reading-claims-attachment-and-couple-relationships` is doing the most work: ⚠️ **selection
versus causation is the hazard that determines whether any claim in this domain means what
it appears to mean**, **and it is the thing advocacy on all sides most reliably elides.**

**High** in §14 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`'s resilience findings. ⚠️ **"One stable supportive adult relationship" as
the most consistent protective factor, and "resilience is ordinary," are both
well-replicated and both under-communicated relative to the ACEs framing** — **and I've
flagged the documented misuse of ACE scores as individual prediction, which is a real
harm.**

**High** in §24.2's trend figures, which come from the BLS American Time Use Survey and
Pew. ⚠️ **The finding I'd most want carried forward is the class-concentration point from
the Survey Center on American Life** — **that marriage, religious organizations and labour
unions supplied social ties disproportionately to Americans without degrees, and all three
declined more steeply in that group.** **That converts a vague cultural narrative into a
specific institutional one.** **I've also included Pew's caution that the strong decline
thesis is contested on measurement grounds.**

⚠️ **§24.1 contains a caveat I consider the single most important thing in this document,
and it comes from a peer-reviewed 2025 review rather than from advocacy**: **that
meaningfully effective interventions for loneliness "have yet to be identified" and that
the human-need framing "is unclear."** **The Surgeon General and WHO figures are accurately
reported and widely cited; ⚠️ the "15 cigarettes a day" line is a communication device
derived from meta-analytic estimates, not a measured equivalence, and I've said so.**
**Presenting the alarm without the intervention caveat would have been the easy version
and a misleading one.**

⚠️ **§13 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving` is deliberately structured as established / disputed rather than as a conclusion,
and that is a choice I'd defend.** **Family structure research is genuinely contested on
the causal question, funded by interested parties across the spectrum, and frequently
presented as settled by people on both sides.** **I've stated where the evidence is
clearer than the public argument suggests — including on same-sex parenting — and where
the disagreement is real.**

⚠️ **§20 → `family-social-capital-community-and-program-evaluation` was written to state the sociological findings on congregations accurately rather
than to avoid the topic**, **which secular treatments of community sometimes do.** **The
attendance-over-belief finding and the selection caveat are both part of the honest
picture, and the civic-infrastructure point stands independent of anyone's theological
position.**

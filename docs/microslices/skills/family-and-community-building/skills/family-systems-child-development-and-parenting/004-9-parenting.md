## §9. Parenting

**⚠️ Baumrind's typology remains the organizing frame, on two axes — DEMANDINGNESS and
RESPONSIVENESS:**
```
                 High responsiveness    Low responsiveness
High demand   ⚠️ AUTHORITATIVE          AUTHORITARIAN
Low demand       PERMISSIVE             NEGLECTFUL
```
**⚠️ Authoritative parenting — warm AND structured, high expectations with high support —
is associated with better outcomes across a wide range of measures.** ⚠️ **This is among
the more robust findings in the field**, **with two honest caveats: ⚠️ the associations are
correlational and partly confounded by heredity** (§10 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`), **and ⚠️ the optimal balance
varies by cultural context — authoritarian-looking parenting has different meanings and
different associated outcomes in different communities, which is well documented.**
**⚠️ What has decent evidence, specifically:**
- **⚠️ Parent training programs work** — **behavioural parent training (Incredible Years,
  Triple P, PCIT and similar) has among the strongest evidence in this whole domain,
  including randomized trials, particularly for child conduct problems.**
- **Warmth and responsiveness; consistency and predictability; scaffolded autonomy.**
- **⚠️ Reading with children and quality of verbal interaction.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — parenting effects are real and smaller than the parenting industry
> implies, and the reason is §10 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`.** ⚠️ **Much of the parent-child correlation is genetic
> confounding — parents pass on genes AND environment — and studies that control for this
> find substantially reduced effects.** **⚠️ This is not licence to disengage: the
> well-evidenced effects concentrate in avoiding harm (abuse, neglect, severe harshness),
> in the relationship itself, and in specific domains like language and education.**
> **The takeaway is calibration, not fatalism — and it should reduce parental guilt about
> ordinary variation in style.**

## §16. Social Capital

**⚠️ The resources available to people through their relationships and networks — and the
crucial distinction is what KIND:**
```
⚠️ BONDING   ties within a homogeneous group. Family, close friends, co-ethnics,
   congregation members. ⚠️ Provides SUPPORT — "getting by"
⚠️ BRIDGING  ties ACROSS groups. Different backgrounds, classes, communities.
   ⚠️ Provides OPPORTUNITY and information — "getting ahead"
⚠️ LINKING   ties across POWER differentials — to institutions, officials,
   professionals. ⚠️ Provides ACCESS and voice
```
**⚠️ The distinction is not academic.** ⚠️ **A community rich in bonding capital and poor
in bridging capital can be warm, mutually supportive, and economically stuck — and can
also be insular or hostile to outsiders.** **Bonding capital has a dark side, and
"community" is not automatically good: gangs, cartels and exclusionary associations are
high in bonding capital.**
**⚠️ The economic-mobility finding worth knowing**: **large-scale analyses of social
networks have found that CROSS-CLASS friendship — "economic connectedness" — is among the
stronger neighbourhood-level correlates of upward mobility, more so than measures of
cohesion or civic density.** ⚠️ **That is bridging capital doing work that bonding capital
does not.**
**⚠️ Putnam's decline thesis** (§24.2 → `family-reference`) — **and the honest note that it has been contested
on measurement grounds (⚠️ some argue association forms shifted rather than disappeared),
though the core time-use trend has held up.**

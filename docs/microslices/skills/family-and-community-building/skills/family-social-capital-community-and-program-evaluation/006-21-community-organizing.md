## §21. Community Organizing

```
⚠️ ALINSKY / IAF (broad-based)  ⚠️ builds POWER through institutional
   membership — congregations, unions, schools. One-to-one relational
   meetings are the core method. Self-interest properly understood; agitate;
   pick winnable fights; ⚠️ "no permanent allies, no permanent enemies"
⚠️ ABCD (Asset-Based Community Development)  ⚠️ start from what a community
   HAS — skills, associations, institutions — rather than from a needs
   assessment. ⚠️ Direct reaction against deficit framing that positions
   residents as clients rather than producers
FAITH-BASED ORGANIZING  ⚠️ uses congregations as the membership base (§20)
POPULAR EDUCATION (Freire)  ⚠️ conscientization; people analyze their own
   conditions rather than receiving analysis
MUTUAL AID  ⚠️ horizontal, solidarity rather than charity, often rapid
COLLECTIVE IMPACT  ⚠️ cross-sector alignment with a backbone organization.
   Popular with funders; ⚠️ evidence for it is notably mixed
```
**⚠️ The relational meeting is the underrated technique.** ⚠️ **One-to-one conversations,
30 minutes, to learn what someone actually cares about and what they'd act on — not to
recruit them to your agenda.** **Organizers who skip this and go straight to mobilization
build brittle campaigns.**
**⚠️ Organizing vs mobilizing vs advocacy is a distinction that matters**: ⚠️ **mobilizing
turns out existing supporters; organizing DEVELOPS new leaders and builds durable capacity;
advocacy speaks on behalf of others.** **They are different activities with different time
horizons, and conflating them is why many campaigns evaporate after one victory.**
**⚠️ The recurring failure mode**: ⚠️ **outside professionals doing FOR rather than WITH,
producing programs that end when the grant does.** **The ABCD critique of deficit framing
is aimed precisely at this, and it's a critique with real force.**

## §18. ⚠️ Loneliness and Social Isolation

**⚠️ Keep the two concepts distinct, because they have different causes and different
remedies:**
```
⚠️ SOCIAL ISOLATION  OBJECTIVE — few relationships, infrequent contact
⚠️ LONELINESS        SUBJECTIVE — the GAP between the connection you have
   and the connection you want. ⚠️ You can be isolated and content, or
   surrounded by people and profoundly lonely
```
**⚠️ The health evidence** — **see §24.1 → `family-reference` for the current state and the important caveat**:
**meta-analytic work associates both with increased mortality risk, and with elevated
cardiovascular, dementia and depression risk.** ⚠️ **The mechanisms proposed include chronic
stress activation, inflammation, sleep disruption, health behaviours, and reduced
practical support.**
**⚠️ Who is most affected**: **young adults report loneliness at rates comparable to or
higher than older adults** (⚠️ **which inverts the stereotype and is now well replicated
across countries**), **along with people with disabilities, caregivers, recent movers, and
those in poor health** — ⚠️ **and reverse causation runs strongly here.**
**⚠️ The intervention evidence is weaker than the problem framing suggests**, **and §24.1 → `family-reference`
says so directly.** **What has the better support: ⚠️ addressing maladaptive social
cognition (loneliness biases attention toward social threat, which is self-perpetuating);
group activities with a shared purpose rather than socializing-for-its-own-sake; and
structural changes that increase proximity and repeated contact** (§17, §19).
**⚠️ If this section describes you**: **loneliness is a signal, not a character defect —
it functions like hunger, indicating an unmet need.** ⚠️ **And the evidence points away
from "try harder socially" and toward joining something that meets REGULARLY around a
shared activity, because that supplies the repetition §17 says is required.** **If it's
persistent and accompanied by low mood, that's worth raising with a doctor or counsellor.**

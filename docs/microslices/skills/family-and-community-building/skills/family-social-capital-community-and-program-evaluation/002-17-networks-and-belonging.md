## §17. Networks and Belonging

**⚠️ Network structure predicts a great deal** (see a psychology reference §19):
**strength of weak ties (Granovetter — ⚠️ weak ties bridge and carry novel information,
which is why they matter for jobs), structural holes, homophily (⚠️ birds of a feather —
which means networks reproduce stratification without anyone intending it), and
Dunbar-style limits on relationship tiers.**
**⚠️ Belonging is distinct from contact.** ⚠️ **You can be surrounded by people and not
belong** (§18) — **belonging requires being known, mattering to others, and having a
recognized place.**
**⚠️ What actually builds relationships, from the research on friendship formation:**
```
⚠️ PROXIMITY      repeated unplanned encounter. The strongest single factor,
   and it's why the loss of shared physical places matters (§19)
⚠️ REPEATED UNPLANNED CONTACT  ⚠️ scheduled one-off meetings build far less
   than incidental repetition. This is why work and school produce friendships
   and adult life after them often doesn't
⚠️ SHARED VULNERABILITY / joint activity toward a common goal
⚠️ TIME            estimates run to dozens of hours for casual friendship and
   substantially more for close friendship. ⚠️ There is no shortcut
```
**⚠️ The adult friendship problem, mechanistically**: ⚠️ **adults lose the institutional
scaffolding — school, dorms, early-career workplaces — that supplied proximity and repeated
unplanned contact for free.** **Which means adult friendship requires deliberately
manufacturing what was previously automatic: standing commitments, recurring events,
membership in something that meets regularly.** ⚠️ **"Structure beats willpower" again.**

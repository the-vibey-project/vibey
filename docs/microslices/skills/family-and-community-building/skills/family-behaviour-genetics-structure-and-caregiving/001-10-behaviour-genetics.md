## §10. ⚠️ Behaviour Genetics

**⚠️ The most robust and most uncomfortable body of findings in family science.**
```
⚠️ FIRST LAW    all behavioural traits are heritable to some degree
⚠️ SECOND LAW   the effect of being raised in the same family is SMALLER than
                the effect of genes
⚠️ THIRD LAW    a substantial portion of variation is NOT explained by genes
                or shared family environment — the "non-shared environment"
```
**⚠️ The shared-environment finding is the one that overturns intuitions**: ⚠️ **for many
adult outcomes — personality, cognitive ability — the shared family environment explains
strikingly little variance once you account for genetics.** **Adoptive siblings raised
together end up little more similar than strangers on several measures.**
**⚠️ Read this carefully, because it is routinely over- and under-interpreted:**
- ⚠️ **It does NOT mean parents don't matter.** **It means measured variation WITHIN the
  normal range of parenting explains less than assumed.** **Abuse, neglect and severe
  deprivation have large effects and are outside that range.**
- ⚠️ **Heritability is a population statistic in a given environment** — **it says nothing
  about immutability** (see a psychology reference §14).
- ⚠️ **Gene-environment correlation means "environment" measures are partly genetic** —
  **parents who read to children also pass on genes associated with reading.**
- **⚠️ The non-shared environment remains poorly characterized**, **and much of it may be
  chance, measurement error, and idiosyncratic experience rather than anything
  systematically manipulable.**

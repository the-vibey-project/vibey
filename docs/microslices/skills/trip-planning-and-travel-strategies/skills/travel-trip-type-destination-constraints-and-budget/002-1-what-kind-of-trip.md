## §1. What Kind of Trip

**⚠️ Naming the trip type prevents most planning arguments, because different types have
incompatible optimizations.**
```
⚠️ REST         minimal decisions, few moves, ⚠️ optimize for FRICTION
                REMOVAL. One base. Ignore "must-sees"
⚠️ EXPLORATION  density of new experience; ⚠️ tolerates fatigue
⚠️ SPECIFIC     an event, a trek, a dive site — ⚠️ everything else
                is logistics around the fixed point
⚠️ SOCIAL       the people are the point; ⚠️ destination is a venue
⚠️ CULTURAL/LEARNING  depth over coverage; ⚠️ fewer places, longer stays
⚠️ ADVENTURE/PHYSICAL  ⚠️ requires honest fitness assessment and
                recovery days built in
⚠️ WORK-ADJACENT  ⚠️ the bleed is the failure mode (§24)
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the most common planning error is building an EXPLORATION itinerary for
> people who need a REST trip.** ⚠️ **It's usually driven by the sense that a long flight
> or an expensive trip must be "maximized," and it reliably produces people who come home
> more tired than they left.** **⚠️ Ask what the trip is FOR, out loud, before anything
> else** — **and if travelling with others, ask them separately** (§23 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`).

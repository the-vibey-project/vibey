## §26. What's Live — verified August 2026

> **⚠️ Both items below change what you must DO before and during a trip. Verify current
> status against official sources before travelling — this area moves.**

### 26.1 ⚠️ EU border systems: EES is live, ETIAS is not yet
**⚠️ The most consequential practical change to European travel in decades, and it is
widely misunderstood in both directions.**

- **⚠️ The Entry/Exit System (EES) is FULLY OPERATIONAL.** ⚠️ **Per the European
  Commission, the phased rollout began 12 October 2025 and the system became fully
  operational on 10 April 2026 across 29 European countries.** **⚠️ It replaces passport
  stamping with a digital record.**
- **⚠️ What happens at the border**: ⚠️ **the system registers name, travel document data,
  BIOMETRIC DATA (facial image and fingerprints — reportedly except fingerprints for
  children under 12), and the date and place of entry and exit.** ⚠️ **Registration is on
  first entry; subsequent crossings may need only facial recognition.** **It also records
  refusals of entry.**
- **⚠️ THERE IS NOTHING TO APPLY FOR IN ADVANCE for EES**, ⚠️ **which is the point most
  commonly got wrong** — **the French foreign ministry states plainly that there is nothing
  to do before travelling.** **⚠️ An optional "Travel to Europe" mobile app allows
  pre-registration of passport data and facial image within 72 hours of reaching an EES
  border, at participating crossings.**
- **⚠️ The 90/180 rule is now calculated automatically**, ⚠️ **which matters if you have
  previously been relaxed about counting — overstay detection is now systematic.**
  **Commission figures reported over 45 million crossings registered with over 24,000
  refusals of entry in the first phase; a later report cited 60 million crossings and
  32,000 refusals.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the rollout has been genuinely rough, and you should plan time for it.**
> ⚠️ **Reported problems include system crashes and longer processing, with Airports
> Council International cited as reporting processing times up 70% in some locations;
> queues reported at 2–5 hours at peak; inconsistent application between airports; and
> some countries pausing EES at specific crossings.**
> ⚠️ **The regulation explicitly permits countries to partially suspend EES operation in
> exceptional circumstances during peak travel, and the Commission has stated this
> flexibility was always part of the legislation.** **⚠️ Practical implication: allow
> substantially more time at Schengen external borders than you would have pre-2026,
> especially in summer and especially on first entry.**

- **⚠️ ETIAS is NOT yet in force.** ⚠️ **It is a travel authorization (like ESTA or the UK
  ETA), not a visa, and is reported as scheduled for the last quarter of 2026 with
  mandatory enforcement following in 2027.** ⚠️ **You cannot currently apply, and the date
  has slipped repeatedly.**
- **⚠️ Therefore, be alert to scams**: ⚠️ **sites charging for ETIAS applications now are
  selling something that does not exist yet.** **⚠️ When it launches, apply only through the
  official EU channel** (§12 → `travel-documents-insurance-health-safety-and-money`).

### 26.2 ⚠️ EU air passenger rights: reformed, and largely preserved
**⚠️ Directly relevant to §25 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`, and the outcome surprised most observers.**

- **⚠️ On 15 June 2026 the European Parliament and Council reached a political agreement on
  the first comprehensive revision of EU261 since 2004**, **after what is described as a
  13-year deadlock.**
- **⚠️ THE HEADLINE: the three-hour threshold and the €250/€400/€600 compensation grid were
  RETAINED.** ⚠️ **The Council, backed by airlines, had pushed for thresholds of four hours
  on shorter flights and six on longer ones; Parliament refused and the three-hour standard
  stayed.** **⚠️ IATA's own commentary describes the outcome as "two losses" and notes the
  original proposal to extend the threshold to five hours "remains at three hours."**
- **⚠️ What's new, per the Council's announcement:**
```
⚠️ 96-HOUR RULE  airlines must inform passengers electronically within
   96 hours of arrival where a delay could ground compensation,
   with instructions on how to claim
⚠️ 30-DAY RESPONSE  airlines must immediately acknowledge a claim and
   then within 30 days either PAY or give a clear justification for refusing
⚠️ CABIN BAGGAGE  a personal item and a small cabin bag included in
   the displayed fare
⚠️ NO-SHOW BAN  ⚠️ airlines can no longer cancel a return ticket
   because you missed the outbound leg
⚠️ FAMILY SEATING  no fee to sit next to your own child or someone
   you're caring for
⚠️ NO MANDATORY APPS  to retrieve a boarding pass
⚠️ Stronger protections for passengers with reduced mobility
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — none of this applies yet, and that's the practically important part.**
> ⚠️ **The agreement still requires formal adoption, and reporting indicates the rules
> apply 12 MONTHS after publication in the Official Journal — most likely 2027.**
> **⚠️ Until then, the EXISTING EU261 rules remain fully applicable, unchanged.**
> ⚠️ **Also note the compensation amounts were set in 2004 and have never been
> inflation-adjusted, which consumer advocates flag as a real-terms halving — so
> "preserved" means preserved in nominal terms.**

**⚠️ Scope, since travellers routinely guess wrong**: ⚠️ **EU261 applies to any flight
DEPARTING an EU airport regardless of airline nationality, and to flights ARRIVING in the
EU operated by an EU carrier.** ⚠️ **Iceland, Norway and Switzerland are within the zone.**
**⚠️ So a Lisbon–New York flight on a European carrier is covered; a New York–Lisbon flight
on a US carrier is not.**
**⚠️ Other jurisdictions differ substantially** — ⚠️ **the UK has its own retained version,
Canada has APPR, and the US has no equivalent compensation regime for delays, only rules
around denied boarding and refunds.** **⚠️ Know which applies to your specific route before
you need it** (§25 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`).

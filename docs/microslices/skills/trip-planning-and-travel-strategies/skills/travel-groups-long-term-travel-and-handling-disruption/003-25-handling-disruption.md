## §25. ⚠️ Handling Disruption

> **⚠️ This is the skill that separates travellers who have good trips from those who
> don't, and it's mostly preparation rather than temperament.**
```
⚠️ BEFORE  ⚠️ build slack (§17) · ⚠️ documents and bookings offline
   (§12) · ⚠️ know your rights on the route you're flying (§26.2) ·
   ⚠️ have the airline's app AND a phone number · ⚠️ know your
   insurance claim process before you need it
⚠️ DURING A FLIGHT DISRUPTION
   ⚠️ 1. Get in the rebooking queue AND call simultaneously —
      ⚠️ phone lines (including foreign-language ones for the same
      airline) are often much faster than the desk
   ⚠️ 2. Know what you want before you reach an agent — specific
      alternative flights, not "what can you do"
   ⚠️ 3. ⚠️ DOCUMENT EVERYTHING: photograph departure boards,
      keep boarding passes, note names and times, get the
      REASON for the delay in writing if you can
   ⚠️ 4. Ask about care obligations (meals, hotel) separately from
      compensation — they're different entitlements
   ⚠️ 5. ⚠️ Keep receipts
⚠️ AFTER  ⚠️ claim directly first (free) before using a claims agency
   (which takes a cut); ⚠️ escalate to the national enforcement body
   if refused
```
**⚠️ The general principle**: ⚠️ **be the person the agent wants to help.** **Calm,
specific, prepared and polite outperforms angry, reliably** — ⚠️ **the agent did not cause
the problem and has discretion they will not exercise for someone shouting at them.**
**⚠️ Lost baggage**: ⚠️ **file the report BEFORE leaving the airport; keep the reference;
know the Montreal Convention liability limit applies internationally; and ⚠️ AirTag-type
trackers have proven genuinely useful for locating bags airlines say they cannot find.**

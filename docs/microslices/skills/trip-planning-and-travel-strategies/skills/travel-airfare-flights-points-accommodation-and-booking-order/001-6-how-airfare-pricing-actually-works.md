## §6. ⚠️ How Airfare Pricing Actually Works

**⚠️ Understanding the mechanism kills most of the folklore.**
```
⚠️ REVENUE MANAGEMENT  seats are sold in FARE BUCKETS at different
   prices; ⚠️ the airline adjusts availability by bucket as it
   forecasts demand. ⚠️ The "price" you see is which bucket is open
⚠️ THEREFORE  ⚠️ prices are not a smooth function of time-to-departure.
   They step, and they can fall as well as rise
⚠️ DYNAMIC PRICING and NDC increasingly personalize offers
⚠️ THE PRICE RESPONDS TO DEMAND ON THAT ROUTE, not to you
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the persistent myths, and why they're wrong.** ⚠️ **"Book on a Tuesday" is
> a fossil of an era when fare filings updated on a weekly cycle; it does not reliably
> hold now.** ⚠️ **"Incognito mode stops prices rising because they track you" is
> essentially folklore — repeated searches do not meaningfully drive up the fare you're
> shown, and the observed variation is usually inventory changing.**
> ⚠️ **"There's a magic number of days ahead" is a population average masquerading as
> a rule; the useful version is that BOOKING WINDOWS VARY BY ROUTE, season and demand.**
> **⚠️ What actually works: flexibility on dates and airports, watching a route over time
> with fare alerts, and knowing what a good price for YOUR route looks like.**

**⚠️ Practical mechanics worth knowing**: ⚠️ **the US 24-hour free cancellation rule for
tickets booked well ahead; ⚠️ error fares exist and airlines sometimes honour and
sometimes cancel them; ⚠️ "hidden city" ticketing violates carrier contracts and carries
real risk (forfeited return legs, loyalty account closure); and ⚠️ basic economy fares
often cost more once bags and seats are added — compare TOTAL cost.**

## ARCHITECTURE DOCUMENTS REQUIRED BEFORE DRAFTING

Do not begin Phase 9 drafting until all five architecture steps below are
complete and documented.

**Step 1: The beat sheet.**
Using the fifteen-beat Save the Cat architecture as the container, produce
a complete beat sheet. Each beat entry: beat name, target page range,
one-sentence description of the specific scene delivering this beat, the
character whose inner change is central, and the relational arc function
this beat serves (drawn from the Phase 6 trust sequence arc). The B Story
(page 30) is where the primary relational arc begins in earnest. The Fun
and Games section (pages 30–55) is where the relationship builds its
relational bank account — the synchrony scenes, the warm interactions,
the honored bids — that makes the All Is Lost rupture devastating when it
arrives.

**Step 2: The scene cards.**
One scene card per scene. Each card: scene number (provisional), slug line
type, one-sentence "what changes" statement, "therefore/but" connection to
the following scene, beat function, and Coppola five-category analysis for
significant scenes. Cards are arranged on a board, color-coded by storyline.
Test three questions before proceeding: Does relational arc card density
maintain even distribution? Do the "therefore/but" connections hold throughout?
Is the All Is Lost moment at the three-quarter mark and is it the lowest
point in the relational arc?

**Step 3: The visual grammar design.**
For each primary location established in the Phase 6 Setting Bible, extend
the thematic setting design into visual grammar terms: what the camera would
naturally do in this space, what the color palette communicates about the
relational state of the characters, and what the signature physical detail
looks like in screen terms. Synchrony scene toolkit scenes require particular
visual grammar attention. These visual choices are not to be specified as
camera directions in the draft — they are designed here so that the action
descriptions in Phase 9 are written with this visual grammar as their implied
grammar. This document — `08e_visual_grammar.md` — carries forward directly
into Phase 10 hub architecture as a production design specification.

**Step 4: The compression character designs.**
For each major character, identify their visual signature: the one observable
behavior, gesture, physical habit, or linguistic tic that most efficiently
communicates their attachment operating system to an audience encountering
it for the first time. Write one sentence per character describing their
visual signature before proceeding to Phase 9. These signatures carry forward
into Phase 10's Character Reference Library.

**Step 5: The title page and series positioning document.**
The title page follows standard professional format. The series positioning
document is a brief paragraph establishing how this screenplay fits within the
larger pipeline and how it engages material from prior phases.

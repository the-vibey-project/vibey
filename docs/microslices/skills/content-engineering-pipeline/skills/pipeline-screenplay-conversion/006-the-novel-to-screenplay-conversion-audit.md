## THE NOVEL-TO-SCREENPLAY CONVERSION AUDIT

Five steps before any architectural decisions are made.

**Step 1: The spine test for every scene and character.**
Apply Peter Jackson's Ring-quest question to every scene: "Does this scene
directly advance [DRAMATIC SPINE]?" Scenes that fail the test are eliminated,
compressed, or relocated. Scenes that fail but cannot be eliminated without
losing trust-sequence arc material must be redesigned — translated from prose
form into visual form.

**Step 2: The internalization protocol.**
Every significant moment of interiority must be converted into an external
behavior, a visual detail, a physical action, or a piece of dialogue. Three
conversion tools: behavioral externalization (what the character does, not
what they think); environmental externalization (what the setting contains —
the synchrony scene toolkit was designed precisely for this); and dramatic
irony (the gap between what the audience knows and what the character knows,
maintained and exploited rather than closed through narration).

**Step 3: The chapter-to-beat mapping.**
Lay the Phase 7 novel's chapter structure alongside Blake Snyder's fifteen-
beat Save the Cat architecture. The fifteen beats and their target positions
in a 110-page screenplay: Opening Image (p. 1), Theme Stated (p. 5), Set-Up
(pp. 1–10), Catalyst (p. 12), Debate (pp. 12–25), Break into Two (p. 25),
B Story (p. 30), Fun and Games (pp. 30–55), Midpoint (p. 55), Bad Guys
Close In (pp. 55–75), All Is Lost (p. 75), Dark Night (pp. 75–85), Break
into Three (p. 85), Finale (pp. 85–110), Closing Image (p. 110). These are
proportional guides, not absolute positions.

> See also: `writing-craft:narrative-structure` (the hero's journey and story
> architecture) and `entertainment-comedy-drama-storytelling`'s
> `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`, which treat beat structure and
> scene design on their own terms.

**Step 4: The compression and invention inventory.**
Two separate lists. Invention is required when the novel handled something
through interiority that has no behavioral external form, or when the
structural requirements of the beat sheet create a scene the novel does not
contain. Invented scenes must be disciplined by Phase 2's steelmanned
opposing arguments and Phase 6's character designs. Compression is required
when the novel's chapter-level development must be condensed into a single
scene. Coppola's compression method: ask "what is the core?" for each unit
of material — the deepest thematic intention beneath the surface content —
and design the compressed form to deliver only the core.

**Step 5: The Coppola five-category analysis.**
For each significant scene, complete a five-category cover sheet: Synopsis
(the scene's essential action in one sentence, not a summary but a statement
of what changes); The Times (relational and contextual grounding — where are
these characters in their attachment arcs); Imagery and Tone (visual and
atmospheric design — what the camera should feel like); The Core (the scene's
deepest thematic intention — which relational mechanism this scene primarily
deploys); Pitfalls (the specific failure modes this scene is vulnerable to —
is the relational science being stated through dialogue rather than shown
through behavior? Is the attachment operating system behavior being played
as character flaw rather than as coherent relational logic?).

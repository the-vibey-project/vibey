## PHASE 2 — TERRAIN MAPPING

### The Critical Distinction: Phase 2 Is NOT Research

This is the most consequential distinction in the pipeline. Research
— the kind that produces citations, develops evidence chains, and
builds the argumentative case — belongs in Phase 3.

What Phase 2 produces is a **map of the intellectual territory** the
Phase 1 claim occupies. The difference between a map and a research
report is the difference between knowing a mountain range exists and
having climbed it.

The Turabian-Booth-Colomb-Williams framework frames this as entering
a conversation that began more than five thousand years ago. You
cannot enter a conversation intelligently if you do not know what
has already been said.

Brant Houston's investigative journalism concept of "building the
wall" is the complementary principle: solid documented prior
understanding built BEFORE in-depth investigation, not during it.
Building them simultaneously produces unsupported sections that fail
under real scrutiny.

**Phase 2 produces breadth, not depth. Inventory, not argument.**
It asks "what exists?" not "what does it prove?"

**Phase 2 does NOT:**
- Produce developed arguments (that is Phase 3 work)
- Verify citations against primary sources (that is Phase 3 work)
- Assign arguments to chapters (that is Phase 4 work)
- Produce prose for the book (that is Phase 4 work)

### The Limits on Means Applied to Terrain Mapping

The temptation is to map selectively — to identify supporting
arguments thoroughly while giving cursory treatment to opposing ones.
This violates the limits on means applied to knowledge production.
A terrain map that produces twenty supporting arguments and two
opposing arguments is not an honest map. It is a document that has
predetermined its conclusions dressed in the form of research.

The gold standard: map the opposing landscape **more carefully** than
the supporting one, because the supporting arguments are already
partially known to the author while the opposing arguments are most
likely to have been underestimated.

### The Five Categories of Terrain

**Category One: Arguments For the Claim**
Organized by discipline — empirical and social science, theological
and biblical, historical and case study, philosophical and
argumentative, practical and operational. The convergence between
disciplines is itself a primary source of awe. Gaps in the supporting
literature are as important as the arguments themselves.

**Category Two: Arguments Against the Claim**
The most important category. Must be at least as extensive as
Category One. Three predictable objection families must be located:
(1) Alternative cause — the phenomenon has causes other than the
one the claim identifies; (2) Counterexample — cases that appear
to disprove the generalization; (3) Definitional — key terms mean
something different from what the author intends.

The steelmanning requirement applies at full force: the goal is
the strongest possible version of every opposing argument, in its
most compelling form, engaged on its own terms. Peter Elbow's
"believing game" is the required posture — temporarily inhabiting
the most intelligent, well-informed, serious opponent of your own
claim.

**Category Three: Adjacent Literature**
Work that addresses the same domain without directly arguing for
or against the Phase 1 claim. For each piece: central argument,
what it gets right, where it falls short, and the specific gap
between its accomplishment and what the Phase 1 claim addresses.
That gap is the author's contribution to the conversation.

**Category Four: Definitional Requirements**
Every term the Phase 1 claim depends on that must be established
before the argument can proceed. This is not dictionary work — it
is the identification of conceptual commitments whose ambiguity
will produce misreadings that no subsequent argument can correct.

**Category Five: Social Currency Candidates**
Findings that are both surprising to a knowledgeable reader AND
sufficiently grounded in evidence. Minimum five candidates required.
Phase 2 identifies; Phase 3 verifies and develops.

### The Citation Confidence System

Every piece of content in the Phase 2 document is tagged:

- **[VERIFIED]** — confirmed against a primary or reliably secondary
  source (rare in Phase 2)
- **[LIKELY — verify in Phase 3]** — plausible; most common tag
- **[UNCONFIRMED — high value if accurate]** — AI tool returned
  something potentially significant; handle with caution
- **[DIRECTIONAL — no specific source]** — general orientation
  of scholarship without a specific citable source

No Phase 3 argument development draws on [UNCONFIRMED] or
[DIRECTIONAL] content without first verifying the specific source.

### The Conversation Positioning Statement

Written at the end of Phase 2. Three components:

1. **Conversation summary** — two to three sentences describing
   where the field currently stands on the question. Recognizable
   to a well-informed reader as accurate.
2. **Entry point** — one sentence: "This book responds to the
   specific gap between [position A's correct insight] and [the
   question position A cannot answer], by arguing that [CLAIM]."
3. **Contribution statement** — one sentence: the specific thing
   this book adds to the existing conversation that was not there
   before it arrived.

### The Phase 2 Quality Gate

- [ ] All five terrain categories represented with substantive content
- [ ] Category Two is at least as extensive as Category One
- [ ] Steelmanning requirement applied to three strongest opposing
      arguments — full development, not summaries
- [ ] Adjacent literature map includes genuine gap analysis for
      every major work
- [ ] Definitional audit complete; Phase 1 revised if required
- [ ] Five Social Currency candidates pass all four tests
- [ ] Citation confidence system applied consistently throughout
- [ ] Conversation Positioning Statement complete with all three
      components

**Output:** `00_phase2_terrain.md` at the root of the project
directory, plus a Phase 3 Priority List organized by urgency.

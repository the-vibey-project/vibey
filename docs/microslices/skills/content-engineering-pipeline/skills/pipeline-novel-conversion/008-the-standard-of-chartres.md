## THE STANDARD OF CHARTRES

The medieval stonemasons who carved the highest vaults of Chartres
Cathedral carved them perfectly, knowing no human eye would ever see
the work up close. The converted novel holds this same standard.

The research book's intellectual architecture — the precise
correspondence between its argument and the novel's plot, the careful
translation of each finding into a scene designed to deliver it at
the exact moment of maximum emotional readiness — will never be
consciously visible to the reader. They will not know that the
eucatastrophe was positioned where it is because the cortisol of
the preceding crisis maximizes the dopamine release of the turn.
They will only know that something happened to them on that page
that they cannot stop thinking about, and that they need to tell
someone.

That is the goal. That is the standard. That is entirely achievable
by any writer who knows the research well enough to love it, who
knows the craft well enough to hide it, and who trusts that a truth
told beautifully is more enduring than a truth argued brilliantly.

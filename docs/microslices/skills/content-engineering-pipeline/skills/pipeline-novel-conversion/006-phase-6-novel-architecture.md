## PHASE 6 — NOVEL ARCHITECTURE

### Before You Write a Word

Answer all five questions in writing before any architecture work
begins. These are the architectural anchors of the entire novel and
will be consulted at every point of uncertainty during Phase 7.

**Question 1: What is the controlling idea the novel exists to prove?**
Not the research book's thesis restated. A controlling idea in McKee's
sense: a single sentence expressing a value and its cause, provable
through a chain of fictional events. It must be capable of being
demonstrated through what happens to characters — not stated through
what characters say to each other.

Conversion task: take the Phase 1 thick thesis and translate it. A
research book's claim that "strategic competence without character
formation produces cultural failure, because strategies are adopted
by institutions but formation changes people" becomes, for example,
"the person who changes a community does so not through strategy but
through the quality of their presence, earned through suffering they
did not seek." Apply the same translation to your own Phase 1 thick
thesis.

Test: if the controlling idea cannot generate a conflict — a force
that would argue the opposite — it is not yet a controlling idea.
Keep reducing until it can be argued against.

**Question 2: Who is the protagonist, and what are their want and
need?**
- **Want** = the conscious desire that drives external action;
  the goal the protagonist pursues throughout the plot
- **Need** = what they actually require to grow; the inner wound
  or false belief the story will force them to confront
- These must be in productive tension: the want is the lie the
  character believes will satisfy them; the need is the truth they
  are resisting

The want and need must be direct translations of the research book's
central argument: the protagonist's want is what the book's target
reader currently believes; their need is what the book exists to
change in that reader. The reader's transformation happens by
inhabiting the protagonist's transformation.

**Question 3: Which archetype does the source material most naturally
inhabit?**
Select from the fifteen archetypes in the Craftsman's Manual. This
is the most important single decision in the conversion, because it
determines the emotional arc, the structural beats, the type of
antagonist, the nature of the climax, and the register of the
resolution. Different archetypes deliver the same intellectual content
with fundamentally different physiological impact. A Rebirth Arc
delivers the same truth as a Sacrifice Narrative but targets
oxytocin and elevation differently. A Quest generates different
cortisol and awe patterns than Overcoming the Monster.

You are not choosing a genre. You are choosing a neurological
delivery mechanism.

**Question 4: What is the eucatastrophe?**
Tolkien's term for the sudden joyous turn that "denies universal
final defeat" without denying the reality of suffering — the moment
when the story's central truth becomes experiential rather than
conceptual.

The Phase 3 confirmed Social Currency list is the direct source for
this identification — specifically, the finding ranked highest for
surprise value to the target reader. In the novel, this content must
be delivered not as a finding but as an event: a plot turn, a
revelation, a character choice that enacts the truth rather than
states it.

The eucatastrophe simultaneously fires dopamine (the better-than-
predicted outcome), oxytocin (restoration of bonds or safety), and
endorphin (the release of sustained tension). Identify this moment
before building any architecture. It is the destination. The entire
novel is the road toward it.

**Question 5: What is the novel's opening image and closing image?**
Following Blake Snyder's Save the Cat architecture:
- **Opening image** (at the 1% mark) — the "before" snapshot; the
  world, protagonist, and status quo before the story begins;
  should embody the condition the research book diagnoses
- **Closing image** (at the 99–100% mark) — the "after" snapshot,
  mirroring the opening to show how completely the world has
  changed; should embody the condition the research book argues is
  possible

Write both images as single vivid paragraphs before proceeding to
the Conversion Audit.

### The Conversion Audit

Before any architectural decisions, every element of the source
research book must be mapped to its narrative equivalent. Do not
skip this. Moving directly from the research book's chapter structure
into architecture produces didactic fiction — illustrated lectures
rather than lived experience.

**Step 1: Thesis-to-controlling-idea translation**
Use the McKee lens to convert the Phase 1 thick thesis into a
controlling idea: "Value is [achieved / destroyed / transformed]
when [condition / cause]." The sentence should make the research
book's author say: "Yes, that is what I was arguing — I just argued
it with data rather than drama."

**Step 2: Social Currency audit**
The Phase 3 confirmed Social Currency list is the direct input. For
each confirmed finding, identify which of three narrative mechanisms
will carry it:

- **Option A: Scene revelation** — the finding is dramatized as a
  plot event the protagonist witnesses or experiences; highest
  impact for findings about human behavior or consequence
- **Option B: Character-embodied truth** — the finding is
  incarnated in a character whose way of being demonstrates what
  the research proved; the reader encounters it as a person before
  understanding the person's coherence; highest impact for findings
  about character formation, moral psychology, or relational dynamics
- **Option C: Setting-as-argument** — the finding is built into
  the physical and sensory world; highest impact for findings about
  systems, cultures, or institutions

**Step 3: Chapter-to-act mapping**
Lay the research book's chapters alongside the three-act structure.
Identify which chapters contain Act One material (diagnosis, inciting
condition, problem space), Act Two material (complications,
escalating failure of conventional approaches, the dark night), and
Act Three material (synthesis, resolution, transformed understanding).
Research books often have their most important content in the middle
chapters, while novels require their highest-stakes material near
the end. The conversion frequently requires restructuring this
sequence entirely.

**Step 4: Case studies as plot events**
Every case study, practitioner account, and documented instance is
raw material. Classify each as: (a) a plot event that can be
fictionalized and placed in the novel's timeline, (b) a character's
history or backstory that informs who they are without being narrated
directly, or (c) a story-within-a-story carried through embedded
narrative. The emotional and structural truth of each case study
must be preserved even as surface particulars are changed.

**Step 5: Author's vantage point as narrative consciousness**
Translate from the credentialing move of the research book ("I am
the right person to tell you this because...") to the identification
move of the novel ("This is a person whose inner experience I
recognize as my own"). The Phase 5 Placeholder Inventory resolution
record governs this translation: Category A and B testimony can be
drawn on freely; Category C testimony presents a distinct challenge
requiring the fictionalization protocol below.

For confirmed Category C scenes: extract the emotional and moral
truth and reconstruct it through a fictional scene with different
surface particulars — different names, changed contexts, composite
characters — while preserving the specific quality of experience
that made the original testimony load-bearing. The limits on means
govern this reconstruction: the fictional scene must be emotionally
and morally true to what was actually experienced, not amplified for
dramatic effect or softened to protect anyone's reputation.
Fictionalization is not license to improve on what happened. It is
the craft of translating what happened into a form that a reader
who was not there can inhabit.

**Step 6: Trust sequence arc mapping**
Before the scene map is built, the novel's relational deepening arc
must be mapped separately from its plot arc. Three research frameworks
govern this mapping:

- **Altman and Taylor's Social Penetration Theory** — relationships
  begin at surface pleasantry and public information, progress
  through personal opinions and preferences, into personal fears
  and vulnerabilities, then into core identity. Rate of progression
  is governed by reciprocity. A character who discloses more deeply
  than the relationship has earned creates the discomfort of violated
  intimacy norms. Refusing to advance inward stalls the relationship
  permanently at acquaintanceship regardless of time spent together.
- **Aron's closeness research** — time together is less important
  than depth of mutual disclosure; two characters in genuinely
  escalating vulnerability for an afternoon will feel closer at
  its end than characters who have spent months in surface contact
- **Brown's trust sequence reversal** — vulnerability does not
  follow trust; it precedes it. Trust is built through repeated
  cycles of choosing to be vulnerable before sufficient evidence
  of safety exists, and having that vulnerability honored. The
  character who waits until they feel safe before disclosing
  never becomes close to anyone.

Answer these questions in writing before building the scene map:
Which relationship is the primary relational arc? At what Social
Penetration layer does it begin, and at what layer does it end?
Which specific scene represents each inward movement through
the disclosure layers? Which scene is the vulnerability-before-trust
leap — the moment where one character discloses before they have
sufficient evidence of safety? What is the oxytocin loop moment —
the specific scene where that vulnerability is honored and the
deepening becomes irreversible? Which scene is the rupture, and
which is the repair?

### Architecture Before Prose — The Five Required Documents

Do not begin Phase 7 drafting until all five architecture documents
are complete.

**Step 1: The Archetype Blueprint (`00b_archetype_blueprint.md`)**
Write out the full construction blueprint for the selected archetype,
populated with the specific content of this novel. Do not use a
generic archetype template. Name the specific ordinary world, the
specific call and its source, the specific mentor and what they
carry, the specific ordeal and what it costs, the specific
resurrection and what it proves. Two to four pages. This is the
most important document in the project and will be consulted at
every point of uncertainty during Phase 7.

**Step 2: The Scene Map (`00e_scene_map.md`)**
Plot every scene before writing any of them. For each scene, write
one sentence answering: "What changes in this scene?" Not what
happens — what changes. If nothing changes (in character knowledge,
relationship dynamic, power balance, protagonist's understanding),
the scene should not exist.

Apply the therefore/but test from Trey Parker and Matt Stone: every
scene transition connects through either "therefore" (direct
consequence) or "but" (obstacle to the character's goal). "And then"
is chronological sequence, not causation. Remove every "and then"
and replace it with "therefore" or "but," or cut the scene.

Trust sequence arc scenes are labeled by function: Social Penetration
layer advance / vulnerability leap / oxytocin loop / rupture /
repair. These labels ensure the relational arc is built into the
scene structure rather than assumed to emerge from proximity.

**Step 3: The Emotional Arc Blueprint (in
`00_conversion_architecture.md`)**
Plan the emotional arc scene by scene using Reagan et al.'s six
fundamental arc shapes. Name the target physiological state for
every narrative beat: cortisol engagement, oxytocin investment,
dopamine anticipation, endorphin release, awe activation. Plan
contrast effects deliberately: the moment of warmth that must
precede the devastating reversal, the comedy or tenderness that
must appear before the ordeal.

**Step 4: Character Design (`00c_character_designs.md`)**
Five required elements for every major character:

1. **Controlling belief** — the core conviction about themselves
   or the world, formed from a wound or formative experience, that
   drives all behavior; every significant decision must be traceable
   to this belief
2. **Want and need** — as defined above; secondary characters'
   wants and needs should create genuine thematic complication
3. **Voice** — specific vocabulary, rhythm, sentence length,
   preoccupations, and speech habits; write three lines of dialogue
   for each character before drafting any scenes
4. **Arc** — the specific transformation, if any; every step must
   be causally produced by the story's events, not asserted
5. **Attachment operating system** — the most consistently omitted
   design element in general craft guidance; as load-bearing as
   any of the four above it

The four attachment styles and their scene-level behavioral signatures:

- **Secure** — comfortable with both intimacy and autonomy;
  approaches rather than flees or freezes under threat; functions
  best as secondary characters modeling what the protagonist needs
  to become, or as the partner whose secure presence provides the
  corrective experience enabling transformation
- **Anxious-preoccupied** — hypervigilant to relational threat;
  bid escalation when bids go unanswered; difficulty tolerating
  ambiguity; rejection sensitive dysphoria (intense physiological
  pain in response to perceived rejection at an intensity
  neurotypical people find difficult to credit); reads between lines
  that may or may not contain what they find there
- **Dismissive-avoidant** — learned not to need others; experiences
  emotional bids as intrusions; stonewalling as self-protection
  experienced by the partner as abandonment; minimizes emotional
  expression; genuine puzzlement at why relationships require so
  much maintenance; deep unexamined belief they are not worth
  staying for
- **Disorganized** — approach and avoidance systems activate
  simultaneously; craves and fears intimacy in the same moment;
  moves toward connection and then sabotages it; requires careful
  handling under the limits on means, which prohibit writing
  disorganized attachment without the inner experience that makes
  the behavior coherent from inside the character's own logic

For neurodivergent characters, the five standard design elements are
supplemented with neurological profile design:

- **Autistic characters** — the masking-unmasking dynamic: sustained
  performance of neurotypical social behavior producing connection
  at the cost of being known, versus the terrifying risk of dropping
  the mask. Design: what does the mask look like in performance?
  what does the unmasked self look like? which character is first
  to encounter the unmasked self? where in the arc does the
  unmasking happen? Milton's double empathy framework: autistic
  relational difficulty is located in the mismatch between two
  different but equally valid social operating systems, not in
  the autistic person's deficits alone.
- **ADHD characters** — rejection sensitive dysphoria (RSD) trigger
  map: the intense, physiologically overwhelming pain many adults
  with ADHD report in response to perceived or anticipated
  rejection (⚠️ RSD is a clinical description, not a DSM
  diagnosis, and the often-quoted "99%" is a clinician's estimate,
  not a measured prevalence; verify before citing a figure); design
  the specific RSD triggers before drafting. Executive function failure patterns as relational
  ruptures: the ADHD character forgot the anniversary not because
  they do not care but because working memory failure cannot be
  overridden by caring. Hyperfocus arc: the absorbed attentiveness
  of ADHD relational hyperfocus in early connection is real, not
  performed; when novelty fades and hyperfocus shifts, the contrast
  is stark enough to constitute a plot event.
- **AuDHD characters** — the interaction between the profiles, not
  their simple sum: the autistic drive for routine and the ADHD
  drive for novelty create internal conflict. The masking-exhaustion-
  shutdown cycle is often more severe. Design the specific load
  conditions: what depletes them, what restores them, what the
  external behavioral signature of each state looks like to other
  characters who lack the explanatory framework.

**Step 5: The Setting Bible (`00d_setting_bible.md`)**
For every major location, complete a setting design using the
thematic setting principle. Every location must do at least one of:
externalize the emotional truth of the scenes within it, argue
thematically for the novel's controlling idea through its physical
properties, or create the specific sensory world that activates the
reader's parahippocampal simulation network and places them physically
inside the scene.

For each location: dominant sensory register across all five senses
(not just visual), the thematic argument the space is making, and
the single most important physical detail that will recur as a motif.

The Setting Bible also contains the **synchrony scene plan**: which
synchrony-producing scene type is assigned to each primary
relationship arc, and which location hosts it. The synchrony scene
toolkit includes: shared meals (activating multiple bonding systems
simultaneously), synchronous physical activity (activating mirror
neuron resonance and the cooperative trust response), shared
adversity (the most powerful bonding mechanism — the relationship
bond that years of pleasant interaction cannot produce), communal
singing or rhythmic movement (activating the social endorphin system
that bonds large groups), and family storytelling (activating the
relational continuity mechanism that produces felt security).

### Phase 6 Quality Gate

- [ ] Five Before You Write questions answered fully and recorded in
      `00_conversion_architecture.md`; controlling idea is a genuine
      McKee-form value-and-cause sentence that can generate conflict;
      eucatastrophe identified from Phase 3 confirmed Social Currency
      list; both opening and closing images written as full paragraphs
- [ ] Conversion Audit complete across all six steps, including
      Social Currency delivery mechanism assignment for every
      confirmed Phase 3 finding, chapter-to-act mapping, case study
      classifications, vantage point translation with Category C
      fictionalization decisions recorded, and trust sequence arc
      mapping with all six relational arc questions answered
- [ ] Archetype blueprint (`00b_archetype_blueprint.md`) populated
      with the specific content of this novel at every stage
- [ ] Scene map (`00e_scene_map.md`) contains one "what changes"
      sentence per scene; every scene connects through "therefore"
      or "but"; all trust sequence arc scenes labeled by function
- [ ] Emotional arc blueprint complete with target physiological
      state named for every narrative beat and contrast effects
      planned explicitly
- [ ] Every major character in `00c_character_designs.md` has all
      five design elements complete; neurodivergent characters have
      full neurological profiles
- [ ] Setting Bible (`00d_setting_bible.md`) has full sensory
      designs for every major location and the synchrony scene plan

When every gate condition is met, Phase 7 begins.

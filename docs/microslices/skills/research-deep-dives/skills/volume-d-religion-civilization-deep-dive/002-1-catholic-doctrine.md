## 1. Catholic doctrine

Catholic doctrine is organized around Scripture, apostolic Tradition, the teaching office of bishops in communion with the bishop of Rome, the Creed, liturgy, sacraments, moral theology, canon law, and theological development. The Catechism presents four organizing pillars: profession of faith, sacraments, moral life, and prayer.

Core teachings include one God in Trinity, the incarnation, death and resurrection of Jesus, the Church as one, holy, catholic and apostolic, grace, resurrection, judgment, and eternal life. Catholic doctrine distinguishes dogma, authoritative teaching, theological opinion, discipline, and local custom.

The seven sacraments are Baptism, Confirmation, Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick, Holy Orders, and Matrimony. The Eucharist has a central role in communion and worship. [Catechism](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en.html); [seven sacraments](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two.html).

Catholic social doctrine emphasizes human dignity, common good, solidarity, subsidiarity, participation, work, care for the poor, family, peace, and the social responsibility of property. These principles are both theological claims and subjects for social-historical study.

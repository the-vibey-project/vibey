## 9. Roman history

Roman history can be divided into traditional Kingdom narratives, Republic, Principate, later Empire, Late Antiquity, and post-imperial transformation. The Republic developed offices, assemblies, magistracies, patronage, citizenship, military expansion, slavery, elite competition, and legal institutions. The Empire combined central authority with local elites, taxation, armies, cities, law, roads, imperial cult, and diverse local arrangements.

Everyday life included household religion, marriage, gendered legal status, education, baths, spectacles, associations, funerals, neighborhood politics, military communities, immigrants, and enslaved people. Elite literary sources overrepresent elite men; archaeology, inscriptions, papyri, coins, and material culture broaden the record.

Roman agriculture, labor, trade, taxation, grain supply, and infrastructure connected countryside and city. “Roman culture” was simultaneously a language of imperial integration and a field of local adaptation.

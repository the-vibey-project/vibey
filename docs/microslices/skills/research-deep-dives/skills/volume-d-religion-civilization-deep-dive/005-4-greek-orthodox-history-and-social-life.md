## 4. Greek Orthodox history and social life

Historical layers include early Christianity in the eastern Mediterranean, Constantinople and the Byzantine Empire, the Ecumenical Councils, the gradual divergence between Greek East and Latin West, Ottoman millet contexts, modern national churches, communist and post-communist societies, and diaspora.

Greek Orthodox can refer to theology, an ecclesial jurisdiction, Greek ethnic identity, a national church, or a cultural inheritance. These must be separated. Parish life may include liturgy, fasting, icons, saints, godparenthood, food traditions, language, burial, charitable work, festivals, and ethnic associations. Diaspora churches can function as religious, cultural, linguistic, and social institutions simultaneously.

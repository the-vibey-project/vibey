## 11. Holy Roman Empire

The Holy Roman Empire was a changing political order conventionally dated from 962 to 1806. It was not a centralized nation-state and was not simply Germany. It contained kingdoms, duchies, bishoprics, cities, dynastic lands, estates, and overlapping courts and jurisdictions.

The emperor’s authority interacted with princes, electors, territorial estates, cities, churches, courts, imperial institutions, and local custom. The Golden Bull, Peace of Augsburg, Peace of Westphalia, Imperial Circles, and Imperial Diet illustrate change but do not reduce the system to one constitutional document.

Religion, law, university life, urban crafts, rural lordship, pilgrimage, printing, festivals, household economy, warfare, and migration shaped social life. Confessional coexistence was uneven. Political fragmentation could produce conflict and also competition, patronage, cultural production, and institutional pluralism.

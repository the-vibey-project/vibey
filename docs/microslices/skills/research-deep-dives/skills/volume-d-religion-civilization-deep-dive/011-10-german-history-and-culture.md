## 10. German history and culture

German history covers many regions and polities: medieval German-speaking lands, the Reformation, Habsburg and Prussian power, the Holy Roman Empire, Enlightenment and Romanticism, nineteenth-century nationalism and unification, empire and colonialism, World War I, Weimar, Nazism and the Holocaust, division into East and West Germany, reunification, and contemporary federal Germany.

German culture includes regional dialects, religion, guilds, universities, music, philosophy, industrial labor, family, migration, class, gender, civic associations, food, festivals, and media. It should not be treated as a timeless national personality.

The Nazi period must be studied through dictatorship, propaganda, racial law, war, genocide, forced labor, resistance, collaboration, and memory. Nazi “Aryan culture” was an ideological construction, not a neutral description of German society.

Sources: [German Historical Institute](https://www.ghi-dc.org/); [German History in Documents and Images](https://germanhistorydocs.org/); [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/).

## 8. The first 200–300 years of Christianity

Early Christianity included diverse Jesus movements, house communities, Jewish and gentile communities, traveling teachers, bishops, deacons, patrons, women benefactors, enslaved and free members, ascetics, and competing theological schools.

Important developments include baptismal practice, Eucharistic worship, local leadership, apostolic succession claims, canon discussions, apologetics, martyr narratives, penitential discipline, care for the poor, Christological debate, and changing relations with Judaism and Roman civic religion.

Do not project fourth-century conciliar language unchanged into the first century. “Persecution” varied by place and time. Evidence includes New Testament writings, apostolic fathers, apologists, inscriptions, archaeology, papyri, martyr acts with source criticism, and Roman sources. Constantine changed the political environment in the fourth century; he did not make earlier diversity disappear overnight.

Sources: [Early Christian Writings](http://www.earlychristianwritings.com/); [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/).

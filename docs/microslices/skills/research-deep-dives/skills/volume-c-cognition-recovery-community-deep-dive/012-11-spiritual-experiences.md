## 11. Spiritual experiences

Experiences described as divine presence, visions, mystical union, conversion, spiritual warfare, synchronicity, or awe can be interpreted religiously, psychologically, neurologically, culturally, or through multiple lenses. Sleep loss, intoxication or withdrawal, medication, neurological illness, trauma, mania, psychosis, grief, and ordinary religious practice can all affect experience.

A respectful assessment asks whether the experience is comforting or distressing, impairing or functional, commanding or voluntary, and whether it involves danger, severe insomnia, substances, or inability to reality-test. Do not pathologize ordinary faith; do not assume frightening commands are divine.

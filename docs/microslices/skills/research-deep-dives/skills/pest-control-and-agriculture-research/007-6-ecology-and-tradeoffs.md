## 6. Ecology and tradeoffs

Pest control changes food webs. Broad-spectrum pesticides can kill predators and parasitoids,
cause pest resurgence, trigger secondary pest outbreaks, reduce pollinators, contaminate water,
and select resistance. Conversely, unmanaged outbreaks can reduce food supply, increase land
pressure, spread pathogens, and create severe economic losses. FAO emphasizes that biodiversity
supports fertility, pollination, water purification, natural pest control, and resilience, but
also notes that intensive agriculture and agrochemical use contribute to biodiversity loss.
[FAO biodiversity and pesticide-risk reduction](https://www.fao.org/pest-and-pesticide-management/pesticide-risk-reduction/risk-reduction-mainstreaming-biodiversity/en/)

Useful ecological design includes flowering strips, hedgerows, beetle banks, reduced drift,
water protection, selective timing, habitat for predators, diversified rotations, and avoiding
unnecessary disturbance. Habitat must be designed locally: a field margin can harbor beneficial
enemies or pests, and pollinator habitat can create exposure if sprayed carelessly.

## §7. Salt, Water and Brining

**⚠️ Salt does far more than taste salty**: **it suppresses bitterness, enhances perceived
sweetness and other flavours, alters protein structure, and controls water activity
(preservation).**
**⚠️ When you salt matters more than how much:**
- ⚠️ **Salting meat well in advance (dry brining) — hours to days — draws moisture out,
  dissolves salt, and the brine is reabsorbed with the salt now distributed through the
  meat.** **Also dries the surface, which helps browning** (§4).
- ⚠️ **Salting immediately before cooking leaves surface moisture that impedes browning.**
  **Either well ahead or right at the pan; the middle is worst.**
- **⚠️ Wet brining adds moisture and can dilute flavour; dry brining concentrates it.**
**⚠️ Salt by WEIGHT, not volume.** ⚠️ **Kosher salt brands differ substantially in
crystal size — a tablespoon of one can weigh nearly twice a tablespoon of another** —
**which is the single most common source of over-salting when following recipes.**
**Osmosis and water activity** — ⚠️ **curing and sugar preservation both work by lowering
water activity below what microbes need** (§12 → `foodenv-food-safety-and-food-service`).

## §34. Method

**§1–§28 → `foodenv-food-science`, `foodenv-food-safety-and-food-service`, `foodenv-cleaning-chemistry`, `foodenv-wastewater-and-waste-management` rest on stable material** — **heat transfer, food chemistry, pathogen destruction
curves, surfactant chemistry, and sanitary engineering** — sourced from §32. ⚠️ **The FDA
temperature thresholds and the reaction temperatures have not moved and are the least
negotiable content here.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **recycling economics and EPR** and **PFAS in
biosolids** — ⚠️ **the two areas where the policy landscape moved recently and materially.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §2–§11 → `foodenv-food-science`'s chemistry, and §2 → `foodenv-food-science` is the section I'd most want read:
⚠️ **framing cooking as heat transfer replaces a large number of memorized rules with one
model**, **and the evaporative-cooling point explains browning failures better than any
technique instruction.**

**High** in §12 → `foodenv-food-safety-and-food-service`'s numbers, which come from FDA/USDA guidance. ⚠️ **The claim I'd emphasise
is that COOLING, not cooking, is where commercial food safety most often fails** —
**it's well documented in inspection data and it's counterintuitive.**

**High** in §20 → `foodenv-cleaning-chemistry` and §22 → `foodenv-cleaning-chemistry`. ⚠️ **The soil-type-to-chemistry matching table is the single most
practically useful thing in Part IV**, **and the mixing dangers are standard, well-
established safety information that I've stated as prominently as I know how.**

**High** in §26 → `foodenv-wastewater-and-waste-management`'s entombment point and §27 → `foodenv-wastewater-and-waste-management`'s resin-code correction — ⚠️ **both are
well-documented and both are very widely misunderstood.**

**Moderate-to-high** in §29.1. ⚠️ **The apparently contradictory recycling numbers — "at
least 70% of what you put in the bin gets recycled" versus "28% of PET bottles, 32% of
cardboard, 30% of cans" — are from different sources measuring different things, and
reconciling them (post-collection fate versus capture rate) is my interpretation.**
**I think it's right and it's the key to reading any recycling statistic.** ⚠️ **Note the
sourcing: the 70% figure comes from industry commentary and the capture figures from a
recycling-advocacy report, and both have positions.** **The industry scepticism about
whether EPR will raise rates is directly reported from a February 2026 conference and is
notable precisely because it comes from inside the industry.**

⚠️ **§29.2 is deliberately presented as contested policy rather than settled fact, and
that's a judgement I'd defend.** **The sequence is documented — January 2025 draft risk
assessment, 25,000+ comments, July 2026 replacement with nine-page voluntary guidance,
comment period to 4 September 2026.** ⚠️ **The characterizations differ sharply by source:
EPA says it is correcting serious flaws; critics say it substitutes voluntary guidance for
enforceable protection.** **I've given both and attributed both, and I've included the
October 2025 ruling separating EPA's biennial review duty from any duty to regulate,
because that legal point explains why the situation can persist.** ⚠️ **I've not attempted
to adjudicate the underlying toxicology.**

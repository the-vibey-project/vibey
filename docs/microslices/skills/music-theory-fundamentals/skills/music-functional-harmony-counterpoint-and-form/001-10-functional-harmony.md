## §10. Functional Harmony

**⚠️ The core insight of common-practice tonality: chords have FUNCTIONS — roles in a
directed motion toward a goal.**
```
TONIC (I, vi, iii)         home, stability, arrival
PREDOMINANT (IV, ii)       ⚠️ departure, preparation
DOMINANT (V, vii°)         ⚠️ TENSION demanding resolution to tonic
```
**⚠️ Why the dominant works, mechanistically**: **V7 contains the LEADING TONE (a semitone
below tonic, with strong melodic pull) and a TRITONE between the 3rd and 7th, which
resolves outward or inward by step.** ⚠️ **The tension is real and specific, not
conventional decoration.**
**The circle of fifths**; **secondary dominants (V/V)**; **tonicization.**
**⚠️ Voice leading is the actual subject** — **and the "rules" are all consequences of two
goals:**
```
⚠️ Move each voice as little as possible (smoothness, singability)
⚠️ Keep the voices INDEPENDENT (so they're heard as separate lines)

⚠️ Parallel fifths/octaves are forbidden BECAUSE they collapse two voices into
   one perceptually — the acoustic fusion is so strong you stop hearing two
   parts. ⚠️ NOT because of a superstition. And when you WANT that fused sound
   (organum, Debussy, power chords, orchestral doubling) you use them
Resolve tendency tones · avoid parallel motion into perfect intervals ·
prefer contrary motion · avoid voice crossing and overlap
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — every voice-leading "rule" is a description of what makes four-part vocal
> counterpoint in a particular style work.** ⚠️ **They are not laws of music, and applying
> them to guitar-based rock or to piano writing produces nonsense.** **Learn WHY each one
> exists and you'll know when it doesn't apply.**

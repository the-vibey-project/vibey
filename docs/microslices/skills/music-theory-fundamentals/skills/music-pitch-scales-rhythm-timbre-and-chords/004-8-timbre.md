## §8. Timbre

**⚠️ Timbre is the "everything else" parameter and it's the one that actually distinguishes
most music people love.**
**Determined by**: **the harmonic spectrum and its evolution over time** (⚠️ **the ATTACK
TRANSIENT is disproportionately important for identification — remove the attack from a
piano note and it becomes hard to identify**), **formants** (⚠️ **fixed resonances — the
basis of vowel identity and of instrument "body"**), **noise components, and inharmonic
partials** (⚠️ **bells and drums have partials that are NOT whole-number multiples, which
is why their pitch is ambiguous**).
**⚠️ Orchestration is timbre theory in practice**: **register, doubling, blend, masking,
and the fact that the same notes can be transparent or opaque depending entirely on
spacing and instrumentation.**
**⚠️ In electronic and produced music, timbre and texture ARE the compositional content**
— **the harmony may be four chords, and the interest lives entirely in the sound design,
the mix, and the arrangement over time.** ⚠️ **Analyzing such music harmonically and
concluding it's simple is a category error** (§18 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`).

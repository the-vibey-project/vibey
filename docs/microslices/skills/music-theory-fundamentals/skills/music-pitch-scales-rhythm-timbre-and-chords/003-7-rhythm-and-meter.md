## §7. Rhythm and Meter

**⚠️ Rhythm is under-theorized in Western classical training relative to its importance,
and this is a real gap** — **most of the world's music organizes rhythm with far more
sophistication than it organizes harmony.**
```
PULSE / BEAT     the felt regular unit
METER            ⚠️ grouping of beats into a repeating pattern of strong/weak
                 Simple (beat divides in 2) vs compound (divides in 3)
TEMPO            rate, BPM
SYNCOPATION      ⚠️ accent displaced from the expected strong position.
                 Requires the meter to be established to work at all
POLYRHYTHM       ⚠️ two independent groupings at once (3:2 = hemiola)
POLYMETER        ⚠️ different meters simultaneously — not the same as polyrhythm
ADDITIVE METER   ⚠️ 2+2+3 rather than a division of 7. Balkan, Indian, much
                 non-Western practice. ⚠️ Western notation handles this badly
```
**⚠️ Two things the notation actively obscures:**
- **⚠️ MICROTIMING carries most of the feel.** **Swing, groove, laid-back and pushed
  playing are systematic, measurable deviations from notated positions** — ⚠️ **and swing
  ratios are not a fixed triplet feel; they vary with tempo and with player, typically
  flattening toward even at fast tempos.** **A quantized performance sounds dead for
  precisely this reason.**
- **⚠️ The BEAT is a perceptual construction, not a property of the signal.** **Listeners
  infer it, can disagree about it, and entrainment to a pulse appears to be a human
  near-universal that is rare in other species.**

**⚠️ Metric dissonance** (Krebs) — **grouping vs displacement conflict** — **is a genuinely
useful analytical concept and applies far better to popular and non-Western music than
harmonic analysis does.**

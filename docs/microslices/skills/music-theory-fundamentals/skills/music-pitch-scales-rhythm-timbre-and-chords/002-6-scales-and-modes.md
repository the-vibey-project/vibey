## §6. Scales and Modes

```
MAJOR (Ionian)      W W H W W W H
NATURAL MINOR       W H W W H W W  (= Aeolian)
HARMONIC MINOR      ⚠️ raised 7 — to get a LEADING TONE and a major V (§10).
                    The augmented 2nd is a side effect, not a goal
MELODIC MINOR       ⚠️ raised 6 and 7 ascending to smooth that gap
```
**⚠️ The seven diatonic modes are rotations of the same set of intervals**, **and thinking
of them as "the white notes starting on different letters" is how they're taught and why
they're misunderstood:**
```
Ionian     bright, = major
Dorian     ⚠️ minor with a RAISED 6th. The characteristic sound of much folk,
           modal jazz, and a great deal of rock
Phrygian   ⚠️ minor with a ♭2. Spanish/flamenco inflection, metal
Lydian     ⚠️ major with a ♯4. Floating, unresolved
Mixolydian ⚠️ major with a ♭7. THE dominant sound of blues-derived rock
Aeolian    = natural minor
Locrian    ⚠️ diminished 5th — no stable tonic. Barely usable as a key
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — a mode is defined by its relationship to a TONIC, not by which notes are
> in it.** ⚠️ **Playing white notes from D to D is not Dorian if the music keeps resolving
> to C — that's just C major.** **Modality requires that the harmony and phrase structure
> actually establish D as home.** **This is the single most common error in how modes are
> taught and used.**

**Other important collections**: **pentatonic** (⚠️ **five notes, no semitones, and it
appears independently across an enormous number of unrelated cultures — one of the better
candidates for a near-universal**), **blues scale** (⚠️ **a notational approximation of
pitch inflections that don't sit on the 12-note grid at all** — §18 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`), **whole tone**
(⚠️ **symmetrical, no leading tone, no stable tonic — Debussy**), **octatonic/diminished**,
**chromatic**, **and the church modes as historically used** (⚠️ **which differ from the
modern "rotation" presentation and involved *finalis*, reciting tone and range**).

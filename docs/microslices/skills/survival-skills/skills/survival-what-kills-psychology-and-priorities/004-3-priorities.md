## §3. ⚠️ Priorities

```
⚠️ IMMEDIATE (minutes)
   ⚠️ Get out of immediate danger (rockfall, water, traffic, fire)
   ⚠️ Catastrophic bleeding, airway, breathing (§10)
⚠️ SHORT TERM (hours)
   ⚠️ THERMOREGULATION — get dry, get insulated, get out of wind (§4)
   ⚠️ SIGNAL — the sooner rescue starts, the shorter everything is (§9)
   ⚠️ Assess: injuries, resources, location, who knows where you are
⚠️ MEDIUM (a day or more)
   Water (§5) · fire if useful (§6) · improve shelter and signals
⚠️ LATE (multiple days)
   Food (§7) — ⚠️ genuinely last, and usually never
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the ordering people actually follow is almost exactly inverted.**
> ⚠️ **The instinct is to look for food and to walk toward where you think help is.**
> **⚠️ Both are usually wrong: food is irrelevant on the timescale of a rescue, and
> walking expands the search area and exhausts you** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`).

**⚠️ The decision that matters most: STAY OR GO.**
```
⚠️ STAY IF  someone knows where you are and when you were due ·
   you're injured · visibility or weather is poor · you have
   shelter, water, or a defensible position · ⚠️ you're not certain
   of the route out
⚠️ GO IF  ⚠️ nobody knows where you are · you're certain of a route
   to help and can complete it · your position is untenable
   (no water, exposed, rising water) · ⚠️ and if you go, LEAVE A
   MARKED TRAIL AND A NOTE saying who you are, when you left,
   and which direction
```
⚠️ **In most recreational contexts the answer is STAY**, **because §9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`'s rescue apparatus is
already looking for you and a moving target is much harder to find.**

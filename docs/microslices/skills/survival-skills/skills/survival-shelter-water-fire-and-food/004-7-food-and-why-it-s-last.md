## §7. Food — And Why It's Last

> **⚠️ A healthy adult can survive weeks without food. Essentially no recreational
> survival situation lasts that long** (§23.1 → `survival-reference`). **⚠️ Time spent seeking food in a
> short-duration emergency is time not spent on shelter and signalling.**
**⚠️ The exceptions that matter**: ⚠️ **diabetics and others with metabolic conditions,
children (much lower reserves), and anyone doing sustained hard work in cold — where
calories directly support thermogenesis** (§4).
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — DO NOT eat wild plants or fungi you cannot identify with certainty, and
> certainty cannot come from a text description or a phone app.** ⚠️ **Several deadly
> species closely resemble edible ones, and mushroom poisoning in particular can present
> after a symptom-free interval during which liver damage is already advanced.**
> **⚠️ The "universal edibility test" taught in some older manuals is NOT reliable — it
> does not detect many toxins, including delayed-onset ones, and testing costs far more
> time than fasting.** ⚠️ **The correct default in a short emergency is to eat nothing
> unidentified.**
> **⚠️ If you want to forage, learn from an expert, in person, one species at a time,
> in your own region.**

**⚠️ Trapping and hunting** are ⚠️ **heavily regulated, generally illegal outside season
and licence, low-yield relative to the calories spent, and irrelevant on rescue
timescales.** **⚠️ In a genuine life-threatening emergency, survival necessity is
recognized in many jurisdictions — but that is a legal defence, not a plan** (see a
hunting/agriculture reference for the regulatory picture).
**⚠️ Insects and fish are generally the higher-yield options** where legal and identifiable.

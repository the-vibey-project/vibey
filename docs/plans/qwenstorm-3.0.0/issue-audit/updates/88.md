# Delivery economics: milestone scoping, graded time and cost predictions, margin trend, and a payment gate that can never touch FOSS work

## Summary

The ask is a commercial layer over the conductor:

1. scope and bill by milestone, or by the hour when work is too small to milestone
2. predict time and cost to launch per milestone from the conductor's own history, each
   prediction stored beside its actual so the estimator is graded
3. forecast profit margin over time
4. stop paid work when a payment fails or is overdue, visibly and reversibly

**FOSS work is structurally outside the gate.** Since this was filed:

- **10.b** makes every payment cryptocurrency. "Card fails" and fiat invoicing are out; a
  payment failure is a crypto settlement that did not arrive.
- **Half of (2) now exists in vibey-gh:** an append-only delivery forecast with a graded
  estimator. It is not per milestone, and it has no measured data yet ("billing ledger
  unavailable").

Milestones, margins and the payment gate are unbuilt.

## Why

- **10.b** (`src/vibey_tools/gh/docs/doctrines.md:390-393`): crypto only, no fiat
  processors.
- **Bill of Rights IV**: FOSS at full capability, never degraded. The payment gate keys off
  the *billing classification*, never account standing (the original's own rule).
- **8.g** (`:316-324`) and **10.f** (`:419`): predictions come from measurements and are
  graded on record. Unknown stays unknown.
- **ADR-0009 / ADR-0024**: a stopped engagement is a parked job with a gate, never a
  blocked worker.
- **7.c** (`:82-91`): estimates, actuals, invoices and payment states are ledger events.

## Scope / Required behaviour

1. **Milestone** as a domain concept on commercial projects: scope boundary, contracted
   price (in the settlement asset, 10.b), and an hourly fallback.
2. **Per-milestone predictions** of time and cost from the conductor's own phase-timing
   distributions, stored as ledger events beside the eventual actuals and graded through
   the family's `GradedEstimator` (10.e, the same one #134 uses).
3. **Margin trend:** projected cost against contracted price per milestone, rolled up
   across engagements, as a series.
4. **Payment gate:** a commercial engagement with an unsettled or overdue payment parks its
   next delivery job as a human gate visible to both sides, and releases on settlement. A
   FOSS project cannot enter this state: type, property test and DB CHECK.
5. Everything here sits on the commercial side of #86's boundary.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Milestones exist only on commercial projects. Constructing one on a FOSS project

- [ ] The margin trend is computed from ledger data in a test with the in-memory fakes.

      project with any account state never parks (property test).

## Ask

A billing-and-forecasting layer over the conductor's delivery pipeline:

1. **Scope and bill by milestone** — or by the hour when the work is too small to
   milestone. A milestone is the conductor's natural unit; billing should follow the same
   boundaries the delivery already has.
2. **Predict time and cost to launch, per milestone** — estimates derived from the
   conductor's own historical phase timings, not vibes; each prediction stored beside the
   eventual actual so the estimator is graded on record.
3. **Predict profit margins over time** — projected cost vs contracted price per milestone,
   rolled up across engagements; the trend, not just a snapshot.
4. **Block paid work on invoicing/payment failures** — an engagement with a failed or
   overdue payment stops consuming delivery cycles until resolved. Automatic, visible to
   both sides, reversible on payment.

## Doctrine boundary — sharp, by design

**The gate in (4) applies to paid work only. FOSS work is structurally outside it.**
A payment state cannot exist on a FOSS engagement (#86: unbillable by construction), so no
payment failure can ever pause one. The blocker must key off the billing classification,
never off account standing — an operator whose card fails still gets their FOSS work run.

All four features live on the commercial side of the split from #86: a self-hosted FOSS
deployment contains none of this code path and loses nothing by its absence.

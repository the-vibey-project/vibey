## Title
docs(ledger): draft the ADR for the ledger segment codec, chunk size, content address and hash walk, with a benchmark method on real ledgers

## Why
Issue #114 (rewrite: `issue-audit/updates/114.md`, Scope 3 "**Chunks** are content-addressed.
Identical chunks dedupe, and each chunk's hash chains into the ledger's digest story. Archive
verification is a hash walk with no decompression", Scope 8 "Benchmarks: ratio, write
amplification and restore latency per tier and codec, generated by a script from real runs (8.g,
10.f)", and "Proposed child issues" 5: "**Design needed first** — codec and chunking. Benchmark
zlib-9 against zstd (a new dependency, or the standard-library module from Python 3.14 once the
floor allows) against lz4, on real ledgers. Choose the chunk size and the content-address scheme.
This records an ADR."). The issue's original text asks to "Pick by measured ratio × restore-speed,
document the numbers".

What exists: one codec, `ZlibCodec` at level 9 (`src/vibey/infrastructure/ledger/compression.py:17-27`,
level at `:21`); the first segment format `jsonl+zlib` (the ledger's own record lines, compressed;
`specs/roadmap-114-tier-aware-reads-p1.md`); the `ledger_segment` row with `codec`, `digest` =
SHA-256 of the stored bytes, and `data` (`specs/roadmap-114-postgres-tier-store-p1.md`); a derived
hash chain that already says how tiers fold over it — "A chunk covering seqs `a..b` is identified
by two links … a chunk manifest that records the two links per chunk is the whole of the
integration" (`src/vibey/domain/ledger_chain.py:20-26`); and the digest story every reader
checks: `digest_event` and `digest_range` (`src/vibey/domain/ledger.py:209-224`), R6
(`src/vibey/domain/noloss.py:149-172`), the handoff `LedgerRef` (`src/vibey/domain/handoff.py:127-132`)
and the shard header's `chain.head` and `published.digest_range`
(`src/vibey/infrastructure/ledger/static_export.py:73-81`). The Python floor is 3.12
(`pyproject.toml:10`), so the standard library's zstd is out of reach without a dependency, and
sub-doctrines 10.a (`src/vibey_tools/gh/docs/doctrines.md:385-388`: "Every dependency is a party
who can be pressured") and 10.e (`doctrines.md:417`) weigh against one. 8.g (`doctrines.md:316-324`)
and 10.f (`doctrines.md:419`) say every number must come from measurement with its scope. The
deliverable is one draft ADR; no code.

4. `## Options considered` weighs, each with consequences (ratio and speed are "to be measured",
   never asserted):
   - codecs: zlib (levels 6 and 9; stdlib), lzma/xz (presets 6 and 9|EXTREME; stdlib), bz2
     (level 9; stdlib), zstd (levels 3, 19 and 22, with and without a trained dictionary; a
     dependency — `zstandard` or `backports.zstd` — until the floor reaches 3.14, then
     `compression.zstd`), lz4 (a dependency), brotli (a dependency), and PostgreSQL's own TOAST
     compression (`pglz`, `lz4`) for the raw tier rather than for segments;
   - chunk size: a fixed record count aligned to seq multiples (segment `k` holds seqs
     `k*W+1 .. (k+1)*W`, deterministic, so a replayed rotation seals the same chunk), versus a
     target byte size, versus one chunk per rotation run; and how the size interacts with
     `TierConfig`'s windows (`specs/roadmap-114-tier-config.md`);
   - content address: SHA-256 of the stored bytes (today's `digest` column), SHA-256 of the
     plaintext (independent of codec and level), and the chain links of `ledger_chain.py:20-26`
     (`link(a-1)` and `link(b)`), alone or combined;
   - the manifest: which of `prev_link`, `end_link`, `plaintext_digest`, `digest_range` over the
     chunk, and the per-event `(seq, digest)` list are stored uncompressed beside `data`
     (columns in `ledger_segment`, or a manifest line inside the segment), and what each costs;
   - where a format name lives: the `codec` column naming the whole encoding
     (`jsonl+zlib`, `jsonl+zstd-19`, …) versus separate `format` and `codec` columns.
5. `## Decision` states exactly:
   - the codec naming scheme and the candidate set the benchmark runs;
   - the **selection rule** the benchmark applies (a stated formula over measured ratio and
     restore latency, with the tie-break), and the default that holds until it has run
     (today's `jsonl+zlib`, unless you argue otherwise);
   - the dependency policy before the floor reaches 3.14 (whether a zstd binding may be taken,
     on what measured margin, and why that is compatible with 10.a and 10.e — or why not);
   - the chunk rule (count or bytes, the value or the rule that picks it, and its alignment);
   - the content-address scheme, the manifest fields, and the migration that adds them to
     `ledger_segment` (the next free number after 0016 at this cutoff) — safe because no row
     exists until a writer is wired;
   - the hash walk, as numbered steps over one project's segments in `first_seq` order, that
     verifies without decompressing: bytes intact (`sha256(data) == digest`), each segment's
     `prev_link` equal to the previous segment's `end_link`, the first one's equal to
     `LedgerChain.genesis(project_id)` when `first_seq == 1`, and the newest segment's
     `end_link` equal to the `prev_link` the first raw event chains from; plus the deep verify
     that does decompress (`LedgerChain.verify(project_id, events, prev_link=…, anchors={b: …})`);
   - how R6's `digest_range` over a tiered range is computed, with or without decompression.
6. `## Benchmark method` (required section): the inputs (full ledgers, not the published shard,
   which withholds events — `static_export.py:83-91`; exported as `LEDGER_LINES` bytes, the
   handoff-ledger format, each corpus identified by its `digest_range` and event count; which
   ledgers — this repository's own deployments — and how they are obtained without publishing
   any payload: results carry sizes and timings only, never event content, SD-01 §1 and 7.c);
   the matrix (codec × level × chunk rule); the metrics and their exact definitions (ratio =
   plaintext bytes ÷ stored bytes; compress throughput; restore latency p50/p95 per segment and

   memory); the machines (Arch Linux and macOS, 8.h, each with CPU model, core count, Python and

   append-only JSON Lines results file and the script path you propose, which the docs table is
   generated from — the table itself is out of scope); and how production keeps measuring (8.g:
   each rotation records its segment's plaintext bytes, stored bytes and duration in the ledger).

"Come in, Daniel!" Professor Feynman shouted, spinning around in his desk chair with a piece of chalk between his fingers. "Don't stand in the hallway like an uncharged particle. Sit down. You look troubled. What is on your mind? Did a calculation blow up?"

"Not in physics, Professor," Daniel said, sitting in the wooden chair. "In life. Nora and I have been married for ten years now, raising our ten-year-old son Caleb and my sixteen-year-old daughter Hannah from my previous marriage. Everywhere I look in the culture, people talk about marriage as a fifty-fifty financial and emotional contract. They say gender roles are arbitrary social inventions. I want to understand the foundational philosophy of marriage and family governance as real, load-bearing truth."

Feynman's eyes lit up. He bounced out of his chair, tossed the chalk in the air, caught it, and walked to the board.

"Splendid! That is the right question, Daniel! When people treat marriage as a temporary contract between two self-interested consumers, it is like designing a bridge where the two ends are merely resting on frictionless rollers. ### 1.2 The Creation Blueprint: Ontological Equality and Functional Differentiation
Feynman drew a coordinate system on the chalkboard.

"Look at Genesis chapter one, verses twenty-six to twenty-eight. God creates man: *'in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them.'* Notice the physics here, Daniel! A carbon atom in diamond is no more or less carbon than one in graphite; both have six protons. Ontological equality is absolute."

"Then where does differentiation come from?" Daniel asked.

"Look at Genesis chapter two," Feynman said, tapping the board. "God forms Adam first from the dust of the ground, puts him in the garden, and gives him the commandment and the duty of cultivation and guarding. Then God makes an observation in verse eighteen: *'It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper fit for him.'*"

Feynman wrote the Hebrew letters on the board:

> **Ezer Kenegdo (Hebrew: *'ezer kenegdo*)**

"Do you know what the commentators do with that word *ezer*?" Feynman asked with a grin. "The shallow ones say, 'Oh, she is just a domestic assistant, a subordinate helper.' That is completely illiterate! Throughout the Old Testament, God Himself is repeatedly called the *ezer* of His people! Psalm 121: *'My help (ezer) comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth.'* Does God helping man make God inferior to man?"

"Of course not," Daniel said. "It means God is an indispensable rescuer."

"Precisely!" Feynman beamed. "An *ezer* is an indispensable counterpart, a vital rescuer who provides what the other fundamentally lacks. And *kenegdo* means standing face-to-face as an equal and complementary opposite. The woman was not taken from Adam's head to rule over him, nor from his heel to be crushed by him, but from his side, right beside his heart, under his protective arm, as [Matthew Henry](https://www.biblegateway.com/resources/matthew-henry/Gen.2.21-Gen.2.25) famously put it."

## Chapter 7: Homework Problem Sets and Solutions

### Problem Set 1: Mechanics of Spousal Order

#### Problem 1.1: The Asymmetric Career Offer
Daniel is offered a position that increases his salary by 50%, but requires him to travel four days a week. His wife, Nora, expresses that their ten-year-old son Caleb and sixteen-year-old daughter Hannah are in critical moral formation years, and Daniel's aging father Thomas needs daily assistance.
1. Formulate the decision using the Responsibility Index ($\mathcal{R}_H \gg \mathcal{R}_W$).
2. Evaluate whether accepting the job fulfills [1 Timothy 5:8](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1timothy/5) or violates [Ephesians 6:4](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/4).
3. Provide the optimal covenantal resolution.

> **Solution to Problem 1.1:**
> 1. *Responsibility Calculus:* The husband holds primary responsibility $\mathcal{R}_H$ for the spiritual protection and moral catechesis of his children. Money is a secondary instrument of provision.
> 2. *Evaluation:* 1 Timothy 5:8 commands provision for the family, but Deuteronomy 6 and Ephesians 6:4 command physical and temporal presence for daily discipleship. Increasing wealth while abdicating presence is an engineering failure.
> 3. *Resolution:* Decline the travel contract. Preserve daily domestic presence. Rely on tighter budgeting and local labor, maintaining covenantal integrity over financial ambition.

### Problem Set 2: Child Discipline and Paternal Restraint

#### Problem 2.1: The Smashed Heirloom
A ten-year-old boy carelessly disobeys household rules, breaks an expensive family heirloom, and lies, claiming the wind blew the window open.
1. Diagnose the severity of the offenses (loss of property vs. breach of truth).
2. Design an authoritative disciplinary response that avoids provoking the child to wrath ([Ephesians 6:4](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/4)).
3. Map the transition from paternal correction to maternal comfort and restoration.

> **Solution to Problem 2.1:**
> 1. *Diagnosis:* Broken glass is physical property ($E = 3$). A deliberate lie to a parent breaches covenant trust ($E = 10$). The moral offense far outweighs the physical loss.
> 2. *Paternal Response:* The father remains completely calm, exposing the lie factually without shouting, insults, or physical force. He administers solemn moral rebuke, immediate loss of evening leisure privileges, and structured Saturday restitution labor in the workshop so the boy earns the exact sum needed to replace the broken item.
> 3. *Maternal Balm:* Once the discipline is received and repentance expressed, the mother opens her arms, confirms that the sin is forgiven and forgotten, and helps the child clean up the mess. The fellowship of the home is completely restored.

## §4 Force structure, logistics, doctrine, intelligence, and procurement

### Force structure and the permanent trilemma

Each domain does something the others cannot:

| Domain | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| Land | Holds and controls territory and people |
| Naval | Controls access and communication |
| Air | Provides reach and speed |
| Space and cyber | Provide the enablers everything else depends on |

The **permanent trilemma is readiness versus modernization versus force size**. Any budget must
trade among a ready force now, a modern force later, and a large force.

The **tooth-to-tail ratio is persistently misunderstood**. Support is not overhead; it is the thing
that makes combat power sustainable. Sustaining a deployment requires several units for each one
deployed, which is why **deployable strength is a fraction of total strength**.

### Logistics

> **AMATEURS TALK TACTICS; PROFESSIONALS TALK LOGISTICS.** This is a cliché because it keeps being
> demonstrated. Logistics covers fuel, ammunition, food and water, maintenance and spares, medical,
> transport, engineering, and the movement of all of it. **Consumption rates in high-intensity war
> exceed peacetime assumptions by large margins** — peacetime planning factors are a poor guide to
> what a real war consumes.

Three consequences follow, and each is routinely missed:

- **The culminating point is usually a logistics phenomenon.** Offensives stop when they outrun
  supply, not when they run out of courage. Clausewitz's concept and the judgement it demands are at
  §1 → `endeavour-military-theory-levels-of-war-and-deterrence`; the mechanism by which the
  culminating point actually arrives is this one.
- **The industrial base is part of logistics.** It is the ability to replace losses, and in a
  protracted war that matters more than the initial inventory.
- **The overwhelming majority of tonnage moves by sea**, which makes shipping a strategic asset that
  is rarely counted as one.

### Doctrine and institutional learning

Doctrine is shared professional understanding — how *this* military fights, so that units who have
never met can cooperate. **Its function is coordination as much as correctness** — a doctrine
that is merely correct but not shared buys nothing.

> **THE DOCTRINE PARADOX.** Doctrine that is too rigid prevents adaptation; doctrine that is too
> loose prevents coordination. Both failure modes are real.

**Realistic force-on-force training against a thinking opponent** is expensive and repeatedly shown
to be the highest-return investment in effectiveness.

**Lessons-learned systems are where militaries most often fail.** Identifying a lesson is easy.
Changing doctrine, training, procurement and organization to reflect it is slow and threatens
established interests — which is the actual reason lessons go unlearned, not any shortage of
insight. This is the third organizing idea of Part I in operation: **what matters is not who fields
a technology first but who can absorb, integrate and redistribute it fastest.** That is an
organizational capability, and it is much harder to buy than equipment.

### Intelligence

The intelligence cycle — **direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination** — is tidier
than practice.

| Discipline | Source |
|---|---|
| HUMINT | Human sources |
| SIGINT | Intercepted signals |
| IMINT / GEOINT | Imagery and geospatial data |
| MASINT | Measurement and signature data |
| OSINT | Openly available information |

**OSINT's importance has grown enormously** with commercial satellite imagery and social media —
material that was once the preserve of national systems is now purchasable or simply published.

The analytical failures are well studied and none of them are collection problems:

- **Mirror imaging** — assuming the adversary reasons as you do.
- **Confirmation bias** — weighting evidence by how well it fits the held view.
- **Politicization** — analysis bending toward what the customer wants.
- **Signal-to-noise** — the warning indicator was present and buried.

> **STRATEGIC SURPRISE IS USUALLY A FAILURE TO BELIEVE, NOT A FAILURE TO COLLECT.** In case after
> case the warning was collected and reported; it was discounted because it contradicted an existing
> assumption. The corollary is the deception finding: **the most effective deception reinforces what
> the target already expects** rather than trying to plant something new.

### Defence procurement

The structural problem is a market shape, not a personnel failure: **a monopsony buyer, few
suppliers, long development cycles, changing requirements, and political constituencies attached to
production.** That combination produces predictable cost growth and schedule slip, and it does so
across states and across decades.

- **Requirements creep is the dominant technical cause.** Each added requirement is individually
  reasonable; the accumulation is what produces unaffordable programmes.
- **The exquisite-versus-mass debate is the live one** — small numbers of highly capable, expensive
  platforms versus large numbers of cheaper, attritable ones.
- **Surge capacity is absent by construction.** Decades of low-rate production optimized for
  peacetime efficiency produced supply chains with no ability to expand.
- **The innovation mismatch** — procurement cycles measured in decades against technology cycles
  measured in months — **is arguably the central defence-management problem of the period.**

## §5 Modern conflict

### Unmanned systems

The category runs from hand-launched reconnaissance quadcopters through loitering munitions to large
armed systems, plus uncrewed surface and underwater vessels. What they **actually change** is four
things:

1. **The cost of persistent surveillance collapses.**
2. **Precision strike is no longer confined to well-funded states.**
3. **Transparency of the battlefield increases enormously.**
4. **The exchange ratio between a cheap attacker and an expensive target inverts.**

Counter-unmanned systems are the corresponding growth area — electronic warfare, directed energy,
guns, nets and interceptor drones.

> **THE COST-PER-ENGAGEMENT PROBLEM.** Using an expensive interceptor against a cheap threat loses
> the economic exchange **even when it works**. A defence that succeeds tactically on every
> engagement and loses on cost is not a solved problem, and this is the reason counter-UAS is a
> genuinely open area rather than a procurement checkbox.

**Autonomy is a spectrum, not a switch**, and public discussion collapses the distinctions.

### Cyber and space

**Cyber operations span espionage, disruption and — rarely — physical effect.** The "cyber Pearl
Harbor" framing has been predicted for over two decades and has not occurred, while persistent
low-level espionage and disruption have been constant. The strategic literature largely concludes
that **cyber is better understood as an intelligence and shaping contest than as a decisive
war-winning instrument**, for three reasons: effects are hard to control, they are often temporary,
and using a capability reveals it.

**Space has moved from sanctuary to contested domain.** Position, navigation and timing,
communications and imagery underpin nearly all modern operations, which is exactly what makes them
targets. The live issues are anti-satellite capability, **debris as a shared risk (Kessler
syndrome)**, and the growing dependence on commercial space services. What those orbital regimes
are and what constrains each is at §26 →
`endeavour-satellites-flight-software-and-instruments`.

### Information operations

The oldest instrument, with new distribution. **Propaganda, deception, psychological operations and
public affairs are distinct activities that get conflated**. What changed is cost, speed,
targeting precision and deniability — plus the fact that
the same platforms carry domestic political speech, **which makes countermeasures politically
fraught in open societies.**

> **THE EVIDENCE IS MORE NUANCED THAN THE ALARM SUGGESTS.** On persuasion effectiveness, the
> repeatedly-found levers are **factual richness and conversational engagement**, not psychographic
> microtargeting. And much influence-operation activity appears aimed at **demoralization,
> polarization and epistemic exhaustion** — reducing confidence that anything can be known — rather
> than at persuading anyone of a specific claim. That is a harder target to defend, because there is
> no false proposition to correct.

### Irregular warfare

The central insight of the classical counterinsurgency literature is that **the contest is for
population and legitimacy, not territory**, and that the political line of effort is primary. Two
asymmetries follow: **the insurgent wins by not losing**, and **time favours whoever has less to
lose from continuation.**

**Counterinsurgency campaigns by external powers have a poor success rate**, and the recurring
failure is structural rather than tactical: a foreign force cannot supply the legitimacy that only a
host government can.

**Hybrid and grey-zone activity** combines deniable forces, economic coercion, information
operations, lawfare and proxies below the threshold that triggers a response. **The defining
characteristic is ambiguity, which is the point** — it is the operational expression of the salami
tactics described under escalation at §3 → `endeavour-military-theory-levels-of-war-and-deterrence`.

### Total defence

Total defence is the premise that **national defence is not solely a military task**. It encompasses
civil preparedness, economic resilience, psychological defence, and the continuity of government and
essential services.

**Finland's comprehensive security model is the most-studied**, and rests on five things:
cross-sector coordination, maintained strategic stockpiles, shelter capacity, conscription with
large trained reserves, and sustained public commitment.

> **RESILIENCE IS DETERRENCE.** The logic is deterrence by denial (§3 →
> `endeavour-military-theory-levels-of-war-and-deterrence`): a society that visibly cannot be quickly
> coerced or collapsed is a less attractive target. This is the **form of deterrence most available
> to smaller states**, because it does not require matching anyone's offensive capability.

## §6 The law of armed conflict

This section is not an appendix. A reader who loads only this skill still meets it.

> **JUS AD BELLUM AND JUS IN BELLO ARE TWO SEPARATE BODIES OF LAW, AND CONFLATING THEM IS A COMMON
> AND CONSEQUENTIAL ERROR.** **Jus ad bellum** concerns *when* force may lawfully be used — the UN
> Charter Article 2(4) prohibition, with self-defence (Article 51) and Security Council
> authorization as the exceptions. **Jus in bello** (international humanitarian law) concerns *how*
> force may be used, and **it applies equally to all parties regardless of who started it or whose
> cause is just.** This equality of application is fundamental and constantly misunderstood: having
> the better *ad bellum* case grants no latitude whatsoever in the conduct of hostilities.

### The four core principles of IHL

| Principle | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **Distinction** | Distinguish between combatants and civilians, and between military and civilian objects — **the foundational rule** |
| **Proportionality** | Expected incidental civilian harm must not be excessive relative to the **concrete and direct military advantage anticipated** |
| **Military necessity** | Permits only what is necessary for a legitimate military purpose, and **does not excuse violations of the other rules** |
| **Humanity** | Avoidance of unnecessary suffering |

> **PROPORTIONALITY IS NOT A CASUALTY COMPARISON.** The rule weighs *expected incidental civilian
> harm* against the *concrete and direct military advantage anticipated* from a particular attack.
> Comparing the dead on one side against the dead on the other is not the test, and it is how the
> principle is almost always misused in public argument. **Military necessity likewise permits only
> what a legitimate military purpose requires, and does not excuse violations of the other rules.**

### Enforcement, and why weak enforcement does not make the law meaningless

**Enforcement is the weak point**: jurisdiction is contested, powerful states are effectively
insulated, and enforcement is selective. Stating that plainly is the honest position.

**It does not follow that the law is meaningless.** It structures military planning, it generates
real legal review inside armed forces, and it provides the vocabulary of accountability. A rule that
is unevenly enforced still shapes what is planned, what is approved, and what can be named
afterwards.

**Command responsibility:** commanders are liable for crimes they **knew or should have known of**
and failed to prevent or punish. The "should have known" limb is what makes the doctrine bite —
ignorance a commander was in a position to cure is not a defence.

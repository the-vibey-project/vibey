# Art Theory Fundamentals: What Kind of Claim, the Elements, Colour, Composition, and Materiality

> **Part 1 of 5** of the *Art Theory Fundamentals* reference (plugin `art-theory-fundamentals`), covering §0–§7. Sibling skills: `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art` (§8–§13), `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon` (§14–§16), `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` (§17–§22), `art-reference` (§23–§27). Section numbers are shared across the set; a reference written as §N → `skill` points into that sibling skill.
>
> **Currency:** Aesthetics and formal theory are stable to the point of being ancient. Two areas are live. See §23 → `art-reference` for AI authorship and copyright, and the state of the art market.

> **⚠️ Read §1 first, because the epistemic situation here is unlike the sciences and
> unlike the humanities that model themselves on sciences.**
>
> **⚠️ Art theory is not one thing. It is at least four things with different standards of
> truth**, **and confusing them is the source of nearly every bad argument about art:**
> **craft knowledge that is testable**, **philosophical claims that are argued**,
> **historical claims that are evidenced**, **and interpretive claims that are neither
> true nor false but better or worse.**
>
> **⚠️ GOTCHA** boxes mark widely-taught claims that are false, or fights that are
> commonly mistaken for settled.
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ "Is it good?" and "is it art?" and "do I like it?" are three different questions**
>    with three different kinds of answer, **and most public argument about art is people
>    answering different ones at each other** (§1, §9–§12 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`).
> 2. **⚠️ The formal layer is genuinely teachable and largely objective in its effects.**
>    **Value structure, edge control and composition do predictable things to a viewer**
>    (§2–§7). **This is the part that's craft, and it's the part most theory-first
>    treatments skip.**
> 3. **⚠️ Almost every "rule" in art instruction is a heuristic with a good hit rate**,
>    **not a law** — **and knowing why it usually works tells you when to break it.**

## §1. ⚠️ What Kind of Claim Is This?

**⚠️ Four registers, four standards. Keep them separate.**

```
1. CRAFT / PERCEPTUAL — ⚠️ largely objective, testable, teachable
   "This composition reads as unbalanced." "The value structure is muddy."
   "Warm advances, cool recedes." ⚠️ Grounded in how vision works (§2–§7).
   You can be WRONG about these

2. HISTORICAL — ⚠️ evidenced, falsifiable
   "Caravaggio influenced Rembrandt." "This pigment wasn't available before 1704."
   Ordinary historical scholarship, with ordinary historical standards

3. PHILOSOPHICAL — ⚠️ argued, not measured
   "Beauty is disinterested pleasure." "Art is the expression of emotion."
   ⚠️ Judged by coherence, explanatory scope, and fit with cases — the standards
   of philosophy, not of experiment. NOT arbitrary; not empirical either

4. INTERPRETIVE / CRITICAL — ⚠️ neither true nor false; better or worse
   "This painting is about mortality." ⚠️ Assessed by how much of the work it
   accounts for, how well it's supported by evidence in and around the work,
   and whether it makes you see more. ⚠️ Some readings ARE bad ones
```

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "it's all subjective" is the single most common move in art argument and
> it is lazy in both directions.** ⚠️ **Register 1 is not subjective: put a light shape on
> a light ground and it will not read, regardless of your feelings.** **Register 4 is not
> arbitrary either: a reading that ignores half the picture, or attributes to the artist
> knowledge they could not have had, is a worse reading, and critics argue about this
> productively all the time.**
> **⚠️ What IS genuinely person-relative is PREFERENCE — and preference was never the
> thing theory was trying to adjudicate** (§10 → `art-aesthetics-and-philosophy-of-art`).

**⚠️ A further honest note.** ⚠️ **Art theory in the academy is substantially a normative
and political discipline as well as a descriptive one** — **much of §19 → `art-interpretation-criticism-and-the-art-world` is explicitly
committed rather than neutral.** **That is not a scandal; it is what interpretive
disciplines are.** ⚠️ **But it means "art theory says X" often means "one committed
tradition within art theory says X," and you should ask which.**

## §2. The Elements

```
LINE      ⚠️ direction, weight, character. Contour vs gesture. Line is a
          CONVENTION — there are no outlines in nature, only edges
SHAPE     2D. ⚠️ Positive and NEGATIVE — the shapes between things are shapes
FORM      3D, or its illusion
VALUE     ⚠️ lightness/darkness. THE most important element (§3)
COLOUR    hue, saturation, value (§4)
TEXTURE   actual vs implied
SPACE     ⚠️ depth cues, and the picture plane as a flat thing (§6)
EDGE      ⚠️ hard, soft, lost, found — the most under-taught element of all
```
**⚠️ Edges deserve their own mention because they're where amateur and expert work most
visibly diverge.** **Uniformly hard edges make everything equally insistent and the image
reads as cut-out.** ⚠️ **Varying edge quality — hard at the focal point, softening and
losing edges elsewhere — is how a painter directs attention and creates air**, and it
tracks how vision actually works: **you have sharp acuity only at the fovea.**

## §3. Value

> **⚠️ Value does more work than colour, and this is the single most useful technical fact
> in visual art.** ⚠️ **Convert any effective image to greyscale and it still reads;
> convert an ineffective one and it collapses into mush.** **Composition, depth, focus and
> legibility are carried primarily by value structure.**

**⚠️ Why, mechanistically**: **the human visual system processes luminance at much higher
spatial resolution than chrominance** — **which is exactly why image and video compression
throws away colour detail first and nobody notices** (see a graphics reference on chroma
subsampling).
**Practical consequences:**
- **⚠️ Squint at the work.** **Squinting suppresses detail and colour and shows you the
  value masses. If it doesn't read squinted, it doesn't read.**
- **⚠️ Notan / big-shape thinking**: **design in three or four value masses before any
  detail.**
- **⚠️ Value CONTRAST directs the eye.** **The greatest light-dark contrast is where the
  viewer looks first — so put it where you want them.**
- **⚠️ Value controls depth**: **atmospheric perspective compresses value range with
  distance** (§6).
- ⚠️ **A common failure is correct local values with no overall structure** — **every
  object rendered accurately, the whole picture reading as noise.**

## §4. Colour

**Dimensions**: **hue, saturation/chroma, value/lightness.** **Systems: Munsell (⚠️
perceptually organized and still the most useful for painters), HSL/HSV, CIELAB
(perceptually uniform-ish), RGB/CMYK (device).**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — red-yellow-blue primaries are a pedagogical fiction, and this is taught in
> schools worldwide.** ⚠️ **RYB cannot mix a large part of the visible gamut — try making
> a clean magenta or a vivid cyan from it.** **The actual situation:**
> - **⚠️ ADDITIVE (light): RGB primaries. This is about the three cone types in your eye.**
> - **⚠️ SUBTRACTIVE (pigment/ink): cyan, magenta, yellow. This is why your printer uses
>   CMYK and not RYB.**
> - **⚠️ Paint mixing is neither, exactly** — **real pigments have complex spectral
>   reflectance, scatter, and are not ideal subtractive filters, which is why mixing
>   behaviour is partly empirical and pigment-specific.**
>
> **⚠️ RYB survives because it produces acceptable results for a limited palette and it's
> historically entrenched, not because it's correct.**

**⚠️ "Colour theory" as commonly taught is a mixture of real perceptual science and
inherited convention, and it's worth knowing which is which:**
```
⚠️ REAL PERCEPTUAL EFFECTS
  Simultaneous contrast — ⚠️ a colour's appearance depends on its surround.
    Robust, dramatic, and the core of Albers' Interaction of Color
  Afterimages and opponent process — red/green, blue/yellow, black/white
  ⚠️ Colour constancy — you perceive surface colour, not the light hitting your
    eye. This is why "the dress" was possible
  Warm advances / cool recedes — ⚠️ a real tendency, partly optical, easily
    overridden by value and context
  Saturation and value interact — ⚠️ you cannot have a very light, very
    saturated blue in pigment. The pigments don't exist

⚠️ CONVENTION DRESSED AS LAW
  "Complementary schemes are harmonious" — ⚠️ a useful default, not a fact
  The 60-30-10 rule and similar formulas — heuristics
  ⚠️ Fixed colour symbolism — HEAVILY culture-dependent (§24). White is
    mourning in parts of East Asia. Blue-for-boys is recent and was
    once reversed
```
**⚠️ The practically decisive point**: **most colour problems are actually VALUE problems
or SATURATION problems.** ⚠️ **Beginners reach for a different hue when what's wrong is
that everything is at the same saturation and mid-value.** **Controlling the saturation
range — keeping most of the picture relatively neutral so a small saturated area can sing
— is the higher-leverage skill.**

## §5. Composition

```
BALANCE      symmetrical, asymmetrical, radial. ⚠️ Visual weight ≠ physical size
             — a small dark or saturated shape can balance a large pale one
EMPHASIS     ⚠️ focal point via value contrast, edge, isolation, convergence, colour
UNITY / VARIETY   ⚠️ the fundamental tension. Too much unity is dull; too much
             variety disintegrates
RHYTHM       repetition with variation; intervals
MOVEMENT     ⚠️ how the eye travels. Leading lines, implied direction, gaze
SCALE / PROPORTION   relations to each other and to the viewer
HIERARCHY    ⚠️ what reads first, second, third
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the rule of thirds and the golden ratio are not what they're presented
> as.** ⚠️ **The rule of thirds is a 19th-century compositional convenience with no
> perceptual basis; it works mainly because it stops beginners centring everything.**
> **The golden ratio's supposed ubiquity in historic art is largely retrospective
> pattern-matching** — ⚠️ **claims about the Parthenon and the Mona Lisa require choosing
> which points to measure between, and the measurements rarely survive scrutiny.**
> **Neither is worthless as a starting device. Both are false as laws.**

**⚠️ What is better supported**: **the picture plane's edges are active** (**shapes relate
to the frame, and tangents — where an edge just touches another — create ambiguity and
read as errors**); **closure and grouping follow Gestalt principles** (**proximity,
similarity, continuity, closure, figure-ground**), ⚠️ **which ARE grounded in perception
and are the most defensible "rules" in composition**; **and the eye is drawn to faces,
contrast, and points of convergence involuntarily.**

## §6. Pictorial Space

```
LINEAR PERSPECTIVE  ⚠️ 1/2/3-point. A CONVENTION formalized in the Italian
   Renaissance (Brunelleschi, Alberti) — ⚠️ geometrically correct for a single
   fixed eye, which is not how anyone looks at anything
ATMOSPHERIC   ⚠️ distance reduces contrast and saturation, shifts toward the
   sky's hue. Physically real (scattering) and reliably convincing
OVERLAP, SCALE, POSITION, TEXTURE GRADIENT, FOCUS   ⚠️ overlap is the strongest
   and simplest depth cue there is
```
**⚠️ Alternative spatial systems are not failed perspective.** **Isometric/axonometric
(much East Asian painting), hierarchical scale (Egyptian, medieval — size denotes
importance), multiple viewpoints (Cubism), and flat pictorial space are DIFFERENT
CONVENTIONS with different purposes.** ⚠️ **Reading them as "not yet having discovered
perspective" is a Renaissance-progressivist error, and one that art history has spent a
century correcting** (§16 → `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon`).
**⚠️ The picture plane is always flat**, **and much of modern art's programme was insisting
on that flatness rather than disguising it** (§15 → `art-history-modernism-and-the-canon`).

## §7. Medium and Materiality

**⚠️ Medium is not neutral delivery — it constrains and suggests.**
**Fresco** (⚠️ **must be painted into wet plaster, so it demands decisiveness and
sectioning**), **egg tempera** (⚠️ **fast-drying, hatched, luminous, hard to blend**),
**oil** (⚠️ **slow-drying — which is what made blending, glazing, and extended revision
possible, and it changed what painting could depict**), **watercolour** (⚠️ **transparent,
unforgiving, the white is the paper**), **acrylic**, **printmaking** (⚠️ **relief,
intaglio, lithography, screen — and the MULTIPLE is a conceptual fact as much as a
technical one**), **photography**, **sculpture** (**carving is subtractive and
irreversible; modelling is additive and provisional**), **and time-based, installation,
performance and digital media.**
**⚠️ Materiality carries meaning**: **gold ground signals the eternal and non-spatial;
marble signals permanence and cost; the choice to work in cardboard is a statement about
both.**

> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ YOUR LEVERAGE IS YOUR ALTERNATIVE, NOT YOUR ARGUMENT** (§3). **What you can do if

>    position comes from improving or accurately assessing that alternative, not from
>    technique at the table.**
> 2. **⚠️ POSITIONS ARE WHAT PEOPLE SAY THEY WANT; INTERESTS ARE WHY** (§4). **Positions
>    conflict far more often than interests do. Nearly all value creation comes from finding

>    that win a single exchange routinely destroy the relationship that produces future
>    ones. The counterparty's willingness to deal with you again is usually worth more than
>    the marginal concession.**

## §1. Two Framings

**⚠️ Negotiation is joint decision-making under partly conflicting interests** — ⚠️ **not a
contest, though it can be run as one.**
**⚠️ Conflict resolution is the broader field**, ⚠️ **covering situations where the parties
may not be at a table at all, where the relationship itself is the problem, and where a
third party may be needed.**
**⚠️ The overlap is large and the emphasis differs**: ⚠️ **negotiation literature tends to
focus on claiming and creating value in a defined transaction; conflict resolution
literature on relationships, process and de-escalation.** ⚠️ **Both matter, and someone
strong in one is often weak in the other.**
**⚠️ The honest framing of what skill buys you**: ⚠️ **technique moves outcomes at the
margin. Preparation, alternatives and the structure of the situation move them far more —
which is why §3 and §5 come before any tactic.**

# PART I — FOUNDATIONS

## §2. Distributive and Integrative

```
⚠️ DISTRIBUTIVE (claiming value)  ⚠️ a fixed pie; ⚠️ what one
   side gains the other loses. ⚠️ Single-issue price haggling
   is the pure case
⚠️ ⚠️ INTEGRATIVE (creating value)  ⚠️ multiple issues valued
   DIFFERENTLY by the parties, permitting trades that make both
   better off
⚠️ ⚠️ THE FIXED-PIE BIAS  ⚠️ negotiators systematically ASSUME
   conflict where interests are actually compatible.
   ⚠️ Experimental work repeatedly finds parties failing to
   identify issues on which they AGREE — leaving value on the
   table in a negotiation they experienced as adversarial
⚠️ ⚠️ ALMOST EVERY REAL NEGOTIATION IS MIXED-MOTIVE  ⚠️ you
   create value and then claim a share of it, and the two
   activities pull in opposite directions (§9)
⚠️ ⚠️ THE COMMON ERROR IS COLLAPSING TO DISTRIBUTIVE TOO EARLY.
   ⚠️ Reducing a rich situation to one number — price, salary,
   damages — throws away the structure that makes trades
   possible (§8)
⚠️ THE DIAGNOSTIC  ⚠️ how many issues are genuinely in play, and
   do the parties rank them differently? ⚠️ If yes, there is
   value to create and haggling is premature
```

## §3. ⚠️ BATNA, Reservation Value and ZOPA

> **⚠️ §1's first organizing idea, and the single most useful concept in the field.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ BATNA  ⚠️ Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement —
   what you will actually do if this deal does not happen
   ⚠️ ⚠️ IT IS AN ALTERNATIVE, NOT A WISH. ⚠️ "I'll find
   something better" is not a BATNA; a written offer from
   someone else is
⚠️ RESERVATION VALUE (walk-away point)  ⚠️ derived FROM the
   BATNA plus the costs of switching and the risks of delay
⚠️ ⚠️ ZOPA  ⚠️ Zone Of Possible Agreement — the range between
   the two reservation values. ⚠️ If they do not overlap, there
   is NO DEAL that beats both sides' alternatives, and the
   skilled move is to recognize that early rather than to
   negotiate harder
⚠️ ⚠️ THREE CONSEQUENCES THAT FOLLOW IMMEDIATELY
   ⚠️ 1. ⚠️ IMPROVING YOUR BATNA IMPROVES YOUR POSITION MORE
      THAN ANY TACTIC. ⚠️ The work happens AWAY from the table
   ⚠️ 2. ⚠️ ESTIMATE THEIR BATNA — ⚠️ people systematically
      OVERESTIMATE the other side's alternatives and
      underestimate their own, which produces unnecessary
      concessions
   ⚠️ 3. ⚠️ A GOOD DEAL IS ONE THAT BEATS YOUR BATNA. ⚠️ Not one
      that beats their offer, not one that feels like a win,
      and not one that matches an arbitrary target
⚠️ ⚠️ THE ANTI-BATNA TRAP  ⚠️ if you have no alternative and
   they know it, technique will not save you. ⚠️ The honest
   answer is to build an alternative before negotiating, or to
   change the game (§19)
⚠️ SUNK COSTS ARE NOT PART OF YOUR RESERVATION VALUE, and
   treating them as such is one of the commonest errors (§11)
```

## §4. ⚠️ Interests, Positions and Issues

> **⚠️ §1's second organizing idea — the core insight of the Harvard/Fisher-Ury tradition.**
```
⚠️ POSITION  ⚠️ what someone says they want. ⚠️ "I need $80,000"
⚠️ ⚠️ INTEREST  ⚠️ WHY they want it — the need, concern or fear
   underneath. ⚠️ Security, recognition, fairness, autonomy,
   predictability, face
⚠️ ⚠️ POSITIONS CONFLICT FAR MORE OFTEN THAN INTERESTS DO.
   ⚠️ The classic illustration — two people want one orange;
   one needs the peel for zest and the other the flesh for
   juice — is trite precisely because the pattern is so common
⚠️ ⚠️ THE OPERATIVE QUESTION IS "WHY?" AND "WHY NOT?"  ⚠️ Why do
   you need that? What would that let you do? What concern does
   my proposal raise? ⚠️ These are cheap, and most negotiators
   never ask them
⚠️ ⚠️ MULTIPLE INTERESTS PER PARTY, and they include
   ⚠️ SUBSTANTIVE (the deal terms) · ⚠️ PROCESS (being heard,
   being treated properly) · ⚠️ RELATIONSHIP (future dealings) ·
   ⚠️ IDENTITY AND FACE (⚠️ hugely underrated — many impasses
   are about not appearing to have capitulated, not about terms)
⚠️ ISSUES are the negotiable items; ⚠️ ADDING ISSUES creates
   trade possibilities where a single-issue frame had none (§8)
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CAVEAT WORTH MAKING  ⚠️ interest-based negotiation is
   not universally superior. ⚠️ Some negotiations really ARE
   distributive, some counterparts exploit openness (§9), and
   the approach assumes a good-faith interest in agreement that
   is sometimes absent (§15)
```

## §5. Preparation

**⚠️ Preparation is the highest-return activity in negotiation and the most commonly
skipped** — ⚠️ **and the research consistently finds structured preparation improves joint
gains and satisfaction** (§28.1 → `negot-reference`).
**⚠️ What to prepare**: ⚠️ **your interests, ranked; your estimate of THEIR interests; the
full issue list; your BATNA and reservation value; your estimate of their BATNA; the
information you need and the information you will and will not share (§9 → `negot-anchoring-concessions-value-creation-and-closing`); objective
standards you can appeal to (§13 → `negot-biases-emotion-fairness-and-trust`); and your opening approach.**
**⚠️ Set an aspiration as well as a reservation value** — ⚠️ **the evidence associates
ambitious but justified targets with better outcomes, while a reservation value alone tends
to become a target.**
**⚠️ Prepare the RELATIONSHIP and PROCESS**, not just the substance: ⚠️ **who attends, in
what order issues are discussed, what the agenda implies, and who has authority** (§17 → `negot-hard-tactics-multiparty-culture-power-and-remote`).
**⚠️ Rehearse their case**, ⚠️ **not yours — being able to state their position better than
they can is both preparation and a persuasion asset** (see a speaking reference).

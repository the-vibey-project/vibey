## §20. How Groups Actually Work

**⚠️ Formation stages** (forming, storming, norming, performing) are ⚠️ **a useful shorthand
and not a law — groups cycle, regress on membership change, and some never leave
storming.**
**⚠️ Norms are set early and by behaviour, not by statements** — ⚠️ **what a group tolerates
in its first months becomes what it is.**
**⚠️ Roles emerge** whether or not they are assigned, ⚠️ **and the informal ones — the
connector, the historian, the person everyone actually asks — matter more than the titles.**
**⚠️ The dynamics that reliably cause harm**: ⚠️ **groupthink; the abilene paradox (everyone
going along with something nobody wants); diffusion of responsibility; ⚠️ and the
TYRANNY OF STRUCTURELESSNESS — ⚠️ Freeman's point that a group without formal structure does
not lack power structure, it has an INVISIBLE and unaccountable one, which is worse.**
**⚠️ Size changes everything**: ⚠️ **a group where everyone knows everyone works by
relationship; past that it needs process, and the transition is where most groups fail
without noticing why.**

## §21. Building a Community

**⚠️ Start with a real purpose and real people** — ⚠️ **not a platform. The tooling question
is downstream and much less important than it feels.**
**⚠️ The founding problem is critical mass**: ⚠️ **an empty room is unattractive, so early
effort goes into a small core who show up reliably, and consistency beats scale.**
**⚠️ Rhythm matters more than content**: ⚠️ **a predictable, sustainable cadence — the same
time, reliably — builds a community faster than occasional excellent events.**
**⚠️ Onboarding is the growth constraint**: ⚠️ **most communities are unintentionally hostile
to newcomers through in-jokes, unexplained context, and nobody assigned to talk to the
person standing alone.** ⚠️ **A named greeter role is a small intervention with a large
effect.**
**⚠️ Explicit norms and a code of conduct**, ⚠️ **with the crucial part being that it is
ENFORCED — an unenforced code is worse than none, because it advertises safety that isn't
there** (§25).
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the founder bottleneck is the most common failure mode.** ⚠️ **A community
> where one person does everything cannot grow, cannot survive that person's absence, and
> quietly discourages others from taking initiative.** **⚠️ Delegating badly early beats
> delegating well later, and succession should be planned before it is needed.**

## §22. ⚠️ Facilitation

> **⚠️ A distinct skill from speaking, frequently assigned to whoever is most senior rather
> than whoever can do it.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE FACILITATOR SERVES THE GROUP'S PROCESS, NOT THE
   OUTCOME. ⚠️ Holding both roles — running the meeting and
   advocating a position — is a conflict, and it should be
   named or separated
⚠️ BEFORE  ⚠️ purpose, desired output, right people, ⚠️ AGENDA
   CIRCULATED IN ADVANCE, pre-reading
⚠️ ⚠️ THE MOST VALUABLE MOVE: DECIDE HOW YOU WILL DECIDE, BEFORE
   DISCUSSING. ⚠️ Consensus, consent, majority, or
   consultative-then-decided — ⚠️ ambiguity about the decision
   rule is the cause of most meeting frustration and most
   post-meeting resentment
⚠️ DURING  ⚠️ start on time · ⚠️ MANAGE AIRTIME actively (⚠️ the
   loudest voice is not the best signal, and quiet people often
   have the objection that matters) · ⚠️ go round to draw people
   in · park tangents visibly · surface disagreement rather
   than smoothing it · ⚠️ name the dynamic when it's the problem
⚠️ AFTER  ⚠️ decisions, owners and dates, circulated. ⚠️ A
   meeting with no recorded decision usually did not have one
⚠️ ⚠️ THE MEETING THAT SHOULD NOT HAVE HAPPENED  ⚠️ if the
   purpose is broadcast, write it down instead
⚠️ ONLINE  harder — ⚠️ shorter, more structured, explicit
   turn-taking, chat as a parallel channel to actually read
```

## §23. Organizing and Mobilizing

**⚠️ The distinction that matters**: ⚠️ **MOBILIZING activates people who already agree;
ORGANIZING builds power by developing people and relationships over time.** ⚠️ **Mobilizing
is faster and does not compound; organizing is slow and does.**
**⚠️ The one-to-one relational meeting** is the core organizing practice — ⚠️ **a
conversation to understand someone's actual interests and motivations, not to recruit them
on the spot.**
**⚠️ Ladders of engagement**: ⚠️ **give people a first step that costs little, then a real
one; and ⚠️ the most common failure is having no rung between "attend" and "run the
thing."**
**⚠️ Power mapping**: ⚠️ **who decides, who influences them, and what they respond to.**
**⚠️ Coalitions** work on narrow shared interest and fracture on scope creep.
**⚠️ Sustaining momentum** — ⚠️ **celebrating small wins is not sentimentality; it is how
people learn that effort produces outcomes, which is the belief the whole thing runs on.**

## §24. ⚠️ Volunteers and Burnout

```
⚠️ ⚠️ VOLUNTEERS ARE NOT UNPAID STAFF. ⚠️ They are paid in
   meaning, belonging, growth and recognition, and the
   management practice has to match that
⚠️ WHY PEOPLE ACTUALLY VOLUNTEER  ⚠️ purpose · relationships ·
   ⚠️ being ASKED (⚠️ the most underrated factor — most people
   who do not participate were never personally asked) · using a
   skill · being needed
⚠️ WHY THEY STOP  ⚠️ the ask was unbounded · unclear
   expectations · ⚠️ feeling unnecessary · being taken for
   granted · ⚠️ conflict that nobody addressed · life changed
   and there was no honourable exit
⚠️ ⚠️ THE SPECIFIC, BOUNDED ASK IS THE WHOLE TECHNIQUE.
   ⚠️ "Would you run the newsletter?" is a life sentence.
   ⚠️ "Would you write the newsletter for the next three
   months?" gets a yes and is renewable
⚠️ ⚠️ BURNOUT  ⚠️ it is a structural problem, not a personal
   weakness — ⚠️ and in mission-driven and faith communities it
   is elevated precisely because the work feels non-negotiable
   ⚠️ THE SIGNS  cynicism, exhaustion, resentment, dread
   ⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL FIXES  ⚠️ term limits · rotation ·
      ⚠️ genuinely making it acceptable to stop · sabbaticals ·
      more than one person per critical function
   ⚠️ ⚠️ "SELF-CARE" ADVICE ADDRESSED TO AN OVERLOADED PERSON
      IS USUALLY A WAY OF AVOIDING THE STRUCTURAL FIX
⚠️ RECOGNITION  ⚠️ specific and public beats generic. ⚠️ And
   thanking people who have left matters more than it seems
```

## §25. Community Conflict

**⚠️ Conflict at group scale differs from §17 → `speaking-listening-rapport-difficult-conversations-and-negotiation`** — ⚠️ **it has an audience, factions form
fast, and history accumulates.**
**⚠️ Address early and directly.** ⚠️ **Hoping it resolves is the standard mistake, and the
cost of an awkward early conversation is always lower than the cost of the late one.**
**⚠️ Handle privately where possible, publicly where the community needs to see it
handled** — ⚠️ **and knowing which is a judgement call about whether the harm was public.**
**⚠️ The missing stair problem**: ⚠️ **a community that quietly works around a person who
harms others is protecting the person and sacrificing everyone who encounters them
unwarned.** ⚠️ **This is common, it is a leadership failure, and it is usually rationalized
as keeping the peace.**
**⚠️ Enforcement**: ⚠️ **consistent, proportionate, documented, with a path back where
appropriate — ⚠️ and the willingness to actually remove someone is what makes every other
norm credible.**
**⚠️ Safeguarding** in any community involving children or vulnerable adults is a formal
obligation with legal requirements, ⚠️ **not a matter of judgement or community feeling, and
it needs written policy, trained people and reporting lines established before anything
happens.**

## §26. ⚠️ Communication Difference

**⚠️ Communication norms are conventions, not standards** — ⚠️ **and treating one culture's
conventions as universal competence excludes people unnecessarily.**
```
⚠️ WHERE NORMS DIFFER LEGITIMATELY  ⚠️ eye contact · directness ·
   interruption and overlap · silence tolerance · emotional
   expressiveness · high vs low context communication ·
   formality · turn-taking
⚠️ ⚠️ THE DOUBLE EMPATHY PROBLEM  ⚠️ Milton's finding, now
   well-supported: communication difficulty between autistic and
   non-autistic people is MUTUAL and bidirectional — ⚠️ not a
   deficit in one party. ⚠️ Autistic people communicate
   effectively with each other; the breakdown is at the
   interface. ⚠️ This reframes "social skills" from a deficit to
   be remediated into a translation problem with shared
   responsibility
⚠️ PRACTICAL ACCOMMODATIONS THAT COST NOTHING  ⚠️ agendas in
   advance · written follow-up · ⚠️ saying what you mean
   literally · ⚠️ not requiring eye contact · allowing
   processing time before demanding an answer · ⚠️ permitting
   fidgeting and movement · lighting and noise · advance notice
   of changes
⚠️ FOR SPEAKERS  ⚠️ captions · handouts · clear structure ·
   ⚠️ not relying on tone alone to carry meaning · describing
   visuals aloud
⚠️ ⚠️ AND NOTE THE ASYMMETRY: most of these help EVERYONE.
   ⚠️ Clear agendas and written decisions are simply better
   practice, which is the usual pattern with accessibility
```

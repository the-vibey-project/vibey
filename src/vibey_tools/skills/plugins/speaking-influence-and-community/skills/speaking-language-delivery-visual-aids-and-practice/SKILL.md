>    modes are in sustaining: the founder bottleneck, volunteer burnout, unhandled conflict,

## §6. Language for the Ear

**⚠️ Written prose and spoken prose are different crafts** (see a prose-craft reference for
the written side).
⚠️ **Shorter sentences; concrete over abstract; active voice; ⚠️ ONE idea per sentence,
because a listener cannot re-parse a nested clause.**
**⚠️ What works aloud**: ⚠️ **repetition (⚠️ which reads as clumsy and lands as emphasis),
parallel structure, deliberate pauses, and short sentences after long ones.**
**⚠️ Jargon** — ⚠️ **define it or drop it, and note that the curse of knowledge (§3 → `speaking-what-the-field-knows-anxiety-audience-and-structure`) makes
you a poor judge of which terms are jargon.**
**⚠️ Numbers need translating**: ⚠️ **an abstract quantity means nothing spoken aloud;
compare it to something the audience can picture.**
**⚠️ Read it aloud in draft.** ⚠️ **Anything you stumble over, the audience will stumble
over — and reading aloud is the single most reliable editing technique for spoken material.**

## §7. Delivery

```
⚠️ VOICE  ⚠️ PROJECTION comes from breath support, not strain ·
   ⚠️ PACE — nerves accelerate you, so deliberately slow down ·
   ⚠️ PAUSES are the most underused tool, and they feel far
   longer to you than to the audience · variation in pitch and
   volume · articulation
⚠️ BODY  ⚠️ stillness with purposeful movement beats either
   pacing or rigidity · gestures that arise from the content ·
   ⚠️ EYE CONTACT distributed around the room, in whole
   sentences to one person at a time rather than sweeping
⚠️ ⚠️ AUTHENTICITY BEATS TECHNIQUE. ⚠️ Audiences detect
   performance, and a slightly awkward speaker who clearly means
   it outperforms a polished one who doesn't
⚠️ NOTES  ⚠️ full script (⚠️ risks reading, which kills contact) ·
   bullet notes (⚠️ usually the best compromise) · memorized
   (⚠️ risky — a blank is catastrophic) · ⚠️ know the opening and
   closing verbatim and the middle by structure
⚠️ FILLER WORDS  ⚠️ far less noticeable to audiences than to the
   speaker. ⚠️ The fix is comfort with silence, not policing
⚠️ ⚠️ AND SEE §27.1 BEFORE BELIEVING ANY PERCENTAGE-BASED CLAIM
   ABOUT BODY LANGUAGE
```

## §8. ⚠️ Visual Aids

> **⚠️ The most reliably misused tool in the field.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CENTRAL ERROR: using slides as speaker notes. ⚠️ Dense
   text on screen forces the audience to choose between reading
   and listening, and they do neither well. ⚠️ This is a real
   cognitive limit, not a style preference
⚠️ WHAT SLIDES ARE GOOD FOR  ⚠️ images that would take a
   paragraph to describe · data that has a SHAPE · diagrams of
   spatial or structural relationships · ⚠️ a single sentence
   you want to sit in the room
⚠️ WHAT THEY ARE BAD FOR  ⚠️ your notes · lists of your points ·
   anything you are about to say anyway
⚠️ ⚠️ THE HANDOUT IS A DIFFERENT DOCUMENT. ⚠️ If people need the
   detail, give them the detail separately — do not compromise
   both by merging them
⚠️ ⚠️ A BLACK SCREEN IS A LEGITIMATE SLIDE. ⚠️ When you want full
   attention, remove the competition
⚠️ THE PRACTICALITIES THAT ACTUALLY GO WRONG  ⚠️ font too small ·
   colour that fails on a projector · ⚠️ video and audio ·
   adapters · presenter view not working · ⚠️ ALWAYS have a
   plan for giving the talk with no slides at all
```

## §9. Q&A and Hostility

**⚠️ Structure**: ⚠️ **repeat or paraphrase the question (⚠️ so the room hears it and you
confirm you understood), answer, check.**
**⚠️ "I don't know" is a strong answer.** ⚠️ **Followed by what you'd do to find out. The
attempt to bluff is what damages credibility, not the gap.**
**⚠️ The difficult types**: ⚠️ **the statement-not-a-question (⚠️ acknowledge, extract a
question if there is one, move on); the multi-part question (⚠️ answer the best part);
the hostile question; the off-topic; the person who asks three in a row.**
**⚠️ On hostility specifically**: ⚠️ **separate the challenge from the tone. ⚠️ Answer the
legitimate part seriously — an audience watching a hostile questioner get a fair, calm
answer draws its own conclusions.** ⚠️ **Do not match aggression, do not be sarcastic, and
do not pretend a real objection is illegitimate because it was rudely put.**
**⚠️ When you are wrong**, say so plainly. ⚠️ **It costs far less than defending an error,
and it buys credibility on everything else.**
**⚠️ Bad-faith questioning** — ⚠️ **the goal is usually to derail rather than to learn.**
⚠️ **Answer once, briefly, for the room's benefit; then move on and do not be drawn into
sequence.**

## §10. Formats

**⚠️ Each has its own physics**, ⚠️ **and applying keynote habits to a panel is a common
failure.**
⚠️ **KEYNOTE — one idea, memorably. ⚠️ TECHNICAL TALK — respect the audience's knowledge and
still structure it; the most common flaw is too much material.
⚠️ TEACHING — retrieval and practice beat exposition; a lecture is not a good learning
technology by itself.
⚠️ PANEL — ⚠️ be brief, build on others, do not deliver a mini-keynote; the moderator's
job is traffic control and it is harder than it looks.
⚠️ PITCH — problem, solution, why you, what you want; ⚠️ and know what the ask is.
⚠️ TOAST or eulogy — short, specific, one real story, personal.
⚠️ SERMON or devotional — text, exposition, application; the discipline is not making the
text serve your point.
⚠️ REMOTE — ⚠️ the hardest, because you lose the feedback loop; camera at eye level, look
at the LENS, shorter segments, deliberate interaction, and assume attention is more fragile.
⚠️ IMPROMPTU — a structure buys you time: point, reason, example, restate.**

## §11. Practice and Getting Better

**⚠️ Rehearse aloud, standing, at full volume** — ⚠️ **silent reading is not rehearsal and
does not reveal what breaks.**
**⚠️ Time it**, ⚠️ **and note that live delivery usually runs longer than rehearsal.**
**⚠️ Record yourself.** ⚠️ **Unpleasant and by far the fastest feedback available.**
**⚠️ Deliberate practice** means working a specific weakness, ⚠️ **not repeating whole talks
— pick one thing per outing.**
**⚠️ Feedback**: ⚠️ **ask specific questions ("was the middle section clear?") rather than
"how was it?", which reliably produces "good."**
**⚠️ The venues that give reps**: ⚠️ **speaking clubs, meetups, teaching, church and
community roles, internal brown-bags.** ⚠️ **§2 → `speaking-what-the-field-knows-anxiety-audience-and-structure`'s point stands — volume of exposure is the
mechanism.**
**⚠️ Keep a record** of what worked and what didn't while it's fresh.

## §6. ⚠️ How Airfare Pricing Actually Works

**⚠️ Understanding the mechanism kills most of the folklore.**
```
⚠️ REVENUE MANAGEMENT  seats are sold in FARE BUCKETS at different
   prices; ⚠️ the airline adjusts availability by bucket as it
   forecasts demand. ⚠️ The "price" you see is which bucket is open
⚠️ THEREFORE  ⚠️ prices are not a smooth function of time-to-departure.
   They step, and they can fall as well as rise
⚠️ DYNAMIC PRICING and NDC increasingly personalize offers
⚠️ THE PRICE RESPONDS TO DEMAND ON THAT ROUTE, not to you
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the persistent myths, and why they're wrong.** ⚠️ **"Book on a Tuesday" is
> a fossil of an era when fare filings updated on a weekly cycle; it does not reliably
> hold now.** ⚠️ **"Incognito mode stops prices rising because they track you" is
> essentially folklore — repeated searches do not meaningfully drive up the fare you're
> shown, and the observed variation is usually inventory changing.**
> ⚠️ **"There's a magic number of days ahead" is a population average masquerading as
> a rule; the useful version is that BOOKING WINDOWS VARY BY ROUTE, season and demand.**
> **⚠️ What actually works: flexibility on dates and airports, watching a route over time
> with fare alerts, and knowing what a good price for YOUR route looks like.**

**⚠️ Practical mechanics worth knowing**: ⚠️ **the US 24-hour free cancellation rule for
tickets booked well ahead; ⚠️ error fares exist and airlines sometimes honour and
sometimes cancel them; ⚠️ "hidden city" ticketing violates carrier contracts and carries
real risk (forfeited return legs, loyalty account closure); and ⚠️ basic economy fares
often cost more once bags and seats are added — compare TOTAL cost.**

## §7. Flight Strategy

**⚠️ Routing**: ⚠️ **nonstop reduces disruption risk substantially — every connection is
another chance to fail.** **⚠️ If connecting, mind MINIMUM CONNECTION TIME, and know that
the legal minimum is not a comfortable one, particularly with terminal changes,
immigration, or a change from a domestic to an international terminal.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — SEPARATE TICKETS are the single most consequential booking mistake.**
> ⚠️ **On one ticket (or interlined), a missed connection is the airline's problem and
> they rebook you.** **⚠️ On separate tickets, a missed connection is entirely YOUR problem
> — the second airline owes you nothing, and your insurance may not cover it either.**
> **⚠️ If you must self-connect, leave hours, not minutes, and prefer separate days for
> anything critical.**

**⚠️ Alliances and interlining** matter for exactly this reason; ⚠️ **low-cost carriers
generally do not interline.**
**⚠️ Seat selection**: **⚠️ SeatGuru-type resources for the specific aircraft; exit rows;
⚠️ and the genuine trade-off that the back of the aircraft is bumpier and later to
disembark but often emptier.**
**⚠️ Overbooking and denied boarding** — ⚠️ **compensation rules are jurisdiction-specific
and are worth knowing before you're at the desk** (§25 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`, §26.2 → `travel-reference`).

## §8. ⚠️ Points and Miles, Honestly

```
⚠️ THE HONEST FRAME  ⚠️ points are a DEVALUING CURRENCY issued by a
   party that controls the exchange rate and can change it without
   notice. ⚠️ Programmes have repeatedly moved from fixed award
   charts to dynamic pricing, which is a devaluation by another name
⚠️ THEREFORE  ⚠️ EARN AND BURN. Hoarding points is holding an asset
   that reliably loses value
⚠️ WHERE THE VALUE ACTUALLY IS
   ⚠️ Sign-up bonuses (⚠️ by far the largest single source for most
      people) · premium cabin redemptions on partner airlines ·
      hotel free-night certificates · ⚠️ elite status perks you'd
      genuinely use
⚠️ WHERE IT ISN'T
   ⚠️ Earning miles on everyday spend at typical rates · redeeming
      for merchandise or gift cards · ⚠️ paying interest to earn
      points, which loses money immediately and completely
⚠️ TRANSFERABLE POINTS  more flexible than airline-specific, because
   they hedge against any one programme devaluing
```
**⚠️ The credit card caveat, stated plainly**: ⚠️ **points strategies assume you pay the
balance in full every month.** **If you don't, the interest exceeds any plausible points
value and this whole section is a net loss.**
**⚠️ Also note the time cost**: ⚠️ **optimizing loyalty programmes is a hobby with a real
hourly rate, and for many people the honest answer is a single flexible card and no
further effort.**

## §9. Accommodation

**⚠️ Match the type to the trip (§1 → `travel-trip-type-destination-constraints-and-budget`):**
```
HOTEL       ⚠️ predictable, serviced, recourse when things go wrong
APARTMENT/RENTAL  ⚠️ space, kitchen, laundry — genuinely better for
   families and long stays; ⚠️ variable, less recourse, and check
   local regulation (some cities restrict short-term rentals)
HOSTEL      ⚠️ social, cheap; private rooms exist and are underrated
GUESTHOUSE / RYOKAN / B&B  ⚠️ often the most local experience
```
**⚠️ Location dominates almost every other factor** — ⚠️ **and "distance to the centre" is
less useful than "distance to the transit line you'll actually use."** **Check the walk at
night, the noise (⚠️ a lively square is charming at 6pm and not at 2am), and the actual
route from the airport.**
**⚠️ Reading reviews**: ⚠️ **read the 3-star reviews, not the 1s or 5s — they're the most
informative.** **⚠️ Filter for recent, and for reviewers like you.** **⚠️ Repeated complaints
about the same specific thing are signal; a single furious review usually isn't.**
**⚠️ Booking direct vs aggregator**: ⚠️ **direct often gets better treatment when something
goes wrong, and sometimes better cancellation terms.**

## §10. Ground Transport

**⚠️ Rail** (see a rail reference for the mechanics): ⚠️ **often faster than flying
city-centre to city-centre under roughly 700 km once you count airport time; ⚠️ passes
versus point-to-point depends heavily on the country, and passes are frequently NOT the
cheaper option; ⚠️ and reservations are mandatory on many high-speed services even with a
pass.**
**⚠️ Driving**: ⚠️ **check whether an International Driving Permit is required; understand
insurance excess and what your card actually covers; ⚠️ note urban restricted zones (ZTL
in Italy, low-emission zones) where fines arrive months later; and ⚠️ parking is often the
real cost and constraint in cities.**
**⚠️ Local transit**: ⚠️ **learn the fare system BEFORE arriving — day passes, contactless
tap, stored-value cards — because getting this wrong at the airport at midnight is a
predictable annoyance.**
**⚠️ Ride-hailing and taxis**: ⚠️ **know the norm and the approximate fare before you need
one, and know which apps work locally.**

## §11. ⚠️ Booking Order and Optionality

```
⚠️ THE ORDER THAT MINIMIZES REGRET
   1. ⚠️ Check documents and entry requirements FIRST (§12) —
      before spending anything
   2. Fix the dates and the biggest constraint
   3. Flights (⚠️ usually the least flexible and most price-volatile)
   4. Accommodation ⚠️ WITH FREE CANCELLATION where available
   5. Insurance ⚠️ EARLY — some coverages depend on buying soon
      after the first booking (§13)
   6. High-demand, timed-entry attractions (§4)
   7. ⚠️ Everything else late, or on the ground
```
**⚠️ Cancellation policy is a hedging instrument**: ⚠️ **a refundable rate at a modest
premium buys you the option to change your mind, and on a volatile trip that option is
worth real money.** ⚠️ **Book refundable early, then rebook non-refundable closer in if
plans firm up.**
**⚠️ Don't over-book the trip in advance** (§17 → `travel-pacing-packing-jet-lag-connectivity-and-culture`) — ⚠️ **prepaid, time-fixed activities are
exactly what makes an itinerary brittle.**

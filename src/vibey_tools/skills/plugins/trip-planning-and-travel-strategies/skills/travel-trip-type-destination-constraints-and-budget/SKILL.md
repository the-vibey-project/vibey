> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ Plan constraints FIRST, destinations second** (§3). **Time, money, energy,
>    companions and season determine what is actually possible; picking a destination
>    first and then discovering it doesn't fit is the common sequence and the wrong one.**
> 2. **⚠️ Under-schedule** (§17 → `travel-pacing-packing-jet-lag-connectivity-and-culture`). **The single most reliable improvement to any itinerary
>    is removing things from it.**
> 3. **⚠️ Optimize for RECOVERY, not for the plan** (§25 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`). **The travellers who have good
>    trips aren't the ones nothing goes wrong for — they're the ones with slack, backups
>    and documentation.**

# PART I — DECIDING

## §1. What Kind of Trip

**⚠️ Naming the trip type prevents most planning arguments, because different types have
incompatible optimizations.**
```
⚠️ REST         minimal decisions, few moves, ⚠️ optimize for FRICTION
                REMOVAL. One base. Ignore "must-sees"
⚠️ EXPLORATION  density of new experience; ⚠️ tolerates fatigue
⚠️ SPECIFIC     an event, a trek, a dive site — ⚠️ everything else
                is logistics around the fixed point
⚠️ SOCIAL       the people are the point; ⚠️ destination is a venue
⚠️ CULTURAL/LEARNING  depth over coverage; ⚠️ fewer places, longer stays
⚠️ ADVENTURE/PHYSICAL  ⚠️ requires honest fitness assessment and
                recovery days built in
⚠️ WORK-ADJACENT  ⚠️ the bleed is the failure mode (§24)
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the most common planning error is building an EXPLORATION itinerary for
> people who need a REST trip.** ⚠️ **It's usually driven by the sense that a long flight
> or an expensive trip must be "maximized," and it reliably produces people who come home
> more tired than they left.** **⚠️ Ask what the trip is FOR, out loud, before anything
> else** — **and if travelling with others, ask them separately** (§23 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`).

## §2. Choosing a Destination

**⚠️ Work backwards from constraints (§3), not forwards from a wish list.**
**⚠️ Useful filters**: **flight time and time zones offset against trip length (⚠️ a
long-haul trip under about five days spends most of itself in transit and adjustment);
season (§4); cost level relative to budget; visa friction (§12 → `travel-documents-insurance-health-safety-and-money`); language and
navigability; and ⚠️ how much infrastructure the trip needs versus how much you want.**
**⚠️ Repeat visits are underrated** — ⚠️ **returning somewhere known removes the entire
orientation cost and is often the better answer for a short or restorative trip.**
**⚠️ Beware destination inflation from social media**: ⚠️ **a place that photographs well
may be crowded, small, or a two-hour detour for a five-minute view.** **Check what the
place is like to BE in, not to photograph.**

## §3. ⚠️ Constraints-First Planning

```
⚠️ THE CONSTRAINTS, in the order they actually bind
   1. ⚠️ TIME — total days INCLUDING travel days. ⚠️ A "week in Japan"
      from Europe or North America is roughly five usable days
   2. ⚠️ MONEY — total, not per-item (§5)
   3. ⚠️ ENERGY — ⚠️ the most-ignored constraint. Health, fitness,
      sleep needs, tolerance for uncertainty, and how you'll be
      feeling AFTER the flight
   4. ⚠️ COMPANIONS — their constraints, honestly gathered (§23)
   5. ⚠️ FIXED DATES — school terms, events, visa windows
   6. ⚠️ DOCUMENTS — passport validity and visa lead times (§12)
      ⚠️ This can eliminate a destination entirely, so check EARLY
```
**⚠️ The travel-day tax**: ⚠️ **count arrival and departure days as roughly half days at
best.** **A "10-day trip" is commonly 8 usable days, and less across time zones.**
**⚠️ The rule of thumb worth adopting**: ⚠️ **decide the number of BASES before the number
of PLACES.** **Each additional base costs a half-day minimum in packing, transit,
check-in and re-orientation** (§17 → `travel-pacing-packing-jet-lag-connectivity-and-culture`).

## §4. Timing and Seasonality

**⚠️ Shoulder season is usually the best answer**: ⚠️ **acceptable weather, lower prices,
fewer crowds, and everything still open.** **⚠️ Check that last one — some destinations
substantially close outside peak.**
**⚠️ What to check before fixing dates**: ⚠️ **monsoon and hurricane seasons (which are
seasonal but not deterministic), extreme heat (⚠️ increasingly a real constraint in
Mediterranean and desert summers), public holidays in the DESTINATION (⚠️ Golden Week,
Lunar New Year, Ramadan, August in southern Europe — things close and prices spike),
school holidays in the source markets, and local festivals you'd want to catch or avoid.**
**⚠️ Weather averages hide variance** — ⚠️ **an average temperature tells you little about
the distribution, and "average rainfall" doesn't distinguish an afternoon shower from
three days indoors.**
**⚠️ Overtourism management is now a real planning variable**: ⚠️ **several destinations
have introduced entry fees, timed-entry booking, visitor caps or accommodation
restrictions, and the popular sites in popular cities increasingly require booking weeks
ahead.** **⚠️ Check specific attraction booking windows before assuming walk-up access.**

## §5. Budgeting

```
⚠️ THE CATEGORIES, roughly in order of underestimation
   ⚠️ GROUND TRANSPORT at destination — chronically underestimated
   ⚠️ FOOD — ⚠️ eating out three times a day is a different budget
      from what people imagine
   ⚠️ ACTIVITIES AND ENTRY FEES — add up fast
   ⚠️ THE "ALREADY HERE" TAX — ⚠️ you will spend more because you're
      there and won't be again. Budget for it rather than pretending
   Accommodation · flights · insurance · visas · gear
   ⚠️ CONTINGENCY — 10-20%. ⚠️ Not optional (§25)
```
**⚠️ Total cost, not nightly rate**: ⚠️ **a cheaper hotel 40 minutes out can cost more in
transport and time than a dearer central one.** **⚠️ Similarly a cheap flight at an awkward
hour may require an extra night's accommodation.**
**⚠️ The sunk cost trap**: ⚠️ **having paid for something does not obligate you to do it.**
**Skipping a booked activity because you're exhausted is frequently the right call.**

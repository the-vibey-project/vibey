## §17. ⚠️ Itinerary Pacing

> **⚠️ The highest-leverage section in this document. The single most reliable improvement
> to any itinerary is REMOVING things from it.**
```
⚠️ ONE MAJOR THING PER DAY. Two if they're close and light.
   ⚠️ Not three, whatever the guidebook implies
⚠️ THE THIRD-DAY SLUMP is real — build a light day in
⚠️ EVERY BASE CHANGE COSTS A HALF DAY minimum (§3)
⚠️ ⚠️ LEAVE UNSCHEDULED TIME. The best parts of trips are usually
   the unplanned ones, and they need room to happen
⚠️ CLUSTER GEOGRAPHICALLY — group by neighbourhood, not by interest
⚠️ CHECK OPENING DAYS. ⚠️ Many museums close Mondays; many
   businesses close on the local rest day
⚠️ BOOK THE FEW THINGS THAT GENUINELY REQUIRE IT, and leave the rest
⚠️ BUILD IN A BUFFER before anything unmissable — ⚠️ never schedule
   a tight connection to a flight, a show or a boat
```
**⚠️ The energy budget is the real constraint** (§3 → `travel-trip-type-destination-constraints-and-budget`): ⚠️ **walking 20,000 steps a day in
heat, in an unfamiliar language, on poor sleep, is genuinely tiring, and travellers
consistently plan as if it isn't.**
**⚠️ Permission to skip things** is worth stating explicitly, especially in groups
(§23 → `travel-groups-long-term-travel-and-handling-disruption`) — ⚠️ **and an afternoon in a park or a café is not a wasted day.**

## §18. Packing

**⚠️ The principles**: ⚠️ **pack less than you think (⚠️ the near-universal report is
having overpacked); choose versatile, layerable, quick-drying items in a coherent colour
palette; ⚠️ plan to do laundry on trips over about a week rather than packing for the
duration; and ⚠️ carry-on only where feasible eliminates baggage delay and loss entirely.**
**⚠️ What actually matters in the bag**: ⚠️ **medication and documents in HAND luggage
always; a change of clothes in hand luggage if you check a bag; a universal adapter;
⚠️ a battery pack (⚠️ lithium batteries must be in the cabin, not the hold); and comfortable
broken-in footwear, which affects the trip more than anything else you pack.**
**⚠️ Check the liquid, battery and sharp-object rules for your specific route** — ⚠️ **they
vary and they change.**
**⚠️ Consider what's cheaper to buy there** than to carry.

## §19. Jet Lag

**⚠️ The mechanism**: ⚠️ **your circadian clock is entrained by LIGHT primarily, and it
shifts roughly an hour a day.** ⚠️ **Eastward travel (advancing the clock) is generally
harder than westward.**
```
⚠️ WHAT HELPS
   ⚠️ LIGHT TIMING is the strongest lever — ⚠️ and the direction
      matters: seek morning light when travelling EAST, evening
      light when travelling WEST (and avoid the wrong-time light)
   ⚠️ Shift sleep timing by an hour or two BEFORE departure
   ⚠️ Adopt destination time immediately on arrival, including meals
   ⚠️ Melatonin can help shift timing (⚠️ timing and dose matter more
      than amount; ⚠️ it's a chronobiotic, not a sedative, and it
      is regulated differently across countries)
   ⚠️ Strategic short naps — ⚠️ short, or they wreck the night
   Hydration; caution with alcohol; caffeine timed deliberately
⚠️ FOR TRIPS UNDER ~3 DAYS  ⚠️ consider NOT adjusting at all
```

## §20. Connectivity and Navigation

**⚠️ Connectivity**: ⚠️ **eSIM has largely solved this where supported — check device
compatibility and carrier lock before travelling.** **⚠️ Compare a local SIM, an eSIM
provider, and your home carrier's roaming pass; ⚠️ home-carrier day passes are convenient
and usually the most expensive.**
**⚠️ Download offline everything before you go**: ⚠️ **maps, translation language packs,
tickets, boarding passes, reservations, and any document you'd need without signal.**
**⚠️ Assume connectivity will fail at the worst moment and plan for it.**
**⚠️ Security**: ⚠️ **public Wi-Fi for anything sensitive is a bad idea; a VPN helps;
⚠️ and note that VPN use is restricted in some countries.**
**⚠️ Navigation**: ⚠️ **maps apps mislead on walking times in hilly cities and on transit in
some countries; ⚠️ local transit apps are often substantially better.**

## §21. Language and Culture

**⚠️ Learning even a little goes further than people expect**: ⚠️ **greetings, please,
thank you, excuse me, numbers, and "I don't speak [language]" in the local language
change interactions materially.** **⚠️ Effort is read as respect regardless of competence.**
**⚠️ Translation apps** with offline packs and camera translation are genuinely
transformative for menus and signage.
**⚠️ Cultural variables worth checking before arrival**: ⚠️ **greeting norms, tipping (§16 → `travel-documents-insurance-health-safety-and-money`),
dress expectations (particularly at religious sites, and ⚠️ this is often stricter than
tourists assume), shoes indoors, dining pace and how to signal for the bill, queuing
norms, photography of people, gestures that don't travel, and directness norms.**
**⚠️ The general posture**: ⚠️ **assume you are the one who doesn't understand the system,
not that the system is broken.** **⚠️ It usually makes sense from the inside.**

## §22. Food and Water

**⚠️ Water**: ⚠️ **check whether tap water is potable at the destination — the answer is
frequently "yes" in places travellers assume otherwise, and vice versa.** ⚠️ **Where it
isn't: sealed bottles, boiling, or a filter/purifier (⚠️ and note filtration and
purification differ — see a water treatment reference); ⚠️ remember ICE, salad washing,
and toothbrushing.**
**⚠️ Food risk heuristics**: ⚠️ **busy places with high turnover are safer than empty ones;
freshly cooked and served hot beats sitting at ambient temperature; ⚠️ street food is not
inherently risky and is often safer than a mediocre restaurant because you can watch it
being cooked.**
**⚠️ Travellers' diarrhoea** is common, usually self-limiting, and ⚠️ **oral rehydration is
the main treatment; ⚠️ seek medical care for high fever, blood, or persistent symptoms.**
**⚠️ Allergies and dietary requirements**: ⚠️ **carry a written translation card, learn the
relevant words, and ⚠️ understand that the concept may not translate — "vegetarian" means
different things in different cuisines, and cross-contamination norms vary.**

## §23. Travelling With Others

**⚠️ The most common source of trip conflict is unstated mismatched expectations** (§1 → `travel-trip-type-destination-constraints-and-budget`).
**⚠️ Talk about it BEFORE booking**: ⚠️ **what kind of trip each person wants, budget level
honestly stated, pace, morning versus evening people, how much together-time versus
separate-time, and how decisions get made.**
**⚠️ The practices that work**: ⚠️ **explicit permission to split up (⚠️ genuinely the
single best group-travel technique), one person owning each logistical domain rather than
deciding everything by committee, and a shared expense-splitting method agreed up front.**
**⚠️ Family travel**: ⚠️ **children's pace is the actual constraint; build in downtime and
playground time; ⚠️ and jet lag with young children is harder than the adult version.**
**⚠️ Accessibility travel** requires much more verification and much earlier: ⚠️ **"accessible"
is defined inconsistently, and photographs and direct questions beat labels.**

## §24. Long-Term Travel

**⚠️ The differences from a holiday are structural, not just longer**: ⚠️ **you must build
routine, rest and boredom into it, because sustained novelty is exhausting.**
**⚠️ Slower is cheaper**: ⚠️ **weekly and monthly accommodation rates drop substantially,
and moving less is both the cheaper and the more restful choice.**
**⚠️ The administrative layer people underestimate**: ⚠️ **visa runs and the legal limits on
them; ⚠️ tax residency, which is a genuine issue and jurisdiction-specific; ⚠️ mail and
banking address requirements; and ⚠️ whether remote work is actually permitted on your
entry status — many tourist entries prohibit it, and digital nomad visas exist precisely
because of this.**
**⚠️ Health and insurance** need long-term-specific policies (§13 → `travel-documents-insurance-health-safety-and-money`).
**⚠️ The social cost is the one that surprises people** — ⚠️ **repeated shallow connections
are not a substitute for durable ones** (see a family/community reference).

## §25. ⚠️ Handling Disruption

> **⚠️ This is the skill that separates travellers who have good trips from those who
> don't, and it's mostly preparation rather than temperament.**
```
⚠️ BEFORE  ⚠️ build slack (§17) · ⚠️ documents and bookings offline
   (§12) · ⚠️ know your rights on the route you're flying (§26.2) ·
   ⚠️ have the airline's app AND a phone number · ⚠️ know your
   insurance claim process before you need it
⚠️ DURING A FLIGHT DISRUPTION
   ⚠️ 1. Get in the rebooking queue AND call simultaneously —
      ⚠️ phone lines (including foreign-language ones for the same
      airline) are often much faster than the desk
   ⚠️ 2. Know what you want before you reach an agent — specific
      alternative flights, not "what can you do"
   ⚠️ 3. ⚠️ DOCUMENT EVERYTHING: photograph departure boards,
      keep boarding passes, note names and times, get the
      REASON for the delay in writing if you can
   ⚠️ 4. Ask about care obligations (meals, hotel) separately from
      compensation — they're different entitlements
   ⚠️ 5. ⚠️ Keep receipts
⚠️ AFTER  ⚠️ claim directly first (free) before using a claims agency
   (which takes a cut); ⚠️ escalate to the national enforcement body
   if refused
```
**⚠️ The general principle**: ⚠️ **be the person the agent wants to help.** **Calm,
specific, prepared and polite outperforms angry, reliably** — ⚠️ **the agent did not cause
the problem and has discretion they will not exercise for someone shouting at them.**
**⚠️ Lost baggage**: ⚠️ **file the report BEFORE leaving the airport; keep the reference;
know the Montreal Convention liability limit applies internationally; and ⚠️ AirTag-type
trackers have proven genuinely useful for locating bags airlines say they cannot find.**

## §20. Site Safety

**⚠️ Construction has a disproportionate share of workplace fatalities, and the causes are
concentrated and well known.**
```
⚠️ THE RECURRING FATAL FOUR  ⚠️ FALLS FROM HEIGHT (the largest by far) ·
   struck-by · caught-in/between · electrocution
⚠️ ALSO  ⚠️ excavation collapse (⚠️ a cubic metre of soil weighs
   well over a tonne, and unshored trenches kill quickly) ·
   confined spaces · lifting operations · ⚠️ silica dust and
   asbestos (⚠️ long-latency, and the biggest killer by total
   deaths in some jurisdictions)
⚠️ THE CONTROLS  design out the hazard first (⚠️ prevention through
   design / CDM Regulations make this a DESIGNER duty in some
   jurisdictions) → engineering controls → then PPE
```
**⚠️ The designer's role is real**: ⚠️ **specifying a detail that can only be installed by
someone working at height on a ladder is a design decision with a safety consequence.**

## §21. Commissioning and Handover

**⚠️ The step that determines whether the building actually works as designed, and the
step that gets compressed when the programme slips.**
**⚠️ Commissioning**: **testing and balancing, controls verification, ⚠️ integrated systems
testing, and ⚠️ seasonal commissioning (a system tuned in October has not been proven in
July).**
**⚠️ Handover**: **O&M manuals, as-built information, training the facilities team,
warranties, spares.** ⚠️ **Handover documentation quality is chronically poor, and the
operator inherits the consequences.**
**⚠️ Soft landings** — ⚠️ **the designer and contractor remain involved after occupation to
tune the building and close the performance gap** (§12 → `building-accessibility-energy-drawings-bim-and-codes`). **⚠️ It works and it is
frequently cut.**
**⚠️ Post-occupancy evaluation** is ⚠️ **the industry's most obvious missing feedback loop:
buildings are rarely measured against their design intent, so the same mistakes recur.**

## §22. Existing Buildings and Retrofit

**⚠️ Most of the buildings that will exist in 2050 already exist, which makes this the
larger part of the work.**
**⚠️ Survey and assessment**: ⚠️ **measured survey, structural appraisal, material testing,
⚠️ and hazardous materials (asbestos, lead paint) surveys BEFORE any work.**
**⚠️ The recurring retrofit hazards**: ⚠️ **unknown structure behind finishes; original
drawings that don't match reality; load path changes from previous alterations nobody
recorded; and ⚠️ CHANGING THE MOISTURE BEHAVIOUR of a historic assembly by insulating it,
which can cause rot in a wall that survived a century** (§8 → `building-structure-foundations-envelope-services-and-fire`).
**⚠️ Heritage constraints** limit intervention and demand reversibility.
**⚠️ Adaptive reuse and the carbon argument** — ⚠️ **retaining structure avoids the embodied
carbon of replacement, which is now a regulated consideration in some jurisdictions**
(§24.2 → `building-reference`).

## §23. ⚠️ Why Projects Fail

```
⚠️ COORDINATION FAILURES AT BOUNDARIES  ⚠️ between disciplines,
   between design and construction, between contractor and
   specialist designer. ⚠️ The largest single category
⚠️ INADEQUATE SITE INVESTIGATION (§2)
⚠️ SCOPE CREEP and late changes (§1's cost-influence curve)
⚠️ OPTIMISM BIAS in cost and programme (see a civil/industrial
   reference for the base rates)
⚠️ INCOMPLETE DOCUMENTATION → RFIs, delays, claims (§13)
⚠️ RISK ALLOCATED TO PARTIES WHO CANNOT CARRY IT (§15)
⚠️ COMPRESSED COMMISSIONING (§21)
⚠️ THE HYATT REGENCY PATTERN  ⚠️ a change that looks like a detail
   silently invalidates the original analysis, and nobody re-derives it
```
**⚠️ What correlates with projects going well**: ⚠️ **early contractor and specialist
involvement, a stable brief, adequate design fees and time (⚠️ under-resourced design is
false economy that shows up as construction cost), realistic programmes, and genuine
early-warning culture rather than retrospective claims.**

## §11. Accessibility

**⚠️ A legal requirement and a design quality issue, and treating it as the former only
produces bad buildings.**
**⚠️ Typical requirements**: **accessible routes and entrances, ⚠️ gradients and ramps,
door widths and clear opening, manoeuvring space, sanitary accommodation, lift provision,
⚠️ means of escape for disabled occupants (refuges and evacuation lifts — frequently
overlooked), signage, acoustics and lighting for sensory impairment.**
**⚠️ Standards**: **ADA and ANSI A117.1 (US), Approved Document M and BS 8300 (UK), and
equivalents elsewhere.**
**⚠️ Universal design is the better framing** — ⚠️ **designing for the widest range of
users produces buildings that work better for everyone, and retrofitting accessibility is
far more expensive than designing it in.**

## §12. Energy and Comfort

**⚠️ The load-order principle**: ⚠️ **reduce demand → use passive measures → efficient
systems → renewables.** **⚠️ Doing this in the wrong order produces oversized plant on a
leaky building.**
**⚠️ Passive measures**: **orientation, form factor (⚠️ surface-to-volume ratio, which
compact buildings win on), insulation continuity, airtightness, shading (⚠️ external
shading is far more effective than internal), glazing ratio, thermal mass, and natural
ventilation.**
**⚠️ Comfort is not temperature alone** — ⚠️ **it's operative temperature, humidity, air
movement, radiant asymmetry and clothing/activity, and the ADAPTIVE comfort model shows
occupants accept a wider range in naturally ventilated buildings than in sealed ones.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — THE PERFORMANCE GAP is real and well documented.** ⚠️ **Buildings routinely
> use substantially more energy than their design models predicted.** **⚠️ Causes:
> modelling assumptions versus real occupancy, construction quality (thermal bridging and
> air leakage as built), controls that were never commissioned properly (§21 → `building-site-safety-commissioning-retrofit-and-why-projects-fail`), and
> occupants defeating systems that annoy them.**
> **⚠️ The lesson is that predicted performance is a design intention, and measured
> performance requires commissioning, metering and someone paying attention afterwards.**

## §13. Drawings, Specifications and BIM

**⚠️ The drawings say WHERE and HOW BIG; the specification says WHAT and HOW GOOD.**
⚠️ **Conflicts between them are resolved by a stated precedence clause in the contract —
and if you don't know which governs, that's a problem waiting.**
**⚠️ Specification types**: ⚠️ **PRESCRIPTIVE (name the product) versus PERFORMANCE (state
the requirement and let the contractor propose).** **⚠️ Performance specs transfer design
risk and require you to be able to judge what comes back.**
**⚠️ BIM**: ⚠️ **a coordinated 3D model with data, used for clash detection, quantities,
sequencing (4D) and cost (5D).** **⚠️ Levels of detail/development matter — a model element
at concept LOD is not a fabrication instruction, and treating it as one causes real
errors.**
**⚠️ What BIM genuinely delivers**: **geometric clash detection and coordinated
quantities.** ⚠️ **What it does not: constructability judgement, tolerance reality, or
maintenance access** (§9 → `building-structure-foundations-envelope-services-and-fire`).
**⚠️ RFIs and submittals** are the formal question-and-approval mechanism during
construction — ⚠️ **and RFI volume is a decent proxy for documentation quality.**

## §14. ⚠️ Codes and Permitting

**⚠️ Codes are law where adopted, and adoption is LOCAL** (§24.1 → `building-reference`).
```
⚠️ THE STRUCTURE  model codes (⚠️ IBC/IRC/IFC and the I-Codes;
   Eurocodes; national building regulations) → adopted by
   jurisdiction, ⚠️ frequently WITH AMENDMENTS → interpreted by
   the local authority having jurisdiction (AHJ)
⚠️ THEREFORE  ⚠️ the model code is not the law. The ADOPTED and
   AMENDED version in that jurisdiction is, and editions lag —
   a state may still be several cycles behind (§24.1)
⚠️ REFERENCED STANDARDS  ⚠️ codes incorporate standards by reference
   (ASCE 7 for loads, ACI 318 for concrete, AISC 360 for steel,
   NFPA for fire), and the referenced EDITION matters
⚠️ THE PROCESS  permit application → plan review → permits →
   ⚠️ INSPECTIONS at defined hold points → certificate of occupancy
⚠️ SPECIAL INSPECTIONS  independent verification of critical work
   (§19)
```
**⚠️ Alternative means and methods** allow non-prescriptive solutions where equivalence is
demonstrated — ⚠️ **the route for innovation, and it costs time and review.**

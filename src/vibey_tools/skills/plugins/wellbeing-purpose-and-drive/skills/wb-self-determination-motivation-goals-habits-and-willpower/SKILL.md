## §11. ⚠️ Self-Determination Theory

> **⚠️ The best-supported motivational framework here, and the one worth actually knowing.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ THREE BASIC PSYCHOLOGICAL NEEDS — Deci and Ryan
   ⚠️ AUTONOMY  ⚠️ acting from one's own volition. ⚠️ NOT
      independence — you can be autonomously interdependent;
      the opposite of autonomy is COERCION, not connection
   ⚠️ COMPETENCE  effectiveness, mastery, visible progress
   ⚠️ RELATEDNESS  connection and mattering to others (§8)
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CLAIM  ⚠️ satisfying these produces motivation and
   wellbeing; frustrating them produces disengagement and
   ill-being — ⚠️ and NEED FRUSTRATION is not merely the
   absence of satisfaction, it is separately damaging
⚠️ ⚠️ THE MOTIVATION CONTINUUM is the useful part, because it
   is not a binary
   ⚠️ Amotivation → ⚠️ EXTERNAL regulation (reward/punishment) →
   ⚠️ INTROJECTED (guilt, shame, ego — ⚠️ "I should," and this
   one is the source of a great deal of joyless productivity) →
   ⚠️ IDENTIFIED (I value the outcome) → ⚠️ INTEGRATED (it fits
   who I am) → INTRINSIC (the activity itself)
   ⚠️ ⚠️ MOVING ALONG THIS CONTINUUM — INTERNALIZATION — IS THE
   PRACTICAL GOAL, and it is more achievable than making a
   task intrinsically fun
⚠️ ⚠️ WHY IT MATTERS FOR MANAGEMENT AND PARENTING  ⚠️ providing
   RATIONALE, acknowledging feelings, offering choice within
   structure and minimizing controlling language all support
   internalization — and cost nothing
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CAVEAT  ⚠️ SDT has a large literature and shares the
   field's general problems; ⚠️ the cross-cultural universality
   of the three needs is genuinely debated
```

## §12. ⚠️ Intrinsic Motivation and Rewards

**⚠️ The overjustification effect**: ⚠️ **rewarding an activity someone already enjoys can
reduce their subsequent intrinsic motivation for it.** ⚠️ **The classic demonstration is
children rewarded for drawing who then drew less freely.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — this is real and it is routinely overstated into "rewards don't work," which
> is false.** ⚠️ **The meta-analytic picture is narrower: the undermining effect is strongest
> for EXPECTED, TANGIBLE rewards CONTINGENT ON MERE ENGAGEMENT in an already-interesting
> task.** **⚠️ Unexpected rewards, verbal praise, and rewards for genuine performance
> quality show little or no undermining — and rewards clearly work for tasks nobody finds
> interesting.**

**⚠️ The practical translation**: ⚠️ **pay people well and fairly, then avoid making the pay
salient in moment-to-moment work** — ⚠️ **the finding that money is a poor motivator applies
above a threshold of feeling fairly paid, not below it.**
**⚠️ Autonomy-supportive versus controlling framing** of the same incentive changes its
effect (§11).
**⚠️ The corollary for personal use**: ⚠️ **gamifying something you already love carries a
real risk, and turning a hobby into an income source is the everyday version of this
effect.**

## §13. ⚠️ Goals

**⚠️ Goal-setting theory** (Locke and Latham) is ⚠️ **among the better-supported findings in
applied psychology: specific, difficult goals outperform vague or easy ones, given
commitment, feedback and ability.**
**⚠️ The conditions matter and are usually dropped**: ⚠️ **the effect depends on the task
being reasonably straightforward, on the person accepting the goal, and on feedback being
available.** ⚠️ **For complex, novel tasks, LEARNING goals outperform performance goals.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — goals have documented SIDE EFFECTS that the popular literature omits.**
> ⚠️ **Narrow specific targets can crowd out unmeasured dimensions, encourage unethical
> behaviour to hit the number, reduce intrinsic motivation, and induce excessive
> risk-taking when someone is just short of a threshold.** **⚠️ This is Goodhart's law
> operating on a person.**

**⚠️ Approach versus avoidance goals**: ⚠️ **framing toward what you want rather than away
from what you fear is associated with better outcomes and persistence.**
**⚠️ Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (WOOP)** has ⚠️ **reasonable evidence
and is more effective than visualizing success alone — ⚠️ indeed, pure positive fantasizing
about outcomes is associated with LOWER achievement, which is one of the more useful
counterintuitive findings that has held up.**

## §14. ⚠️ Habits and Behaviour Change

**⚠️ Habits are context-cued automatic responses** — ⚠️ **which means the leverage is in the
CONTEXT, not in the intention.**
**⚠️ Implementation intentions** — ⚠️ **"when situation X arises, I will do Y" — have a solid
meta-analytic base and are among the more reliable tools here, because they pre-decide and
remove the moment of choice.**
**⚠️ Friction is the underrated lever**: ⚠️ **changing the number of steps required to start
or to stop something does more than motivation typically does, and it does not deplete.**
**⚠️ Habit formation time**: ⚠️ **the "21 days" claim has no basis; the study usually cited
found a median around 66 days with an enormous range (roughly 18 to 254), which is a very
different message.**
**⚠️ Identity-based framing** — ⚠️ **"I am someone who..." — is popular and rests on thinner
evidence than the popular treatments imply; ⚠️ it maps onto SDT's integration (§11), which is
where its plausibility comes from.**
**⚠️ The transtheoretical (stages of change) model** is widely taught and ⚠️ **has been
substantially criticized on evidence, which is worth knowing if you encounter it as
settled.**

## §15. ⚠️ Willpower

```
⚠️ ⚠️ EGO DEPLETION — ⚠️ the claim that self-control draws on a
   limited resource that depletes with use, and that glucose
   restores it — WAS THE DOMINANT MODEL FOR TWO DECADES
⚠️ ⚠️ IT LARGELY FAILED TO REPLICATE. ⚠️ Large preregistered
   multi-lab studies found effects near zero, and the glucose
   version fared particularly badly
⚠️ ⚠️ WHAT SEEMS TO REMAIN  ⚠️ effort has real subjective costs
   and motivational and attentional accounts fit the data
   better than a depleting fuel does. ⚠️ People shift what they
   want to do rather than becoming unable to do it
⚠️ ⚠️ THE PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE IS ACTUALLY LIBERATING
   ⚠️ If self-control were a fuel tank, the strategy would be
      rationing and building capacity
   ⚠️ ⚠️ IF IT IS ABOUT MOTIVATION, ATTENTION AND SITUATION, the
      strategy is DESIGNING SITUATIONS THAT REQUIRE LESS OF IT
      (§14) — ⚠️ and the trait-self-control research supports
      this: ⚠️ people who score high on self-control report
      resisting FEWER temptations, not winning more fights.
      They have arranged their lives to avoid the fights
⚠️ ⚠️ THEREFORE  ⚠️ "I lack discipline" is usually a
   misdiagnosis of an environment problem
```

>    reduce their moment-to-moment happiness. Optimizing for one is not optimizing for the
>    other.**

## §7. ⚠️ What Actually Holds Up

**⚠️ The list is shorter and duller than the bookshelf suggests, which is itself
informative.**
**⚠️ Reasonably robust:**
- ⚠️ **SOCIAL CONNECTION as a major factor in both wellbeing and physical health** (§8,
  §28.2 → `wb-reference`) — ⚠️ **the strongest single finding in the field.**
- ⚠️ **SLEEP, and its bidirectional relationship with mood** (§9).
- ⚠️ **PHYSICAL ACTIVITY, with small-to-moderate effects on mood and depressive symptoms**
  (§9).
- ⚠️ **The impact bias in affective forecasting** (§4 → `wb-traditions-measurement-adaptation-money-and-replication`).
- ⚠️ **Logarithmic income effects** (§5 → `wb-traditions-measurement-adaptation-money-and-replication`).
- ⚠️ **Unemployment's large and poorly-adapted-to negative effect** (§4 → `wb-traditions-measurement-adaptation-money-and-replication`, §26 → `wb-interventions-burnout-limits-and-the-industry-critique`).

## §8. ⚠️ Relationships

**⚠️ If there is one finding to act on, it is this one** (§28.2 → `wb-reference`).
**⚠️ The Harvard Study of Adult Development** — ⚠️ **running since 1938 — reports
relationship quality in midlife as a better predictor of late-life health and satisfaction
than most conventional measures.** ⚠️ **Note the caveats an honest account requires: the
original cohort was small, male, and unrepresentative, and observational data cannot settle
causation.** ⚠️ **The direction is corroborated by much larger epidemiological work** (§28.2 → `wb-reference`).
**⚠️ Quality over quantity**: ⚠️ **a small number of close, reliable relationships does more
than a large network, and CONFLICTUAL relationships can be worse than none.**
**⚠️ Loneliness and social isolation are distinct**: ⚠️ **loneliness is the subjective gap
between desired and actual connection; isolation is the objective absence. ⚠️ You can be
isolated without lonely and lonely without isolated, and they affect health differently.**
**⚠️ What builds connection** is unglamorous: ⚠️ **repeated unplanned contact, shared
activity, and — the practical constraint most adults hit — proximity and recurring
structure, which is why connection is harder to sustain after school and after a job
change.**

## §9. Sleep, Movement and the Physical Substrate

**⚠️ Sleep** is bidirectional with mood — ⚠️ **poor sleep worsens mood, low mood worsens
sleep — and sleep deprivation experiments show reliable effects on emotional regulation and
on the negative interpretation of ambiguous events.**
**⚠️ CBT-I (cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia)** has a strong evidence base and is
⚠️ **the recommended first-line treatment for chronic insomnia ahead of medication.**
**⚠️ Physical activity** shows ⚠️ **small-to-moderate effects on depressive symptoms and mood
across many trials, with the honest caveats that publication bias applies here too, that
blinding is impossible, and that effect sizes shrink in higher-quality studies.**
⚠️ **The dose that has evidence is modest; the "exercise is better than antidepressants"
claim outruns it.**
**⚠️ Daylight, particularly morning light,** for circadian regulation (see a
thermodynamics/biology reference on circadian mechanisms).
**⚠️ Alcohol** is worth naming plainly: ⚠️ **it is a depressant, it degrades sleep
architecture even at doses that do not feel disruptive, and using it for mood regulation has
a well-documented trajectory.**

## §10. Time and Attention

**⚠️ How time is spent predicts affect better than most demographic variables**, ⚠️ **and the
day reconstruction work found commuting, and time with a direct supervisor, among the
lowest-rated common activities.**
**⚠️ Attention is the medium of experience**: ⚠️ **the mind-wandering research found people
report lower mood when their mind is wandering than when engaged, largely independent of
what they are doing — ⚠️ though as a large correlational study it cannot fully settle
direction.**
**⚠️ Time affluence and time poverty** — ⚠️ **the subjective sense of having enough time
predicts wellbeing independently of income, and buying time (outsourcing disliked tasks)
shows modest positive effects.**
**⚠️ Digital media**: ⚠️ **the evidence on screen time and wellbeing is genuinely contested,
with effect sizes in large datasets frequently very small and the causal direction
unresolved.** ⚠️ **The stronger and less controversial claims concern DISPLACEMENT — of
sleep, of exercise, of in-person contact — rather than direct harm from the medium.**

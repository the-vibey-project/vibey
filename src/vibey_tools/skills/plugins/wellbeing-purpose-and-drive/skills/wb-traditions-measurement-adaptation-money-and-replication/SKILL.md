> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ THE EFFECT SIZES ARE SMALLER THAN THE BOOKS SAY, AND THE DIRECTIONS ARE MOSTLY
>    RIGHT** (§6, §28.1 → `wb-reference`). **The replication crisis hit this field hard. The honest position
>    is neither "it's all nonsense" nor the confident claims of the popular literature —
>    it's small real effects, oversold.**
> 2. **⚠️ CIRCUMSTANCES MATTER LESS THAN PEOPLE PREDICT, AND CONDITIONS MATTER MORE THAN
>    ADVICE ADMITS** (§4, §26 → `wb-interventions-burnout-limits-and-the-industry-critique`). **Adaptation blunts most changes in circumstance. But
>    poverty, isolation, chronic pain and unsafe work are not adaptation problems, and
>    treating them as mindset problems is the field's characteristic failure.**
> 3. **⚠️ MEANING AND HAPPINESS COME APART** (§18 → `wb-flow-procrastination-meaning-purpose-and-adversity`). **They are correlated but distinct, they
>    have different sources, and some of the most meaningful things people do reliably
>    reduce their moment-to-moment happiness. Optimizing for one is not optimizing for the
>    other.**

# PART I — WHAT WELLBEING IS

## §2. Two Traditions

```
⚠️ ⚠️ HEDONIC  ⚠️ wellbeing as pleasure and satisfaction —
   positive affect, low negative affect, life satisfaction.
   ⚠️ Measurable, and it is what "happiness" usually means
⚠️ ⚠️ EUDAIMONIC  ⚠️ wellbeing as living well — the Aristotelian
   line, taken up by Ryff as autonomy, environmental mastery,
   personal growth, positive relations, purpose in life, and
   self-acceptance
⚠️ ⚠️ THEY ARE CORRELATED BUT DISTINCT, and this is not a
   philosophers' quibble — ⚠️ they diverge in practice (§18)
⚠️ SUBJECTIVE WELL-BEING (Diener) is the standard operational
   construct: ⚠️ life satisfaction + positive affect + low
   negative affect
⚠️ PERMA (Seligman)  positive emotion, engagement,
   relationships, meaning, accomplishment — ⚠️ influential as a
   framework, ⚠️ and criticized as not empirically distinct
   from simpler measures
⚠️ ⚠️ THE PHILOSOPHICAL QUESTION UNDERNEATH DOES NOT GO AWAY:
   ⚠️ is wellbeing how you FEEL, whether your DESIRES are
   satisfied, or whether certain OBJECTIVE goods are present in
   your life? ⚠️ Hedonism, desire-satisfaction and objective-list
   theories give different answers, and the empirical literature
   mostly assumes one without arguing for it
⚠️ ⚠️ WHY IT MATTERS PRACTICALLY  ⚠️ a policy or a personal
   choice that raises satisfaction may lower meaning, and the
   measure you pick decides which one you notice
```

## §3. ⚠️ Measurement

**⚠️ Nearly everything here rests on SELF-REPORT**, ⚠️ **usually single-item or short-scale,
which carries real limitations that the confident summaries omit.**
**⚠️ The known problems**: ⚠️ **mood at the moment of answering contaminates life-satisfaction
judgements; scale use differs across cultures and languages; ⚠️ what people report
retrospectively differs systematically from what they reported in the moment.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the REMEMBERING self and the EXPERIENCING self give different answers, and
> Kahneman's work showed the gap is large.** ⚠️ **Retrospective evaluation is dominated by
> peaks and endings and largely ignores DURATION.** **⚠️ So "was that a good holiday?" and
> "were you happy during it?" are genuinely different questions — and we mostly make
> decisions to please the remembering self.**

**⚠️ Better methods exist and are expensive**: ⚠️ **experience sampling, the day
reconstruction method, and behavioural or physiological measures — all of which agree with
self-report less than one might hope.**
**⚠️ National measures** — ⚠️ **the World Happiness Report's Cantril ladder, ONS wellbeing
questions, OECD guidelines — are useful for comparing populations over time and are weak
for ranking countries against each other, because scale use and translation differ.**
**⚠️ The practical caution**: ⚠️ **when you read "X increases happiness by 20%," ask what was
measured, for how long after, and against what control** (§28.1 → `wb-reference`).

## §4. ⚠️ Adaptation and the Set Point

```
⚠️ ⚠️ HEDONIC ADAPTATION  ⚠️ people return toward a baseline
   after most changes in circumstance, good and bad.
   ⚠️ The observation is solid; the strong "hedonic treadmill"
   claim is not
⚠️ THE ORIGINAL EVIDENCE  ⚠️ the lottery-winner and accident
   study is the famous one, ⚠️ and it was small and has been
   over-read for fifty years
⚠️ ⚠️ WHAT LONGITUDINAL DATA ACTUALLY SHOW — a more interesting
   picture than the treadmill
   ⚠️ Adaptation is often INCOMPLETE, and it varies enormously
      between individuals
   ⚠️ ⚠️ SOME EVENTS SHOW LITTLE OR NO ADAPTATION — ⚠️ notably
      long-term unemployment, chronic pain and disability,
      caregiving strain, and widowhood, where baselines can
      shift durably
   ⚠️ ⚠️ THEREFORE "you'll get used to it" IS FALSE FOR SOME OF
      THE MOST IMPORTANT THINGS, which matters for policy (§26)
⚠️ ⚠️ HERITABILITY  ⚠️ twin studies put a substantial share of
   variance in stable wellbeing under genetic influence — often
   summarized around 30-50%. ⚠️ THIS DOES NOT MEAN CHANGE IS
   IMPOSSIBLE; heritability is a population statistic about
   VARIANCE, not a ceiling on any individual (a point worth
   holding whenever heritability appears)
⚠️ ⚠️ AFFECTIVE FORECASTING IS BAD  ⚠️ people overestimate both
   the INTENSITY and the DURATION of future emotional reactions
   — ⚠️ the IMPACT BIAS, and it is among the more robust
   findings here. ⚠️ You will be less devastated, and less
   delighted, and for less time, than you expect
   ⚠️ ⚠️ THE USEFUL COROLLARY: ⚠️ your prediction that a given
   outcome will make or ruin your life is systematically wrong,
   which is genuinely consoling in both directions
```

## §5. ⚠️ Money

**⚠️ The relationship is real, positive, and logarithmic** — ⚠️ **which means each additional
increment of wellbeing requires a PROPORTIONAL, not absolute, increase in income.** ⚠️ **Going
from $25k to $50k does roughly what $50k to $100k does.**
**⚠️ The famous plateau claim** — ⚠️ **that emotional wellbeing stopped improving above about
$75,000 — was later challenged by work using experience sampling that found continued
improvement.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the adversarial collaboration between those research teams is the model
> outcome and the more interesting result.** ⚠️ **The reconciliation was roughly that
> wellbeing continues rising with income for most people, while an UNHAPPY MINORITY shows a
> plateau — because for people with a serious source of suffering, money stops helping past
> a point.** **⚠️ Both original findings were partly right, and the disagreement resolved by
> finding the moderator rather than by one side winning.**

**⚠️ What consistently matters more than level**: ⚠️ **RELATIVE income and comparison
(⚠️ which is why national income growth raises wellbeing less than individual income growth
does), ⚠️ financial SECURITY and volatility, and ⚠️ debt.**
**⚠️ How you spend it** has modest but real effects: ⚠️ **experiences over goods, buying time,
and spending on others — ⚠️ though several of these findings are exactly the kind that §6
counsels holding loosely.**
**⚠️ The honest summary**: ⚠️ **money matters most where it removes deprivation and
insecurity, and matters progressively less as a source of positive wellbeing above that.**

# PART II — THE EVIDENCE BASE

## §6. ⚠️ The Replication Problem

> **⚠️ The first organizing idea above. Read this before believing anything in the popular
> literature.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ THIS FIELD WAS HIT HARD BY THE REPLICATION CRISIS, and
   several load-bearing findings did not survive
   ⚠️ ⚠️ EGO DEPLETION — ⚠️ the claim that willpower is a
      depletable resource. ⚠️ Large multi-lab replications
      found effects near zero (§15)
   ⚠️ ⚠️ POWER POSING'S hormonal and behavioural claims —
      ⚠️ the original author has publicly disavowed the
      hormonal findings
   ⚠️ ⚠️ MANY PRIMING EFFECTS · ⚠️ the "positivity ratio" of
      2.9013:1 was retracted after its mathematics was shown to
      be invalid
   ⚠️ ⚠️ GROWTH MINDSET — ⚠️ real but MUCH smaller than
      popularized, with effects concentrated in specific
      subgroups; large registered trials found small average
      effects
   ⚠️ ⚠️ GRIT — ⚠️ meta-analysis found it predicts performance
      weakly and is largely redundant with conscientiousness
⚠️ ⚠️ THE MECHANISMS THAT PRODUCED THIS  ⚠️ small samples ·
   publication bias against null results · flexible analysis ·
   ⚠️ and a strong incentive to produce counterintuitive,
   marketable findings
⚠️ ⚠️ WHAT NOT TO CONCLUDE  ⚠️ "psychology is fake" is the wrong
   inference. ⚠️ The field diagnosed its own problem, and
   preregistration, multi-lab replication and larger samples
   are now standard in a way they were not
⚠️ ⚠️ THE PRACTICAL RULE  ⚠️ prefer LARGE, PREREGISTERED,
   MULTI-SITE studies; ⚠️ prefer findings that replicated
   before you needed them to; ⚠️ and be most suspicious of the
   findings that are most fun to tell people about
```

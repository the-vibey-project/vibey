# Survival: What Actually Kills People, Survival Psychology, and Priorities

> **Part 1 of 6** of the *Survival Skills* reference (plugin `survival-skills`), covering §0–§3. Sibling skills: `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food` (§4–§7), `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine` (§8–§12), `survival-environments-cold-desert-tropical-water-and-urban` (§13–§17), `survival-planning-kit-practice-and-myths-that-kill` (§18–§22), `survival-reference` (§23–§28). Section numbers are shared across the set; a reference written as §N → `skill` points into that sibling skill.
>
> **Currency:** The physiology is fixed. Two areas are live. See §23 → `survival-reference` for search-and-rescue base rates, and current wilderness medicine guidance.

> **⚠️ The single most important reframe in this entire document: survival skills are
> mostly PREVENTION skills, and the dramatic ones are the least useful.** ⚠️ **The
> popular image — friction fire, foraging, trapping — describes a situation you should
> never reach, and it distracts from the things that actually determine outcomes:
> telling someone where you're going, carrying insulation, and being findable** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`,
> §23.1 → `survival-reference`).
>
> **⚠️ SCOPE AND LIMITS, stated plainly.** ⚠️ **This is a conceptual reference, not
> training. Wilderness first aid, avalanche safety, swiftwater and technical rescue
> require hands-on instruction and cannot be learned from text.** **⚠️ Nothing here
> substitutes for professional medical care, and where a real emergency is in progress
> the answer is almost always to activate rescue rather than to improvise** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`).
> **⚠️ Plant identification in particular cannot be done safely from written description**
> (§7 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
>
> **Complements a travel reference (trip planning and disruption), a
> refrigeration/food-water reference (water treatment), and an exercise physiology
> reference (thermoregulation).**
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ PSYCHOLOGY is the primary variable** (§2). **People die from denial, panic and
>    poor decisions far more often than from lacking a skill.**
> 2. **⚠️ HYPOTHERMIA is the wilderness killer, and it happens in mild weather** (§4 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
>    **Not starvation, not thirst, not animals.**
> 3. **⚠️ BEING FOUND beats surviving indefinitely** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`). **Almost every survival
>    situation is resolved by rescue, so everything that makes rescue faster is worth
>    more than everything that extends endurance.**

## §1. ⚠️ What Actually Kills People

```
⚠️ THE REALITY, from search-and-rescue and coroner data (§23.1)
   ⚠️ FALLS and TRAUMA
   ⚠️ HYPOTHERMIA — ⚠️ frequently in temperatures well above freezing
   ⚠️ DROWNING — ⚠️ including river crossings and unexpected immersion
   ⚠️ HEAT ILLNESS and DEHYDRATION
   ⚠️ PRE-EXISTING MEDICAL EVENTS — cardiac events are a large share
   ⚠️ AVALANCHE in relevant terrain
   ⚠️ Getting lost, then any of the above
⚠️ WHAT PEOPLE PREPARE FOR  starvation, predators, water purification
   ⚠️ — largely the wrong end of the risk distribution
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the "rule of threes" (3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter,
> 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food) is a MNEMONIC, not a physiological law.**
> ⚠️ **The real numbers vary enormously with temperature, exertion, wetness, body
> composition and health.** **⚠️ Its genuine value is the ORDERING it teaches — air,
> then temperature, then water, then food — and that ordering is correct and
> under-appreciated.** **⚠️ Its danger is being taken literally: you can die of
> hypothermia in far less than three hours when wet and windblown, and you can survive
> considerably longer than three days without water in cool conditions at rest.**

**⚠️ The prevention hierarchy, which is the whole game:**
```
⚠️ 1. Don't get into the situation — planning, weather, turnaround times (§18)
⚠️ 2. Be findable if you do — itinerary left, beacon carried (§9)
⚠️ 3. Stay warm and dry — insulation, shelter (§4)
⚠️ 4. Everything else
```

## §2. ⚠️ Survival Psychology

> **⚠️ The evidence from survival case studies is consistent: the decisive variable is
> psychological, not technical.**
```
⚠️ THE DENIAL PHASE  ⚠️ people continue walking in the wrong direction
   for HOURS rather than accept they're lost. ⚠️ This single behaviour
   converts a small problem into a large one more than any other
⚠️ PANIC  ⚠️ produces running, exhaustion, injury, discarded gear,
   and sweat that later freezes (§4)
⚠️ "BENDING THE MAP"  ⚠️ forcing observed terrain to match where you
   believe you are. A recognized and dangerous error mode
⚠️ SUMMIT FEVER / GOAL FIXATION  ⚠️ continuing past a turnaround time
   because of investment already made. ⚠️ Sunk cost, in a place
   where it kills
⚠️ SOCIAL FACILITATION  groups take risks individuals wouldn't;
   ⚠️ nobody wants to be the one who says turn back
```
**⚠️ What correlates with surviving**, **from the survival psychology literature**:
⚠️ **rapid acceptance of the real situation, taking small purposeful actions to regain
control, maintaining a reason to continue, humour, and organizing the environment.**
**⚠️ The common thread is agency — doing something useful, however small.**
**⚠️ STOP is the standard mnemonic and it is genuinely good**: ⚠️ **Stop. Think. Observe.
Plan.** **⚠️ The instruction to physically stop moving is the load-bearing part**, **because
continuing to move while disoriented is what expands the search area** (§8 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`, §9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`).
**⚠️ Children and survival**: ⚠️ **young children often HIDE from searchers — from
strangers calling their name — which is why "hug a tree and stay put" programmes exist
and why searchers announce themselves differently for children.**

## §3. ⚠️ Priorities

```
⚠️ IMMEDIATE (minutes)
   ⚠️ Get out of immediate danger (rockfall, water, traffic, fire)
   ⚠️ Catastrophic bleeding, airway, breathing (§10)
⚠️ SHORT TERM (hours)
   ⚠️ THERMOREGULATION — get dry, get insulated, get out of wind (§4)
   ⚠️ SIGNAL — the sooner rescue starts, the shorter everything is (§9)
   ⚠️ Assess: injuries, resources, location, who knows where you are
⚠️ MEDIUM (a day or more)
   Water (§5) · fire if useful (§6) · improve shelter and signals
⚠️ LATE (multiple days)
   Food (§7) — ⚠️ genuinely last, and usually never
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the ordering people actually follow is almost exactly inverted.**
> ⚠️ **The instinct is to look for food and to walk toward where you think help is.**
> **⚠️ Both are usually wrong: food is irrelevant on the timescale of a rescue, and
> walking expands the search area and exhausts you** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`).

**⚠️ The decision that matters most: STAY OR GO.**
```
⚠️ STAY IF  someone knows where you are and when you were due ·
   you're injured · visibility or weather is poor · you have
   shelter, water, or a defensible position · ⚠️ you're not certain
   of the route out
⚠️ GO IF  ⚠️ nobody knows where you are · you're certain of a route
   to help and can complete it · your position is untenable
   (no water, exposed, rising water) · ⚠️ and if you go, LEAVE A
   MARKED TRAIL AND A NOTE saying who you are, when you left,
   and which direction
```
⚠️ **In most recreational contexts the answer is STAY**, **because §9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`'s rescue apparatus is
already looking for you and a moving target is much harder to find.**

# Survival: Preventive Planning, Kit That Earns Its Weight, Vehicle and Home, Practising, and Myths That Kill

> **Part 5 of 6** of the *Survival Skills* reference (plugin `survival-skills`), covering §18–§22. Section numbers are shared across the set; a reference written as §N → `skill` points into that sibling skill.
>
> **Currency:** The physiology is fixed. Two areas are live. See §23 → `survival-reference` for search-and-rescue base rates, and current wilderness medicine guidance.

> **⚠️ The single most important reframe in this entire document: survival skills are
> mostly PREVENTION skills, and the dramatic ones are the least useful.** ⚠️ **The
> popular image — friction fire, foraging, trapping — describes a situation you should
> never reach, and it distracts from the things that actually determine outcomes:
> telling someone where you're going, carrying insulation, and being findable** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`,
> §23.1 → `survival-reference`).
>
> **⚠️ SCOPE AND LIMITS, stated plainly.** ⚠️ **This is a conceptual reference, not
> training. Wilderness first aid, avalanche safety, swiftwater and technical rescue
> require hands-on instruction and cannot be learned from text.** **⚠️ Nothing here
> substitutes for professional medical care, and where a real emergency is in progress
> the answer is almost always to activate rescue rather than to improvise** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`).
> **⚠️ Plant identification in particular cannot be done safely from written description**
> (§7 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
>
> **Complements a travel reference (trip planning and disruption), a
> refrigeration/food-water reference (water treatment), and an exercise physiology
> reference (thermoregulation).**
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ PSYCHOLOGY is the primary variable** (§2 → `survival-what-kills-psychology-and-priorities`). **People die from denial, panic and
>    poor decisions far more often than from lacking a skill.**
> 2. **⚠️ HYPOTHERMIA is the wilderness killer, and it happens in mild weather** (§4 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
>    **Not starvation, not thirst, not animals.**
> 3. **⚠️ BEING FOUND beats surviving indefinitely** (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`). **Almost every survival
>    situation is resolved by rescue, so everything that makes rescue faster is worth
>    more than everything that extends endurance.**

## §18. Preventive Planning

```
⚠️ BEFORE EVERY TRIP
   ⚠️ Leave an itinerary with a specific overdue time and
      instructions to call SAR (§9). ⚠️ THE highest-value action
   ⚠️ Check weather AND the forecast trend, not a snapshot
   ⚠️ Check conditions (snowpack, river levels, closures, fire)
   ⚠️ Set a TURNAROUND TIME and commit to it out loud
   ⚠️ Match the objective to the WEAKEST member's capability
   ⚠️ Know the bail-out options and where they are
⚠️ THE TEN ESSENTIALS (systems, not items)  navigation ·
   sun protection · ⚠️ INSULATION · illumination · first aid ·
   fire · repair kit and tools · nutrition · hydration ·
   ⚠️ EMERGENCY SHELTER
```
**⚠️ The day-hiker problem is the central finding of §23.1 → `survival-reference`**: ⚠️ **day hikers dominate SAR
statistics precisely because a short outing feels like it doesn't warrant preparation.**
**⚠️ The correction is small: a headtorch, an insulating layer, an emergency bivvy or large
bag, water, and a whistle — on every walk, including the short ones.**

## §19. ⚠️ Kit That Earns Its Weight

```
⚠️ THE HIGH-VALUE ITEMS, by usefulness per gram
   ⚠️ 1. Communication — phone, and ⚠️ a PLB or satellite messenger
   ⚠️ 2. INSULATION LAYER — the thing that prevents §4
   ⚠️ 3. EMERGENCY SHELTER — bivvy bag or a large heavy-duty
        plastic bag. ⚠️ Trivial weight, disproportionate value
   ⚠️ 4. HEADTORCH plus spare battery — ⚠️ benightment is a common
        precipitating cause of SAR callouts
   ⚠️ 5. WHISTLE — ⚠️ grams, and it works when your voice doesn't
   ⚠️ 6. Water and a treatment method
   ⚠️ 7. Fire — lighter AND ferro rod (§6)
   ⚠️ 8. First aid, including a tourniquet if trained (§10)
   ⚠️ 9. Knife or multitool · ⚠️ 10. Cordage · 11. Duct tape
   ⚠️ 12. Signal mirror · 13. Navigation, paper and digital
   14. Calories · 15. Battery pack
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the survival-kit industry inverts the value ordering.** ⚠️ **Kits are
> sold heavy on fire-starting gadgets, wire saws, fishing kits and compasses nobody can
> use, and light on the things that actually matter: insulation, shelter, light and
> communication.** **⚠️ Buy for §1 → `survival-what-kills-psychology-and-priorities`'s risk list, not for the fantasy.**
> **⚠️ And note the general principle: ⚠️ gear you have not used is gear you do not have.**
> **Test everything before it matters** (§21).

## §20. Vehicle and Home

**⚠️ Vehicle**: ⚠️ **in a breakdown or being stranded, STAY WITH THE VEHICLE in almost all
cases — it's shelter, it's visible, and it's what searchers look for.**
**⚠️ Winter vehicle kit**: ⚠️ **blankets or sleeping bags, extra clothing, food and water,
shovel, traction aid, ⚠️ and a candle or small heat source — and if running the engine for
heat, ⚠️ CLEAR THE EXHAUST PIPE OF SNOW, because carbon monoxide entering the cabin is a
recurring cause of death in stranded vehicles.**
**⚠️ Home**: ⚠️ **water, non-perishable food, medications, first aid, light, radio, cash,
documents copied, ⚠️ working smoke and CO alarms (⚠️ the highest-value safety device in
any home by a wide margin), and a fire extinguisher you know how to use.**

## §21. Practising

**⚠️ Reading this document does not confer any capability.** ⚠️ **The skills that matter
degrade without practice and fail differently under stress.**
**⚠️ What to practise, in priority order**: ⚠️ **wilderness first aid (take a certified
course); navigation with map and compass in real terrain; ⚠️ fire in bad conditions, not
good ones; shelter building against a clock; and ⚠️ using your own kit before you need it.**
**⚠️ Practise TIRED, COLD and IN THE DARK**, ⚠️ **because that is the condition under which
you'll need it, and everything is dramatically harder then.**
**⚠️ Formal training is worth far more than gear**: **wilderness first aid or WFR,
avalanche courses for winter terrain, swiftwater for river travel, and navigation
courses.**

## §22. ⚠️ Myths That Kill

```
⚠️ "Suck out snake venom" / cut and suck / suction devices —
   ⚠️ NO benefit, causes tissue damage (§23.2)
⚠️ "Apply a tourniquet for snakebite" — ⚠️ concentrates venom and
   worsens local tissue destruction (§23.2)
⚠️ "Rub frostbite to warm it" — ⚠️ causes mechanical tissue damage
⚠️ "Rewarm frostbite immediately" — ⚠️ NOT if refreezing is possible
⚠️ "Give alcohol to warm someone up" — ⚠️ vasodilation increases
   core heat loss
⚠️ "Rub snow on" anything — ⚠️ no
⚠️ "Eat snow for water" — ⚠️ costs body heat. Melt it (§5)
⚠️ "Moss grows on the north side" — ⚠️ unreliable
⚠️ "Follow a river and you'll find help" — ⚠️ true in some terrain,
   fatal in canyons and slot country (§8)
⚠️ "The universal edibility test" — ⚠️ does not detect delayed
   toxins (§7)
⚠️ "Play dead for all bears" — ⚠️ species and behaviour dependent
⚠️ "Drink your urine" — ⚠️ worsens dehydration
⚠️ "You must find food quickly" — ⚠️ almost never true (§7)
⚠️ "Keep moving to stay warm when lost" — ⚠️ expands the search
   area, exhausts you and produces sweat (§2, §9)
⚠️ "Ration water strictly" — ⚠️ ration sweat instead (§14)
⚠️ "A big knife is the essential survival tool" — ⚠️ insulation and
   a whistle outrank it comfortably (§19)
```

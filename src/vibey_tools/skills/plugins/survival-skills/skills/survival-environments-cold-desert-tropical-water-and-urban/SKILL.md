## §13. Cold Environments

**⚠️ Layering**: ⚠️ **base (moisture management), mid (insulation), shell (wind and water).**
**⚠️ COTTON KILLS is a genuine rule, not a slogan** — ⚠️ **cotton absorbs moisture, loses
essentially all insulating value when wet, and dries very slowly.** **⚠️ Wool and synthetics
retain insulation when wet.**
**⚠️ Manage sweat actively**: ⚠️ **ventilate and remove layers BEFORE you sweat, because
the sweat becomes the hypothermia risk when you stop moving** (§4 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
**⚠️ Frostbite**: ⚠️ **do NOT rewarm if there is any chance of refreezing — a refreeze
cycle causes far worse damage than remaining frozen; never rub; ⚠️ rapid rewarming in warm
water is the definitive treatment and should be done where it can be sustained.**
**⚠️ Snow shelters** (quinzhee, snow trench, tree well) — ⚠️ **snow is an excellent
insulator, and ⚠️ ventilation is mandatory (carbon monoxide, and suffocation risk if a
shelter collapses).**
**⚠️ Avalanche terrain requires formal training, a transceiver, probe and shovel, and
partners who know how to use them** — ⚠️ **and survival is a function of burial duration,
which is why companion rescue rather than organized rescue is what saves people.**
**⚠️ Ice**: ⚠️ **assume unsafe; if you fall through, get horizontal and kick back out the
way you came, then ROLL away from the hole.**

## §14. Desert

**⚠️ Heat, water, and enormous diurnal temperature swing** — ⚠️ **desert nights cause
hypothermia, which surprises people.**
**⚠️ The counterintuitive rule: RATION SWEAT, NOT WATER.** ⚠️ **Drink what you need;
carrying water in your stomach is more useful than carrying it in a bottle you're
saving.** **⚠️ Reduce exertion, travel at night or in the cool hours, and shelter from sun
during peak heat.**
**⚠️ Cover up rather than strip down**: ⚠️ **loose light clothing reduces evaporative loss
and radiant gain; the traditional desert dress is not accidental.**
**⚠️ Shade off the ground** — ⚠️ **ground surface temperature far exceeds air temperature,
so shade a foot above the ground is dramatically cooler than lying on it.**
**⚠️ FLASH FLOODS are the paradoxical desert killer**: ⚠️ **rain miles away produces a
wall of water in a dry wash under clear skies.** **⚠️ Never camp in a wash.**

## §15. Tropical

**⚠️ Heat plus humidity means evaporative cooling fails** (§11 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`) — ⚠️ **wet-bulb limits bind
here.**
**⚠️ Water is usually abundant and usually contaminated** (§5 → `survival-shelter-water-fire-and-food`).
**⚠️ The dominant risks are disease and infection**: ⚠️ **mosquito-borne illness; and
⚠️ minor wounds infect fast and badly in humid heat — aggressive cleaning of small cuts is
genuinely important.**
**⚠️ Keep feet dry where possible** — ⚠️ **immersion foot is disabling and it ends trips.**
**⚠️ Navigation is hard**: ⚠️ **no long sightlines, canopy blocks GPS, and travel is slow —
plan distances accordingly.**

## §16. Water and Maritime

**⚠️ COLD WATER IMMERSION kills in a documented sequence**, ⚠️ **and knowing it changes
behaviour:**
```
⚠️ 1. COLD SHOCK (first 1-3 min) — ⚠️ involuntary gasp and
      hyperventilation. ⚠️ THIS is what drowns people, not
      hypothermia. Control breathing; do not panic
⚠️ 2. SWIM FAILURE (3-30 min) — ⚠️ loss of manual dexterity and
      swimming ability BEFORE core temperature drops meaningfully
⚠️ 3. HYPOTHERMIA (30+ min)
⚠️ 4. POST-RESCUE COLLAPSE
```
⚠️ **The practical consequence: swim to safety IMMEDIATELY if it's close, or get as much
of yourself out of the water as possible.** ⚠️ **HELP position and huddling reduce heat
loss.** **⚠️ A lifejacket is what makes the first minute survivable.**
**⚠️ River crossings are a major and underestimated killer**: ⚠️ **unbuckle the hip belt,
face upstream, use a pole, cross at the widest shallowest point, and ⚠️ if it looks
marginal, don't — wait or walk around.**
**⚠️ At sea**: ⚠️ **stay with the vessel if at all possible (it's bigger and more visible),
EPIRB, liferaft, ⚠️ and do not drink seawater.**

## §17. Urban and Disaster

**⚠️ Different risk profile entirely**: ⚠️ **structural collapse, fire, utility failure,
contaminated water, supply-chain interruption, and social disruption.**
**⚠️ The evidence-based basics**: ⚠️ **water storage is the top priority (roughly 4 litres
per person per day, more in heat); ⚠️ shelter-in-place versus evacuation as a decision made
in advance; ⚠️ a family communication plan with an out-of-area contact (⚠️ local circuits
saturate first); ⚠️ redundant information sources including a battery radio; and cash in
small denominations.**
**⚠️ Utility knowledge**: ⚠️ **know how and when to shut off gas, water and electricity —
and know that gas should be shut off only if you suspect a leak, because restoring it
requires a professional.**
**⚠️ Carbon monoxide is the recurring post-disaster killer** — ⚠️ **generators, grills and
camp stoves used indoors or in attached garages.** **⚠️ Never.**
**⚠️ The honest finding from disaster research**: ⚠️ **the most reliable determinant of
household outcomes is SOCIAL CONNECTION — neighbours who know each other, and who check on
each other — which outperforms stockpiling** (see a family/community reference).

## §4. ⚠️ Thermoregulation and Shelter

> **⚠️ The most important practical section. Hypothermia is the wilderness killer, and
> the conditions that produce it are unremarkable.**
```
⚠️ HEAT LOSS MECHANISMS, and where the leverage is
   ⚠️ CONDUCTION  ⚠️ the ground steals heat FAST. Insulation UNDER you
      matters more than a blanket over you, and this is the most
      commonly missed point in improvised shelter
   ⚠️ CONVECTION  wind. ⚠️ A windbreak is worth more than most insulation
   ⚠️ EVAPORATION  ⚠️ WET is the multiplier. Wet clothing loses heat
      dramatically faster than dry
   RADIATION  head, neck, hands
⚠️ THEREFORE THE PRIORITY IS: DRY → OUT OF WIND → OFF THE GROUND →
   INSULATED
```
**⚠️ Hypothermia is a spectrum and the early signs are cognitive:**
```
⚠️ MILD  shivering, ⚠️ CLUMSINESS and POOR JUDGEMENT (⚠️ the "umbles" —
   stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles). ⚠️ The impaired judgement
   IS the danger, because the person stops helping themselves
⚠️ MODERATE  ⚠️ shivering STOPS (a serious sign, not an improvement),
   confusion, slurred speech
⚠️ SEVERE  ⚠️ rigidity, unresponsiveness, undetectable pulse
⚠️ PARADOXICAL UNDRESSING occurs in severe cases and is a recognized
   phenomenon, not irrationality
```
**⚠️ Field management principles** (§23.2 → `survival-reference` for current guidance): ⚠️ **handle GENTLY —
rough handling of a severely hypothermic person can precipitate cardiac arrhythmia;
remove wet clothing; insulate including underneath and provide a vapour barrier; add
external heat to the trunk rather than the extremities; give warm sweet drinks ONLY if
fully conscious and able to swallow; ⚠️ and "no one is dead until warm and dead" reflects
the real phenomenon of survival after prolonged severe hypothermia — do not abandon
resuscitation on temperature grounds alone.**
**⚠️ Shelter principles**: ⚠️ **small volume beats large (you are the heater); insulate
below; block wind; ⚠️ and the fastest shelter is usually improvement of existing terrain —
a hollow, a deadfall, a rock overhang — rather than construction.** **⚠️ A large plastic
bag or emergency bivvy is remarkably effective for its weight and is why it appears in
every serious kit list** (§19 → `survival-planning-kit-practice-and-myths-that-kill`).

## §5. Water

**⚠️ Dehydration degrades judgement before it threatens life**, ⚠️ **which links it back to
§2 → `survival-what-kills-psychology-and-priorities` — the mistake it causes is the danger.**
**⚠️ Finding water**: ⚠️ **descend and follow drainages; look for green vegetation, animal
trails converging, insect activity; collect rain and dew; snow must be MELTED before
consumption (eating snow costs body heat you cannot spare — §4).**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — treatment removes different things and they are not interchangeable.**
> ⚠️ **BOILING kills all pathogens (a rolling boil, longer at altitude) and removes NO
> chemical contamination.** ⚠️ **FILTERS remove protozoa and bacteria by pore size but
> generally NOT viruses.** ⚠️ **CHEMICAL treatment (chlorine dioxide, iodine) handles
> viruses and bacteria well and ⚠️ requires long contact times against *Cryptosporidium*,
> which is notably chlorine-resistant.** **⚠️ UV requires clear water.**
> **⚠️ Solar still and transpiration bag yields are famously poor relative to the effort
> and sweat expended — treat them as a last resort, not a plan.**

**⚠️ What NOT to drink**: ⚠️ **seawater, urine, and alcohol all make dehydration worse.**
**⚠️ In a genuine short-term emergency with clear-running water and no treatment
available, the risk calculus can favour drinking** — ⚠️ **giardiasis has an incubation
period measured in days to weeks, and dehydration acts in hours.** **That is a judgement
call, not a recommendation.**

## §6. Fire

**⚠️ Fire matters for warmth, morale, signalling (§9 → `survival-navigation-signalling-and-field-medicine`), water treatment and cooking —
in roughly that order.** ⚠️ **Its morale value is genuinely significant and shouldn't be
dismissed** (§2 → `survival-what-kills-psychology-and-priorities`).
```
⚠️ THE THREE REQUIREMENTS  heat, fuel, oxygen — ⚠️ and the near-universal
   failure is FUEL PREPARATION, not ignition
⚠️ THE PROGRESSION  tinder (⚠️ takes a spark) → kindling (pencil-thin
   to thumb-thick) → fuel. ⚠️ Prepare far more than you think, and
   ALL of it, BEFORE striking anything
⚠️ IGNITION  ⚠️ carry a lighter AND a ferrocerium rod AND stormproof
   matches. ⚠️ Redundancy, because they fail differently
⚠️ WET CONDITIONS  ⚠️ standing dead wood beats ground wood; split to
   the dry interior; ⚠️ resinous wood and birch bark light wet;
   build a platform off wet ground
⚠️ FIRE IN WIND AND RAIN  a reflector and a windbreak, and shelter
   the fire lay while it establishes
```
**⚠️ Friction fire is a genuine skill and a poor emergency plan**: ⚠️ **it is difficult
under ideal conditions, extremely difficult when wet, cold and exhausted, and it consumes
energy and time you may not have.** **⚠️ Learn it if you want; do not rely on it.**
**⚠️ Safety and legality**: ⚠️ **check fire restrictions, clear a perimeter, never build
under snow-laden branches or on peat, and ⚠️ carbon monoxide accumulates fatally in
enclosed shelters — never burn inside an unventilated snow shelter, tent or vehicle.**

## §7. Food — And Why It's Last

> **⚠️ A healthy adult can survive weeks without food. Essentially no recreational
> survival situation lasts that long** (§23.1 → `survival-reference`). **⚠️ Time spent seeking food in a
> short-duration emergency is time not spent on shelter and signalling.**
**⚠️ The exceptions that matter**: ⚠️ **diabetics and others with metabolic conditions,
children (much lower reserves), and anyone doing sustained hard work in cold — where
calories directly support thermogenesis** (§4).
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — DO NOT eat wild plants or fungi you cannot identify with certainty, and
> certainty cannot come from a text description or a phone app.** ⚠️ **Several deadly
> species closely resemble edible ones, and mushroom poisoning in particular can present
> after a symptom-free interval during which liver damage is already advanced.**
> **⚠️ The "universal edibility test" taught in some older manuals is NOT reliable — it
> does not detect many toxins, including delayed-onset ones, and testing costs far more
> time than fasting.** ⚠️ **The correct default in a short emergency is to eat nothing
> unidentified.**
> **⚠️ If you want to forage, learn from an expert, in person, one species at a time,
> in your own region.**

**⚠️ Trapping and hunting** are ⚠️ **heavily regulated, generally illegal outside season
and licence, low-yield relative to the calories spent, and irrelevant on rescue
timescales.** **⚠️ In a genuine life-threatening emergency, survival necessity is
recognized in many jurisdictions — but that is a legal defence, not a plan** (see a
hunting/agriculture reference for the regulatory picture).
**⚠️ Insects and fish are generally the higher-yield options** where legal and identifiable.

## §14. ⚠️ Reading Instruction

> **⚠️ One of the few places in education where the evidence really is one-sided, and
> where practice diverged from it for decades at large cost.**
```
⚠️ THE SIMPLE VIEW OF READING
   ⚠️ Reading Comprehension = DECODING × LANGUAGE COMPREHENSION
   ⚠️ MULTIPLICATIVE — if either is zero, comprehension is zero
⚠️ SCARBOROUGH'S ROPE  word recognition strands (phonological
   awareness, decoding, sight recognition) + language comprehension
   strands (background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures,
   verbal reasoning, literacy knowledge)
⚠️ SYSTEMATIC PHONICS  ⚠️ explicit, systematic instruction in
   grapheme-phoneme correspondences. ⚠️ Supported by the National
   Reading Panel, multiple national reviews and meta-analyses
⚠️ ORTHOGRAPHIC MAPPING  ⚠️ how words become instant sight words —
   through decoding, NOT through memorizing whole shapes
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — THREE-CUEING is the specific practice the science of reading movement
> targets, and understanding why matters more than the label.** ⚠️ **Cueing teaches
> children to guess unfamiliar words from pictures, context and first letters.** **⚠️ The
> problem is that it teaches the strategy SKILLED readers do NOT use — skilled reading
> involves rapid, automatic decoding of virtually every letter, and context-guessing is
> what WEAK readers do.** ⚠️ **So cueing trains children into the habit of poor readers,
> and it masks decoding failure until the texts get hard.** **States have moved to ban it
> in instruction** (§25.2 → `pedagogy-reference`).

**⚠️ The part the movement itself sometimes underplays**: ⚠️ **the Simple View has TWO
terms.** **Phonics solves decoding and does NOT produce comprehension** — **that requires
knowledge and vocabulary (§5 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`'s baseball study), built over years through a
content-rich curriculum** (§16). **⚠️ A phonics-only reform will plateau.**

## §15. Mathematics Instruction

**⚠️ The recurring false dichotomy**: **procedural fluency versus conceptual understanding.**
⚠️ **The evidence indicates they develop ITERATIVELY and reinforce each other; neither
comes first as a rule.**
**⚠️ Fluency with number facts matters because of §2 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`** — ⚠️ **automaticity frees working
memory for the actual problem.** **This is not "rote learning" versus "understanding"; it
is a working memory argument.**
**⚠️ Manipulatives and the concrete-representational-abstract sequence**: ⚠️ **useful, with
the caveat that concrete materials do not automatically transfer to abstraction — the
bridge must be taught explicitly.**
**⚠️ Word problems fail on the translation step more than the arithmetic**, ⚠️ **and the
research on problem schemas suggests teaching problem TYPES explicitly.**
**⚠️ Maths anxiety is real and bidirectional with performance** — ⚠️ **it consumes working
memory directly** (§2 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`).

## §16. Curriculum and Sequencing

**⚠️ Given §5 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`, curriculum design is one of the highest-leverage decisions available.**
```
⚠️ KNOWLEDGE-RICH CURRICULUM  ⚠️ because comprehension and reasoning
   depend on domain knowledge, sequencing knowledge deliberately
   matters more than teaching generic skills
⚠️ SPIRAL vs MASTERY  ⚠️ spiral revisits (compatible with §4's
   spacing, if genuinely cumulative); mastery completes before
   moving on. ⚠️ A badly-implemented spiral is superficial repetition
⚠️ PREREQUISITE SEQUENCING  ⚠️ the strongest constraint — build the
   components before the composite
⚠️ THRESHOLD CONCEPTS  ideas that gate everything after them
⚠️ COHERENCE  ⚠️ the same idea encountered across contexts, deliberately
```
**⚠️ Backward design** (**Wiggins and McTighe: outcomes → assessment → activities**) —
⚠️ **and its value is mostly in preventing activity-first planning.**
**⚠️ Cumulative and spaced review must be BUILT IN** (§4 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`), **or it won't happen.**

## §17. Classroom Practice

**⚠️ Routines and expectations reduce cognitive load for everyone** (§3 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`) — ⚠️ **behaviour
management is substantially a design problem, not a personality one.**
**⚠️ What the evidence supports**: **clear expectations taught explicitly, consistency,
high ratios of positive to corrective interaction, ⚠️ momentum and minimal transitions
(lost time is the largest silent cost), and ⚠️ addressing the lowest-level disruption early
rather than escalating.**
**⚠️ Relationships matter and are not a substitute for instruction** — ⚠️ **the honest
framing is that a warm classroom enables demanding teaching, not that warmth alone
teaches.**
**⚠️ Homework**: ⚠️ **effects are small and age-dependent — very weak at primary, larger at
secondary — and QUALITY dominates quantity.** **⚠️ Retrieval-practice homework (§4 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`) is far
more defensible than "finish the worksheet."**

## §18. ⚠️ Differentiation

> **⚠️ A case where a humane intention produced a practice with weak evidence and high
> cost.**
⚠️ **The strong version — separate tasks, materials and objectives per student or group —
has thin evidence, enormous workload, and a documented risk of systematically lowering
expectations for students given the "easier" version.**
**⚠️ What the evidence better supports instead:**
```
⚠️ Same ambitious CURRICULUM for all; vary SUPPORT and TIME, not goals
⚠️ Responsive teaching — adapt in the moment based on checking (§11)
⚠️ Scaffolding that is deliberately FADED (§9)
⚠️ Pre-teaching vocabulary and prerequisites to those who need it
⚠️ Additional instruction/tutoring rather than different content
⚠️ ⚠️ HIGH-DOSAGE TUTORING has among the strongest evidence of any
   intervention — small groups, frequent, sustained, trained tutors,
   during the school day. ⚠️ Its problem is COST and scale, not efficacy
```
**⚠️ Ability grouping**: ⚠️ **overall effects are small and the risks are real — group
assignment tends to become permanent, and the lower groups often receive a slower,
less demanding curriculum, which is a self-fulfilling mechanism.** **⚠️ Within-class
flexible grouping by current attainment on a specific topic is more defensible than
fixed streaming.**

## §19. Higher Education

**⚠️ The lecture is not inherently bad — an unstructured, non-interactive lecture to
novices is.** ⚠️ **Freeman et al.'s meta-analysis found active learning improved exam
performance and cut failure rates substantially in STEM.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — students often RATE active learning lower while LEARNING more from it**,
> ⚠️ **because effortful learning feels less fluent than a polished lecture** (§7 → `pedagogy-reliability-memory-cognitive-load-and-robust-effects`).
> **⚠️ Deslauriers et al. demonstrated this directly: measured learning up, perceived
> learning down.** **⚠️ This is a serious problem for institutions that manage teaching by
> student satisfaction scores.**
**⚠️ Also note**: ⚠️ **student evaluations of teaching correlate weakly with learning and
carry documented biases** — **useful for some things, not a measure of teaching quality.**

## §20. Adult and Workplace Learning

**⚠️ "Andragogy" (Knowles) is a useful set of heuristics** — ⚠️ **adults bring experience,
want relevance and autonomy** — **but it is not a well-evidenced separate theory of
learning.** **⚠️ Adults learn by the same mechanisms as children; the CONTEXT and
motivation differ.**
**⚠️ The transfer problem is the central issue in workplace training**: ⚠️ **most training
fails to transfer to the job, and the determinants are largely organizational — manager
support, opportunity to apply, and whether the environment rewards the new behaviour.**
**⚠️ Deliberate practice** (Ericsson) ⚠️ **requires a well-defined task at appropriate
difficulty, immediate feedback, and repetition with refinement** — **and note the
subsequent literature substantially reduced its estimated share of performance variance
across domains.** **⚠️ Most workplace "experience" is not deliberate practice.**
**⚠️ The 70-20-10 model has NO credible empirical basis** and is repeated as if it did
(§23 → `pedagogy-reading-research-and-zombie-ideas`).

## §21. Educational Technology

**⚠️ The historical pattern is remarkably consistent: each new technology is predicted to
transform education, and meta-analyses find small average effects with enormous
variance.**
⚠️ **The variance is the finding — implementation, pedagogy and context dominate the
technology itself.**
```
⚠️ WHAT TECH IS GENUINELY GOOD AT
   ⚠️ Spaced retrieval at scale (§4) — algorithmic scheduling is a
      genuine improvement over a human doing it
   ⚠️ Immediate feedback on well-defined tasks
   Simulation of the inaccessible; adaptive practice sequencing
   Access and accessibility
⚠️ WHAT IT DOESN'T FIX
   ⚠️ Weak curriculum, weak explanation, or weak assessment
⚠️ KNOWN NEGATIVES
   ⚠️ Laptops for note-taking underperform handwriting in several
      studies; ⚠️ multitasking is genuinely costly; ⚠️ the presence
      of devices distracts even when unused
```
**⚠️ The evaluation caution**: ⚠️ **edtech efficacy studies are frequently vendor-funded,
short, and measure engagement or usage rather than learning** (§22 → `pedagogy-reading-research-and-zombie-ideas`).

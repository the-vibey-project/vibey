## §24. Wildlife Management

**⚠️ Modern wildlife management is applied population ecology, and the North American Model
of Wildlife Conservation is the institutional frame in the US** — **wildlife as a public
trust, prohibition on commercial market hunting, allocation by law rather than by wealth,
and ⚠️ hunter- and angler-funded conservation through licence fees and the Pittman-Robertson
excise tax on arms and ammunition.**
```
CARRYING CAPACITY  ⚠️ K. Biological vs CULTURAL carrying capacity (what
   people will tolerate — usually the binding constraint near towns)
COMPENSATORY vs ADDITIVE MORTALITY  ⚠️ THE key concept. Below K, harvest
   may substitute for natural mortality rather than adding to it. Above
   some level it becomes additive
RECRUITMENT   ⚠️ fawns/poults surviving to join the population. Habitat-driven
SEX/AGE STRUCTURE  ⚠️ harvesting antlerless animals controls population;
   harvesting bucks mostly does not
HABITAT       ⚠️ food, cover, water, space — and the LIMITING one governs (§4's
   Liebig logic applies to wildlife too)
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "protect the deer by not shooting does" is the most persistent and most
> damaging piece of management folklore.** ⚠️ **Population growth is driven by
> reproductive females. A herd over carrying capacity degrades its own habitat, and
> browse damage cascades into forest regeneration failure and reduced fawn survival.**
> **⚠️ Antlerless harvest is the primary population control tool, and reluctance to use it
> is a well-documented management problem across the whitetail range.**

**⚠️ Habitat management is mostly deliberate disturbance** (§6 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`): **prescribed fire (⚠️ the
most under-used and most effective tool in much of the eastern and southern US), timber
stand improvement, edge feathering, native warm-season grass establishment, and food
plots — ⚠️ which are a supplement and a hunting tool, not a substitute for habitat.**
**⚠️ Predator-prey dynamics rarely work the way people assume**: ⚠️ **predator removal
frequently produces smaller and shorter-lived effects on prey populations than expected,
because habitat and weather usually dominate recruitment.**

## §25. ⚠️ Hunting: Law, Ethics, Safety

**⚠️ LAW FIRST, and it is genuinely non-negotiable:**
```
⚠️ Regulations are STATE-specific and change ANNUALLY. Your state wildlife
   agency's current published regulations are the authority — not a book,
   not a forum, not this document
⚠️ Licences, tags, permits, hunter education requirement
⚠️ Seasons, legal hours (⚠️ usually tied to sunrise/sunset offsets), bag limits
⚠️ Legal methods and equipment by season (archery/muzzleloader/firearm)
⚠️ Baiting rules — ⚠️ vary enormously and are increasingly restricted for
   disease reasons (§28.2)
⚠️ Written landowner permission; trespass law
⚠️ Tagging and reporting requirements — often immediate, before moving
⚠️ Transport rules, including CARCASS MOVEMENT restrictions (§28.2)
```
**⚠️ FIREARM SAFETY — the four rules, and they are the standard because they are
redundant. Any one of them, followed, prevents the incident:**
```
1. ⚠️ Treat every firearm as loaded
2. ⚠️ Never point the muzzle at anything you are not willing to destroy
3. ⚠️ Keep your finger off the trigger until your sights are on the target
   and you have decided to shoot
4. ⚠️ BE CERTAIN OF YOUR TARGET AND WHAT IS BEYOND IT
```
**⚠️ Rule 4 is the one that kills people in the field.** ⚠️ **Positive target
identification — the whole animal, identified, not a shape or a sound or a movement — is
mandatory, and "I was sure" is what every such incident report says.** **Know where your
hunting partners are. Wear the required blaze orange.**
**⚠️ Tree stand falls cause more hunting injuries than firearms in many jurisdictions**,
**and the mitigation is simple and widely ignored: ⚠️ a full-body harness worn from the
ground up, a lifeline, three points of contact, and inspecting stands and straps every
season.**
**⚠️ Other field safety**: **tell someone your plan and expected return; carry
communication and know your coverage; hypothermia is the most common serious outdoor
medical problem; and ⚠️ dragging a heavy animal is a genuine cardiac risk in unfit
hunters.**

**⚠️ Fair chase and ethics** — **the tradition's own standard: no unfair technological or
positional advantage, respect for the animal, ⚠️ take only shots you are confident of
(§26), make every reasonable effort to recover, use the meat, and respect landowners and
other hunters.** ⚠️ **Legal and ethical are not the same set, and the ethical standard is
generally the stricter one.**

## §26. Shot Placement and Recovery

**⚠️ The ethical obligation is a quick, certain kill, and that is an accuracy and
patience problem far more than an equipment problem.**
**⚠️ Anatomy over caliber**: **the target is the heart-lung (thoracic) vault** — ⚠️ **a
large, forgiving target that produces rapid unconsciousness.** **Broadside and
quartering-away present it best; ⚠️ quartering-toward and head-on shots are far less
forgiving and are generally best declined.**
**⚠️ Know your effective range and shoot inside it.** **Your effective range is the
distance at which you can reliably place every shot in the vital zone from a FIELD
position — not from a bench.** ⚠️ **It is shorter than most people believe, and practising
from field positions is how you find out.**
**Archery differs mechanically**: ⚠️ **arrows kill by haemorrhage, not shock, so shot
placement tolerance is tighter, sharp broadheads matter enormously, and waiting before
tracking is usually correct.**
**⚠️ Recovery discipline**: **mark the spot and the animal's direction; ⚠️ wait an
appropriate period (varies by hit and species — pressing a wounded animal usually pushes
it further); take up the blood trail systematically with markers; grid-search where the
trail is lost.** ⚠️ **The obligation to make a genuine, exhaustive recovery effort is not
optional, and a lost animal should change your future shot selection.**

## §27. ⚠️ Field Care and Game Meat Safety

**⚠️ Meat quality is decided in the first two hours, and the enemy is heat.**
```
1. ⚠️ FIELD DRESS PROMPTLY. Body cavity heat is what spoils meat
2. ⚠️ Avoid puncturing the digestive tract; if you do, trim generously
   and rinse — GI contents are the main contamination source
3. ⚠️ COOL FAST. Get the carcass below ~40°F/4°C as quickly as possible.
   ⚠️ In warm weather this may mean quartering and icing within hours,
   NOT hanging
4. AGING requires CONTROLLED refrigeration — ⚠️ "hanging in the shed" in
   50°F+ weather is spoilage, not aging
5. ⚠️ Keep it clean, dry and airflowing; a game bag keeps flies off
```
**⚠️ Personal safety while handling**: **wear gloves — ⚠️ brucellosis, tularemia (rabbits
especially) and leptospirosis are real occupational risks of field dressing.** ⚠️ **Do not
handle or consume an animal that appeared sick or behaved abnormally; report it to your
wildlife agency instead** (§28.2 → `agri-reference`).
**⚠️ Cook game to safe internal temperatures** — **whole-muscle venison to at least
145°F/63°C with rest, ground to 160°F/71°C, and wild birds to 165°F/74°C.**
⚠️ **Trichinella remains a genuine risk in bear and wild pig** — **those must be cooked
thoroughly, and freezing does NOT reliably kill the species found in wild game.**
**⚠️ Lead fragments**: **lead bullets fragment extensively and fragments spread well beyond
the wound channel** — ⚠️ **radiographic studies have found fragments distant from the
visible damage.** **Trim generously around wound channels; ⚠️ many hunters switch to
copper or other non-lead projectiles, which also removes the documented lead-poisoning
risk to scavenging raptors and condors.**

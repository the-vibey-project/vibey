>

> can hurt you or your animals.
>

> for either:**
> - ⚠️ **Plant identification for consumption: never eat anything identified from a text
>   description.** **§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science` explains why, and it is not a formality — the deadliest lookalikes
>   are genuinely hard.**
> - ⚠️ **Hunting is governed by state and local law that changes annually.** **§24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting` gives
>   you the framework; your state agency's current regulations are the authority.**
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ Everything here is a system with feedback, and single-variable optimization
>    reliably backfires** — **maximize yield and lose soil; maximize milk and lose
>    fertility; maximize deer numbers and lose the habitat that fed them.**
> 2. **⚠️ Soil and rumen are the two engines** (§10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, §16 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`). **Most of agriculture is
>    managing microbial communities you cannot see.**
> 3. **⚠️ Confident folklore is dense in these fields** — **passed down, plausible, and
>    frequently wrong.** **The gotcha boxes are doing real work.**

## §28. What's Live — verified August 2026

### 28.1 ⚠️ H5N1 in dairy cattle
**⚠️ A novel and continuing situation: an avian virus established in a mammalian
livestock species, which was not expected.**

- **⚠️ HPAI H5N1 (clade 2.3.4.4b) was confirmed in US dairy cattle for the first time in
  March 2024** — **the same clade circulating in wild birds and poultry.** **The AVMA
  reported confirmations in dairy cattle across 19 states.**
- **⚠️ The transmission picture is not what people assume.** **Analysis indicates most
  livestock outbreaks began with spillover from wild birds, followed by dairy-to-dairy and
  dairy-to-poultry spread** — ⚠️ **and USDA states it is NOT currently believed that
  migratory waterfowl are seeding dairy or poultry premises directly, supported by both
  genomic and epidemiological data.** **⚠️ The virus moves on EQUIPMENT, PEOPLE AND ITEMS
  travelling farm to farm.** **That makes it a biosecurity problem you can actually
  control** (§20 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`).
- **⚠️ Surveillance shifted from farm-level to bulk-milk testing.** **USDA-APHIS launched
  a National Milk Testing Strategy in December 2024, using bulk tank samples**; **states
  have progressively moved to less intensive sampling as they clear** — **Minnesota
  reached "Unaffected" status in August 2025 and moved to silo sampling at plants in
  February 2026.** ⚠️ **The April 2024 Federal Order requiring testing before interstate
  cattle movement remains a central control.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the milk safety question has a clear answer and a clear exception.**
> ⚠️ **Pasteurization inactivates H5N1**, **milk from symptomatic cows is diverted or
> destroyed, and FDA testing of pasteurized dairy products has not detected infectious
> virus.** **⚠️ Retail monitoring during April–May 2024 found H5N1 viral RNA in 36% of
> retail milk samples — which indicated widespread undetected infection in herds, NOT that
> the milk was infectious**, **since RNA detection is not the same as viable virus.**
> ⚠️ **The exception is RAW milk: FDA and USDA advise it should not be consumed.**
> **Infected cats on affected dairies — including from raw milk — have been a recurring
> and lethal feature of this outbreak.**

**⚠️ Human risk is characterized as low for the general public**, **with sporadic cases in
dairy and poultry workers.** ⚠️ **CDC noted in July 2025 that it had streamlined its
reporting cadence to reflect the current situation** — **and USDA animal-detection data is
no longer mirrored on the CDC site, so ⚠️ go to APHIS for animal numbers and CDC for human
surveillance.**
**⚠️ Practical implications**: **biosecurity is the primary control — dedicated equipment,
controlled traffic, PPE for workers, and separating dairy and poultry operations;
poultry vaccines exist and cattle vaccine work continues; and ⚠️ USDA has offered producer
financial support for biosecurity and losses.**

### 28.2 ⚠️ Chronic wasting disease
**⚠️ A prion disease of cervids that is always fatal, has no treatment or vaccine, and is
expanding.**

- **⚠️ Reported distribution varies by source and by counting method, and I'd flag that
  rather than pick.** **CDC stated in January 2026 that CWD has spread to animals in 36
  states; one state agency reported 37 states plus five Canadian provinces, South Korea,
  Norway, Sweden and Finland as of July 2026; another 2026 source counts 33 states for
  FREE-RANGING cervids specifically.** ⚠️ **The differences are largely captive-versus-wild
  and detection-versus-established.** **Check your state agency.**
- **⚠️ The prion is extraordinarily persistent.** **Shed in saliva, urine, feces, blood and
  antler velvet; transmitted directly or through contaminated environments; ⚠️ resistant to
  heat, UV and disinfectants, and it can persist in soil for years to decades.** **This is
  why carcass-movement rules and feeding bans exist.**
- **⚠️ Long incubation is why you cannot judge by appearance.** **Typically at least 16
  months, and infected animals may look healthy for 12–24 months before showing weight
  loss, stumbling, drooling and loss of fear.** ⚠️ **A healthy-looking deer from an endemic
  area is not evidence of anything.**
- **⚠️ Management is being reassessed, and one case is worth knowing.** **Illinois — after
  a 23-year sharpshooting program that earned national recognition for slowing spread —
  suspended targeted culling in 2026 after detections in three new counties took it to 28,
  including cases beyond the endemic leading edge.** ⚠️ **The agency's stated reason: the
  disease had expanded beyond its capacity to manage that way.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the human-risk question is genuinely unresolved, and both overstating and
> dismissing it are wrong.** ⚠️ **No CWD infection in a human has ever been reported, and
> the CDC says the disease "hasn't been shown to infect people."** **BUT public health
> officials uniformly recommend avoiding exposure, and ⚠️ CDC recommends against eating
> meat from CWD-positive animals.** **The precaution exists because BSE — a related prion
> disease — did cross to humans.**
> **⚠️ And a critical technical point most hunters don't know: available CWD tests are NOT
> food safety tests.** ⚠️ **Results are "positive" or "no prions detected" — not
> "safe."** **In early infection, prions may be below detection.** **A 2026 laboratory
> study detecting limited interspecies prion transmission drew attention, and the authors
> and other experts cautioned it does not confirm human risk** — ⚠️ **though the failure of
> standard tests to detect prions in infected but asymptomatic macaques deepened at least
> one researcher's concern about relying on those tests to judge venison.**

**⚠️ What hunters should actually do**, **consolidating agency guidance:**
```
⚠️ KEEP HUNTING — and in CWD areas, agencies generally ask for MORE harvest,
   not less. Reducing density and preventing congregation are the tools
⚠️ Get animals tested where testing is offered
⚠️ Wear gloves; ⚠️ avoid sawing through brain or spinal cord; minimize
   handling of brain, spinal cord, eyes, spleen and lymph nodes
⚠️ DON'T MOVE CARCASSES. Bone out meat; many states allow importing only
   boned meat, quarters without spinal column or head, cleaned skull plates
⚠️ Process animals individually; ⚠️ label packages by animal so a positive
   result lets you remove that animal's meat from the freezer
⚠️ Don't shoot or eat an animal that looks or behaves sick — report it
⚠️ Don't feed or bait where prohibited (congregation drives transmission);
   ⚠️ natural urine-based lures are banned in some states over contamination
   and congregation concerns
```

## §29. Misconceptions

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| Plants get their mass from soil | ⚠️ **Mostly from CO₂ in air** (§4 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`) |
| Fertilizer fixes poor growth | ⚠️ **Check pH and compaction first** (§4 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`, §10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| More fertilizer, more yield | ⚠️ **Liebig — only the limiting nutrient responds** (§4 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`) |
| Soil test is optional | ⚠️ **It's the cheapest input decision you'll make** (§11 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| Soil is inert substrate | ⚠️ **It's a living system; biology does the work** (§10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| Tilling improves soil | ⚠️ **It costs structure, organic matter and mycorrhizae** (§10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, §12 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| A photo app can ID a plant for eating | ⚠️ **Not sufficient. Lethal lookalikes exist** (§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`) |
| Common names identify plants reliably | ⚠️ **They don't. Use Latin names** (§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`) |
| Organic means no pesticides | ⚠️ **It means restricted inputs; some approved ones are toxic** (§16 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| Regenerative ag sequesters carbon at scale | ⚠️ **Saturates, reversible, hard to measure** (§16 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| GM food safety is unsettled | ⚠️ **Safety is settled; agronomic/economic debates are real** (§15 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| Cows digest grass | ⚠️ **Rumen MICROBES do. You feed the microbes** (§17 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`) |
| Cattle just eat human food | ⚠️ **Ruminants upgrade cellulose humans can't eat** (§17 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`) |
| Deworm everyone on a schedule | ⚠️ **That caused resistance. Use targeted treatment + refugia** (§20 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`) |
| Best genetics = best results | ⚠️ **Match the animal to the environment** (§21 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`) |
| Overgrazing is about animal numbers | ⚠️ **It's about TIME — recovery period** (§22 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`) |
| Efficient irrigation saves basin water | ⚠️ **It often increases total consumption** (§14 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`) |
| Don't shoot does, protect the herd | ⚠️ **Antlerless harvest is the population control tool** (§24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Mature forest is great deer habitat | ⚠️ **Early successional habitat is** (§6 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`, §24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Killing predators boosts game populations | ⚠️ **Usually smaller effect than habitat and weather** (§24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Food plots substitute for habitat | ⚠️ **Supplement and hunting tool, not substitute** (§24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Hang the deer in the shed to age it | ⚠️ **Above ~40°F that's spoilage** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Trim the wound channel and lead is gone | ⚠️ **Fragments spread well beyond it** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Freezing kills trichinella in wild game | ⚠️ **Not reliably. Cook thoroughly** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`) |
| Pasteurized milk is an H5N1 risk | ⚠️ **Pasteurization inactivates it; RAW milk is the risk** (§28.1) |
| RNA found in retail milk = infectious milk | ⚠️ **RNA detection ≠ viable virus** (§28.1) |
| A CWD test certifies meat as safe | ⚠️ **It is not a food safety test** (§28.2) |
| A healthy-looking deer can't have CWD | ⚠️ **12–24 months asymptomatic** (§28.2) |

## §30. Numbers

```
SOIL     ⚠️ ideal ~45% mineral / 5% OM / 25% water / 25% air
         ⚠️ optimal pH for most nutrient availability ~6.0–7.0
PLANTS   ⚠️ transpiration ratio: hundreds of g water per g dry matter
         C3 vs C4 — cool-season vs warm-season peak production (§2)
GESTATION ⚠️ cattle ~283 d · sheep/goat ~150 d · pig ~114 d · horse ~340 d
ESTROUS  ⚠️ cattle ~21 d · sheep ~17 d · pig ~21 d
GRAZING  ⚠️ "take half, leave half"; ⚠️ rest period is the key variable
QUARANTINE  ⚠️ 21–30 days for incoming livestock
MEAT     ⚠️ cool carcass below 40°F/4°C fast
         ⚠️ venison whole muscle 145°F/63°C + rest · ground 160°F/71°C
         ⚠️ wild birds 165°F/74°C · bear and wild pig: thoroughly (trichinella)
CWD      ⚠️ incubation ≥16 months; asymptomatic 12–24 months
         ⚠️ detected in 33–37 states depending on counting method (§28.2)
H5N1     ⚠️ dairy cattle confirmed in 19 states; clade 2.3.4.4b (§28.1)
         ⚠️ 36% of retail milk samples had viral RNA (Apr–May 2024) — not
         infectious virus
```

### 32.2 ⚠️ Safety non-negotiables
- [ ] ⚠️ **Never eat a plant/fungus ID'd from text alone** (§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Four firearm rules, every time — especially "what's beyond"** (§25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Positive target ID: the whole animal, identified** (§25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Full-body harness from the ground up in a tree stand** (§25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] Current state regulations checked this season (§25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] Written landowner permission (§25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Gloves when field dressing; don't handle sick animals** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Carcass cooled below 40°F fast** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Cook to safe internal temperature; bear/pig thoroughly** (§27 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Quarantine incoming livestock 21–30 days** (§20 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`)
- [ ] Withdrawal times observed on any treated animal (§20 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`)

## §33. Method

**§1–§27 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science`, `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`, `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting` rest on stable material** — **plant physiology, soil science, ruminant nutrition,
population ecology, and long-standing safety practice** — sourced from §31. ⚠️ **None of
it needed verification; RuBisCO, Liebig's law and the rumen have not changed.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **H5N1 in dairy cattle** and **chronic wasting
disease** — ⚠️ **the two live situations in this domain that actually change what a
producer or hunter does this season.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §10 → `agri-soil-fertility-and-crop-production`, §17 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare` and §22 → `agri-livestock-nutrition-health-and-welfare`, which are the mechanistic cores. ⚠️ **"You
feed the rumen microbes, not the cow" and "overgrazing is a function of time, not animal
numbers" are the two ideas that most change how someone manages livestock**, **and both
follow from mechanism rather than from tradition.**

**High** in §24 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting`'s antlerless-harvest point, which I'd defend firmly — ⚠️ **it is
well-established management science and it remains one of the most resisted
recommendations in the field.**

**High** in §28.1's facts, which come substantially from primary agency sources — **USDA
APHIS, FDA, CDC and a CDC *Emerging Infectious Diseases* paper.** ⚠️ **The most
misunderstood item is the 36% retail milk figure: it indicated widespread undetected herd
infection, and it is viral RNA, not infectious virus.** **I've stated that explicitly
because the number circulates without the distinction.** ⚠️ **The waterfowl point is also
counterintuitive and well-sourced — USDA says genomic and epidemiological data do not
support direct migratory-bird seeding of premises, which redirects attention to equipment
and people, where control is actually possible.**

⚠️ **§28.2 contains a numerical disagreement I've flagged rather than resolved.** **CDC
says 36 states (January 2026); a state agency says 37 states plus provinces and four other
countries (July 2026); another 2026 source says 33 states for free-ranging cervids.**
**The spread is explained largely by captive-versus-wild counting and by
detection-versus-establishment, and I've directed the reader to their own state agency.**

⚠️ **The most important item in §28.2 is not a number**: **available CWD tests are not food
safety tests, and results read "no prions detected" rather than "safe."** **That is
Virginia DWR's own framing and it is the thing hunters most often get wrong.** ⚠️ **I've
presented the human-risk question as genuinely unresolved — no human case has ever been
reported, and every public health body still recommends avoiding exposure — and included
the 2026 interspecies-transmission lab study with the authors' own caution that it does
not confirm human risk.** **Overstating and dismissing are both errors here.**

⚠️ **Two places where I deliberately declined to be authoritative**: **§7 → `agri-botany-and-plant-science` (plant ID for
consumption) and §25 → `agri-wildlife-management-and-hunting` (hunting regulations).** **In both cases a confident text answer is
the failure mode, and the correct output is a pointer to a regional key or a state
agency.**

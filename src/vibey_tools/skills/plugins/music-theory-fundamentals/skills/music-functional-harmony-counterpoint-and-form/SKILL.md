## §10. Functional Harmony

**⚠️ The core insight of common-practice tonality: chords have FUNCTIONS — roles in a
directed motion toward a goal.**
```
TONIC (I, vi, iii)         home, stability, arrival
PREDOMINANT (IV, ii)       ⚠️ departure, preparation
DOMINANT (V, vii°)         ⚠️ TENSION demanding resolution to tonic
```
**⚠️ Why the dominant works, mechanistically**: **V7 contains the LEADING TONE (a semitone
below tonic, with strong melodic pull) and a TRITONE between the 3rd and 7th, which
resolves outward or inward by step.** ⚠️ **The tension is real and specific, not
conventional decoration.**
**The circle of fifths**; **secondary dominants (V/V)**; **tonicization.**
**⚠️ Voice leading is the actual subject** — **and the "rules" are all consequences of two
goals:**
```
⚠️ Move each voice as little as possible (smoothness, singability)
⚠️ Keep the voices INDEPENDENT (so they're heard as separate lines)

⚠️ Parallel fifths/octaves are forbidden BECAUSE they collapse two voices into
   one perceptually — the acoustic fusion is so strong you stop hearing two
   parts. ⚠️ NOT because of a superstition. And when you WANT that fused sound
   (organum, Debussy, power chords, orchestral doubling) you use them
Resolve tendency tones · avoid parallel motion into perfect intervals ·
prefer contrary motion · avoid voice crossing and overlap
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — every voice-leading "rule" is a description of what makes four-part vocal
> counterpoint in a particular style work.** ⚠️ **They are not laws of music, and applying
> them to guitar-based rock or to piano writing produces nonsense.** **Learn WHY each one
> exists and you'll know when it doesn't apply.**

## §11. Cadence and Phrase

```
AUTHENTIC (V–I)     ⚠️ perfect authentic = strongest close available
HALF (ends on V)    ⚠️ open, question-like — the comma of tonal syntax
DECEPTIVE (V–vi)    expectation set up and denied
PLAGAL (IV–I)       ⚠️ "amen"; weaker, and much more common in rock than in
                    common practice
PHRYGIAN HALF       ♭II6–V, Baroque
```
**Phrase structure**: **antecedent/consequent periods, sentences (presentation →
continuation → cadence), and hypermeter.** ⚠️ **Musical phrases behave much like sentences
— they set up expectation and satisfy or frustrate it — and this is the most productive
analogy in music theory.** **Cadences are punctuation.**

## §12. Modulation and Chromaticism

**Modulation types**: **common chord/pivot, direct, chromatic, enharmonic, sequential.**
**Closely related keys** (⚠️ **one accidental apart — the well-trodden paths**).
**Chromatic harmony**: **borrowed chords / modal mixture** (⚠️ **the ♭VI and ♭VII in a
major key — enormously common in film scoring and rock**), **Neapolitan (♭II6)**,
**augmented sixths (Italian/French/German)**, **and the common-tone diminished seventh** —
⚠️ **the fully-diminished 7th is symmetrical, so it can resolve almost anywhere, which is
exactly why it's the pivot of choice for dramatic modulation.**
**⚠️ Late-Romantic chromaticism progressively weakened functional gravity** — **Wagner's
Tristan chord is the standard emblem** — ⚠️ **and the eventual arrival at atonality (§20 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`)
was a continuation of that trajectory rather than a rupture.**

## §13. Counterpoint

**⚠️ Counterpoint is the combination of independent melodic lines, and it is the historical
foundation harmony was later abstracted from** — **not the other way round.**
**Species counterpoint** (Fux, 1725) — **a graded pedagogy from note-against-note through
florid** — ⚠️ **and it remains an outstanding training method because it isolates voice
leading from harmonic vocabulary.**
**Devices**: **imitation, canon, fugue** (⚠️ **subject, answer — real vs tonal —
countersubject, episode, stretto**), **invertible counterpoint, augmentation, diminution,
retrograde, inversion.**
**⚠️ The conceptual point worth carrying**: **in a genuinely contrapuntal texture the
chords are a BY-PRODUCT of simultaneous lines, not a pre-existing scaffold.** ⚠️ **Analyzing
Bach purely as chord progressions inverts how it was made.**

## §14. Form

```
BINARY (AB) · ROUNDED BINARY (ABA') · TERNARY (ABA)
⚠️ SONATA FORM   exposition (1st theme–transition–2nd theme in a NEW KEY–
   closing) → development → recapitulation (⚠️ 2nd theme now in the TONIC).
   The form is a KEY drama first and a theme drama second — which is what
   textbook diagrams obscure
RONDO (ABACA) · THEME AND VARIATIONS · FUGUE · STROPHIC
⚠️ 12-BAR BLUES  I-I-I-I-IV-IV-I-I-V-IV-I-V. The most generative form of
   the 20th century
⚠️ POP FORMS  verse–chorus, verse–prechorus–chorus–bridge, and the loop-based
   structures of electronic music where variation is textural, not thematic
```
**⚠️ Formal function matters more than the letter diagram**: **is this passage presenting,
transitioning, developing, or closing?** ⚠️ **Two pieces with the same ABA shape can be
doing entirely different things.**

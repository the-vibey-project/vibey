## §5. Pitch, Intervals, Notation

**Notation is a technology with a history and its biases show**: ⚠️ **staff notation
encodes pitch and duration precisely, and timbre, articulation, microtiming and intonation
badly or not at all** — **which is exactly why it serves Western art music well and serves
jazz, folk and non-Western traditions poorly** (§18–§19 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`).
**Intervals**: **number + quality** (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished).
⚠️ **Enharmonic equivalence — that A♯ and B♭ are "the same" — is TRUE ONLY IN EQUAL
TEMPERAMENT** (§4 → `music-claims-harmonic-series-psychoacoustics-and-tuning`), **and the distinct spellings persist because they mean different
things functionally.**
**Inversion, compound intervals, consonance/dissonance classification** (⚠️ **and note
from §3 → `music-claims-harmonic-series-psychoacoustics-and-tuning` that the classification is stylistic**).

## §6. Scales and Modes

```
MAJOR (Ionian)      W W H W W W H
NATURAL MINOR       W H W W H W W  (= Aeolian)
HARMONIC MINOR      ⚠️ raised 7 — to get a LEADING TONE and a major V (§10).
                    The augmented 2nd is a side effect, not a goal
MELODIC MINOR       ⚠️ raised 6 and 7 ascending to smooth that gap
```
**⚠️ The seven diatonic modes are rotations of the same set of intervals**, **and thinking
of them as "the white notes starting on different letters" is how they're taught and why
they're misunderstood:**
```
Ionian     bright, = major
Dorian     ⚠️ minor with a RAISED 6th. The characteristic sound of much folk,
           modal jazz, and a great deal of rock
Phrygian   ⚠️ minor with a ♭2. Spanish/flamenco inflection, metal
Lydian     ⚠️ major with a ♯4. Floating, unresolved
Mixolydian ⚠️ major with a ♭7. THE dominant sound of blues-derived rock
Aeolian    = natural minor
Locrian    ⚠️ diminished 5th — no stable tonic. Barely usable as a key
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — a mode is defined by its relationship to a TONIC, not by which notes are
> in it.** ⚠️ **Playing white notes from D to D is not Dorian if the music keeps resolving
> to C — that's just C major.** **Modality requires that the harmony and phrase structure
> actually establish D as home.** **This is the single most common error in how modes are
> taught and used.**

**Other important collections**: **pentatonic** (⚠️ **five notes, no semitones, and it
appears independently across an enormous number of unrelated cultures — one of the better
candidates for a near-universal**), **blues scale** (⚠️ **a notational approximation of
pitch inflections that don't sit on the 12-note grid at all** — §18 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`), **whole tone**
(⚠️ **symmetrical, no leading tone, no stable tonic — Debussy**), **octatonic/diminished**,
**chromatic**, **and the church modes as historically used** (⚠️ **which differ from the
modern "rotation" presentation and involved *finalis*, reciting tone and range**).

## §7. Rhythm and Meter

**⚠️ Rhythm is under-theorized in Western classical training relative to its importance,
and this is a real gap** — **most of the world's music organizes rhythm with far more
sophistication than it organizes harmony.**
```
PULSE / BEAT     the felt regular unit
METER            ⚠️ grouping of beats into a repeating pattern of strong/weak
                 Simple (beat divides in 2) vs compound (divides in 3)
TEMPO            rate, BPM
SYNCOPATION      ⚠️ accent displaced from the expected strong position.
                 Requires the meter to be established to work at all
POLYRHYTHM       ⚠️ two independent groupings at once (3:2 = hemiola)
POLYMETER        ⚠️ different meters simultaneously — not the same as polyrhythm
ADDITIVE METER   ⚠️ 2+2+3 rather than a division of 7. Balkan, Indian, much
                 non-Western practice. ⚠️ Western notation handles this badly
```
**⚠️ Two things the notation actively obscures:**
- **⚠️ MICROTIMING carries most of the feel.** **Swing, groove, laid-back and pushed
  playing are systematic, measurable deviations from notated positions** — ⚠️ **and swing
  ratios are not a fixed triplet feel; they vary with tempo and with player, typically
  flattening toward even at fast tempos.** **A quantized performance sounds dead for
  precisely this reason.**
- **⚠️ The BEAT is a perceptual construction, not a property of the signal.** **Listeners
  infer it, can disagree about it, and entrainment to a pulse appears to be a human
  near-universal that is rare in other species.**

**⚠️ Metric dissonance** (Krebs) — **grouping vs displacement conflict** — **is a genuinely
useful analytical concept and applies far better to popular and non-Western music than
harmonic analysis does.**

## §8. Timbre

**⚠️ Timbre is the "everything else" parameter and it's the one that actually distinguishes
most music people love.**
**Determined by**: **the harmonic spectrum and its evolution over time** (⚠️ **the ATTACK
TRANSIENT is disproportionately important for identification — remove the attack from a
piano note and it becomes hard to identify**), **formants** (⚠️ **fixed resonances — the
basis of vowel identity and of instrument "body"**), **noise components, and inharmonic
partials** (⚠️ **bells and drums have partials that are NOT whole-number multiples, which
is why their pitch is ambiguous**).
**⚠️ Orchestration is timbre theory in practice**: **register, doubling, blend, masking,
and the fact that the same notes can be transparent or opaque depending entirely on
spacing and instrumentation.**
**⚠️ In electronic and produced music, timbre and texture ARE the compositional content**
— **the harmony may be four chords, and the interest lives entirely in the sound design,
the mix, and the arrangement over time.** ⚠️ **Analyzing such music harmonically and
concluding it's simple is a category error** (§18 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`).

## §9. Chords

**Triads**: **major (4:5:6), minor, diminished, augmented.** **Sevenths**: **dominant 7,
major 7, minor 7, half-diminished, fully diminished.** **Extensions**: **9, 11, 13** —
⚠️ **which are just continued stacking of thirds, and which become the primary vocabulary
in jazz** (§17 → `music-analysis-jazz-popular-non-western-and-meaning`).
**Inversions and figured bass** — ⚠️ **figured bass is worth understanding because it
reveals that Baroque musicians thought in terms of intervals above a bass line, not
"chords" as objects.** **The chord-as-object concept is a later abstraction (Rameau).**
**Voicing, spacing, doubling** — ⚠️ **the same chord can sound completely different
depending on register and spacing, and close low voicings turn muddy because of critical
bands** (§3 → `music-claims-harmonic-series-psychoacoustics-and-tuning`).

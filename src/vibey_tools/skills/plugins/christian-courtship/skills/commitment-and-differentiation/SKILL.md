# Commitment and Differentiation of Self

## Overview
This skill operationalizes modern empirical relationship science within Christian courtship. It synthesizes Scott Stanley and Howard Markman's commitment theory with Murray Bowen's Family Systems Theory, enabling couples and pastoral counselors to diagnose relational inertia, prevent emotional fusion, and measure genuine personal dedication.

## Invocation Triggers
Invoke this skill when:
- Assessing the balance between dedication commitment and constraint commitment.
- Detecting relationship sliding versus conscious decision-making.
- Diagnosing emotional fusion, enmeshment, or emotional cutoff.
- Establishing an autonomous "I-position" grounded in Christ.
- Evaluating a couple's capacity for conflict resolution and emotional self-regulation.

## Input Schema
```yaml
empirical_assessment:
  dedication_score: float (0.0 to 10.0) # partner priority, we-identity, long-term intent
  constraint_score: float (0.0 to 10.0) # external exit costs, financial ties, social pressure
  cohabitation_status: boolean
  differentiation_level: string (low_fusion | low_cutoff | moderate | high_differentiation)
  triangulation_risk: boolean
```

## Key Empirical Models

### 1. The Two Springs of Commitment (Stanley and Markman)
Commitment operates via two distinct mechanical forces:
- Dedication Commitment: The internal motivation to build a shared future, prioritize partner welfare, cultivate an identity as "we," and make intentional sacrifices.
- Constraint Commitment: The structural barriers making relationship exit costly, including financial debts, shared apartment leases, family pressure, and social stigma.

### 2. The Inertia Principle: Sliding versus Deciding
- Sliding: Couples gradually slide into increased co-dependency, cohabitation, and shared assets based on convenience or convenience without explicit commitment decisions. Constraints accumulate before dedication is tested, trapping fragile unions.
- Deciding: Couples make deliberate, conscious transitions at each threshold, verifying character and establishing dedication prior to accumulating external constraints.

### 3. Differentiation of Self (Murray Bowen)
Differentiation measures the capacity to balance emotional reactivity with intellectual reflection:
- High Differentiation: Maintaining an autonomous, Christ-centered "I-position" while sustaining deep, warm connection with a romantic partner. High capacity to remain calm during disagreement.
- Low Differentiation (Fusion): Emotional boundaries dissolve; an individual takes on the partner's anxiety, feeling compelled to control or pacify them to soothe internal panic.
- Low Differentiation (Cutoff): Emotional defensiveness resulting in stone-walling, ghosting, and physical isolation to escape relational tension.

## Operational Checklist
- [ ] Ensure dedication commitment leads relational progression at every state transition.
- [ ] Maintain separate living quarters and finances during courtship to keep exit constraints low until wedding vows.
- [ ] Verify that both partners can articulate personal convictions calmly during disagreements.
- [ ] Identify and eliminate triangulation (bringing third parties into personal disputes to manage anxiety).

## Failure Modes
- Constraint Trapping: Accumulating shared leases or mutual loans before confirming marital intent.
- Anxiety-Driven Fusion: Sacrificing moral convictions or personal boundaries to preserve immediate peace.
- Defensive Cutoff: Walking away from constructive hard conversations due to low emotional stamina.

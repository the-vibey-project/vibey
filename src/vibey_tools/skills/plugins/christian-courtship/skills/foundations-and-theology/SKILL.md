# Foundations and Theology of Christian Courtship

## Overview
This skill operationalizes the historical and dogmatic lineages of Christian marital theology. It equips counselors, pastors, suitors, and candidates to evaluate marital candidates using the complementary insights of the three major historic Christian traditions.

## Invocation Triggers
Invoke this skill when:
- Establishing theological criteria for evaluating courtship candidates.
- Comparing contractual exchange against covenantal commitment.
- Analyzing Augustine's three marital goods or Aquinas's efficient cause of marriage.
- Applying John Calvin's public consistory oversight or Martin Luther's creation estate.
- Implementing the Eastern Orthodox theology of martyrdom crowns and the domestic church.

## Theological Principles

### 1. Orthodox Protestant Tradition
- Martin Luther (*The Estate of Marriage*, 1522): Marriage is an honorable estate of creation ordained by God for mutual companionship, procreation, and protection against sin.
- John Calvin (*Genevan Marriage Ordinances*, 1545): Marriage is a sacred covenant requiring mutual consent, public witness, parental counsel, and pastoral consistory accountability.
- Westminster Confession of Faith (Chapter 24): Lifelong monogamous covenant between one man and one woman in the Lord. Lawful dissolution occurs solely upon adultery (Matthew 19:9) or willful desertion by an unbelieving spouse (1 Corinthians 7:15).

### 2. Roman Catholic Tradition
- Thomas Aquinas (*Summa Theologiae* Supplement, questions 41 through 68): Efficient cause of marriage is mutual consent in words of the present tense (*per verba de praesenti*), distinguished from forward betrothal promises (*sponsalia de futuro*).
- Augustine's Three Goods: *bonum prolis* (children and godly education), *bonum fidei* (chaste mutual fidelity), and *bonum sacramenti* (indissoluble sign of Christ and Church).
- Karol Wojtyla (Pope John Paul II, *Theology of the Body*): Personalistic norm; persons must be loved as subjects of intrinsic worth, achieving self-realization solely through a sincere gift of self (*communio personarum*).

### 3. Eastern Greek Orthodox Tradition
- Mystery of Crowning (*Stephana*): Crowns symbolize royal victory over personal passions and the crown of martyrdom: dying to self-will for the other person (Ephesians 5:25).
- The Domestic Church (*Ecclesiola*): The home operates as an outpost of the Kingdom of God.
- Ascetical Discipline: Spiritual sobriety (*nepsis*) and discernment (*diakrisis*). Pastoral economy (*oikonomia*) recognizes human tragedy while upholding the pristine divine standard.

## Operational Checklist
- [ ] Confirm shared confessional orthodoxy and active local church membership.
- [ ] Confirm that both parties define marriage as an indissoluble, lifelong covenant before God.
- [ ] Verify mutual agreement to submit courtship decisions to pastoral and parental counsel.
- [ ] Reject all consumerist dating assumptions that treat marriage as a temporary contract.

## Failure Modes
- Consumerist Contractualism: Treating marriage as a conditional trade subject to exit when feelings fade.
- Clandestine Romance: Hiding courtship from parents, pastors, and church elders.
- Theological Mismatch: Attempting to build a covenant with someone who rejects essential Christian dogma.

## §4. Protein and Amino Acids

**⚠️ Nine essential amino acids; ⚠️ protein QUALITY reflects amino acid profile and
digestibility (DIAAS has largely superseded PDCAAS).**
**⚠️ Functions**: **structural, enzymatic, transport, immune, and ⚠️ a minor energy
substrate — protein is not primarily fuel.**
**⚠️ The "complete protein" myth**: ⚠️ **you do not need complementary proteins at the same
meal.** **The body maintains an amino acid pool; combining across the day is sufficient,
and this correction is decades old but still repeated wrongly.**
**⚠️ Reference intakes** (⚠️ **the RDA of ~0.8 g/kg/day is a population-level minimum to
prevent deficiency, NOT an optimum**) — ⚠️ **and the research literature on older adults
and on resistance training generally supports higher intakes than the RDA** (see an
exercise physiology reference).
**⚠️ Protein and kidneys**: ⚠️ **higher protein intake is not established as harmful to
healthy kidneys; it does matter in existing kidney disease, which is where the concern
originated.**
**⚠️ Plant vs animal**: ⚠️ **plant proteins are generally lower in leucine and less
digestible, which is addressable by quantity and variety.**

## §5. Carbohydrates and Fibre

```
SIMPLE  monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose), disaccharides
COMPLEX  starch (amylose, amylopectin), ⚠️ RESISTANT STARCH (behaves
   like fibre; ⚠️ cooking and COOLING starchy foods increases it)
⚠️ FIBRE  soluble (viscous — cholesterol and glycaemic effects) vs
   insoluble (bulk and transit). ⚠️ FERMENTABLE fibre feeds the
   microbiome and produces short-chain fatty acids (§14)
⚠️ GLYCAEMIC INDEX  ⚠️ a property measured in isolation, on a fixed
   carbohydrate dose, with wide between-person variability.
   ⚠️ GI of a single food poorly predicts the response to a MEAL,
   which is why GI-based advice has underperformed
```
**⚠️ Fructose**: ⚠️ **metabolized largely in the liver; the concern is dose and context.**
**⚠️ Fruit is not the problem — the fibre, water and volume make overconsumption difficult;
sugar-sweetened beverages deliver the same sugar with no satiety** (§13 → `nutrition-digestion-energy-balance-appetite-and-food-processing`).
**⚠️ Fibre is one of the better-supported nutrients** — ⚠️ **and intake is well below
recommended levels in most Western populations.**

## §6. Fats and Lipids

```
SATURATED (SFA) · MONOUNSATURATED (MUFA) · POLYUNSATURATED (PUFA)
⚠️ TRANS  ⚠️ industrial trans fats are the clearest dietary harm ever
   established, and their near-elimination is a real public health win
⚠️ OMEGA-3  ALA (plant) → EPA/DHA conversion is POOR in humans;
   marine sources supply EPA/DHA directly
OMEGA-6  ⚠️ linoleic acid. ⚠️ The "omega-6 causes inflammation" claim
   is much weaker than commonly stated — trials generally do not
   show the predicted inflammatory effect
⚠️ CHOLESTEROL  ⚠️ DIETARY cholesterol has modest effects on SERUM
   cholesterol for most people. ⚠️ This was the basis for removing
   the specific dietary cholesterol limit from guidelines
LIPOPROTEINS  ⚠️ LDL-C, HDL-C, ⚠️ ApoB and Lp(a) as better markers
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — saturated fat is the genuinely contested case, and both camps overstate.**
> ⚠️ **The evidence that saturated fat raises LDL-C is strong.** **⚠️ The evidence on hard
> outcomes depends entirely on the REPLACEMENT (§1 → `nutrition-epistemics-study-design-and-measurement`): replacing SFA with PUFA appears
> beneficial; with refined carbohydrate, not.** **⚠️ Meta-analyses disagree, partly by which
> substitutions they model.**
> **⚠️ Meanwhile "saturated fat was never a problem" overstates the revisionist case,
> which is why §24.2 → `nutrition-live-evidence-and-reading-a-claim`'s guidelines retained the 10% limit while simultaneously promoting
> foods high in it — a contradiction critics have flagged.**

## §7. Alcohol

**⚠️ Ethanol provides ~7 kcal/g; metabolized via alcohol dehydrogenase to acetaldehyde
(⚠️ a carcinogen and the cause of the flushing response in people with ALDH2 variants).**
**⚠️ The J-curve is the important reversal**: ⚠️ **older observational studies suggested
moderate drinkers had lower mortality than abstainers.** **⚠️ Subsequent analyses found
the abstainer group contaminated by "sick quitters" — people who stopped drinking because
of illness — and Mendelian randomization studies generally do not support a protective
effect.** ⚠️ **The current scientific direction is that there is no clearly established
level of alcohol that improves health, and cancer risk rises from low levels.**
**⚠️ Note §24.2 → `nutrition-live-evidence-and-reading-a-claim`'s guidelines weakened the specific numeric limits, which is contested.**

## §8. Vitamins

**⚠️ Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K — stored, and ⚠️ toxicity is possible) vs water-soluble
(B complex, C — excess largely excreted, with exceptions like B6 neuropathy at high
doses).**
**⚠️ Notable specifics:**
- ⚠️ **Vitamin D is the most-studied and most-disappointing supplement of recent decades:
  strong observational associations, and large RCTs (VITAL and others) largely failing to
  show benefit for the outcomes predicted.** ⚠️ **Low vitamin D may be substantially a
  MARKER of poor health rather than a cause.** **Deficiency is real and treatable; the
  supplement-everyone hypothesis did not hold.**
- ⚠️ **B12 is genuinely required from animal sources or supplements** — **a real concern
  for vegans, and deficiency causes irreversible neurological damage.**
- ⚠️ **Folate fortification is one of public health nutrition's clearest successes**, **and
  ⚠️ folic acid can mask B12 deficiency, which is why the interaction matters.**
- ⚠️ **Vitamin A toxicity from retinol is real; beta-carotene is not equivalent.**

## §9. Minerals

**⚠️ Macrominerals (calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride) and trace
(iron, zinc, iodine, selenium, copper, manganese, chromium, molybdenum, fluoride).**
```
⚠️ IRON  heme (animal, well absorbed) vs non-heme (plant, poorly
   absorbed; ⚠️ vitamin C enhances, phytates and tannins inhibit).
   ⚠️ Deficiency is the most common nutrient deficiency globally;
   ⚠️ excess is harmful and iron is not a supplement to take casually
⚠️ SODIUM/POTASSIUM  ⚠️ the RATIO matters, and most populations are
   high sodium and low potassium. ⚠️ Sodium reduction lowers blood
   pressure; the magnitude of mortality benefit is contested
⚠️ CALCIUM  ⚠️ supplementation shows weaker fracture benefit than long
   assumed, with some cardiovascular signals debated
⚠️ IODINE  ⚠️ deficiency remains the leading preventable cause of
   intellectual disability worldwide; salt iodization is a triumph
⚠️ ZINC and SELENIUM  narrow windows between deficiency and toxicity
```

## §10. ⚠️ Supplements

> **⚠️ The general finding across decades: supplements correct DEFICIENCIES well and
> produce disappointing results in replete populations.**
```
⚠️ GOOD EVIDENCE  folate in pregnancy · B12 where intake is absent ·
   vitamin D where deficient · iron where deficient · iodine ·
   ⚠️ specific clinical situations (bariatric surgery, malabsorption)
⚠️ MIXED / WEAK  omega-3 for cardiovascular prevention · multivitamins
   in well-nourished people · most single antioxidants
⚠️ EVIDENCE OF HARM  ⚠️ beta-carotene in smokers · high-dose vitamin E ·
   ⚠️ antioxidants around training may blunt adaptation
```
**⚠️ The regulatory reality in the US and many jurisdictions**: ⚠️ **supplements are not
approved for safety or efficacy before sale, and independent testing repeatedly finds
products that do not contain the stated amount, or that contain undeclared
pharmaceuticals.** **⚠️ Third-party certification is the practical mitigation.**
**⚠️ Interactions are real** — ⚠️ **St John's wort induces drug metabolism, vitamin K
interacts with warfarin, and high-dose supplements can interfere with medication.**

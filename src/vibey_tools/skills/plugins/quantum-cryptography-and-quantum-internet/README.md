# Quantum Cryptography, Quantum Encryption and the Quantum Internet Plugin

Quantum cryptography and the quantum internet, starting from the distinction that most coverage gets wrong: quantum key distribution and post-quantum cryptography are two completely different things solving different problems. Qubits and measurement, the no-cloning theorem, entanglement and Bell inequalities, channels and loss; BB84, entanglement-based and continuous-variable QKD, and turning raw detections into a usable key; what unconditional security actually claims, the attacks that work on real hardware, device-independent approaches and the trusted-node problem; quantum repeaters, memory, entanglement distribution and satellite QKD; then the official position, quantum random number generation, the quantum computing threat, and how to choose.

One reference, split into 6 skills along its section groups so a task loads only the part it needs. Section numbers (§N) are shared across the set and cross-references into a sibling skill are written as §N → `skill`. Reference, not tutorial: sections are independent, every claim is tagged by how durable it is (stable fundamentals vs. versioned specifics vs. genuinely contested questions), and a currency snapshot (checked August 2026) flags what goes stale first.

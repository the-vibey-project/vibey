# Physical Security, Self-Defense, Firearms and the ATF Substances Plugin

People and buildings, force and law, and how alcohol, tobacco, cannabis and firearms actually
intersect with medicine and self-defense.

One reference, split into 8 skills, so a task loads only the part it needs.

**Why these four share one reference**, in the source's §0: federal law has historically grouped
them — the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives exists because all of them were
originally regulated through excise taxes. **And its answer** to how they "work" for medicine and
for self-defense:

> The honest answer is lopsided: firearms have a real self-defense role and essentially no medical
> one; alcohol, tobacco and cannabis have narrow, specific medical roles (mostly not in the forms
> people consume recreationally) and essentially no self-defense role — but they matter enormously
> to self-defense **legally and physiologically**, because intoxication changes both what you can do
> and what the law will forgive.

And the first of the five ideas that organize everything else: **most harm is prevented, not
repelled.** Awareness, avoidance, hardening and being findable beat any fighting skill or weapon.

## ⚠️ What this reference is and is not

**Every skill opens with the source's own limits**, because any one of them may be loaded alone:
**not legal advice** — self-defense law is fact-specific; talk to a criminal-defense attorney before
you need one; **not medical advice** — no dosing, no regimens, and where a medical use exists it
runs through a clinician; **not a substitute for hands-on training** — firearms handling, medical
skills and physical self-defense cannot be learned from text.

**Firearms material is conceptual and safety-oriented, exactly as the source gives it.** §14
explains how a cartridge and the action types work in outline and why shot placement matters more
than caliber; §15–§16 are safety rules, storage, the risk ledger, training pathways and what to do
after an incident. The pack adds nothing below the source's level of detail: no instruction on
modifying or manufacturing a firearm or loading ammunition appears in any of its eight skills, and
the defensive guidance in §16 is the source's own, word for word.

**Safe storage and crisis access are carried as load-bearing.** The source's risk ledger (§15) puts
suicide at 62% of US gun deaths in 2024 **\[STRONG\]** and recommends locked storage whenever a gun
is not on your body and a pre-agreed temporary off-site storage plan for anyone in the household in
crisis. The foundations, personal-security, both firearms, substances and decision-tools skills
open with the source's crisis lines, and `physsec-reference` lists them in §27: the **988** Suicide &
Crisis Lifeline, the National DV Hotline **1-800-799-7233** and the SAMHSA National Helpline
**1-800-662-4357**.

**The law is dated and local.** Everything tagged **\[LAW-2026\]** is the legal state as of
September 2026 and must be verified before relying on it — and where the source states law without
the tag (pepper-spray legality in §12, the *Hemani* block in §20, intoxication in §21, the sale age
in §22), a pack note says the same. The only state whose law the reference sets out is **South
Carolina**. The source's own list of what will go stale first — marijuana rescheduling,
§ 922(g)(3) after *Hemani*, the NFA registration litigation, the hemp THC cap, cytisinicline, SC
medical cannabis, alcohol guidance — is §29 in `physsec-reference`.

**Contested questions stay contested.** Defensive-gun-use frequency, permitless carry and crime,
stand-your-ground laws and medical cannabis for chronic pain are presented with both sides and the
source's evidence grade, and the pack takes no side the source does not.

## Source defects flagged rather than silently repaired

Five of the source's internal pointers name the wrong section. Each is left as the source wrote it,
with a pack note naming the section that actually holds the material:

| Where | The source says | The material is at |
|---|---|---|
| Compiled-on note | sources "listed in §12" | §30 → `physsec-reference` |
| §0 | substances: "Part V covers all of that" | Part VI, §19–§23 → `physsec-alcohol-tobacco-and-cannabis` (Part V is firearms) |
| §6, Tier 0 | commercial tourniquet "(§11)" | §13 → `physsec-personal-security-and-self-defense` |
| §9 | SC's self-defense statute "(§16)" | §18 → `physsec-firearms-and-self-defense-law` |
| §9 | secure storage "(§14)" | §15 → `physsec-firearms-mechanics-safety-and-carry` |

Two within-source tensions, and one gap between the source's method note and its body, are carried
with a note rather than resolved:

- **§9 paraphrases the protective-order presumption as intent "to harm"**; §18 states the statute's
  own terms (§ 16-11-440(E)): intent "to commit an unlawful act". The pack note at §9 sends the
  reader to §18.
- **§19's summary table says the firearm prohibition "changed substantially in June 2026"**; §20 is
  the detail and stresses that *Hemani* is narrow — § 922(g)(3) was not struck down. The pack note
  at the table says to read §20 before relying on the summary.
- **The method note says industry-funded findings, including "TASER effectiveness from the
  manufacturer", are marked as such.** The alarm-deterrence finding is (§5); §12's TASER-vs-OC
  comparison carries no such mark and is graded **\[MODERATE\]**, sourced in §30 to *Criminal Justice
  Policy Review* via a Force Science summary. Whether it is the figure the method note means, the
  source does not say; pack notes at §12 and at the method note say so.

Four smaller inconsistencies are left as printed, because none changes a fact the reference relies
on: §27 cites RAND's *Gun Policy in America* (5th edition) where §30 lists *The Science of Gun
Policy* (4th ed. 2024; 5th ed.); §8 and §27 say IS-907 where §30 says IS-907.A; Part VIII attributes
the "~3 in 10" cannabis-use-disorder figure to the JAMA review, where §20 says it came from a
meta-analysis the review cites; and §0's list of layers has "respond" where §3 has "Defend/Respond".

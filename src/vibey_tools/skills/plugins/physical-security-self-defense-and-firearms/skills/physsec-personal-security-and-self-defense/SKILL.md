## §9 Personal security

**Awareness without paranoia.** Popular frameworks (e.g., "Cooper's color codes," the OODA loop) are useful **teaching heuristics**, not physiological laws. What's robust from cognitive psychology is humbling: **inattentional blindness and change blindness are real and large** — people miss obvious events when attending elsewhere, and eyewitness confidence is a poor indicator of accuracy. Practical takeaways:

- Phones down in transitional spaces (parking lots, doorways, ATMs, gas stations).
- Notice exits and **baselines** (what's normal here?) so anomalies stand out.
- Trust the "something's off" feeling enough to create distance — avoidance costs nothing.

**The "known person" majority.** Because most violence comes from acquaintances and partners:

- **Domestic violence / stalking safety planning** is a core personal-security skill: documentation, safe contacts, go-bag, changed routines, protective orders, trained advocates (National DV Hotline: 1-800-799-7233). The most dangerous period is often **around separation**. SC's self-defense statute explicitly presumes intent to harm when someone forcibly enters in violation of a protective order (§16). **\[Pack note: the source's pointer says §16; the South Carolina self-defense statute is §18 → `physsec-firearms-and-self-defense-law`, which states this presumption (§ 16-11-440(E)) as intent "to commit an unlawful act" rather than intent to harm — read §18 for the statute's own terms.\]**
- **Stalking/doxxing countermeasures**: data-broker opt-outs, separate mailing address (PO box/virtual mailbox), audit location-sharing on phones and apps, check for unknown Bluetooth trackers (both iOS and Android now alert on unknown AirTag-type trackers), lock down social media, and consider your state's address-confidentiality program if eligible.

**Vehicles.** Lock doors while driving in slow traffic; leave space to pull out when stopped behind a car; don't sit in parked cars absorbed in your phone; if carjacked at gunpoint, **give up the car** — property is replaceable.

**Rideshare / dating / meetups.** Verify vehicle and driver; share trip status; meet first in public; tell someone where you're going and when you'll check in. (The survival literature's single highest-value action — *tell someone your plan and when to raise the alarm* — applies here too.)

**Travel.** Research local crime patterns (the State Dept. travel advisories are a start), keep a low profile, carry a door wedge/portable lock for hotels, know hotel exits, split your money and cards.

**Children.** Teach "safe adults," body autonomy, and that secrets from parents are never required. For firearms: **don't rely on teaching kids not to touch guns** — controlled studies found children who'd received safety instruction frequently handled real guns anyway when they found one. Secure storage is the control that works (§14). **\[Pack note: the source's pointer says §14; storage is §15 → `physsec-firearms-mechanics-safety-and-carry`.\]**

**Executive-protection principles scale down to families:** advance planning, routine variation for high-risk people, low profile, a "cover and evacuate" mindset (not "fight"), and an established medical plan.

## §10 The hierarchy

**Avoid → escape → de-escalate → comply (property) → less-lethal → physical force → deadly force.**

Every step down that ladder increases legal, physical and psychological cost. Most "fights" are avoidable; most robberies end without injury when property is surrendered; many confrontations are **ego contests** — winning them is losing.

**Verbal de-escalation (works across contexts):**

- Calm, low voice; open palms ("fence" position — hands up, open, between you and them, which also protects you).
- Acknowledge feelings without conceding facts ("I can see you're angry").
- Give them a face-saving exit; don't corner people.
- Create distance and a barrier; keep moving toward exits.
- Recognize **pre-attack indicators**: target glancing (looking around for witnesses), grooming gestures, blading the body, clenching fists, closing distance, sudden calm after agitation, a hand hidden.

## §11 What the evidence says about training

- **Empowerment self-defense works — and it's one of the best-evidenced interventions in this whole document.** The Enhanced Assess, Acknowledge, Act (EAAA) program — \~12 hours covering risk detection, verbal and physical resistance — was tested in a randomized trial of **893 first-year university women** (Senn et al., *NEJM* 2015): one-year completed rape fell from roughly **9.8% to 5.2%**, attempted rape fell even more, with effects lasting at least two years. **\[STRONG\]** Physical and verbal resistance skills plus risk *recognition* (not just fighting) is the active ingredient.
- **Martial arts**: favor systems with **live, resisting practice (sparring)** — boxing, Muay Thai, wrestling, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, judo — because skills that are never pressure-tested fail under adrenaline. Systems marketed as "too deadly to spar" can't verify that anything works. Reality-based systems (e.g., Krav Maga) vary enormously by gym; judge by how much resisting practice they do. **\[MODERATE — expert consensus, little RCT evidence\]**
- **Stress physiology** is real: tunnel vision, auditory exclusion, time distortion, loss of fine motor control, memory gaps. Specific heart-rate thresholds popularized in some training literature are **\[CONTESTED/WEAK\]**, but the direction — gross motor skills and trained responses survive; complex fine-motor plans fail — is well supported. Train simple, repeat often, and add stress inoculation.
- **Ground-fighting reality**: a large share of real fights go to the ground; basic grappling defense (getting up) is high-value.

## §12 Less-lethal tools

| Tool | Evidence and practicalities |
| --- | --- |
| **OC (pepper) spray** | Police data shows it's usually effective but not reliable against highly resistant or impaired subjects. Baltimore County's early field test found incapacitation in \~90% of 174 confrontations; a later retrospective study of 936 incidents found it reduced violence in 93% of cases but often worked only within \~2 m and after several seconds of spraying, with frequent cross-contamination of officers. A department-level comparison found TASERs effective \~90% of the time versus \~74% for OC. **\[Pack note: the source's method note says TASER effectiveness figures from the manufacturer are marked as such; this comparison carries no such mark, and §30 sources it to *Criminal Justice Policy Review* via a Force Science summary. The source does not say whether this is the figure its method note means.\]** **\[MODERATE\]** Practical: choose a stream or gel (less wind blowback), practice with inert trainers, replace every 2–4 years, and plan to **spray and move**. |
| **Conducted energy weapons (TASER-type)** | Probe-based devices incapacitate via neuromuscular interference when both probes connect with adequate spread; misses and thick clothing defeat them; one shot at a time. Contact "stun guns" rely on pain compliance and are far less effective. Legal status varies by state and city. |
| **Impact tools (batons, etc.)** | Legally treated closer to weapons; poor retention; require training. |
| **Knives** | Legally and morally a deadly-force tool, not a "less-lethal" one; carrying one "for defense" invites deadly-force scrutiny. |
| **Flashlight** | Underrated: identify threats (and non-threats), disorient, signal. |

**Legality check:** South Carolina generally permits adults to carry pepper spray and similar devices, but federal buildings, schools, airports and private policies restrict them. Verify local rules before carrying anything. **\[Pack note: a legal statement as of September 2026 that the source does not tag LAW-2026; its §0 definition of that tag applies — verify before relying on it.\]**

## §13 Medical self-defense: Stop the Bleed

Uncontrolled bleeding is a leading cause of preventable trauma death and can kill in minutes — before EMS arrives. Evidence for tourniquets:

- A Baghdad combat-hospital study found near-complete survival when tourniquets were applied **before shock** set in; casualties who needed but didn't get tourniquets did not survive. **\[MODERATE\]**
- In civilian US trauma centers (Texas Tourniquet Study Group), prehospital tourniquets were independently associated with roughly **six-fold lower odds of death** in peripheral vascular injury, without more amputations. **\[MODERATE\]**
- Systematic reviews rate civilian evidence as low-quality but **consistently favorable**, with few, transient harms. **\[MODERATE\]**

Action: take a **Stop the Bleed** course (\~1–2 hours, often free), carry a commercial windlass tourniquet (buy from reputable sellers — counterfeits fail), and keep one in the car, home and range bag. Anyone who carries a firearm should carry a tourniquet.

## §24 Personal security plan (fill-in template)

1. **Threat inventory**: Who might realistically harm me or my household? (Include known people, not just strangers.)
2. **Life safety**: smoke/CO alarms tested? Escape plan practiced? Tourniquet + kit present?
3. **Home**: doors (frame screws, Grade 1–2 deadbolts), sliders, garage, windows (with egress), lighting, alarm armed daily, cameras secured.
4. **Safe room**: which room, what's in it, who goes there, who calls 911.
5. **Communications**: emergency contacts, check-in habits, location sharing (with whom — and only with whom).
6. **Mobility**: vehicle habits, parking, rideshare/meetup protocol, travel plan.
7. **Digital-to-physical**: data-broker opt-outs, tracker checks, social media exposure.
8. **Self-defense**: training plan (awareness, de-escalation, empowerment self-defense, grappling basics), chosen tools, legal knowledge.
9. **Firearms (if any)**: storage for every gun at every moment; household access rules; crisis off-site storage plan; training schedule; attorney contact; no-impairment rule.
10. **Review cadence**: every 6 months, or after any life change (move, breakup, new job, new family member, threat).

## §25 Spending priorities (rough expected-value order for a typical US household)

1. Smoke/CO alarms, extinguishers, escape plan
2. Door/frame reinforcement and good deadbolts — **and using them**
3. Lighting and landscaping for sightlines
4. Stop the Bleed training + tourniquet/first-aid kit
5. Monitored alarm with cellular backup
6. Empowerment self-defense / awareness training
7. Cameras (secured) and a video doorbell
8. Safe room hardening
9. Firearms — *if chosen* — **only with** secure storage, training, and legal preparation budgeted alongside the gun

## §26 Organizational checklist

- Risk assessment on file and reviewed annually
- BTAM team and anonymous reporting channel
- Access control with anti-tailgating culture and visitor management
- Emergency action plan: Run/Hide/Fight training, lockdown hardware compliant with egress codes
- Stop the Bleed kits with AEDs; staff trained
- Periodic authorized physical penetration test with a findings-to-fixes loop
- Liaison with local police, fire and EMS; floor plans shared
- Post-incident recovery plan (EAP, communications, continuity)

## Part VIII — Contested Questions (Both Sides)

| Question | One side | Other side | Where evidence sits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Does owning a gun make a household safer? | DGUs prevent crimes and injuries; many uses involve no shots fired | Access raises suicide, domestic-homicide and accident risk | Risk side **\[STRONG\]** for suicide; benefit side **\[CONTESTED\]** in magnitude. Net effect depends heavily on who lives in the house. |
| How many DGUs per year? | Millions (Kleck-style surveys) | Tens of thousands (NCVS) | **\[CONTESTED\]**; measurement problem unresolved. |
| Permitless/shall-issue carry and crime | Deters criminals; lawful carriers rarely offend | RAND rates evidence as supportive that expanded carry is associated with more violent crime/homicide | **\[CONTESTED\]**; methodological disputes persist (e.g., Lott vs. Donohue lines of research). |
| Stand-your-ground laws | Protect victims from second-guessing | Associated with more firearm homicides | RAND: supportive evidence of increased homicides; **\[CONTESTED\]** |
| Medical cannabis for chronic pain | Patients report relief; opioid-sparing hopes | 2025 JAMA review: insufficient evidence; \~3 in 10 medical users meet CUD criteria | Leaning **insufficient** for most uses |
| Moderate drinking and health | Older cohort data showed lower heart disease | Confounding (sick quitters); causal cancer risk at low doses | Leaning **no net benefit** |
| Nicotine pouches | Big harm reduction for switchers | Youth uptake; limited long-term data | FDA judged net benefit for adult switchers; **\[MODERATE\]** |
| Martial arts for self-defense | Confidence and skill | Sport rules ≠ street; weapons and multiple attackers | Pressure-tested training + avoidance skills best supported |

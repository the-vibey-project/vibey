>    political concepts — democracy, power, legitimacy, polarization — are hard to measure,

## §1. ⚠️ What Political Science Can and Cannot Establish

```
⚠️ THE RELIABILITY GRADIENT
   ⚠️ ROBUST  Duverger-type effects of electoral rules on party
      systems · incumbency advantage · the strength of partisan
      identity as a predictor · ⚠️ the DEMOCRATIC PEACE as a
      statistical regularity between established democracies ·
      collective action logic · Arrow's theorem (⚠️ a PROOF, not
      a finding)
   ⚠️ REASONABLY SUPPORTED  modernization correlates with democracy
      (⚠️ the causal direction is disputed) · resource curse ·
      proportional systems produce more parties and more coalitions ·
      institutional veto points slow policy change
   ⚠️ CONTESTED  causes of democratization and backsliding ·
      whether polarization is elite-led or mass-led · media effects
      on vote choice · the magnitude of most campaign effects ·
      ⚠️ how much of measured democratic decline is real (§26)
   ⚠️ NORMATIVE, not empirical  what makes authority legitimate ·
      how to weigh liberty against equality · what a state owes
      non-citizens · ⚠️ these are ARGUMENTS, and evidence
      constrains them without settling them
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the field's central methodological problem is that YOU CANNOT RANDOMIZE
> COUNTRIES.** ⚠️ **There is no control group for a constitution, and n is small, cases are
> interdependent, and the interesting variables are endogenous to each other.**
> **⚠️ This is why political science leans on natural experiments, regression discontinuity,
> comparative case studies and formal theory — and why confident causal claims about
> national-level outcomes should be treated cautiously** (§25 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`).

**⚠️ The subfields**: **political theory (normative), comparative politics, international
relations, public administration and policy, methods, and — depending on the department —
political economy.**

# PART I — FOUNDATIONS

## §2. Power, Authority and Legitimacy

**⚠️ Power has more than one face, and the distinction matters:**
```
⚠️ FIRST FACE (Dahl)  A gets B to do what B would otherwise not do
⚠️ SECOND FACE (Bachrach & Baratz)  ⚠️ AGENDA CONTROL — power to
   keep issues from ever being decided. ⚠️ "Non-decisions"
⚠️ THIRD FACE (Lukes)  ⚠️ shaping preferences themselves, so B
   doesn't want the thing that would serve B's interests
   ⚠️ Powerful and genuinely hard to operationalize — critics say
   it requires the analyst to know B's "real" interests better
   than B does, which is a serious objection
```
**⚠️ Authority is power regarded as legitimate**, ⚠️ **and Weber's three ideal types —
traditional, charismatic, legal-rational — remain the standard vocabulary.**
**⚠️ Legitimacy is a belief about rightfulness, not a synonym for legality or popularity**,
⚠️ **and it is what makes governing cheap: a state that must coerce every act of compliance
cannot afford to govern.**
**⚠️ The normative question of what MAKES authority legitimate** — ⚠️ **consent, procedural
fairness, output performance, divine mandate, tradition — is genuinely contested and this
document does not adjudicate it** (§4).

## §3. The State

**⚠️ Weber's definition remains the workhorse**: ⚠️ **an entity claiming a monopoly on the
legitimate use of physical force within a territory.** **⚠️ Note "claiming" and
"legitimate" — both are doing work, and many real states hold neither fully.**
**⚠️ Sovereignty**: ⚠️ **internal (supremacy within) and external (recognition without), and
the Westphalian model is a useful idealization that historians increasingly qualify.**
**⚠️ On state FORMATION, several accounts compete rather than one being settled:**
⚠️ **Tilly's "war made the state and the state made war" (⚠️ bellicist — extraction for
warfare built fiscal and administrative capacity); contractarian accounts; and accounts
emphasizing that the European path generalizes poorly, since post-colonial states inherited
borders and institutions without the same formation process.**
**⚠️ Failed, fragile and weak states** — ⚠️ **and the terminology is itself contested, with
critics arguing it measures deviation from a Western template rather than a functional
property.**

## §4. Theory Traditions, Briefly and Plurally

**⚠️ Presented as positions, not as a ranking.**
```
LIBERALISM  individual rights, limited government, consent
   (Locke, Mill, ⚠️ Rawls's justice as fairness)
CONSERVATISM  inherited institutions embody accumulated wisdom;
   scepticism of rationalist redesign (Burke, Oakeshott)
SOCIALISM / MARXISM  material relations and class structure as
   the underlying determinant of political forms
REPUBLICANISM  ⚠️ liberty as NON-DOMINATION rather than
   non-interference (Pettit) — a genuinely different concept
LIBERTARIANISM  strong property rights, minimal state (Nozick)
COMMUNITARIANISM  the self is constituted by community
   (Sandel, MacIntyre, Taylor)
⚠️ FEMINIST, POSTCOLONIAL and CRITICAL theory  ⚠️ argue that the
   above traditions systematically omitted whose experience
   counted as political
DELIBERATIVE DEMOCRACY  legitimacy from reasoned public
   deliberation (Habermas)
```
**⚠️ The enduring disputes are real and not resolvable by data**: ⚠️ **what liberty is; how
to weigh it against equality; whether justice is procedural or distributive; who counts as
part of the demos; and what obligations exist across borders and generations.**

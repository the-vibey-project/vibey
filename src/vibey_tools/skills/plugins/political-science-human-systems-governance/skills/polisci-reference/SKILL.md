> 1. **⚠️ COLLECTIVE ACTION PROBLEMS are the discipline's deepest structure** (§5–§6 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`).
>    **Individually rational behaviour producing collectively bad outcomes explains
>    government, taxation, regulation, unions, cartels and treaties — and Ostrom's work
>    showed the standard remedies are not the only ones.**
> 2. **⚠️ INSTITUTIONS SHAPE BEHAVIOUR, and the effects are often perverse** (§9–§15 → `polisci-constitutional-design-electoral-systems-and-institutions`, `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`).
>    **Electoral rules, veto points and federal structures produce predictable
>    consequences that designers frequently did not intend.**
> 3. **⚠️ MEASUREMENT IS THE FIELD'S BINDING CONSTRAINT** (§25 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`, §26). **Most interesting
>    political concepts — democracy, power, legitimacy, polarization — are hard to measure,
>    and disputes that look substantive are often measurement disputes underneath.**

## §26. What's Live — checked August 2026

> **⚠️ I've paired these two deliberately. The first is the headline empirical claim about
> the state of democracy; the second is the live methodological dispute about whether that
> claim's evidence base can bear the weight.** ⚠️ **Reading either alone gives a misleading
> picture.**

### 26.1 ⚠️ What the democracy indices currently report
**⚠️ Reported as V-Dem's findings, attributed, because this is measurement output rather
than uncontested fact** (§26.2).

- **⚠️ The V-Dem Institute (University of Gothenburg) published its 10th Democracy Report
  in March 2026, titled "Unraveling The Democratic Era?"** ⚠️ **It draws on over 32 million
  data points covering 202 countries from 1789 to 2025, using more than 4,200 experts to
  code over 600 attributes.**
- **⚠️ The headline claim**: ⚠️ **global democracy for the average world citizen has
  regressed to 1978 levels, which the report characterizes as effectively erasing most
  gains of the "third wave of democratization."**
- **⚠️ The distribution**: ⚠️ **92 autocracies against 87 democracies; a reported 74% of the
  world's population (about 6 billion) living in autocracies, and only 7% (about 600
  million) in liberal democracies.**
- **⚠️ Direction of travel**: ⚠️ **44 countries identified as currently autocratizing (up
  from 42), home to a record 41% of the world's population, against 18 democratizing
  accounting for 5%.** ⚠️ **Newly identified democratizers include Botswana, Guatemala and
  Mauritius; the ten new autocratizers named include Croatia, Italy, Slovakia, Slovenia,
  the United Kingdom and the United States.**
- **⚠️ Of the 44 autocratizers, V-Dem states 28 were democracies when the decline began and
  15 have already broken down into autocracy.**
- **⚠️ Freedom of expression is identified as the most deteriorating right, declining in 44
  countries in 2025, with media censorship described as the most widespread tool of
  backsliding.**
- **⚠️ On the United States specifically, V-Dem reports it has lost its long-term
  classification as a liberal democracy, attributing this to concentration of powers in the
  presidency, and reports legislative constraints losing a third of their value in 2025.**
  ⚠️ **I report this as the index's finding and its stated reasoning, not as an independent
  verdict.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — indices disagree with each other, which is itself informative.**
> ⚠️ **The Bertelsmann Transformation Index's 2026 regional report described "no pronounced
> trend" over its review period, with developments pointing to "either stagnation or
> continuity, depending on perspective," and no country experiencing a regime shift in
> either direction.** **⚠️ It separately reported that 56% of states under review are
> governed autocratically and two-thirds of countries assessed since 2006 are now less
> democratic than 20 years ago.**
> **⚠️ Different projects, different concepts, different coding rules, different pictures.
> Cite the index, not "democracy."**

### 26.2 ⚠️ The live dispute: is measured backsliding real?
**⚠️ A genuine, published, unresolved exchange in the discipline — and exactly the kind of
thing that gets lost when index findings are reported as plain fact.**

- **⚠️ THE CHALLENGE.** ⚠️ **Little and Meng, published in PS: Political Science & Politics
  (2024), argue that most backsliding studies rest largely or entirely on SUBJECTIVE
  indicators depending on expert coder judgement.** ⚠️ **They survey OBJECTIVE indicators —
  such as incumbent performance in elections — and report finding "little evidence of
  global democratic decline during the past decade."**
- **⚠️ Their proposed explanation for the discrepancy**: ⚠️ **either coder bias, or leaders
  using more subtle undemocratic methods that only subjective judgement detects.** ⚠️ **They
  state the simplest explanation is changing coder bias — while explicitly conceding they
  "cannot rule out" that decline is occurring only in ways requiring subjective judgement,
  and framing their claim as that the prevailing narrative "is not justified by existing
  evidence."**
- **⚠️ THE RESPONSES, which are substantive.** ⚠️ **V-Dem-affiliated scholars (Knutsen,
  Marquardt, Lindberg and colleagues) argue that subjectivity pervades ALL measurement
  including seemingly objective indicators, and present theoretical and empirical tests
  which they argue show it is "highly unlikely" that time-varying expert bias drives the
  observed declines.** ⚠️ **They further argue Little and Meng's chosen measures are
  ill-suited to the question.**
- **⚠️ A separate response makes what I think is the sharpest point**: ⚠️ **objective
  indicators like incumbent electoral performance fail to track democratic quality WITHIN
  democracies, and so cannot test the cases where backsliding is best documented.**
  ⚠️ **The author's image is looking for keys under the streetlight — searching where the
  light is rather than where the decline is.**
- **⚠️ Work has continued in both directions.** ⚠️ **Event-based datasets (the Democratic
  Erosion Event Dataset) attempt to resolve the dispute with coded events rather than
  ratings, and a 2025 study tested LLMs as democracy coders — finding they largely align
  with human experts but deviate in consistent directions, with some models systematically
  pessimistic and others optimistic.** ⚠️ **That result is a caution about substituting one
  judgement-based coder for another rather than a solution.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the honest position, and I want to state it carefully because it is easy
> to misuse in either direction.**
> ⚠️ **This dispute does NOT establish that backsliding is fictional. The critics
> themselves decline to claim that, and the responses raise serious objections to their
> method.**
> ⚠️ **Nor does it leave the indices untouched: the point that expert-coded measures could
> in principle move with elite and media zeitgeist is legitimate, was made in a leading
> disciplinary journal, and has not been dismissed.**
> **⚠️ The defensible reading is that the DIRECTION is supported by multiple independent
> lines of evidence, the MAGNITUDE is genuinely uncertain, and precise index figures should
> carry wider error bars than their decimal places imply** (§1 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory`, §25 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`).
> **⚠️ Anyone citing "democracy has fallen to 1978 levels" as a hard fact, and anyone citing
> Little and Meng as proof the whole thing is manufactured, is overreading their source.**

## §27. Misconceptions

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| Arrow's theorem proves voting is pointless | ⚠️ **It proves no rule satisfies all those axioms. It's about trade-offs** (§7 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| "The will of the majority" always exists | ⚠️ **Condorcet cycles mean it may not** (§7 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Some voting system is neutral | ⚠️ **Every rule embeds a theory of representation** (§8 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Strategic voting could be designed away | ⚠️ **Gibbard-Satterthwaite says no** (§7 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Ranked choice is monotonic | ⚠️ **IRV can fail monotonicity** (§8 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Duverger's Law is a law | ⚠️ **A strong tendency. India and Canada qualify it** (§8 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| The commons is inevitably a tragedy | ⚠️ **Ostrom documented durable self-governed commons** (§6 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Hardin described a managed commons | ⚠️ **He described OPEN ACCESS. Different thing** (§6 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Democracy means majority rule | ⚠️ **Liberal democracy also constrains majorities** (§9 → `polisci-constitutional-design-electoral-systems-and-institutions`, §13 → `polisci-constitutional-design-electoral-systems-and-institutions`) |
| Presidentialism is proven unstable | ⚠️ **Linz is influential and contested** (§11 → `polisci-constitutional-design-electoral-systems-and-institutions`) |
| Big diffuse majorities win in democracies | ⚠️ **Olson: concentrated interests organize better** (§5 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`) |
| Campaigns swing elections | ⚠️ **Field experiments find persuasion effects small** (§16 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Voters reward good policy | ⚠️ **They also punish droughts and shark attacks** (§16 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Economic voting is objective | ⚠️ **Partisans disagree about economic facts** (§16 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| The margin of error covers polling uncertainty | ⚠️ **Sampling only. Real uncertainty is wider** (§17 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| A 30% forecast that happens was wrong | ⚠️ **That's not how probabilities work** (§17 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Americans have become ideologically extreme | ⚠️ **Affective polarization is better evidenced than ideological** (§18 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Social media clearly caused polarization | ⚠️ **Large studies find mixed and smaller effects** (§18 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Implementation follows from legislation | ⚠️ **Street-level discretion makes policy** (§14 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`, §24 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`) |
| Targets improve public services | ⚠️ **Goodhart's Law. Often gamed** (§14 → `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`) |
| Corruption indices measure corruption | ⚠️ **Mostly they measure PERCEPTIONS** (§21 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`) |
| Wealth causes democratization | ⚠️ **Przeworski: it better predicts SURVIVAL** (§19 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`) |
| War happens when bargaining fails irrationally | ⚠️ **Information, commitment, indivisibility** (§23 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`) |
| Peacekeeping doesn't work | ⚠️ **Evidence is more favourable than the reputation** (§23 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`) |
| Democracy indices are objective measurement | ⚠️ **Mostly expert judgement, and that's disputed** (§26.2) |
| The backsliding critique proves decline is fake | ⚠️ **Its own authors decline to claim that** (§26.2) |
| All democracy indices agree | ⚠️ **V-Dem and BTI report different pictures** (§26.1) |

## §31. Method

**§1–§25 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory`, `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`, `polisci-constitutional-design-electoral-systems-and-institutions`, `polisci-bureaucracy-parties-voter-behaviour-and-polarization`, `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems` rests on the discipline's standard curriculum** — **collective action and social
choice theory, comparative institutional analysis, the behaviour literature, IR schools,
and the methodological self-criticism.** ⚠️ **The formal results (Arrow, Condorcet,
Gibbard-Satterthwaite) are proofs and need no verification; the empirical findings are
reported at the confidence §1 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory` assigns them.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, deliberately paired: ⚠️ **the current state of
democracy measurement, and the live scholarly dispute about whether those measurements are
trustworthy.** **⚠️ Reporting the first without the second would have been the easier file
and a worse one.**

**⚠️ A note on how I handled contested political questions.** ⚠️ **This document reports
what political scientists have found and where they disagree; it does not advance my own
positions on contested political or normative questions, and where a normative dispute is
live — judicial review, electoral system choice, federalism's merits, what legitimacy
requires — I have tried to give the strongest version of more than one side rather than
adjudicating.** ⚠️ **Where a finding involves a specific country's current politics, I have
attributed it to the source that produced it rather than asserting it in my own voice.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §5–§7 → `polisci-collective-action-commons-social-choice-and-voting`, which are the sections I'd most want read. ⚠️ **Collective
action logic explains an enormous amount of political structure, and Olson's asymmetry —
that concentrated interests organize more easily than diffuse ones — is the single most
useful thing here for making sense of why democratic outcomes so often disappoint
majorities.** ⚠️ **Ostrom's correction to Hardin belongs beside it, because "privatize or
nationalize" is a false binary that a great deal of real institutional design escapes.**

**High** in §1 → `polisci-what-the-field-establishes-power-the-state-and-theory` and §25 → `polisci-regimes-state-capacity-corruption-ir-and-field-problems`'s framing, which I'd defend as the honest position: ⚠️ **you cannot
randomize countries, the interesting variables are endogenous, and confident causal claims
about national outcomes deserve more scepticism than they usually get.**

**High** on §26.1's reporting of what V-Dem says, which comes from V-Dem's own report and
press release and is consistent across coverage: ⚠️ **the 2026 report's scale, the 1978
comparison, the 92/87 and 74%/7% figures, the 44 autocratizing versus 18 democratizing, and
the countries named.** ⚠️ **I have framed all of it as V-Dem's findings rather than as
established fact, and included BTI's notably different picture, because index disagreement
is itself evidence about how much weight any single number carries.**

**High** on §26.2's existence and content — ⚠️ **the Little and Meng article, the V-Dem-side
response, and the "streetlight" critique are all published in PS: Political Science &
Politics and are readable in full.** ⚠️ **I have taken care not to let either side stand as
the conclusion: the critics explicitly decline to claim decline is fictional, and the
responders raise real objections to using incumbent electoral performance to detect
within-democracy erosion.**
**⚠️ The position I'd defend is the one in the section: direction supported by multiple
lines of evidence, magnitude genuinely uncertain, and precise index figures carrying wider
error bars than their decimal places suggest.** ⚠️ **That is less satisfying than either
confident story, and I think it is what the evidence actually supports.**

>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ Adaptation is a response to a stress the body wasn't prepared for, followed by
>    recovery** (§8 → `exercise-training-principles-strength-endurance-and-recovery`). **No overload, no adaptation. No recovery, no adaptation either —
>    and the second half is where most people fail.**
> 2. **⚠️ Specificity dominates.** **You get better at what you do, in the range you do it,
>    at the speed you do it.** **Most "best exercise" arguments dissolve once you ask
>    "best for what?"**
> 3. **⚠️ Consistency over years beats optimization within a session by an enormous
>    margin** (§16 → `exercise-programming-periodization-and-aging`). **The programme you actually do is better than the optimal one you
>    don't.**

## §19. Fuelling

> **⚠️ This section describes physiology, not a prescription.** **Individual needs vary
> with size, activity, health status and goals, and anyone with a medical condition or a
> complicated relationship with food should work with a registered dietitian rather than
> a reference document** (§21).

**⚠️ Energy availability is the foundational concept** — **energy intake minus exercise
energy expenditure, relative to fat-free mass** — ⚠️ **and chronically low availability
drives the problems in §21 regardless of body composition.**
**⚠️ Protein**: ⚠️ **the research consensus for people training for strength or size sits
in roughly the 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day range, with limited additional benefit above that.**
**Distribution across meals appears to matter modestly; ⚠️ total daily intake matters far
more than timing.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the "anabolic window" is essentially a myth as popularly stated.**
> ⚠️ **The claim that protein must be consumed within 30–60 minutes post-training does not
> hold up; the practically relevant window is many hours wide, and total daily intake
> dominates.** **⚠️ It was a surrogate-endpoint conclusion** (§1 → `exercise-physiology-muscle-energy-and-body-systems`) — **built on acute
> muscle protein synthesis rather than measured long-term outcomes.**

**⚠️ Carbohydrate** — **the limiting fuel for high-intensity and prolonged work; glycogen
availability affects performance and training quality.** ⚠️ **Carbohydrate needs scale with
training volume and intensity, and low-carbohydrate approaches impair high-intensity
performance specifically.**
**⚠️ Fat** — **essential; very low intakes affect hormonal function** (§21).
**⚠️ Around-session fuelling matters most when sessions are long, frequent, or close
together** — **and matters very little for a single moderate session in a well-fed person.**

## §20. ⚠️ Supplements — Evidence Tiers

> **⚠️ The supplement industry is large, lightly regulated in the US (⚠️ marketed largely
> without pre-market efficacy or safety approval), and funds much of its own supporting
> research** (§1 → `exercise-physiology-muscle-energy-and-body-systems`). **⚠️ Contamination with banned substances is a documented and recurring
> problem — third-party testing (NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport) matters for
> anyone subject to testing.**

```
⚠️ STRONG EVIDENCE — small number of things
  CREATINE MONOHYDRATE  ⚠️ the best-evidenced ergogenic aid there is.
     Improves high-intensity repeated-effort performance and training
     adaptations; extremely well studied for safety; ⚠️ cheap; ⚠️ monohydrate
     is as good as any expensive form. ⚠️ Does not "damage kidneys" in
     healthy people — that claim has been examined repeatedly
  CAFFEINE      ⚠️ robust performance benefit; tolerance and sleep costs are real
  PROTEIN POWDER ⚠️ food in convenient form. Works if it closes a real gap
  BETA-ALANINE  ⚠️ modest, specific to ~1–4 min high-intensity efforts
  NITRATE/BEETROOT  ⚠️ modest endurance benefit, mainly in less-trained people
  BICARBONATE   ⚠️ real for specific efforts; GI distress is common

⚠️ WEAK, MIXED, OR CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
  HMB · BCAAs (⚠️ largely pointless with adequate total protein) ·
  most "testosterone boosters" · most pre-workout blends beyond their
  caffeine content · glutamine · tart cherry (⚠️ modest for soreness —
  and see §13's blunting concern) · collagen (⚠️ some tendon evidence)

⚠️ VITAMINS AND MINERALS
  ⚠️ Correct a DEFICIENCY and you'll see benefit; supplement beyond
  sufficiency and you generally won't. ⚠️ Vitamin D and iron are the
  common real deficiencies — ⚠️ test rather than guess, and iron
  supplementation without deficiency is harmful
```
**⚠️ The ordering point from §16 → `exercise-programming-periodization-and-aging` stands: supplements are the last few percent, and they
only exist as a percentage of a foundation that's already there.**

## §21. ⚠️ Under-Fuelling, Overtraining and Health-First Framing

> **⚠️ This section matters more than the optimization sections above it, and it is the
> one most often left out of technical treatments.**

**⚠️ RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport)** — **the current framework, expanded from
the earlier female athlete triad because ⚠️ it affects men too.** **Chronic LOW ENERGY
AVAILABILITY — insufficient intake relative to training expenditure — produces
system-wide consequences:**
```
⚠️ Endocrine disruption (⚠️ menstrual dysfunction; suppressed testosterone in men)
⚠️ REDUCED BONE MINERAL DENSITY and stress fracture risk
⚠️ Impaired immune function · GI problems · cardiovascular effects
⚠️ Impaired training adaptation and performance — the thing being pursued
⚠️ Depressed mood, irritability, impaired concentration
```
⚠️ **It occurs at any body size and is NOT diagnosable by appearance.** **It can be
unintentional — simply not eating enough for a training load that increased.**
**⚠️ Overtraining exists on a continuum**: **functional overreaching (planned, recovers in
days, and is part of normal training) → non-functional overreaching (weeks to recover) →
overtraining syndrome (⚠️ months, and it is serious).** **Warning signs: performance
declining despite training, persistent fatigue, disturbed sleep, elevated resting heart
rate or altered HRV, mood change, recurrent illness or injury, loss of motivation.**
⚠️ **The remedy is rest and adequate fuelling, and the instinct — to train harder because
performance is dropping — makes it worse.**

> **⚠️ On disordered patterns, said plainly and without euphemism.** ⚠️ **Exercise and
> nutrition tracking can become compulsive**, **and the traits that make people good at
> training — discipline, tolerance for discomfort, willingness to override signals — are
> the same traits that make this progression hard to notice from inside.**
> **⚠️ Signs worth taking seriously**: **training through injury or illness; distress or
> guilt when a session is missed; exercise crowding out relationships, work or rest;
> rigid food rules that expand over time; rising volume paired with falling
> performance; a sense that the number — on a scale, a lift, a watch — determines
> self-worth.**
> ⚠️ **If several of those are familiar, that's worth raising with a doctor, a registered
> dietitian, or a therapist.** **These are common and treatable, and getting help earlier
> is straightforwardly better than later.** **⚠️ Nothing in the optimization sections above
> is worth acquiring an eating disorder or a stress fracture for, and I'd rather say that
> directly than bury it.**

**⚠️ The health-first framing that the epidemiology actually supports**: ⚠️ **the largest
health returns come from moving from SEDENTARY to SOME activity — the dose-response curve
is steepest at the low end** — **and additional volume beyond moderate levels yields
progressively smaller health returns.** **⚠️ Which means the person with the most to gain
is the one doing nothing, not the one optimizing their fifth weekly session.**

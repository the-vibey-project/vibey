## §9. Development

**⚠️ Piaget's stage sequence is directionally right and the ages are wrong** — **later
methods showed infants understand far more, far earlier, than his tasks revealed.**
**Vygotsky** — **the zone of proximal development and the social scaffolding of learning;
influential and hard to test rigorously.**
**Attachment** (Bowlby, Ainsworth) — ⚠️ **the secure/insecure distinction has real
predictive validity, though weaker than popular treatments imply, and adult "attachment
style" as a personality typology is a considerable extrapolation from infant research.**
**⚠️ Behavioural genetics is where developmental psychology's most robust and most
uncomfortable findings live** (§14): **nearly everything is partly heritable; shared
family environment explains less of adult outcomes than intuition demands; and
gene-environment correlation means "environment" measures are partly genetic.**
**⚠️ Adolescence**: **real neurological development (prefrontal maturation extending into
the twenties, differential timing of reward and control systems)** — ⚠️ **and popular
"the teenage brain" accounts routinely overstate the determinism.**
**Ageing**: ⚠️ **fluid abilities decline from early adulthood; crystallized abilities hold
or improve into later life.**

## §10. Personality

**⚠️ The Big Five (OCEAN) is one of psychology's genuine successes**: **Openness,
Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.** ⚠️ **Derived
bottom-up from the statistical structure of trait language and behaviour rather than
imposed by theory, replicated across many cultures and languages, substantially heritable,
reasonably stable in adulthood, and predictive of real outcomes.**
- ⚠️ **Conscientiousness predicts job performance across nearly every occupation and
  predicts longevity.**
- **Neuroticism predicts mental and physical health outcomes.**
- ⚠️ **Traits are continuous distributions, not types.** **There are no "introverts" and
  "extraverts" as discrete kinds — there is a distribution.**
- **Personality changes gradually with age (the maturity principle: conscientiousness and
  agreeableness tend to rise, neuroticism to fall).**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the MBTI is not a valid instrument and its ubiquity in corporate settings
> is a genuine embarrassment.** ⚠️ **It has poor test-retest reliability (a substantial
> fraction of people get a different type on retest within weeks), it imposes false
> dichotomies on continuous distributions, its factor structure doesn't hold up, and it
> lacks predictive validity for job performance.** **The same caution applies to
> Enneagram, DISC, colour-based typologies and most commercial personality products.**
> **⚠️ If you need a personality measure, use a validated Big Five inventory.**

**⚠️ The person-situation debate resolved sensibly**: **Mischel showed situations predict
behaviour more than traits in any single instance; traits predict AGGREGATE behaviour
across many instances well.** **Both are true and they operate at different levels.**
**Dark triad** (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy) — **real constructs**,
⚠️ **and heavily over-applied in popular usage to describe people one dislikes.**

## §11. Motivation

**Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation; Self-Determination Theory** (autonomy, competence,
relatedness) — ⚠️ **well-supported and among the more durable frameworks.**
**⚠️ The overjustification effect is real but narrower than advertised**: **rewarding an
already-intrinsically-motivating activity can undermine it** — ⚠️ **the effect is
strongest for expected, tangible, performance-independent rewards on tasks the person
already enjoys, and does not generalize to "incentives don't work."**
**Goal setting** (Locke & Latham) — **specific, difficult goals outperform "do your
best"** — ⚠️ **and goals distort behaviour toward what's measured** (Goodhart; see a
business reference §13).
**⚠️ Growth mindset is a cautionary tale worth knowing.** **The original claim was
enormous; large preregistered replications and meta-analyses found effects far smaller
than claimed, concentrated in specific subgroups, and inconsistent.** **Something is
probably there. It is not what was sold to schools.**

## §12. Social Psychology

**⚠️ This is the subfield the replication crisis hit hardest, and the classic studies need
individual assessment rather than blanket acceptance or blanket rejection.**

**⚠️ Survived, with qualification:**
- **Conformity (Asch)** — **replicates, with cultural and contextual variation.**
- **Obedience (Milgram)** — ⚠️ **the core finding replicates in modified ethical forms,
  but archival work showed the procedure was less standardized than reported, prods were
  improvised, and many participants expressed doubt the shocks were real.** **The
  headline is roughly right; the tidy story is not.**
- **Bystander effect** — **replicates, though ⚠️ field data on real emergencies shows
  intervention is more common than the lab literature suggests.**
- **Minimal group paradigm** — **robust. In-group favouritism arises from arbitrary
  categorization.**
- **Cognitive dissonance** — **broadly holds.**

**⚠️ Substantially damaged:**
- **⚠️ The Stanford Prison Experiment is not usable evidence.** **Tapes and interviews
  showed guards were coached toward brutality, at least one "breakdown" was reported as
  feigned, and it had no control condition.** **It is a demonstration, and a
  methodologically compromised one.**
- **⚠️ Social priming as a research programme** — **elderly-words-cause-slow-walking and
  its many descendants.** **Broad replication failure.**
- **⚠️ Stereotype threat** — **the phenomenon may exist; the large effects claimed, and
  their use to explain population-level achievement gaps, are not well supported by
  replication and show clear signs of publication bias.**
- **⚠️ The IAT** — **measures something, but has poor test-retest reliability for
  individual scoring and weak prediction of individual discriminatory behaviour.**
  ⚠️ **It should not be used as a personal diagnostic, which is precisely how it is
  usually deployed in training.**

**⚠️ The Fundamental Attribution Error is worth keeping** — **overattributing others'
behaviour to disposition and one's own to situation** — ⚠️ **though it's weaker in
collectivist cultures, which is itself a §24 → `psych-sociology-institutions-culture-and-the-weird-problem` lesson.**
**⚠️ Persuasion and influence** (Cialdini's principles) — **the effects are real and the
effect sizes in field settings are typically much smaller than the anecdotes suggest**,
and **several specific studies in that literature have replication problems.**

## §13. Clinical Psychology — and What Actually Works

**⚠️ Diagnosis is categorical by convention, not by nature.** **The DSM/ICD categories are
useful administrative and communicative tools built by committee**, ⚠️ **and they do not
carve nature at its joints** — **comorbidity is the norm rather than the exception,
boundaries with normality are fuzzy and shift between editions, and few categories have
identified biomarkers.** **Dimensional alternatives (HiTOP, RDoC) exist for this reason.**

**⚠️ What the outcome literature actually shows** — **and this is among the more
practically valuable knowledge in this document:**
- **⚠️ Psychotherapy works.** **Effect sizes are moderate and comparable to many medical
  interventions.**
- **⚠️ The DODO BIRD VERDICT: different bona fide therapies produce broadly similar
  outcomes for most common conditions.** **This has held up reasonably well across
  decades and is deeply unpopular with brand-name therapy advocates.**
- **⚠️ COMMON FACTORS explain much of the variance**: **the therapeutic alliance, the
  therapist, client expectancy, and a coherent rationale** — **often more than the
  specific technique.**
- **⚠️ Specific exceptions where technique DOES matter and matters a lot**: **exposure
  therapies for anxiety, OCD and phobias, where exposure-based approaches clearly
  outperform.** **This is the strongest technique-specific finding in the field.**
- **⚠️ Therapist effects are large.** **Which therapist you see predicts outcome better
  than which modality they practice.**
- **⚠️ Therapy has side effects.** **A minority of clients deteriorate, and this is
  under-discussed.**

**⚠️ The chemical imbalance framing of depression is not supported as stated.** **The
simple serotonin-deficiency account has been substantially undermined; antidepressants
have real average benefits that are modest and larger in severe depression, and the
mechanism is not the one in the marketing.** ⚠️ **This is not an argument against
medication — it is an argument against the explanation.**
**⚠️ Regression to the mean is the single most under-appreciated confound in clinical
work.** **People seek help when they are at their worst; they would improve somewhat
regardless.** **Any uncontrolled intervention will therefore look effective.**

> **⚠️ A note on the popular literature.** **Trauma and attachment have become
> enormously popular frames** — ⚠️ **and popular treatments frequently overstate what the
> evidence supports about mechanism, universality and physiological encoding.** **The
> underlying phenomena are real, PTSD is real and treatable, and the confident
> mechanistic stories layered on top often are not.**

## §14. Intelligence

**⚠️ This is a domain where the empirical findings are unusually robust and the public
discourse is unusually poor, in both directions.**

**What is well established:**
- **⚠️ The positive manifold**: **performance across diverse cognitive tasks correlates
  positively, yielding a general factor, g.** **This is one of the most replicated
  findings in psychology.**
- **⚠️ IQ tests are reliable and have real predictive validity** for **educational
  attainment, job performance (rising with job complexity), income and health outcomes.**
  ⚠️ **Predictive, meaning at the group-statistical level — not determinative for
  individuals.**
- **⚠️ Substantially heritable in adulthood, with heritability INCREASING with age**
  (the Wilson effect) — **which is counterintuitive and well replicated.**
- **⚠️ The Flynn effect**: **large IQ gains across the 20th century, far too fast to be
  genetic.** ⚠️ **This alone demonstrates powerful environmental effects on measured
  scores**, and **the effect has stalled or reversed in several developed countries.**

**⚠️ What is NOT established, and where public discussion goes wrong:**
- ⚠️ **Heritability is a population statistic about VARIANCE in a specific environment.**
  **It says nothing about immutability, and nothing whatsoever about the causes of
  differences BETWEEN groups.** **This inference is invalid and is the most common error
  in the area.**
- ⚠️ **Environmental effects are large** — **nutrition, schooling, lead exposure, iodine
  deficiency and illness all measurably affect cognitive development, and several have
  produced large population-level changes.**
- **⚠️ g is not "the whole of intelligence" in any everyday sense**, and **multiple
  intelligences (Gardner) is popular but has not held up as a psychometric model.**
- **⚠️ Test scores are influenced by motivation, familiarity and circumstance**, and
  **measurement invariance across groups has to be demonstrated, not assumed.**

**⚠️ The responsible summary**: **g is real, measurable and predictive; it is not destiny,
not fixed, not the sum of human worth, and not a licence for any claim about groups.**

## §15. Neurodiversity

**⚠️ Autism and ADHD are among the more strongly heritable psychiatric-classified
conditions, and both are dimensional rather than categorical** — **traits are continuously
distributed in the population and the diagnostic threshold is a clinical convention drawn
on a continuum.**
**⚠️ The medical and neurodiversity framings are both partially right and the argument
between them is often a category error**: **impairment is real and support needs are real;
so is the observation that much disability arises from environmental mismatch rather than
from the trait itself.** **The social model's core insight — that changing the environment
can reduce disability without changing the person — is well founded and applies
straightforwardly here.**
**⚠️ What the evidence supports:** **co-occurrence is the norm rather than the exception**
(autism and ADHD frequently co-occur, and both with anxiety and mood conditions);
**diagnosis in adulthood is increasingly common and reflects prior underdiagnosis rather
than an epidemic**; ⚠️ **presentation varies widely, and historical diagnostic criteria
were built on a narrow sample, which produced systematic underdiagnosis in girls, women,
and people who mask effectively.**
**⚠️ Executive function differences are central to the ADHD picture and often central to
the autistic one too**, and **support that targets environment and structure generally
outperforms support that targets willpower** — ⚠️ **which is the §11 motivation literature
applied honestly.**
**⚠️ Be sceptical of confident mechanistic accounts.** **The genetics are highly polygenic,
the neuroimaging literature is heterogeneous and has its own replication problems, and
popular single-cause explanations outrun the evidence considerably.**

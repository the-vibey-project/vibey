## §16. Sociological Thinking

**⚠️ The core move is that social facts are real and not reducible to individual
psychology.** **Durkheim's demonstration — that suicide rates are stable and patterned
across groups even though each instance is intensely individual — remains the cleanest
statement of why a separate discipline exists.**

```
⚠️ STRUCTURE vs AGENCY   the field's central tension. People act; structures
   constrain and enable; and structures are reproduced BY that action
   (Giddens' structuration; Bourdieu's habitus)
SOCIAL CONSTRUCTION   ⚠️ categories like race, gender, madness and childhood are
   built and maintained socially — which does NOT mean they are unreal
   or lack material consequence. This is the most misread idea in sociology
THE SOCIOLOGICAL IMAGINATION  ⚠️ Mills — linking personal troubles to public issues
LEVELS   micro (interaction) · meso (organizations) · macro (institutions)
```
**⚠️ Emergence is the key conceptual point**: **social phenomena have properties that do
not exist at the individual level.** **Segregation can emerge from mild individual
preferences with no one intending it (Schelling); traffic jams from no jam-causing car.**
⚠️ **Explaining a social outcome by the intentions of individuals is usually a mistake.**

## §17. Theory Traditions

```
FUNCTIONALISM        ⚠️ institutions persist because they serve functions. Durkheim,
                     Parsons, Merton. Criticized as conservative and teleological;
                     ⚠️ Merton's manifest/latent function distinction survives well
CONFLICT             ⚠️ society as competition over resources and power. Marx, Weber.
                     Illuminating on inequality; can overreach into total explanation
SYMBOLIC INTERACT.   ⚠️ meaning is constructed in interaction. Mead, Blumer, Goffman.
                     ⚠️ Goffman's dramaturgy and "face" work remain unusually useful
RATIONAL CHOICE      individuals as goal-seeking; strong formalism, thin psychology
FEMINIST / CRITICAL  ⚠️ gender and power as organizing categories of analysis;
                     standpoint and intersectionality (Crenshaw)
FIELD THEORY         ⚠️ Bourdieu — habitus, field, and forms of capital (economic,
                     social, cultural, symbolic). Genuinely powerful analytically
```
**⚠️ These are lenses, not competing truth claims to adjudicate.** **A given phenomenon
usually looks different and partly correct through each.** **Treating one as the theory of
everything is the characteristic failure mode.**

## §18. Stratification and Mobility

**Class, status and party** (Weber's three dimensions — ⚠️ **and the separation of
economic class from social status is one of sociology's most useful distinctions**).
**⚠️ Social mobility findings that are robust**: **absolute mobility depends heavily on
structural change in the occupational distribution; relative mobility (positional
competition) is much stickier**; ⚠️ **intergenerational income elasticity varies
substantially across countries**, and **the Great Gatsby curve — higher inequality
associating with lower mobility — is a well-replicated cross-national pattern.**
**⚠️ Cultural capital and the hidden curriculum** (Bourdieu) — **schools reward dispositions
acquired at home, so apparently meritocratic sorting reproduces advantage.** **Well
supported.**
**Neighbourhood and place effects** — ⚠️ **the causal identification here has improved
substantially through moving experiments and administrative-data natural experiments, and
place effects on children's long-run outcomes appear real and meaningful.**
**⚠️ Homophily is one of the most robust findings in the field** — **birds of a feather —
and it means networks reproduce stratification without discriminatory intent** (§19).

## §19. Networks

**⚠️ Network position is often more explanatory than individual attributes, and this is
network analysis's core contribution.**
- **⚠️ THE STRENGTH OF WEAK TIES (Granovetter)** — **weak ties bridge otherwise
  disconnected clusters and are therefore disproportionately valuable for novel
  information, notably job leads.** **Among the most robust findings in sociology, and
  since supported by very large-scale field experiments on professional networks.**
- **Structural holes (Burt)** — **brokerage across gaps confers advantage.**
- **⚠️ Small world / six degrees** — **short path lengths in large networks; real, and
  the original experiment had a low completion rate, so treat the specific number as
  folklore.**
- **⚠️ Diffusion and contagion** — **behaviours, norms and information spread through
  networks.** ⚠️ **Distinguishing genuine contagion from homophily is genuinely hard and
  many published "social contagion" claims do not survive that scrutiny.**
- **Preferential attachment** — **rich-get-richer produces heavy-tailed degree
  distributions.**

## §20. Institutions and Organizations

**⚠️ Institutions are the rules of the game — formal and informal** — **and they are
sticky, path-dependent, and often persist long after the conditions that produced them.**
**⚠️ Isomorphism (DiMaggio & Powell)** — **organizations in a field come to resemble one
another through coercive, mimetic and normative pressure** — ⚠️ **which explains why
competitors converge on the same practices regardless of whether the practices work.**
**Bureaucracy** (Weber) — **the rational-legal form, its genuine efficiency advantages,
and its pathologies: goal displacement, where following the rule replaces achieving the
purpose.**
**Principal-agent problems, loose coupling** (⚠️ **the formal structure and the actual work
can be substantially decoupled, and the formal structure may exist mainly for
legitimacy**), **and organizational culture.**
**⚠️ Normal Accidents (Perrow) vs High Reliability Organizations** — **whether catastrophic
failure is inevitable in tightly-coupled complex systems, or whether some organizations
genuinely achieve reliability.** **Both literatures are worth knowing and they disagree
productively** (see an IT infrastructure reference §20).

## §21. Deviance and Social Control

**Labelling theory** — ⚠️ **deviance is partly produced by the response to it; labels can
become self-fulfilling.**
**Anomie/strain** (Merton) — **mismatch between cultural goals and legitimate means.**
**Social control and differential association.**
**⚠️ Broken windows is a cautionary case study**: **enormously influential in policy, and
the empirical support is much weaker and more contested than its policy uptake implied.**
**⚠️ What the crime literature better supports**: **the CERTAINTY of sanction deters more
than its SEVERITY** — **a robust finding that policy consistently ignores** — **and the
age-crime curve is one of criminology's most stable empirical regularities.**
**⚠️ Moral panics** (Cohen) — **episodic societal alarm about a group or behaviour,
amplified by media, disproportionate to the actual threat.** ⚠️ **A genuinely useful
analytic concept, and one that is itself frequently deployed rhetorically to dismiss
concerns that turn out to be legitimate** — **the label is not an argument** (§25.2 → `psych-reference`).

## §22. Religion and Secularization

**⚠️ The classical secularization thesis — that modernization inevitably produces religious
decline — has been substantially revised, and this is one of the more interesting
corrections in modern sociology.**
**⚠️ What the evidence supports**: **Western Europe secularized markedly; the United States
did not follow the same path for most of the 20th century (though religious
disaffiliation has risen notably since the 1990s); and globally, religious populations
have grown.** ⚠️ **"Modernization → secularization" is not a law but a description of one
regional trajectory generalized too confidently.**
**Competing accounts worth knowing**: **religious market theory (pluralism and competition
sustain religious vitality rather than eroding it)**; **existential security theory
(Norris & Inglehart — religiosity declines where material and physical security is high,
which fits the cross-national data reasonably well)**; and **believing without belonging,
where affiliation falls faster than belief.**
**⚠️ Sociology of religion has robust findings independent of any truth claim about
religion itself**: **religious participation correlates with civic engagement, social
support, charitable giving and some health outcomes** — ⚠️ **and disentangling the
contribution of belief from that of community and regular attendance is hard and
incompletely resolved.** **Attendance tends to be the stronger predictor, which is
itself informative.**
⚠️ **A methodological caution the field takes seriously**: **religious self-report is
subject to social desirability in both directions depending on context, and attendance is
consistently over-reported in surveys relative to time-use and observational data.**

## §23. Culture and Anthropology

**⚠️ Culture is not a possession or a checklist of traits — it is a distributed, learned,
transmitted repertoire of practices, meanings and norms.**
**Anthropology's central methodological contributions**: **ethnography and participant
observation** (⚠️ **deep, contextual, non-generalizable by design — and the tradeoff
against survey breadth is real and honest**), **thick description** (Geertz — **the
difference between a twitch and a wink is the meaning, and only context supplies it**),
**and cultural relativism as a METHODOLOGICAL stance** — ⚠️ **understanding a practice on
its own terms before judging it, which is distinct from moral relativism and is
routinely conflated with it.**
**⚠️ Emic vs etic** — **the insider's categories vs the analyst's** — **and both are
needed.**
**Ritual, kinship, exchange and reciprocity** (⚠️ **Mauss's gift — obligation to give,
receive and reciprocate — is one of anthropology's most durable ideas**).
**⚠️ Anthropology's reflexive turn was a genuine epistemic advance**: **the observer's
position shapes the account, ethnographic authority is constructed, and the discipline's
colonial origins are not incidental to its early categories.**

## §24. Cross-Cultural — the WEIRD Problem

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the single most important methodological finding about psychology as a
> body of knowledge.** **Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan showed that the overwhelming
> majority of psychological research samples come from Western, Educated, Industrialized,
> Rich, Democratic populations** — ⚠️ **and that these populations are frequent OUTLIERS
> on measures where cross-cultural data exists: visual illusions, spatial reasoning,
> fairness in economic games, individualism, analytic vs holistic cognition, moral
> reasoning.** **So "people do X" in most psychology papers means "American undergraduates
> do X," and the generalization to humanity is often unwarranted.**

**⚠️ Hofstede's dimensions — useful and heavily criticized.** **Individualism-collectivism,
power distance, uncertainty avoidance and the rest are the most-cited cultural framework
in business and management.** ⚠️ **The criticisms are serious: the original data came from
IBM employees in the 1960s-70s, nations are treated as culturally homogeneous units,
within-country variation exceeds between-country variation on several dimensions, and the
dimensions are often applied to individuals when they are country-level aggregates —
which is an ecological fallacy.** **Use them as a rough orienting vocabulary, not as
measurement.**
**⚠️ Better-supported cross-cultural findings**: **analytic vs holistic cognitive styles
(Nisbett)**, **tight vs loose cultures (Gelfand — strength of norms and tolerance of
deviance, with a plausible ecological-threat account)**, **and honour vs dignity vs face
cultures.**
**⚠️ Universals do exist**: **some emotional expressions (contested in scope, §8 → `psych-perception-memory-learning-cognition-and-emotion`),
incest avoidance, reciprocity norms, language, music, kinship systems, and religion in
some form.** **The interesting scientific work is at the boundary between the universal
and the variable, not at either pole.**
**Cultural evolution** (Boyd, Richerson, Henrich) — ⚠️ **culture as a genuine inheritance
system subject to selection, with cumulative culture and the ratchet effect as the human
distinctive.** **Henrich's argument that much adaptive cultural knowledge is opaque to its
users — that people follow practices that work without knowing why — is one of the more
striking ideas here.**

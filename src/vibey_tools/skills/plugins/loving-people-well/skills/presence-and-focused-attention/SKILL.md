# Presence and Focused Attention in Relational Care

## Overview
This skill operationalizes Level 1 of the reverse architecture: the foundational ground state of human attention. It equips practitioners to eliminate digital technoference, remove mobile devices from dining tables, and direct undivided sensory focus toward the person in the room with genuine, humble curiosity.

## Invocation Triggers
Invoke this skill when:
- Establishing device-free meal and conversation protocols.
- Overcoming continuous partial attention and digital distraction in family life.
- Preparing for sensitive, high-stakes pastoral or personal conversations.
- Practicing unhurried listening and waiting out conversational silence.
- Cultivating deep curiosity regarding another person's daily burdens and joys.

## Governing Principles

### 1. Eliminating Digital Technoference
In modern life, interpersonal connection is fragmented by ubiquitous digital screens. Psychologists define technoference as everyday interruptions in interpersonal communication caused by digital devices. Research by Kushlev, Dwyer, and Dunn confirms that the mere presence of a visible smartphone on a dining table reduces empathy, fragments conversational depth, and communicates that the person across the table is secondary to external digital noise.

### 2. The Physical Isolation Protocol
Practicing genuine presence requires intentional physical boundaries:
1. Physical Removal: Place smartphones in another room or upon a high shelf before sitting down for meals or dialogue.
2. Full Sensory Focus: Align physical posture, eye contact, and auditory focus entirely toward the person in the room.
3. Substantive Curiosity: Inquire deeply into the other person's weekly labor, fears, and joys, viewing them as an image-bearer of God (Proverbs 27:23).

### 3. Waiting Out the Silence
Proverbs 20:5 observes that the purposes of a person's heart are deep waters, and one who has insight draws them out. Listening well requires comfort with silence, allowing the counterpart time to formulate complex thoughts without interruption.

## Operational Checklist
- [ ] Place all mobile phones and digital screens out of sight prior to sitting down.
- [ ] Direct body posture and eye contact toward the speaker.
- [ ] Ask open, thoughtful questions regarding their weekly burdens and feelings.
- [ ] Wait out pauses in silence, allowing the other person time to reflect and speak.
- [ ] Offer undivided presence with joyful gratitude (Luke 10:41-42).

## Failure Modes
- Phubbing: Checking text messages or glanceable screen notifications during conversation.
- Premature Advice: Interrupting the speaker with hasty solutions before understanding their heart.
- Self-Centered Monologue: Using conversation as a stage for personal performance.

# Trading Mechanics Plugin

How trading actually works mechanically, written evidence-first: it opens with what the outcome literature shows about retail trading rather than burying it, then covers market microstructure, orders and execution, the asset classes, derivatives, an honest assessment of technical analysis, quantitative and factor approaches, the trading styles, real versus apparent arbitrage, forex, DeFi mechanics, risk and position sizing, costs and frictions, backtesting pitfalls, psychology and harm, and regulation and tax. This is a mechanics reference for understanding markets and building systems — it is not investment advice, and it does not offer a strategy expected to be profitable.

One reference, split into 5 skills along its section groups so a task loads only the part it needs. Section numbers (§N) are shared across the set and cross-references into a sibling skill are written as §N → `skill`. Reference, not tutorial: sections are independent, every claim is tagged by how durable it is (stable fundamentals vs. versioned specifics vs. genuinely contested questions), and a currency snapshot (verified August 2026) flags what goes stale first.

- **trading-asset-classes-derivatives-and-analysis** — Asset Classes, Derivatives, Technical Analysis, and Quantitative Approaches (§4–§7): Asset Classes; Derivatives; Technical Analysis, Honestly; Quantitative and Factor Approaches.
- **trading-styles-arbitrage-forex-and-defi** — Trading Styles, Arbitrage, Forex, and DeFi Mechanics (§8–§12): Swing Trading; Day Trading; Arbitrage — Real vs Apparent; Forex; DeFi Mechanics.
- **trading-risk-costs-backtesting-and-psychology** — Risk and Position Sizing, Costs, Backtesting Pitfalls, Psychology and Harm, and Regulation (§13–§17): Risk and Position Sizing; Costs and Frictions; Backtesting Pitfalls; ⚠️ Psychology and Harm; Regulation and Tax.
- **trading-reference** — Misconceptions, Numbers, and Canon (§18–§22): Misconceptions; Numbers; Books; Quick Reference; Method.

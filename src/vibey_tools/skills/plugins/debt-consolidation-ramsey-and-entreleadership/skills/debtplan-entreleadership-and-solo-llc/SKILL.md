## Part 3. EntreLeadership

## What it is: a 2011 book, a brand, and a business arm of Ramsey Solutions

*EntreLeadership: 20 Years of Practical Business Wisdom from the Trenches* was published September 20, 2011 by Howard Books (Simon & Schuster), a hardcover with 15 chapters plus an introduction (the store lists 320 pages; another summary lists 306) at a list price of $17.99 ([Ramsey store](https://store.ramseysolutions.com/business-and-career/books/entreleadership/); [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/EntreLeadership/Dave-Ramsey/9781451617856)). The term combines an entrepreneur's passion with a leader's character, and the book defines it as the process of leading to cause a venture to grow and prosper ([Ramsey framework](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/entreleadership-framework); [Bookey](https://www.bookey.app/book/entreleadership)). According to Wikipedia, Ramsey began teaching it in 2005 inside his company before adapting it into a workshop, the book, a podcast, and live events ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsey_Solutions)). The brand today is the business section of ramseysolutions.com rather than a separate legal entity, as far as the research could tell.

**What does not exist.** No source shows an official list of "Business Baby Steps," an "EntreLeadership Pyramid," or a "Six Steps" model, and none should be attributed to Ramsey. The formal frameworks are the **Five Stages of Business** and the **Six Drivers** below. Likewise the company's official core values are **not** a three-word "Excellence, Integrity, Heart" slogan; Ramsey's company page and careers page list 14 values (below).

## Two live frameworks: Five Stages and Six Drivers

The **Five Stages of Business** diagnose where an owner is stuck: (1) Treadmill Operator, where too much depends on the owner (skills: delegation, time management, hiring, business budgeting); (2) Pathfinder, with no clear direction (mission and vision, values, role clarity, communication); (3) Trailblazer, with no leadership team or scaling plan (planning, process improvement, leadership development, culture); (4) Peak Performer, where the business has become too comfortable; and (5) Legacy Builder, with no succession plan ([Ramsey framework](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/entreleadership-framework); [Stages of Business](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/entreleadership/resources/delegation/stages-of-business)). Ramsey says Treadmill Operator should be a season, not a permanent place. A free 10-minute assessment places you in a stage. The **Six Drivers** are Personal, Purpose, People, Plan, Product, and Profit, with Profit covering budgeting, accounting, getting out of debt and compensation ([Ramsey: 6 drivers](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/6-drivers-business-growth)). Stage 1 emphasizes Personal, People and Profit ([EntreLeadership System](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/entreleadership/system)). The book predates the Drivers branding, so mapping chapters to drivers is a reading, not a Ramsey statement.

## Business money rules: Ramsey's Baby Steps logic ported to a company

Ramsey's debt-free-business article says no business debt is acceptable: keep cash on hand, live on less than you earn, pay off any existing debt in three to five years, pay cash, rent, outsource or buy used, use a debit card instead of a credit card, and grow "at the speed of cash" ([Debt-free business](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/how-to-run-a-business-debt-free)). His seven tips include a monthly budget paired with a profit-and-loss statement, avoiding negative cash flow, saving retained earnings, and generosity. A **business emergency fund of about six months of operating expenses** belongs in a separate high-interest account; if the company has debt, pay yourself a modest living wage and split profits, for example 80% to debt and 20% to retained earnings ([Business emergency fund](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/business-emergency-fund)). The six small-business money mistakes are mixing business and personal finances, borrowing to build, not knowing your numbers, ego or tax-write-off purchases, paying bills and taxes late (set aside about 25% for quarterly taxes, confirmed with a tax professional), and focusing only on profit ([Money mistakes](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/small-business-money-mistakes)). The page's 51% and 53% statistics appear without sources. Ramsey said in March 2026 that SBA loans "suck," in a context about a personal investment property rather than a business loan ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/really-hate-small-business-administration-160107475.html)).

What differs from the personal Baby Steps is structure: no numbered business ladder exists. The hiring page lists 12 components (including referrals with a cash bounty after 90 days, DISC and skills tests, a review of the candidate's personal budget, a written Key Results Area, a spousal dinner, and a 90-day onboarding plan), treats a candidate's first question about benefits as a red flag, and says a bad hire costs "six to nine months' salary" ([How to hire](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/how-to-hire-employees)). "Hire slow, fire fast" is not on that page; it appears only in third-party coverage and a podcast blurb. Firing is a five-step process (cut access, cover the role, morning timing, a short conversation with a second leader, then documentation), and improvement plans typically run 90 days ([How to fire](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/how-to-fire); [PIP](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/performance-improvement-plan)). Pay is base plus commission off the bottom line for leaders, with profit sharing starting around 10% of profits after a one-year wait ([Compensation](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/how-to-create-an-employee-compensation-plan); [Profit sharing](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/business/how-to-create-profit-sharing-plan)). The hiring bounty and spousal dinner are Ramsey-specific practices, not general HR standards. The "right person in the right seat" phrase could not be found in Ramsey's materials and should not be attributed to him.

The 14 official core values are Colossians 3:23, Crusade, Excellence in the Ordinary, Family, Fear Not, Marketplace Service, Momentum Theorem, Never Give Up, No Gossip, Righteous Living, Self-Employed Mentality, Share the Profits, Shoot Sacred Cows, and Team; the mission is biblically based, commonsense education and empowerment ([Ramsey company](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/company); [careers](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/company/careers); [court filing](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.tnmd.83303/gov.uscourts.tnmd.83303.23.1.pdf)). "Righteous Living" is the value tied to the conduct policy in the litigation described in Part 2. That context matters for evaluating "culture by values" advice: values enforcement that reaches employees' private lives carries litigation and reputational exposure distinct from whether the management advice is sound.

## What the evidence says about the no-debt rule

No study compares debt-free and leveraged small-firm outcomes, so the case is argued from adjacent data. The Federal Reserve's 2026 report on employer firms found 69.6% had outstanding debt, 80.8% of those applying for financing were approved, 59% of indebted firms used a personal guarantee, and 47.3% were profitable at end of 2024 ([Fed SBCS employer report](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/fsbsite/reports/2026/2026-report-on-employer-firms/2026-report-on-employer-firms.pdf)). The nonemployer report (2025 survey, released July 7, 2026) found about half of nonemployers had no debt, 31% did not regularly use external financing, and 64% used owner personal funds to meet financial challenges ([Fed SBCS nonemployer](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2026/2026-report-on-nonemployer-firms)). Startup-financing surveys show personal and family savings dominate: Kauffman found 64.4% of employer firms used them in 2016, with 9.1% using personal credit cards with balances and 6.3% home equity; in the Kauffman Firm Survey 44% had no debt in year one yet 48% used personal debt ([Kauffman 2019](https://www.kauffman.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Access-To-Capital_2019.pdf); [Kauffman KFS](https://www.kauffman.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/kfs_08.pdf)). That blurs "no debt" for founders, because personal cards and home equity count as personal debt used for business. A Census working paper found that higher invested capital (not specifically debt) is associated with nonemployer survival, and that incorporated forms survive more than sole proprietorships, a correlation that may reflect owner commitment rather than legal form ([Census CES-WP-25-26](https://www2.census.gov/ces/wp/2025/CES-WP-25-26.pdf)).

Survival data supply the reserve rationale. For establishments born in March 2013, 79.6% survived one year, 50.6% five years, and 34.7% ten years ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/34-7-percent-of-business-establishments-born-in-2013-were-still-operating-in-2023.htm)); closures are not necessarily failures. BLS has no nonemployer series; the closest substitutes put one-year inactive-or-exit rates for nonemployers at 36.4% for sole proprietors, 26.1% for sole proprietors with EINs, 20.3% for S corporations and 12.5% for partnerships in 2018 to 2019 ([NBER w34252](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34252/w34252.pdf)), which should not be quoted as failure rates. Only 0.5% of nonemployer startups became employers within seven years ([Census](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2024/econ/garage-entrepreneurs-just-self-employed-investigation-into-nonemployer-entrepreneurship.pdf)). Nonemployer firms numbered **30,427,808 in 2023**, with nearly $1.8 trillion in receipts, about 6.4% of GDP ([Census](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/small-business-week.html)); dividing gives roughly $59,000 per establishment (self-computed, not a Census statistic). An even-handed reading: no-debt is a risk-reduction rule that limits personal-guarantee exposure at the cost of slower capital-intensive growth, and the cost is small for low-capital solo service businesses. A Substack critique argues the rule suits salaried households better than owners, but it offers opinion without data ([Commentary on Wealth](https://commentaryonwealth.substack.com/p/debt-free-doesnt-mean-stress-free)). For an LLC, the liability shield generally does not protect against a personal guarantee (a general principle to verify with counsel).

## How EntreLeadership compares with other frameworks

| Framework | Built for | Core ideas | Fit for a solo LLC |
|---|---|---|---|
| EntreLeadership (Ramsey, 2011) | Small-business owners, first-time managers | Team ownership, no-debt ethos, six-month reserve, Five Stages | Money chapters fit; hiring and culture chapters premature |
| Profit First (Michalowicz) | Owner-operators, including solo | Sales minus Profit equals Expenses; allocate each deposit to Profit, Owner's Pay, Tax and OpEx accounts | Closest conceptual fit; the author's allocation percentages look internally inconsistent, so do not rely on them |
| The E-Myth Revisited (Gerber) | Founder-"technicians" | Entrepreneur, manager, technician roles; work on the business, not in it | Names the founder-as-worker trap |
| Traction / EOS (Wickman) | Leadership teams of small-to-mid firms | Vision/Traction Organizer, Rocks, Level 10 meeting, Scorecard | Heavy for one person (inference) |
| Good to Great / Built to Last (Collins) | Established large companies | Hedgehog concept, Level 5 leadership, BHAGs | Descriptive; Collins himself writes "Correlations, Not Causes" |

Scaling Up, High Output Management, Radical Candor and The Lean Startup are team- or product-oriented and add little for a one-person firm (inference). ## Solo-LLC mechanics: taxes, unemployment rules, and what scales down

The parts of EntreLeadership that scale to one person are separate business and personal accounts, a monthly profit-and-loss review, a set-aside for taxes, a reserve, and the Stage 1 skills of time management and budgeting. A reading order the research suggests (the report's own recommendation, not a Ramsey sequence): the debt-free-business and emergency-fund articles, the money-mistakes article, the free Stages assessment, then book Chapters 1, 2, and 9, with 7 and 15 only when a first hire or contractor is on the table. At $4,500 for a retreat plus travel, the paid events conflict with Ramsey's own pay-cash-and-build-reserve advice for a thin-reserve solo owner.

**IRS estimated tax.** Estimated payments are required when you expect to owe at least $1,000; the safe harbor is the lesser of 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000) ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/estimated-tax)). For 2026, 1040-ES installments fall on April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. The self-employment tax rate is 15.3%, calculated on 92.35% of net profit, with a 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500 ([2026 Form 1040-ES](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040es.pdf)). Ramsey's 25% set-aside is a heuristic; a 27% to 30% rule of thumb from a tax-software site is not official. Unemployment benefits are taxable (optional withholding of 10% federal and 7% state), which could raise a later safe-harbor base (inference) ([DEW handbook](https://dew.sc.gov/sites/dew/files/Documents/Claimant%20Handbook_As%20of%209.3.2025.pdf)).

**South Carolina DEW and self-employment.** DEW's claimant handbook (as of September 3, 2025) says claimants are not eligible if self-employed full-time, requires weekly certification, two weekly job searches, reporting gross earnings from any work, including self-employment, for the week earned rather than paid, and a one-week unpaid waiting period. Partial benefits apply when working under full-time hours with pay below the weekly benefit amount, and earnings up to 25% of the weekly benefit are not deducted ([DEW handbook](https://dew.sc.gov/sites/dew/files/Documents/Claimant%20Handbook_As%20of%209.3.2025.pdf); [SC Code 41-35-60](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t41c035.php)). Unreported earnings are treated as fraud, with possible disqualification up to 52 weeks, fines up to $100,000, up to 10 years in prison, repayment and tax-refund interception ([DEW](https://www.dew.sc.gov/individuals/how-unemployment-insurance-works)). A 2017 DEW guide says a person "may not be considered unemployed" if self-employed in any activity, and the statute turns on self-employment "returning or promising" remuneration above benefit amounts ([DEW guide](https://dew.sc.gov/sites/dew/files/Documents/work-search-all-rev-10-9-17.pdf); [SC Code 41-35-110](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-41/chapter-35/section-41-35-110/)). Whether forming an LLC and building it before income counts as reportable activity is a fact-specific question that only DEW can answer, and no current DEW statement on a no-income startup was found. The current maximum weekly benefit was also not retrieved.

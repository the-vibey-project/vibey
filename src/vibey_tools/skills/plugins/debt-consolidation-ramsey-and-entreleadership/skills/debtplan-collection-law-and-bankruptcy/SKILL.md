> **The key ideas:**
> 1. **South Carolina is unusually debtor-protective on wages and old debt:** state law bars garnishing earnings on consumer credit debts, the statute of limitations on contract debt is three years, and any part-payment on an old debt can revive it (section 15-3-120).
> 2. **The Chapter 7 means test looks backward six months and counts unemployment compensation but not Social Security,** so a recent income drop can matter a great deal.
> 3. **Secured consolidation can turn debt that SC's exemption and garnishment rules make hard to collect into debt that is not.**

## South Carolina collection law decides how much leverage creditors actually have

This section is where the reader's state matters most. **Wages:** South Carolina's Consumer Protection Code provides that for debts from consumer credit sales, leases, loans, or rental-purchases the creditor "may not attach unpaid earnings" by garnishment, and SCDCA says garnishment is currently prohibited for the collection of most debts ([SC Code 37-5-104](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t37c005.php); [SCDCA FAQs](https://consumer.sc.gov/consumer-faqs/fair-debt-collection-faqs)). Whether this covers medical bills, non-consumer-credit judgments, child support, taxes, or federal student loans was not researched. Federal law elsewhere caps ordinary garnishment at the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or earnings above 30 times the minimum wage ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/30-cppa)).

**Exempt income:** South Carolina's exemption statute protects the right to receive, or property traceable to, Social Security, **unemployment compensation**, veterans' and disability benefits, alimony and support, and pension payments from attachment, levy and sale ([SC Code 15-41-30](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c041.php)). Federal rules separately protect directly deposited Social Security, SSI, VA, railroad and federal retirement benefits in bank accounts, generally two months' worth, without the account holder having to claim an exemption; the federal list names Railroad Unemployment Insurance but not state unemployment, so state unemployment protection rests on the South Carolina statute ([31 CFR 212.6](https://ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-212/section-212.6); [CFPB](https://consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-can-i-find-an-attorney-who-specializes-in-elder-law-en-1981)). The popular label "judgment-proof" is not a statutory term. The honest synthesis is that someone living on protected income with no non-exempt assets is hard to collect from on card debt, but still faces lawsuits, judgment liens, collection calls, and credit damage.

**Old debt:** the South Carolina limitations period for a contract action is **three years** (section 15-3-530), and section 15-3-120 states that payment of any part of principal or interest is equivalent to a written promise, reviving the claim ([SC Code Title 15, Ch. 3](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c003.php); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/title-15/chapter-3/section-15-3-530)). So a token payment on a card more than three years past default can restart a full three years. Including a time-barred debt in a consolidation loan or plan might do the same (inference; no case on a DMP payment was found). A bankruptcy court applying South Carolina law held that merely listing a stale debt in schedules did not revive it ([D.S.C. Bankr. opinion](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-scb-3_15-bk-02896/pdf/USCOURTS-scb-3_15-bk-02896-0.pdf)). Federal Regulation F bars suing or threatening to sue on time-barred debt ([NCLC](https://library.nclc.org/article/limits-collection-time-barred-debt-and-new-fdcpa-rules)), presumes a violation above seven calls in seven days about a debt ([12 CFR 1006.14](https://ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1006/subpart-B/section-1006.14)), and requires a validation notice within five days of first contact; writing to dispute within 30 days stops collection until proof is sent ([SCDCA FAQs](https://consumer.sc.gov/consumer-faqs/fair-debt-collection-faqs)).

The crucial consolidation implication is that **a new secured loan creates a creditor whose remedies sit outside these protections.** Exempt-income rules do not block foreclosure on a home or repossession of a car. Consolidation also does not provide the automatic stay that bankruptcy does, and it does not stop collectors on debts you leave out.

## Chapter 7 and Chapter 13: the other end of the spectrum

Chapter 7 liquidates non-exempt assets and discharges most unsecured debts; over 99% of individual cases not dismissed or converted end in discharge. The means test applies when current monthly income exceeds the state median; abuse is presumed if projected five-year disposable income meets thresholds tied to unsecured debt (25% of it or $10,275, whichever is greater, or $17,150, whichever is lower) ([US Courts Ch. 7](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics)). For cases filed since April 1, 2026, **South Carolina median family income is $64,808 (1 person), $83,761 (2), $95,672 (3), and $116,314 (4)** ([USTP](https://www.justice.gov/ust/eo/bapcpa/20260401/bci_data/median_income_table.htm); [USTP means testing](https://www.justice.gov/ust/means-testing)). "Current monthly income" is the average over the six full months before filing, from all sources; unemployment compensation is entered unless you contend it is a Social Security Act benefit (then listed separately), and Social Security benefits are excluded ([Official Form 122A-1](https://www.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/form_b122a-1.pdf)). A recent income drop can therefore pull the figure below the median, though the outcome depends on facts and allowed expenses. Above the thresholds, a case may be challenged and the filer may be steered to Chapter 13.

**Exemptions:** South Carolina opted out of the federal exemption scheme ([SC Code 15-41-35](https://scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c041.php)). The inflation-adjusted amounts effective July 1, 2024 were a **$76,125 homestead ($152,250 for multiple owners of one unit), $7,600 vehicle, $6,100 household goods, $1,525 jewelry, $7,600 liquid assets (for debtors not claiming a homestead), $2,275 tools of trade, and a $7,600 wildcard** ([SC Bankruptcy Court](https://www.scb.uscourts.gov/news/reminder-south-carolina-exemption-amount-adjustments)). The statute requires adjustment in even-numbered years, so a July 1, 2026 update exists but was not found; a figure of about $80,125 for the homestead surfaced on a non-official site and is unverified. Nolo reports a 730-day domicile requirement to use current South Carolina exemptions ([Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/south-carolina-bankruptcy-homestead-exemption.html)). Equity above the cap is at risk of trustee liquidation.

**Costs and timeline:** the US Courts list a $245 filing fee, $75 administrative fee, and $15 trustee surcharge for Chapter 7 (total $335), with up to four installments; South Carolina firms quote $338, a discrepancy the research could not reconcile (verify with the court). Attorney fees in South Carolina are quoted at roughly $1,100 to $3,000 for Chapter 7 by a lead-generation site, a weak source, and up to $4,500 for Chapter 13 ([Ascend](https://tryascend.com/bankruptcy/south-carolina/cost)). Pre-filing credit counseling from an agency on the Justice Department's approved list is required within 180 days before filing, and a debtor-education course precedes discharge ([USTP approved list](https://www.justice.gov/ust/list-credit-counseling-agencies-approved-pursuant-11-usc-111)). The meeting of creditors typically occurs 21 to 40 days after filing and discharge generally 60 to 90 days after that meeting. Items not discharged in Chapter 7 include support obligations, certain taxes, certain educational loans and government-guaranteed loans, and willful and malicious injury. Chapter 13 sets a three-to-five-year plan (three years below state median income, five above), with debt limits of $526,700 unsecured and $1,580,125 secured, a $235 filing fee plus $75 administrative fee, and unsecured creditors receiving at least what Chapter 7 would pay ([US Courts Ch. 13](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-13-bankruptcy-basics)). Not researched: preference or fraud issues from paying selected creditors or taking a new loan shortly before filing, the effect on mortgages and cars, and reaffirmation. Those are exactly the questions for a South Carolina bankruptcy attorney.

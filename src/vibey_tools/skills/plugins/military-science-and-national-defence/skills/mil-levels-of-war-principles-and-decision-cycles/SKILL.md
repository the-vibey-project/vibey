>    they absorb, adapt and redistribute what they learn — and that is organizational, not
>    technological.**

## §5. ⚠️ The Levels of War

```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE THREE LEVELS, and conflating them is the most common
   error in public discussion of war
   ⚠️ STRATEGIC  ⚠️ national objectives, resource allocation,
      what the war is FOR
   ⚠️ ⚠️ OPERATIONAL  ⚠️ campaigns — the sequencing of
      engagements to achieve strategic aims. ⚠️ Formalized as a
      distinct level by Soviet theorists and adopted in the West
      only in the 1980s
   ⚠️ TACTICAL  ⚠️ individual engagements
⚠️ ⚠️ TACTICAL SUCCESS DOES NOT AGGREGATE INTO STRATEGIC
   SUCCESS. ⚠️ This is the single most important thing in this
   section. ⚠️ A military can win every engagement and lose the
   war, because the war is decided by whether the political
   objective is achieved (§2)
   ⚠️ Vietnam is the canonical Western example; the point
   generalizes
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CORRESPONDING ERROR RUNS UPWARD TOO  ⚠️ a strategic
   objective the military instrument cannot deliver — "defeat
   an ideology," "create a stable democracy" — is not made
   achievable by tactical excellence
⚠️ ⚠️ THE GRAND STRATEGIC LEVEL sits above all three (§12),
   integrating diplomatic, informational, military and economic
   instruments — ⚠️ and military force is usually the least
   efficient of them
```

## §6. Principles and Their Limits

**⚠️ The classic lists** — ⚠️ **objective, offensive, mass, economy of force, manoeuvre, unity
of command, security, surprise, simplicity, and national variants — are useful mnemonics.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — they are heuristics, not laws, and several contradict each other.**
> ⚠️ **Mass conflicts with economy of force; surprise conflicts with security; simplicity
> conflicts with sophistication.** **⚠️ Judgement lies in knowing which principle governs in
> a given situation, which is exactly what the list cannot tell you — and treating them as a
> checklist is a recognized failure mode.**

**⚠️ Lanchester's laws** model attrition mathematically — ⚠️ **the square law suggesting that
for aimed fire, combat power scales with the SQUARE of numbers, which is the formal argument
for concentration.** ⚠️ **Their empirical validity is contested and they are best treated as
an illustration of why numbers matter more than linearly, not as a predictive tool.**
**⚠️ Force ratios** — ⚠️ **rules of thumb about attacker-to-defender advantage are widely
cited, weakly grounded, and highly dependent on terrain, preparation and quality.**

## §7. Manoeuvre, Attrition and Decision Cycles

**⚠️ The manoeuvre-versus-attrition distinction**: ⚠️ **attrition seeks to destroy the
opponent's forces; manoeuvre seeks to break their cohesion and decision-making so that
their remaining strength becomes irrelevant.**
**⚠️ Boyd's OODA loop** — ⚠️ **observe, orient, decide, act — with the argument that operating
inside an opponent's decision cycle causes disorientation and collapse.** ⚠️ **Note that
ORIENT is the part Boyd considered decisive and the part popular versions omit; the loop is
usually reduced to "be fast," which loses the point.**
**⚠️ Mission command (Auftragstaktik)** — ⚠️ **specify the intent and the task, delegate the
method.** ⚠️ **It requires trust, shared doctrine and tolerance of subordinate error, which
is why it is easy to declare and hard to practise** (§17 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`).
**⚠️ The honest caveat**: ⚠️ **the manoeuvrist school became somewhat doctrinaire in
Western militaries, and §26.1 → `mil-reference` shows attrition returning to prominence — the two are
complementary rather than opposed.**

# Military Science: What's Live, Misconceptions, Concepts and Figures, and Sources

> **Part 6 of 6** of the *Military Science and National Defence* reference (plugin `military-science-and-national-defence`), covering §26–§31. Sibling skills: `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power` (§0–§4), `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles` (§5–§7), `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy` (§8–§12), `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement` (§13–§18), `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law` (§19–§25). Section numbers are shared across the set; a reference written as §N → `skill` points into that sibling skill.
>
> **Currency:** The theory is old and durable. Two areas are moving. See §26 for what Ukraine has actually demonstrated, and European rearmament.

> **⚠️ SCOPE. This is pitched where a war college survey course sits — strategy, doctrine,
> institutions and policy.** ⚠️ **It contains no operational, tactical or technical
> instruction, no weapons information, and nothing that would assist anyone in causing
> harm. The material here is what an informed citizen, policy analyst or historian needs
> to reason about defence — and it is deliberately at the level where the interesting
> questions actually live.**
>
> **Complements a resource-extraction reference (strategic materials), a semiconductor
> reference (export controls and technology competition), a wireless reference (spectrum
> and electronic warfare), and a speaking-and-influence reference (§18's persuasion
> evidence bears directly on §21 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`).**
>
> **⚠️ GOTCHA** boxes mark where popular strategic intuition is reliably wrong.
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ WAR IS A POLITICAL INSTRUMENT OR IT IS NOTHING** (§2 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`). **Clausewitz's central
>    claim is not a slogan. Military action detached from a political objective it can
>    actually achieve is the recurring cause of strategic failure, and the pattern repeats
>    across two centuries.**
> 2. **⚠️ DEFENCE IS AN ECONOMIC AND INDUSTRIAL PROBLEM BEFORE IT IS A MILITARY ONE**
>    (§14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §26.2). **Logistics, industrial capacity and the ability to replace losses
>    decide protracted wars. Amateurs discuss tactics; the professional literature discusses
>    sustainment.**
> 3. **⚠️ THE HARDEST CAPABILITY TO COPY IS INSTITUTIONAL LEARNING** (§15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §26.1). **Any
>    state can buy equipment. What distinguishes effective militaries is the speed at which
>    they absorb, adapt and redistribute what they learn — and that is organizational, not
>    technological.**

## §26. What's Live — checked August 2026

### 26.1 ⚠️ What Ukraine has demonstrated — and the argument about it
**⚠️ §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s institutional-learning problem made concrete, with a genuine analytical dispute
that is worth understanding rather than resolving.**

- **⚠️ THE SCALE OF THE SHIFT IS NOT DISPUTED.** ⚠️ **Australian Army research assesses
  tactical uncrewed aerial vehicles as accounting for 60–70% of Russian materiel attrition
  while inflicting approximately 80% of battlefield casualties and providing 86% of
  targeting data available to Ukrainian commanders** — ⚠️ **"performing at twice the combined
  lethality and intelligence utility of every other weapons system."** ⚠️ **NATO figures
  cited by CSIS attribute more than 65% of destroyed Russian tanks to Ukrainian drones.**
- **⚠️ THE ECONOMIC INVERSION IS THE STRATEGIC POINT** (§19 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **cheap systems destroying
  expensive ones at scale, described as a shift from "exquisite scarcity" to "intelligent
  mass" — large numbers of good-enough, networked, attritable systems.**
  ⚠️ **CEPA frames it as "mass is back — in the form of thousands of cheap drones."**
- **⚠️ THE PRODUCTION DIMENSION** (§14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`): ⚠️ **Ukraine reportedly planned 7 million
  military drones in 2026, up from 4 million produced the previous year — which is an
  industrial statistic, not a technological one.**
- **⚠️ CONTESTED AIR AND ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM.** ⚠️ **CSIS notes neither side achieved
  air superiority, contributing to the war's positional and attritional character — and that
  this "contrasts sharply with Western doctrines of air power that evolved from the Gulf
  War"** (§4 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`).
- **⚠️ COMMERCIAL SPACE AS A TACTICAL DEPENDENCY** (§20 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **IFRI assesses that commercial
  satellite communications, navigation and Earth observation now underpin daily operations —
  effectively a "space-enabled Internet of the Battlefield" — while creating vulnerabilities
  through single-provider dependence, electronic warfare exposure and geofencing risk.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — there is a serious argument that the obvious lesson is the wrong one, and it
> deserves weight.** ⚠️ **A Modern War Institute piece argues that drone proliferation has
> "revived and deepened, rather than diminished" reliance on attrition over manoeuvre,
> reaffirming artillery as decisive — and warns that a US Army optimizing for drones could
> lose its manoeuvre advantage.**
> ⚠️ **The deeper version comes from Breaking Defense: what Ukraine actually built was not a
> drone fleet but "an institution capable of absorbing new technology, adapting doctrine and
> returning improved capabilities to the front in weeks rather than years" — and the
> conclusion is that "any government can buy Ukraine's drones. The advantage will belong to
> those who build what Ukraine actually built: a system that learns."**
> **⚠️ That is §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement` and §1 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`'s third organizing idea, stated by a practitioner.**

**⚠️ And the institutional-learning failure is documented rather than alleged.** ⚠️ **The
Lowy Institute notes a 2024 investigation finding key US military study centres treating the
war as one topic among many — with the Marine Corps not having directly interviewed
Ukrainian personnel and the Navy unable to describe its approach — and that Congress
legislated a reporting requirement in the FY2025 NDAA.** ⚠️ **Lowy's own observation is the
sharpest: "that such a requirement needed to be legislated into existence is itself a
measure of the depth of the institutional failure."** ⚠️ **Progress is reported in 2026,
including a US Army force-wide effort to redevelop drone doctrine.**
**⚠️ Sourcing note: this draws on think tanks and military research centres across several
countries with differing institutional positions, which is why I have presented the dispute
rather than a verdict.** ⚠️ **Casualty and attrition percentages originate substantially in
Ukrainian claims and should be read as contested.**

### 26.2 ⚠️ European rearmament and the 5% pledge
**⚠️ §11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s burden-sharing dispute and §18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s industrial-base problem meeting a concrete
commitment.**

- **⚠️ WHAT WAS AGREED.** ⚠️ **At the June 2025 Hague Summit, NATO allies committed to
  investing 5% of GDP annually on defence by 2035, structured in two parts: at least 3.5% of
  GDP for core defence requirements and NATO Capability Targets, plus 1.5% for broader
  defence- and security-related areas.** ⚠️ **This more than doubles the 2% guideline set at
  Wales in 2014.**
- ⚠️ **National roadmaps were due by mid-2026, with a collective progress review scheduled
  for 2029 and the final deadline in 2035.** ⚠️ **The 2026 summit in Ankara was the first
  major test of whether roadmaps represent genuine investment.**
- **⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL CHANGES ARE REAL.** ⚠️ **Germany and Sweden rewrote debt rules to
  accommodate the increase — with Germany's constitutional "debt brake" amendment described
  as perhaps the most consequential structural change the commitment produced.**

> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the spending figure is the least informative part, and several serious
> analysts say so.** ⚠️ **SIPRI's assessment is that the commitment "was primarily about
> sending political messages" while carrying significant fiscal and operational
> implications, and SIPRI researchers have warned specifically about "creative accounting"
> in how the 1.5% category is counted.**
> ⚠️ **DEFENCE COST INFLATION means nominal increases translate into smaller real capability
> gains than the numbers imply — the same arithmetic as an infrastructure reference §27.1.**
> ⚠️ **And a 2026 academic assessment argues that focusing narrowly on expenditure targets
> "risks obscuring more fundamental challenges," concluding that security depends "less on
> uniform spending benchmarks than on effective capabilities, societal resilience and
> coordinated contributions"** (§23 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`).

**⚠️ The industrial base is the binding constraint** (§18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`): ⚠️ **analysis points to
FRAGMENTATION across European producers causing "wasted investments, higher production
costs, the loss of economies of scale, strained supply chains," with underspending having
aged production lines and constrained surge capacity — and European defence firms described
as too small to compete globally.**
**⚠️ The political economy is the other constraint** (§11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, §24 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`): ⚠️ **high debt and social
spending are identified as the biggest impediments, with "guns or butter" trade-offs likely
to generate electoral grievance, and uneven threat perceptions across members risking wider
internal divides.**
**⚠️ Sourcing note: the pledge structure and dates come from NATO's own Hague Declaration
and topic pages — primary.** ⚠️ **The critical assessments come from SIPRI, academic
economists and think tanks spanning quite different policy positions, including
restraint-oriented ones; I have reported the criticisms because they converge from sources
that disagree about almost everything else.**

## §27. Misconceptions

| Misconception | Correction |
|---|---|
| "War is politics by other means" is cynical | ⚠️ **It's normative — force detached from achievable policy fails** (§2 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`) |
| Winning battles wins wars | ⚠️ **Tactical success does not aggregate to strategic success** (§5 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`) |
| Sun Tzu is the sophisticated alternative to Clausewitz | ⚠️ **Misreads both. Different contexts, overlapping conclusions** (§3 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`) |
| Strategic bombing breaks enemy will | ⚠️ **Repeatedly predicted, repeatedly disappointing** (§4 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`) |
| The principles of war are rules | ⚠️ **Heuristics, and several contradict each other** (§6 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`) |
| OODA means "be fast" | ⚠️ **Boyd thought ORIENT was decisive** (§7 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`) |
| Deterrence is about capability | ⚠️ **It's psychological. Credibility is the hard part** (§8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`) |
| Deterrence working can be verified | ⚠️ **No attack is consistent with no intent to attack** (§8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`) |
| Compellence is deterrence in reverse | ⚠️ **Much harder — it requires visible compliance** (§8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`) |
| Nuclear deterrence has worked reliably | ⚠️ **Compatible with having been repeatedly lucky** (§9 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`) |
| Alliances mainly aggregate power | ⚠️ **They also constrain members. Abandonment vs entrapment** (§11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`) |
| Support troops are overhead | ⚠️ **They're what makes combat power sustainable** (§13 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`) |
| Wars are decided by weapons | ⚠️ **Protracted wars are decided by sustainment** (§14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`) |
| Militaries learn from experience | ⚠️ **Identifying a lesson ≠ changing the institution** (§15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, §26.1) |
| Intelligence failure means missing information | ⚠️ **Usually failure to believe available warning** (§16 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`) |
| Better comms enable better command | ⚠️ **They enable micromanagement from a distance** (§17 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`) |
| Procurement overruns are mismanagement | ⚠️ **Structural — monopsony, requirements creep, politics** (§18 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`) |
| Cyber will produce a decisive strike | ⚠️ **Predicted for 20+ years. It's a shaping contest** (§20 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Microtargeting drives influence ops | ⚠️ **Measured effects are small; exhaustion is the aim** (§21 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Counterinsurgency is about territory | ⚠️ **Population and legitimacy — and outsiders can't supply it** (§22 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Defence is the military's job | ⚠️ **Total defence: resilience IS deterrence** (§23 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Civilian control means civilians are right | ⚠️ **It means they get to be wrong** (§24 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Just cause permits more in combat | ⚠️ **IHL applies equally regardless of who started it** (§25 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Proportionality compares casualties | ⚠️ **Incidental harm vs anticipated military advantage** (§25 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) |
| Drones made manoeuvre obsolete | ⚠️ **Contested — some argue they deepened attrition** (§26.1) |
| Buy the drones, get the capability | ⚠️ **What Ukraine built was a system that learns** (§26.1) |
| 5% of GDP means 2.5× the capability | ⚠️ **Cost inflation and accounting definitions** (§26.2) |

## §28. Concepts and Figures

```
⚠️ Clausewitz's trinity  ⚠️ passion (people) · chance (army) ·
   reason (government) — unstable between all three
⚠️ Levels of war  strategic · operational · tactical
   ⚠️ (+ grand strategic above)
⚠️ Deterrence requires  ⚠️ capability + CREDIBILITY + communication
⚠️ Deterrence forms  ⚠️ punishment vs DENIAL (usually more credible)
⚠️ IHL core principles  ⚠️ distinction · proportionality ·
   military necessity · humanity
⚠️ ⚠️ Proportionality  ⚠️ incidental civilian harm vs ANTICIPATED
   MILITARY ADVANTAGE — not a casualty comparison
⚠️ ⚠️ UAV share of Russian materiel attrition  ⚠️ 60-70%
⚠️ ⚠️ UAV share of battlefield casualties  ⚠️ ~80%
⚠️ ⚠️ UAV share of Ukrainian targeting data  ⚠️ 86%
   (⚠️ all three: Australian Army Research Centre, contested)
⚠️ Drones and destroyed Russian tanks  ⚠️ >65% (NATO, via CSIS)
⚠️ Ukrainian drone production  ⚠️ 4m (2025) → 7m planned (2026)
⚠️ ⚠️ NATO Hague pledge  ⚠️ 5% of GDP by 2035 =
   3.5% core defence + 1.5% defence-related
⚠️ Previous benchmark  ⚠️ 2% (Wales, 2014)
⚠️ Roadmaps due mid-2026 · ⚠️ review 2029 · deadline 2035
```

### 30.2 Evaluating a defence claim
- [ ] ⚠️ **What is the POLITICAL objective, and can force deliver it?** (§2 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Which level of war is this claim about?** (§5 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`)
- [ ] Ends, ways and means actually matched? (§12 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`)
- [ ] ⚠️ **Is sustainment addressed, or only the platform?** (§14 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`)

- [ ] ⚠️ **Are the legal constraints stated accurately?** (§25 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`)

## §31. Method

**§1–§25 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`, `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`, `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`, `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law` rests on an established academic and professional literature** — **Clausewitz and
the strategic canon, Schelling on deterrence and coercion, the levels of war, the logistics
literature, military innovation studies, civil-military relations theory, and international
humanitarian law.** ⚠️ **None needed verification; *On War* is from 1832 and Schelling's
work is sixty years old and still the reference.**

**⚠️ On scope: I have deliberately kept this at the strategic and institutional level.**
⚠️ **There is nothing operational, tactical or technical here — no targeting, no weapons
detail, no methods.** ⚠️ **That is not evasion: the questions that actually determine
whether a country is defended well are the ones in Parts II to IV, and they are exactly the
ones public discussion skips in favour of hardware.**

**Two searches were run in August 2026**, on **Ukraine's demonstrated lessons** and
**European rearmament** — ⚠️ **the first because §15 → `mil-force-structure-logistics-doctrine-intelligence-and-procurement`'s institutional-learning problem is
being tested in public and the answer is genuinely disputed, the second because §11 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s
burden-sharing argument has become a concrete commitment with measurable milestones.**

**Confidence.** **High** in §5 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles` and §8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`, which are the sections I'd most want read.
⚠️ **"Tactical success does not aggregate into strategic success" is the correction that
most improves anyone's reading of a war, in either direction — it explains both how a
militarily dominant power loses and why counting engagements tells you little.**
⚠️ **§8 → `mil-deterrence-nuclear-strategy-alliances-and-grand-strategy`'s point that deterrence is unfalsifiable in principle is the one that most improves
scrutiny of defence budgets: absence of attack is consistent with successful deterrence and
with an opponent who was never going to attack, and no amount of spending resolves that
ambiguity.**
**⚠️ §25 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`'s proportionality definition is the correction I would most want carried into
public argument, because the casualty-comparison misuse is nearly universal and the actual
legal test is quite different.**

**Moderate-to-high** on §26.1. ⚠️ **The scale of the drone shift is corroborated across
research centres in several countries; the specific percentages originate substantially in
Ukrainian claims and I have marked them contested.**
⚠️ **What I have deliberately NOT done is resolve the analytical dispute — because a Modern
War Institute argument that drones have deepened attrition rather than replaced manoeuvre,
and the Breaking Defense argument that the transferable lesson is institutional rather than
technological, are both serious and neither is settled.** **⚠️ The documented
lessons-learned failure, legislated into an NDAA reporting requirement, is the most concrete
evidence in the section.**

**High** on §26.2's facts, which come from NATO's own declaration: ⚠️ **5% by 2035 split
3.5/1.5, roadmaps due mid-2026, review in 2029.**
⚠️ **The critical assessments come from SIPRI, academic economists and think tanks
spanning restraint-oriented and Atlanticist positions — and I have reported them because
they CONVERGE from sources that disagree about nearly everything else, which is the
strongest form of corroboration available on a politically contested subject.**
**⚠️ I have described the debates rather than taking positions on contested defence policy
questions, which are political judgements about interests and risk rather than technical
ones.**

## §13. Force Structure

**⚠️ The services and their logics**: ⚠️ **land forces hold and control territory and people;
naval forces control access and communication; air forces provide reach and speed; and the
newer space and cyber organizations provide enablers everything else depends on.**
**⚠️ Readiness versus modernization versus force size** is the permanent trilemma —
⚠️ **any budget must trade among a ready force now, a modern force later, and a large
force.**
**⚠️ The tooth-to-tail ratio** — ⚠️ **and the persistent misunderstanding that support is
overhead. It is the thing that makes combat power sustainable** (§14).
**⚠️ Manpower models**: ⚠️ **all-volunteer forces, conscription, and reserve systems — with
genuine trade-offs between professional quality, cost, mobilization depth and the political
question of who bears the burden** (§23 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`, §24 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`).
**⚠️ Force generation and rotation** — ⚠️ **sustaining a deployment requires several units
for each one deployed, which is why deployable strength is a fraction of total strength and
why this surprises people looking at headline numbers.**

## §14. ⚠️ Logistics

> **⚠️ §1 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`'s second organizing idea. The literature is unanimous and public discussion
> ignores it.**
```
⚠️ ⚠️ "AMATEURS TALK TACTICS; PROFESSIONALS TALK LOGISTICS" is a
   cliché because it keeps being demonstrated
⚠️ WHAT IT COVERS  ⚠️ fuel · ammunition · food and water ·
   maintenance and spares · medical · transport · engineering ·
   and the movement of all of it
⚠️ ⚠️ CONSUMPTION RATES IN HIGH-INTENSITY WAR EXCEED PEACETIME
   ASSUMPTIONS BY LARGE MARGINS — ⚠️ and Ukraine demonstrated
   that Western artillery ammunition stockpiles and production
   rates were sized for a conflict far shorter and less
   intense than the one that occurred (§26.2)
⚠️ ⚠️ THE CULMINATING POINT (§2) IS USUALLY A LOGISTICS
   PHENOMENON  ⚠️ offensives stop when they outrun supply, not
   when they run out of courage
⚠️ ⚠️ STRATEGIC MOBILITY  ⚠️ sealift and airlift capacity
   determine what can actually be moved and how fast —
   ⚠️ and the overwhelming majority of tonnage moves by sea
   (see a maritime reference), which makes shipping a strategic
   asset that is rarely counted as one
⚠️ ⚠️ THE INDUSTRIAL BASE IS PART OF LOGISTICS  ⚠️ the ability
   to REPLACE losses, which in a protracted war matters more
   than the initial inventory (§18, §26.2)
⚠️ HOST NATION SUPPORT, prepositioning, and — ⚠️ the modern
   vulnerability — supply chains that are globally distributed
   and therefore exposed (see a resource-extraction reference
   §27.1 on critical minerals)
```

## §15. ⚠️ Doctrine and Institutional Learning

> **⚠️ §1 → `mil-theory-clausewitz-sun-tzu-and-sea-and-air-power`'s third organizing idea, and §26.1 → `mil-reference`'s central finding.**
```
⚠️ DOCTRINE IS SHARED PROFESSIONAL UNDERSTANDING — ⚠️ how this
   military fights, so that units who have never met can
   cooperate. ⚠️ Its function is COORDINATION as much as
   correctness
⚠️ ⚠️ THE DOCTRINE PARADOX  ⚠️ doctrine that is too rigid
   prevents adaptation; doctrine that is too loose prevents
   coordination. ⚠️ Both failure modes are observable
⚠️ TRAINING  ⚠️ realistic force-on-force training against a
   thinking opponent is expensive and repeatedly shown to be
   the highest-return investment in effectiveness
⚠️ ⚠️ LESSONS LEARNED SYSTEMS ARE WHERE MILITARIES MOST OFTEN
   FAIL. ⚠️ Identifying a lesson is easy; ⚠️ changing doctrine,
   training, procurement and organization to reflect it is
   slow and threatens established interests
⚠️ ⚠️ MILITARY INNOVATION RESEARCH consistently finds that
   PEACETIME innovation requires either external shock,
   civilian intervention, or a service seeking a new mission —
   ⚠️ and that organizations rarely innovate against their own
   dominant career paths and budget lines
⚠️ ⚠️ THE ADAPTATION SPEED PROBLEM  ⚠️ what matters is not who
   fields a technology first but who can absorb, integrate and
   redistribute it fastest (§26.1). ⚠️ That is an
   ORGANIZATIONAL capability and it is much harder to buy than
   equipment
```

## §16. Intelligence

**⚠️ The cycle**: ⚠️ **direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination — and the
observation that the cycle model is tidier than practice.**
**⚠️ The disciplines**: ⚠️ **HUMINT, SIGINT, IMINT/GEOINT, MASINT, and ⚠️ OSINT, whose
importance has grown enormously with commercial satellite imagery and social media —
to the point where open sources now do work that once required national technical means.**
**⚠️ The analytical failures** are well studied: ⚠️ **mirror imaging, confirmation bias,
politicization, and the SIGNAL-TO-NOISE problem — ⚠️ Pearl Harbor and 9/11 both involved
available indicators lost in volume, which is why "we had the information" and "we knew" are
different claims.**
**⚠️ Warning versus surprise**: ⚠️ **strategic surprise usually reflects a failure to BELIEVE
rather than a failure to collect, because the warning contradicted an existing assumption.**
**⚠️ Counterintelligence and deception** — ⚠️ **and the deception literature's finding that
the most effective deception reinforces what the target already expects.**

## §17. Command and Control

**⚠️ Unity of command versus unity of effort** — ⚠️ **the latter is what coalitions actually
achieve, and the difference matters.**
**⚠️ Centralized versus decentralized execution**: ⚠️ **modern communications make
centralization technically possible, which creates the temptation to micromanage from a
distance — the "strategic corporal" and its inverse, the "tactical general."**
**⚠️ Mission command** (§7 → `mil-levels-of-war-principles-and-decision-cycles`) requires ⚠️ **commander's intent stated clearly enough that
subordinates can act without instruction when communications fail — which is also the
resilience argument for it.**
**⚠️ Joint and combined operations** — ⚠️ **integrating services and nations, where doctrine,
terminology and equipment differences generate most of the friction.**
**⚠️ The modern vulnerability**: ⚠️ **command systems dependent on networks and space assets
that an opponent will attack** (§20 → `mil-modern-conflict-irregular-warfare-resilience-and-the-law`) — ⚠️ **hence degraded-operations training and the
return of interest in operating without connectivity.**

## §18. ⚠️ Defence Procurement

```
⚠️ ⚠️ THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM  ⚠️ a monopsony buyer, few
   suppliers, long development cycles, changing requirements,
   and political constituencies attached to production —
   ⚠️ which produces predictable cost growth and schedule slip
   (the same pattern as an infrastructure reference §20, for
   the same underlying reasons)
⚠️ ⚠️ REQUIREMENTS CREEP is the dominant technical cause.
   ⚠️ Each added requirement is individually reasonable and the
   accumulation is what produces unaffordable programmes
⚠️ ⚠️ THE EXQUISITE-VERSUS-MASS DEBATE is the live one
   ⚠️ Small numbers of highly capable, expensive platforms vs
   large numbers of cheaper, attritable ones
   ⚠️ ⚠️ Ukraine has pushed this argument hard toward mass —
   and §26.1 contains the caveat
⚠️ ⚠️ THE INDUSTRIAL BASE PROBLEM  ⚠️ decades of low-rate
   production optimized for peacetime efficiency produced
   supply chains with NO SURGE CAPACITY. ⚠️ Reconstituting it
   requires sustained, predictable orders — and the political
   system supplies neither reliably
⚠️ ⚠️ THE INNOVATION MISMATCH  ⚠️ procurement cycles measured in
   decades against technology cycles measured in months
   (§26.1). ⚠️ This is arguably the central defence-management
   problem of the period
⚠️ EXPORT CONTROLS and technology transfer (see a
   semiconductor reference §26) · offsets · the political
   economy of the defence industrial constituency
```

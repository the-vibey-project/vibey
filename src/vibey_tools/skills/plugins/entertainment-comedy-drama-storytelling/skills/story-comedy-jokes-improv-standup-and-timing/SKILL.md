## §12. ⚠️ How Comedy Works

**⚠️ The theories, none of which is complete, and each of which explains part:**
```
⚠️ INCONGRUITY        the dominant account. Humour arises from a violation
   of expectation that then RESOLVES into a new frame. ⚠️ Best-supported,
   and explains the most jokes
SUPERIORITY           we laugh at others' misfortune or folly (Hobbes).
   ⚠️ Explains mockery and slapstick; fails on wordplay
RELIEF                release of nervous energy (Freud). ⚠️ Explains taboo
   humour and nervous laughter; weak generally
⚠️ BENIGN VIOLATION   (McGraw) — something violates how the world OUGHT to
   be, AND is simultaneously perceived as benign. ⚠️ The most useful
   modern synthesis, and it explains the FAILURES: too benign = boring,
   too violating = offensive (§19)
```
**⚠️ The benign violation frame is genuinely predictive and worth internalizing**:
⚠️ **whether something is funny depends on psychological distance — temporal ("too soon"),
social (in-group vs out-group), spatial, and hypothetical.** **The same joke lands or
bombs based entirely on that distance, which is why context and audience are not
incidental to comedy but constitutive of it.**
**⚠️ Comedy is unusually empirical for a craft**: ⚠️ **stand-ups and comedy writers test
material on live audiences and iterate, which is a genuine feedback loop most art forms
lack.** **This is why comedians are often more technically precise about their craft than
dramatists.**

## §13. Joke Mechanics

```
⚠️ SETUP → PUNCHLINE. The setup creates an assumption; the punchline
   reveals a second, incompatible interpretation that was always available
⚠️ MISDIRECTION   the setup must point AWAY from the punchline while
   remaining honest — the reinterpretation has to be legitimately supported
⚠️ THE CONNECTOR  the ambiguous element the two interpretations hinge on
⚠️ ECONOMY        the funny word goes LAST. Any word after the punchline
   dissipates it. ⚠️ This is the most mechanical rule in comedy and it's real
⚠️ SPECIFICITY    "a dog" is less funny than "a beagle." Concrete and
   specific beats general, almost always
⚠️ HARD CONSONANTS  the "K sound" folklore — ⚠️ weakly evidenced, widely
   repeated. Rhythm is real; the phonetics claim is mostly tradition
```
**⚠️ Tags** — **additional punchlines off the same setup** — ⚠️ **are the cheapest laughs
available once you've done the work of the setup, and undervalued by beginners.**
**⚠️ Act-outs** — **performing a character rather than describing them** — **consistently
outperform narration in live comedy.**

## §14. Comedic Structure

```
⚠️ RULE OF THREE   establish pattern, confirm pattern, BREAK pattern.
   ⚠️ Two isn't enough to establish; four is usually one too many
⚠️ CALLBACK        rewards attention and creates the pleasure of an in-group.
   ⚠️ The most reliable structural device in long-form comedy
⚠️ ESCALATION      each beat raises the absurdity while remaining internally
   logical. ⚠️ The logic is what makes escalation work rather than just noise
⚠️ RUNNER          a repeated element across a piece
⚠️ HEIGHTEN AND EXPLORE  (from improv, §16) — find the unusual thing and
   pursue its implications rather than adding new unusual things
⚠️ COMIC PREMISE / "GAME"  ⚠️ the single most important concept in modern
   comedy writing: identify the one unusual pattern, then play IT
   repeatedly with variation, rather than being randomly funny
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "random" is not a comedic technique, and this is the most common failure
> in amateur comedy.** ⚠️ **Comedy needs INTERNAL LOGIC — the situation is absurd, and
> the characters behave consistently within it.** **The funniest scenes are usually people
> taking an insane premise entirely seriously.** **⚠️ Randomness has no pattern to violate,
> so there's nothing to be incongruous WITH** (§12).

## §15. Character Comedy and Status

**⚠️ Status is the underrated engine** (Johnstone): ⚠️ **most comic scenes are about status
transactions — who's high, who's low, and what happens when it inverts.** **The pompous
person humiliated, the servant who's smarter than the master, the incompetent given
authority.**
**⚠️ Comic character construction**: **a character is funny when a single strong trait
collides with a situation that punishes it.** ⚠️ **The trait must be consistent — comic
characters are usually LESS flexible than dramatic ones, and their inflexibility is the
engine.**
**⚠️ The straight man is not a lesser role** — ⚠️ **the reaction is frequently where the
laugh is, and comedy without a grounded perspective has nothing to measure the absurdity
against.**
**⚠️ Comedy of manners vs farce vs satire vs parody vs absurdism** — **and ⚠️ satire
requires a target and a position; without them it's just mockery.**

## §16. Improv

**⚠️ The principles transfer far beyond performance — into writing rooms, meetings and
teaching:**
```
⚠️ "YES, AND"        accept the offer and add. ⚠️ Blocking (denying the
   premise) kills scenes. ⚠️ Note the widespread misreading: it does NOT
   mean agree with everything — it means accept the REALITY established
⚠️ MAKE YOUR PARTNER LOOK GOOD  ⚠️ the actual core ethic
⚠️ FIND THE GAME     identify the first unusual thing and play it (§14)
⚠️ SPECIFICITY and emotional commitment over cleverness
⚠️ DON'T TRY TO BE FUNNY  ⚠️ play the truth of the situation; the comedy
   comes from behaviour, not from writing jokes in your head
```
**⚠️ Long-form (Harold) vs short-form (games)** — **and ⚠️ the great value of improv training
for writers is that it teaches scene-level instincts (§5 → `story-rules-character-structure-and-scene`): who wants what, what's the
first unusual thing, where's the turn.**

## §17. Standup

**⚠️ The most craft-explicit comedy form because the feedback is immediate and brutal.**
**⚠️ Structure**: **bit → tag → callback; opener, middle, closer; ⚠️ the "hour" as the unit
of ambition.**
**⚠️ Persona is the foundational choice** — ⚠️ **the audience must know who is talking to
them within roughly a minute.** **Every joke reads differently through a different
persona, which is why stealing another comic's material rarely works even mechanically.**
**⚠️ Crowd work, riffing, and handling hecklers are separate skills from writing.**
**⚠️ The development process is the thing outsiders underestimate**: ⚠️ **material is
written, tested in small rooms, revised based on where laughs actually land, and iterated
over months to years.** **A polished hour represents an enormous amount of failed
material** — **and ⚠️ tape your sets, because you will misremember what worked.**

## §18. Timing

**⚠️ Timing is rhythm and it is largely learnable, despite the folklore that it's innate.**
```
⚠️ THE PAUSE BEFORE the punchline creates anticipation
⚠️ THE PAUSE AFTER  lets the laugh happen. ⚠️ Talking over your own laugh
   is the most common novice error, and it kills the next joke too
⚠️ RIDING THE LAUGH  hold, then come in as it declines — not after silence
⚠️ ECONOMY          cut every word that isn't load-bearing (§13)
⚠️ RULE OF THREE rhythm  short, short, LONGER — the third beat carries the turn
```
**⚠️ In edited media, timing is largely made in the EDIT** (§23 → `story-acting-directing-medium-and-industry`) — ⚠️ **frame counts on a
reaction shot change whether something lands**, **which is why comedy editing is a
specialized skill and why the same performance can be funny or flat depending on the cut.**

## §19. ⚠️ Offence, Honestly

> **⚠️ I'm going to set out the craft considerations and the actual disagreement rather
> than adjudicate it, because the disagreement is genuine and both positions contain
> real arguments.**

**⚠️ What the mechanics tell us** (§12): **benign violation predicts that a joke fails in
two directions — too benign is boring, too violating is offensive** — ⚠️ **and where that
line falls depends on the audience's psychological distance from the subject, which
varies between people and shifts over time.** **⚠️ "Too soon" is a real and measurable
phenomenon; the same joke becomes acceptable as distance grows.**

**⚠️ The positions, each in its strongest form:**
- **⚠️ The transgressive case**: **comedy's social function is to say the unsayable, and
  taboo subjects are where the tension that fuels laughter lives.** **Sanitized comedy is
  weak comedy; audiences self-select; and a comedian who is never offensive to anyone is
  probably not being interesting to anyone either.**
- **⚠️ The "punching up" case**: **jokes carry a target and a perspective, and jokes that
  reinforce contempt toward people with less power do social work regardless of comedic
  intent.** **The direction of the joke is a legitimate craft consideration, not merely a
  political one.**
- **⚠️ The complication both sides underweight**: ⚠️ **"punching up/down" assumes a stable
  and agreed power hierarchy, and in practice people disagree about who is up and who is
  down.** **⚠️ And plenty of enduring comedy punches at everyone, including the comedian
  — self-deprecation is the oldest solution to the problem.**

**⚠️ The craft observation that survives the argument**: ⚠️ **the target matters, and
audiences read it accurately.** **A joke ABOUT a stereotype and a joke THAT IS the
stereotype are different objects, and audiences reliably distinguish them even when the
surface content is similar.** **⚠️ Comedians who get in trouble usually do so because the
target was ambiguous, not because the subject was forbidden.**

## §22. ⚠️ How Laundering Actually Works

**⚠️ SINNER'S CIRCLE: four factors that trade off against each other. Reduce one and you
must increase another to get the same result.**
```
⚠️ TEMPERATURE · CHEMISTRY · MECHANICAL ACTION · TIME
```
⚠️ **This is why cold washing works — modern detergents with effective enzymes and
surfactants substitute chemistry for temperature — and why cold washing with an old
detergent and a short cycle disappoints.**
```
DETERGENT COMPONENTS
  ⚠️ SURFACTANTS  lift and suspend soil (hydrophilic head, lipophilic tail)
  ⚠️ ENZYMES      protease (protein), amylase (starch), lipase (fat),
     ⚠️ cellulase (removes fuzz and pills — and gradually thins cotton)
  BUILDERS      soften water so surfactant isn't wasted
  ⚠️ OPTICAL BRIGHTENERS  fluorescent dyes that make white LOOK whiter.
     ⚠️ They don't clean anything, and they build up
  ⚠️ BLEACH  chlorine (⚠️ NOT on wool, silk, elastane) vs OXYGEN
     (percarbonate — gentler, colour-safe, ⚠️ needs warmth to activate)
```
**⚠️ Water hardness matters more than people think**: ⚠️ **hard water wastes surfactant and
deposits minerals that make fabric stiff and grey.** **In hard water use more detergent or
a builder, not more fabric softener.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — fabric softener is actively harmful to several fabric types.** ⚠️ **It
> works by depositing a cationic lubricant film, which destroys the absorbency of towels,
> ruins the wicking of technical activewear, degrades the flame-resistance of children's
> sleepwear, and builds up in microfibre.** **⚠️ For softening, white vinegar in the rinse
> or wool dryer balls do the job without the coating.**

**⚠️ Overloading is the most common laundering error** — ⚠️ **it removes the mechanical
action leg of Sinner's circle entirely**, **and clothes come out unclean regardless of what
you put in the drawer.**

## §23. Fibre-Specific Care

```
⚠️ COTTON/LINEN  tolerate heat and agitation. ⚠️ Shrinkage is the risk
   (unless pre-shrunk); hot drying is what actually shrinks garments
⚠️ WOOL  ⚠️ NEVER heat + moisture + agitation together — that's felting,
   and it is IRREVERSIBLE. Cool water, wool-specific (NEUTRAL or slightly
   acidic) detergent, ⚠️ NO alkali, no wringing. ⚠️ Dry FLAT — wet wool
   is heavy and hanging it stretches the garment permanently
⚠️ SILK  ⚠️ alkali-sensitive; no chlorine bleach; ⚠️ sunlight degrades it.
   Cool water, neutral detergent, avoid prolonged soaking
⚠️ RAYON/VISCOSE  ⚠️ VERY weak wet (§1). Support the weight; don't wring;
   often genuinely dry-clean-only
⚠️ POLYESTER  easy, but ⚠️ holds oils and odour. Wash promptly, use a
   detergent with lipase, avoid fabric softener, and ⚠️ avoid high heat
   which sets oily stains permanently
⚠️ ELASTANE BLENDS  ⚠️ heat kills elastane. Warm wash at most, air dry
DOWN  ⚠️ needs a specific detergent and thorough drying with agitation
   (dryer balls) or it clumps and mildews
```
**⚠️ "Dry clean only" vs "dry clean"**: ⚠️ **the former is a genuine warning (structure,
interfacing, dye, or fibre); the latter is often the manufacturer being cautious.**
**⚠️ Home dry-cleaning kits are a dryer-based refresh, not solvent cleaning.**

## §24. Stain Removal

```
⚠️ THE UNIVERSAL RULES
  ⚠️ BLOT, never rub — rubbing drives it in and abrades the fibre
  ⚠️ COLD water FIRST on unknown stains. ⚠️ HEAT SETS protein and many
     other stains permanently. This is the mistake that ruins garments
  ⚠️ Work from the OUTSIDE of the stain inward
  ⚠️ Test on a hidden area
  ⚠️ NEVER put a stained garment in the dryer — heat sets what remains

⚠️ BY CLASS — match the chemistry to the stain
  PROTEIN (blood, egg, dairy, sweat)  ⚠️ COLD water + enzyme (protease).
     ⚠️ Hot water cooks it into the fibre
  TANNIN (tea, coffee, wine, fruit)  ⚠️ hot water + detergent;
     ⚠️ NO soap-based bar (soap sets tannin)
  OIL/GREASE  ⚠️ solvent or a degreasing detergent, warm.
     ⚠️ Absorbent powder (cornstarch) first on fresh grease
  DYE (grass, ink, berry)  ⚠️ alcohol or oxygen bleach
  COMBINATION (makeup, gravy, chocolate)  ⚠️ treat the GREASE first,
     then the pigment
  RUST  ⚠️ acid/oxalic — ⚠️ chlorine bleach makes rust stains WORSE
```
**⚠️ Old stains that "reappear"** are ⚠️ **usually residual sugar or tannin that oxidizes,
or detergent residue wicking soil back** — **rinse more thoroughly.**

## §25. Drying, Ironing and Storage

**⚠️ Drying**: ⚠️ **heat is what causes most shrinkage, damages elastane, and sets stains.**
**Line-dry where practical; ⚠️ dry knits and wool FLAT; ⚠️ sun bleaches (useful on white
cotton, destructive on silk and coloured fabric).**
**⚠️ Lint in the dryer is your clothing eroding** — **and the lint filter is a fire hazard
when neglected.**
**⚠️ Ironing temperature by fibre** (⚠️ **synthetics melt — start low; use steam on cotton
and linen; press wool with a cloth; ⚠️ iron silk damp and on the reverse**).
**⚠️ Storage**: ⚠️ **clean before storing (soil and body oils attract pests and set into
stains); breathable cotton, not plastic (⚠️ plastic traps moisture and yellows textiles);
⚠️ fold knits, hang wovens; acid-free tissue for heirlooms; and ⚠️ never store in a
cardboard box long-term — the acid migrates.**

## §26. Mending and Pests

**⚠️ Mending**: **darning (⚠️ weaving a new patch across a hole), Swiss darning/duplicate
stitch for knits, patching (⚠️ from the inside, on the grain), sashiko and visible mending,
reweaving, and button and zip replacement.**
**⚠️ Reinforce BEFORE the hole**: ⚠️ **thin areas are far easier to save than holes, and
elbow, knee and crotch wear is predictable.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — clothes moths and carpet beetles eat KERATIN, so they attack wool, silk,
> fur and feathers, not cotton or synthetics.** ⚠️ **The larvae do the damage, not the
> adults, and they prefer soiled fabric — sweat, food and body oils are what attract
> them.** **⚠️ Therefore: CLEAN before storing is the single most effective control.**
> **⚠️ Cedar and lavender are repellents at best and do not kill larvae; freezing
> (several days at −18°C, ideally with a thaw and refreeze cycle) or thorough heat does.**
> **Sealed containers and vacuuming the dark undisturbed places — under furniture, closet
> corners — is the rest of it.**

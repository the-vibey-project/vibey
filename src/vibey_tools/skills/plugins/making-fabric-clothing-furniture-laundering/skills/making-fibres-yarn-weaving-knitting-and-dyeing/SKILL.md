# Fabric, Clothing and Furniture: Fibres, Yarn and Spinning, Weaving, Knitting, Nonwovens, Dyeing and Finishing, and Fabric Properties

> **Part 1 of 5** of the *Making Fabric, Clothing and Furniture — and Caring for Them* reference (plugin `making-fabric-clothing-furniture-laundering`), covering §0–§7. Sibling skills: `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction` (§8–§15), `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery` (§16–§21), `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending` (§22–§26), `making-reference` (§27–§32). Section numbers are shared across the set; a reference written as §N → `skill` points into that sibling skill.
>
> **Currency:** The crafts are centuries old and stable. Two areas moved. See §27 → `making-reference` for microfibre shedding and filter regulation, and PFAS restrictions in textiles and furniture.

> **⚠️ Four crafts unified by one principle: the MATERIAL'S behaviour determines
> everything downstream.** ⚠️ **Fibre decides yarn, which decides fabric, which decides
> what garment is possible and how it must be washed.** **Wood species and grain decide
> joinery, which decides what will survive a winter of dry indoor air.** **⚠️ Almost every
> failure in this document is someone fighting the material instead of designing with it.**
>
> **Complements a cooking/cleaning/waste reference (surfactant chemistry and disinfection
> in general), and a materials or thermo reference for the underlying physics.**
>
> **⚠️ GOTCHA** boxes mark the received wisdom that's wrong, and the mistakes that destroy
> the work irreversibly.
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ GRAIN is the master concept in both textiles and wood** (§8 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`, §16 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`). **Fabric
>    behaves differently along the warp, the weft and the bias; wood moves across the
>    grain and not along it. Both wreck the work when ignored.**
> 2. **⚠️ Wood MOVES, seasonally and forever** (§16 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery`). **Every joinery decision in §17 → `making-woodworking-joinery-finishing-and-upholstery` is
>    downstream of that one fact, and cross-grain construction is the single most common
>    reason furniture self-destructs.**
> 3. **⚠️ Laundering has four interchangeable levers** (§22 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`). **Temperature, chemistry,
>    mechanical action and time trade off against each other — which is why cold washing
>    works if you adjust the others, and fails if you don't.**

# PART I — FIBRE AND FABRIC

## §1. ⚠️ Fibres

**⚠️ Everything else follows from this table. Learn it and most care and construction
questions answer themselves.**
```
⚠️ CELLULOSIC (plant)
  COTTON   absorbent, comfortable, strong WET (⚠️ unusual — most fibres
     weaken wet), wrinkles, shrinks, no elastic recovery
  LINEN (flax)  ⚠️ very strong, highly absorbent, excellent conductor of
     heat (why it feels cool), creases badly, low elasticity, gets
     softer with washing
  RAYON/VISCOSE  ⚠️ regenerated cellulose — behaves like a weak cotton and
     is MUCH WEAKER WET. ⚠️ The single most common laundering disaster
  LYOCELL/TENCEL  regenerated, stronger wet, closed-loop solvent process

⚠️ PROTEIN (animal)
  WOOL   ⚠️ insulates when wet, resists wrinkles, flame-resistant,
     naturally elastic. ⚠️ FELTS irreversibly with heat + moisture +
     agitation (the scales interlock). Damaged by alkali (§23)
  SILK   strong, lustrous, ⚠️ weakened by sunlight and by alkali;
     water-spots on some weaves
  CASHMERE/ALPACA/MOHAIR  finer, warmer, weaker, more prone to pilling

⚠️ SYNTHETIC
  POLYESTER  ⚠️ strong, hydrophobic, holds a crease, resists wrinkles,
     ⚠️ HOLDS OILY SOIL AND ODOUR because it's oleophilic — that's why
     activewear smells even after washing
  NYLON/POLYAMIDE  ⚠️ the most abrasion-resistant common fibre; elastic
  ACRYLIC  wool-like, ⚠️ pills badly, sheds heavily (§27.1)
  ELASTANE/SPANDEX/LYCRA  ⚠️ degraded by heat, chlorine and oils. It is
     what dies first in a garment blend
  OLEFIN/POLYPROPYLENE  ⚠️ essentially unwettable — solution-dyed only
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the wet-strength inversion is the most useful single fact here.**
> ⚠️ **Cotton and linen get STRONGER when wet; rayon/viscose gets dramatically WEAKER; wool
> loses strength and becomes vulnerable to felting.** **⚠️ This is why you can scrub a
> cotton shirt and why wringing a wet rayon garment destroys it — the fibre is at its most
> fragile exactly when you're handling it hardest.**

**⚠️ Blends inherit the WORST care requirement of their components**, ⚠️ **not an average.**
**A cotton/elastane blend must be washed for the elastane.**

## §2. Yarn and Spinning

**⚠️ Staple (short fibres spun together) vs FILAMENT (continuous).** ⚠️ **Silk and all
synthetics are natively filament; everything else is staple, and synthetics are often cut
into staple deliberately to mimic natural hand.**
**⚠️ TWIST is the variable that does the work**: ⚠️ **more twist gives strength and a
harder, cooler hand; less twist gives softness and loft.** **Crepe yarns are extremely
high-twist, which is what produces their texture.**
**Ply** (⚠️ **plied yarns are rounder, stronger and more balanced than singles — and
singles skew, which matters in knitting**), **and yarn COUNT systems** (⚠️ **confusingly
inverted: in cotton count and worsted count HIGHER = FINER, while in denier and tex
HIGHER = HEAVIER**).
**⚠️ Worsted vs woollen preparation**: ⚠️ **worsted combs fibres parallel for a smooth,
strong, dense yarn; woollen leaves them jumbled for a lofty, insulating, weaker yarn.**
**Same fleece, entirely different fabric.**

## §3. Weaving

```
WARP  ⚠️ lengthwise, under tension on the loom — stronger, more stable
WEFT (filling)  crosswise
⚠️ SELVEDGE  the self-finished lengthwise edges

BASIC WEAVES
⚠️ PLAIN    over-one-under-one. Most interlacings → firmest, most stable,
   least drape. Poplin, muslin, chiffon, canvas
⚠️ TWILL    diagonal float line. Fewer interlacings → more drape, better
   abrasion resistance, hides soil. ⚠️ Denim, gabardine, chino
⚠️ SATIN    long floats → maximum lustre and drape, ⚠️ poor abrasion
   resistance and snags easily. The floats ARE the weakness
DERIVATIVES  basketweave, rib, herringbone, jacquard (⚠️ patterned by
   individual warp control — the loom that inspired punched cards)
PILE  velvet, corduroy, terry — ⚠️ pile has DIRECTION (nap), which must
   run consistently through a garment or panels will read as different colours
```
**⚠️ The structural point**: ⚠️ **fewer interlacings per unit area = more drape, more
lustre, less stability, less abrasion resistance.** **That single tradeoff explains the
entire weave family.**

## §4. Knitting

**⚠️ Knits are LOOPS, not interlacings — which is why they stretch and wovens don't.**
```
⚠️ WEFT KNIT   one continuous yarn across. ⚠️ CAN RUN/LADDER when broken
   Jersey (⚠️ curls at the edges — a structural consequence of stitch
   asymmetry, not a defect), rib (stretches crosswise, doesn't curl),
   interlock (stable, both faces alike), purl
⚠️ WARP KNIT   many yarns lengthwise. ⚠️ Does NOT run. Tricot, raschel
GAUGE  needles per inch — finer gauge, finer fabric
```
**⚠️ Practical consequences**: ⚠️ **knits need stretch stitches or a serger (§11 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`) because a
straight lockstitch will SNAP when the fabric stretches; they generally don't need much
ease (§9 → `making-garment-drafting-seams-and-construction`) since the fabric provides it; and they grow and recover rather than holding a
shape.**
**⚠️ Hand knitting and crochet** — ⚠️ **crochet uses substantially more yarn than knitting
for the same area and produces a denser, less elastic fabric.** **⚠️ Gauge swatching is not
optional: a half-stitch-per-inch error compounds across a garment into inches.**

## §5. Nonwovens and Felt

**⚠️ Fabric made without yarn.** **⚠️ True felt is WOOL matted by heat, moisture, agitation
and alkali — the scales interlock irreversibly, which is the same mechanism that ruins a
wool sweater in a hot wash** (§23 → `making-laundering-stain-removal-care-and-mending`).
**Needle-punched, spunbond, meltblown, and wet-laid nonwovens**; ⚠️ **interfacings (fusible
and sew-in) are the nonwoven a garment maker uses daily, and they're what makes collars,
cuffs and waistbands hold shape.**

## §6. Dyeing and Finishing

```
⚠️ DYE CLASSES ARE FIBRE-SPECIFIC — this is the fact home dyers miss
  REACTIVE   ⚠️ cellulose. Forms a covalent bond — excellent wash-fastness
  DIRECT     cellulose, simple, ⚠️ poor wash-fastness
  ACID       ⚠️ protein (wool, silk) AND nylon. Needs acid + heat
  DISPERSE   ⚠️ polyester ONLY, and needs ~130°C under pressure —
     which is why you CANNOT reliably dye polyester at home
  VAT        ⚠️ indigo. Insoluble; reduced to dye, oxidizes on the fibre —
     which is why denim fades the way it does (the dye sits on the
     surface of the yarn, and abrasion removes it)
STAGE  fibre → yarn → piece → garment dyeing (⚠️ later = cheaper and
   more responsive to fashion, but less even)
```
**⚠️ Fastness types are separate properties**: **wash, light, crock (rubbing), perspiration.**
⚠️ **A dye can be excellent at one and poor at another** — **and "crocking" is why new dark
denim transfers onto upholstery.**
**Finishes**: **mercerization (⚠️ caustic treatment of cotton that increases lustre,
strength and dye uptake), sanforization (pre-shrinking), calendering, brushing/napping,
resin finishes for wrinkle resistance (⚠️ which reduce strength and abrasion resistance),
and water/stain repellents** (§27.2 → `making-reference`).

## §7. Fabric Properties

**⚠️ HAND (how it feels), DRAPE (how it falls), GSM or oz/yd² (weight), opacity, thermal
behaviour, moisture management (⚠️ wicking vs absorbency are DIFFERENT — polyester wicks
without absorbing; cotton absorbs without wicking, which is why cotton stays wet).**
**⚠️ Pilling**: ⚠️ **caused by fibre ends migrating and tangling, and made permanent when
the fibre is too STRONG to break off** — **which is why polyester and acrylic pill visibly
and wool eventually sheds its pills.** **Strong fibres pill worse, counterintuitively.**

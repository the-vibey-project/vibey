## §8. ⚠️ Grain

**⚠️ The single most important concept in garment making, and the one beginners skip.**
```
⚠️ LENGTHWISE GRAIN (warp)  parallel to selvedge. Strongest, LEAST stretch
⚠️ CROSSWISE GRAIN (weft)   slight stretch
⚠️ BIAS  45° to both. ⚠️ MAXIMUM stretch and fluid drape — this is why
   bias-cut dresses cling and move, and why bias binding goes round curves
```
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — cutting off-grain produces a garment that twists on the body and CANNOT be
> fixed afterwards.** ⚠️ **Side seams spiral toward the front, hems hang unevenly, and no
> amount of pressing corrects it.** **⚠️ Straighten the grain before cutting: tear or
> thread-trace a crosswise line and align it square to the selvedge.** **If the fabric is
> printed off-grain — common in cheap prints — you must choose between following the grain
> (correct hang, crooked print) or the print (straight print, twisting garment).** **⚠️ Go
> with the grain.**

**⚠️ Bias behaviour**: ⚠️ **bias-cut pieces GROW under their own weight.** **Hang a bias
garment for 24 hours before hemming, or the hem will drop unevenly after you wear it.**

# PART II — GARMENT MAKING

## §9. Pattern Drafting and Ease

```
⚠️ BLOCK / SLOPER  a fitted base pattern with no design features. ⚠️ Build
   ONE that fits you, and every subsequent pattern is a modification
⚠️ EASE
   WEARING ease  the minimum to move and breathe (⚠️ roughly 2–4" at the
      bust for a woven garment; ⚠️ knits can have ZERO or NEGATIVE ease)
   DESIGN ease   the extra that creates the silhouette
DARTS  ⚠️ the mechanism for turning flat cloth into 3D shape. ⚠️ Dart
   MANIPULATION — rotating the same amount of shaping to a different
   seam or into gathers — is the core drafting skill
GRADING  scaling between sizes (⚠️ NOT uniform scaling — body proportions
   don't scale linearly, which is why big sizes from naive grading fit badly)
```
**⚠️ Seam allowance conventions vary by pattern source** (⚠️ **commercial US patterns often
5/8"; many European and industrial patterns 1 cm; some include none at all**) — **check
before cutting.**

## §10. Cutting and Layout

**⚠️ Layout**: **fold direction, ⚠️ NAP layouts (all pieces one direction for pile,
one-way prints and some twills), pattern matching at seams (⚠️ costs significant extra
fabric — plan for it), and cutting doubled vs single.**
**⚠️ Preshrink before cutting.** ⚠️ **Wash and dry the fabric the way you'll wash the
finished garment.** **Otherwise the first laundering shrinks a fitted garment out of fit,
and different components shrink differently.**
**⚠️ Marking**: **tailor's tacks, chalk, wax, tracing wheel — ⚠️ test any marker on a scrap
for removability first.**
**⚠️ Cutting**: **shears (⚠️ never on paper), rotary cutter with a mat, and ⚠️ cut with the
fabric FLAT on the table — lifting it to cut distorts the piece.**

## §11. Seams and Stitches

```
⚠️ MACHINE STITCHES
  LOCKSTITCH   ⚠️ the standard home-machine stitch. Two threads locking
     in the fabric. ⚠️ DOES NOT STRETCH — it snaps in a stretched knit
  ZIGZAG       stretches; finishes edges
  ⚠️ OVERLOCK/SERGER  trims, seams and finishes in one pass; stretches
  COVERSTITCH  the twin-line hem on commercial knitwear
  CHAINSTITCH  ⚠️ stretches, and unravels from one end (why feed sacks
     open in one pull)

SEAM TYPES
  PLAIN + finish (⚠️ pinked, zigzagged, overlocked, bound, or Hong Kong)
  ⚠️ FRENCH     encloses raw edges. Sheer and lightweight fabrics
  ⚠️ FLAT-FELLED  encloses and is very strong. ⚠️ Jeans, work shirts
  BOUND · lapped · welt
```
**⚠️ Seam finish exists because woven edges FRAY, and the finish determines how long the
garment survives laundering** — ⚠️ **it's the difference between a garment that lasts five
years and one that disintegrates internally.**
**⚠️ Stitch length and tension**: ⚠️ **most puckering is tension or the wrong needle (§14),
not user error.** **Longer stitches for thick fabric, shorter for fine.**

## §12. Construction Order

**⚠️ There is a logic to the sequence and it isn't arbitrary:**
```
⚠️ 1. Interfacing and any marking
   2. Darts and shaping
   3. Small units (pockets, plackets) — ⚠️ while pieces are still FLAT
   4. Join major seams (shoulders, then sides)
   5. Collars and cuffs
   6. Sleeves — ⚠️ flat-set before side seams, or set-in after
   7. Waistbands, closures
   8. Hem — ⚠️ LAST, after hanging (§8)
⚠️ PRESS AT EVERY STAGE. Pressing as you go is what separates
   home-made from handmade, and you CANNOT press a seam once
   it's enclosed by another
```
**⚠️ Pressing is not ironing**: ⚠️ **lift and lower with weight and steam; do NOT slide,
which distorts the fabric.** **⚠️ Use a press cloth on anything that can shine, and a ham
or roll for curved seams — a curved seam pressed flat is a curved seam destroyed.**

## §13. Fitting

**⚠️ Fit reads from the FABRIC, and the wrinkles tell you what's wrong:**
```
⚠️ HORIZONTAL wrinkles  → too TIGHT in that direction (fabric being pulled)
⚠️ VERTICAL wrinkles    → too LOOSE (excess fabric collapsing)
⚠️ DIAGONAL wrinkles    → point TOWARD the area that needs more room
   (drag lines radiate from the tight point)
```
**⚠️ Common adjustments**: **full/small bust adjustment, sway back, sloping or square
shoulders, forward shoulder, length adjustments at the marked LENGTHEN/SHORTEN lines
(⚠️ not at the hem, which changes the shape of the piece).**
**⚠️ Fit ONE side and transfer, and make a toile/muslin for anything fitted or expensive.**
⚠️ **The cheapest fabric you'll ever buy is the one you make the mistakes in.**

## §14. Machines, Needles and Thread

```
⚠️ NEEDLE TYPE MATTERS MORE THAN ANYTHING ELSE, AND IS THE MOST IGNORED
  UNIVERSAL  general
  ⚠️ BALLPOINT/JERSEY  KNITS — pushes between loops instead of piercing
     and cutting them. ⚠️ A sharp needle in a knit CUTS yarns, and the
     garment develops holes along the seam later
  ⚠️ MICROTEX/SHARP  fine wovens, silk, microfibre
  DENIM/JEANS · LEATHER (⚠️ cutting point) · TOPSTITCH (large eye)
⚠️ SIZE  Euro 60–120 / US 8–19. Match to fabric weight AND thread
⚠️ CHANGE THE NEEDLE. A dull needle causes skipped stitches, puckering
   and snags — roughly every 6–8 hours of sewing
THREAD  polyester (⚠️ the general default; has slight stretch),
   cotton (⚠️ no stretch — for wovens and quilting), silk, ⚠️ and
   thread should generally be WEAKER than the fabric so the seam
   fails before the cloth does
```
**⚠️ Machine maintenance**: ⚠️ **lint in the bobbin case and feed dogs causes more problems
than any other single cause.** **Clean it; oil per the manual; ⚠️ and rethread completely
(with the presser foot UP, so the tension discs are open) before diagnosing a tension
problem** — **most "tension problems" are threading problems.**

## §15. Hand Sewing

**Running, backstitch (⚠️ the strong one), slip/ladder stitch (⚠️ invisible closures),
catch stitch (⚠️ flexible hems), blanket, whip, and the pad stitch (⚠️ tailoring, to shape
a lapel).**
**⚠️ Hand-sewing still wins for**: **invisible hems, attaching linings, easing a set-in
sleeve, buttons, and any point where control beats speed.**
**⚠️ Practical**: ⚠️ **short thread (~18"; longer tangles and abrades), thread conditioner
or beeswax, a thimble, and match the needle to the job (sharps, betweens, milliners,
darners).**

> 1. **⚠️ Selection versus causation is THE methodological problem here** (§1). **People
>    who marry, attend church, join clubs or move to a neighbourhood differ from those
>    who don't, in ways that predict the outcome being measured.**
> 2. **⚠️ Relationships and communities are systems, not collections of individuals**
>    (§7 → `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`, §16 → `family-social-capital-community-and-program-evaluation`). **Fixing "the problem person" reliably fails.**
> 3. **⚠️ Structure beats willpower.** **Both in families and in communities, the durable
>    interventions change the environment — routines, physical spaces, standing
>    commitments — rather than exhorting people to try harder** (§11 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`, §19 → `family-social-capital-community-and-program-evaluation`, §23 → `family-social-capital-community-and-program-evaluation`).

## §1. ⚠️ How to Read Claims in This Field

**⚠️ Four specific hazards, beyond the general replication problem:**

```
1. ⚠️ SELECTION vs CAUSATION — the dominant problem
   People who marry, attend religious services, join clubs or volunteer differ
   BEFORE they do those things: in income, health, conscientiousness, and
   social support. ⚠️ A raw association between the behaviour and the outcome
   is mostly uninterpretable

2. ⚠️ REVERSE CAUSATION
   Does low social connection cause depression, or does depression reduce
   social connection? ⚠️ Both, and disentangling them requires longitudinal
   or experimental designs most studies don't have

3. ⚠️ IDEOLOGICAL LOADING
   Family structure, parenting, gender roles and religion are politically
   charged. ⚠️ Researchers, funders and advocacy organizations on ALL sides
   have positions, and the same dataset gets reported very differently

4. ⚠️ THE SELF-HELP GAP
   ⚠️ The most popular relationship and parenting frameworks are often the
   least rigorously validated. Popularity selects for memorability and
   intuitive appeal, which anti-correlate with nuance
```
**⚠️ What good evidence looks like here**: **longitudinal designs following people over
time; sibling and twin comparisons (⚠️ which control for shared family background — see
§10 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`); natural experiments and policy discontinuities; randomized trials where ethically
possible (⚠️ they exist for parenting and community programs more than people assume);
and preregistered replications.**
**⚠️ And the honest caveat that applies throughout**: ⚠️ **population-level findings do not
determine individual cases.** **"Children of X do worse on average" is a statement about
distributions with enormous overlap, and it tells you almost nothing about any particular
child.**

## §2. Attachment

**⚠️ Bowlby and Ainsworth's infant work is well established: infants form attachment
patterns with caregivers (secure, avoidant, ambivalent, later disorganized), measurable in
the Strange Situation, and these relate to caregiver sensitivity and responsiveness.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — "adult attachment style" as a personality typology is a substantial
> extrapolation from infant research, and the popular version is much stronger than the
> evidence.** ⚠️ **Infant classifications predict adult outcomes far more weakly than
> popular treatments imply; attachment is relationship-specific rather than a fixed trait
> (⚠️ you can be differently attached to different people); and it changes over time —
> "earned security" is well documented.**
> **⚠️ The self-help version — that you have an attachment style that explains your
> relationships and dates back to infancy — outruns the science considerably.** **The
> underlying phenomena are real; the deterministic story is not.**

**⚠️ What IS well supported and practically useful**: **caregiver SENSITIVITY — accurately
reading and responding to a child's signals — predicts secure attachment, and it is
teachable.** ⚠️ **Interventions targeting sensitivity have among the better evidence bases
in parenting research** (§9 → `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`). **"Serve and return" interaction is the same idea in
developmental-science language** (§8 → `family-systems-child-development-and-parenting`).

## §3. Couple Relationships: What Predicts Stability

**⚠️ Gottman's observational work is the best-known and deserves a calibrated reading.**
**⚠️ The findings that hold up reasonably well:**
- **⚠️ The "Four Horsemen" — criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling — predict
  distress and dissolution, with CONTEMPT the strongest single predictor.** **This is
  robust enough to be useful.**
- **⚠️ Positive-to-negative interaction ratios during conflict differ markedly between
  stable and unstable couples.**
- **⚠️ Bids for connection and "turning toward" versus away.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — the famous "94% accuracy predicting divorce" figure has been seriously
> criticized on methodological grounds.** ⚠️ **The core objection: the predictive models
> were fitted and evaluated on the same or closely related samples, which inflates accuracy
> — genuine out-of-sample prediction is much weaker.** **⚠️ The behavioural observations
> are valuable; the prediction claim is not what it appears.**

**⚠️ Other predictors with reasonable support**: **commitment (⚠️ distinct from
satisfaction, and it independently predicts stability), shared expectations, sexual
satisfaction, similar values, financial stress (⚠️ economic strain is one of the more
consistent predictors of relationship distress, which makes some "relationship problems"
downstream of material problems), and social network support for the relationship.**
**⚠️ The perennial null result worth knowing**: ⚠️ **similarity of personality predicts
relationship satisfaction much less than people expect**, **and "opposites attract" is
also unsupported.** **What matters more is how the pair handles difference.**

## §4. Conflict and Repair

**⚠️ Conflict is not the problem; unrepaired conflict is.** ⚠️ **All couples fight, and
Gottman's estimate that a large majority of couple conflicts are PERPETUAL — rooted in
enduring personality and value differences rather than solvable — reframes the goal from
resolution to dialogue.**
```
⚠️ SOFT STARTUP    how a conversation begins strongly predicts how it ends
⚠️ REPAIR ATTEMPTS bids to de-escalate. ⚠️ Whether they're ACCEPTED matters
                   more than whether they're skillfully made
⚠️ FLOODING        physiological arousal that makes productive talk impossible.
                   ⚠️ Take a real break — 20+ minutes — and RETURN
DEMAND-WITHDRAW    ⚠️ the most-documented destructive pattern. One pursues,
                   one retreats, each escalating the other
```
**⚠️ Accountability without self-abasement is the useful repair posture**: **acknowledge
the specific thing, don't perform contrition, don't counter-attack.** ⚠️ **Over-apology and
defensiveness fail for the same reason — both move attention off the actual issue.**
**⚠️ On violence, stated plainly**: ⚠️ **intimate partner violence is not a communication
problem and couples counselling is generally contraindicated where it is present.** **It
is a safety matter requiring specialized resources.**

## §5. Transitions and Dissolution

**⚠️ Relationship transitions are among life's higher-stress events, and the research on
them is more useful than the folklore.**
- **⚠️ Conflict exposure, not separation per se, drives much of the child impact** (§13 → `family-behaviour-genetics-structure-and-caregiving`) —
  **children in high-conflict intact homes often do comparably to or worse than children
  whose parents separated.** ⚠️ **This is one of the more consistent findings and it cuts
  against simple narratives in both directions.**
- **⚠️ Co-parenting quality after separation predicts child adjustment substantially**,
  and **it is more modifiable than most other factors.**
- **⚠️ Economic disruption is a major mediating pathway**, **particularly for the
  lower-earning household.**
- **Adaptation**: ⚠️ **most people recover their baseline wellbeing within a few years,
  though the variance is wide and a minority do not.** **Affective forecasting errors mean
  people over-predict both the misery and the relief** (see a psychology reference §8).

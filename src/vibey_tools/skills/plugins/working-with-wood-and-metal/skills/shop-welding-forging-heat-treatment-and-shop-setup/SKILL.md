> **⚠️ Two materials that punish opposite mistakes.** ⚠️ **Wood MOVES forever and forgives
> imprecision; metal is dimensionally stable and forgives nothing.** **⚠️ Wood is worked
> with the grain and cut fast; metal is worked against resistance and cut slowly.**
> **The habits that make you good at one will actively hurt you at the other.**
>
> **Complements a manufacturing reference (industrial process and tolerancing), a
> materials/furniture reference (wood movement and finishing at the design level), and a
> thermodynamics reference (heat treatment physics).**

> **⚠️ SAFETY IS §2 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock` AND IT IS FIRST FOR A REASON.** ⚠️ **This document describes techniques;
> it does not substitute for supervised instruction on machinery. The table saw, angle
> grinder and welding arc each injure large numbers of people every year, and the
> mechanisms are specific and learnable.**
>
> **⚠️ GOTCHA** boxes mark the habits that cause injuries and the received wisdom that's
> wrong.
>
> **The three ideas that organize this document:**
> 1. **⚠️ SHARP IS SAFE** (§7 → `shop-woodworking-tools-sharpening-joinery-and-finishing`). **A dull tool requires force, force removes control, and
>    loss of control is how people get hurt. Sharpening is not a side skill — it's the
>    gateway to everything else.**
> 2. **⚠️ The material is not neutral** (§3 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`, §14 → `shop-metalwork-cutting-drilling-and-machining`). **Wood grain direction, and metal's
>    heat and work-hardening behaviour, determine technique. Fighting the material is
>    the definition of doing it wrong.**
> 3. **⚠️ The invisible hazards are the ones that get you** (§2 → `shop-materials-safety-reading-wood-and-stock`, §26, §27.2 → `shop-reference`). **Wood dust
>    and welding fume are classified carcinogens with no warning symptoms — and they're
>    the risks hobbyists systematically ignore in favour of the dramatic ones.**

## §19. ⚠️ Welding Processes

```
⚠️ SMAW / "STICK"  ⚠️ flux-coated electrode. Cheap, portable,
   works outdoors and on dirty or rusty metal. ⚠️ Slag to chip,
   steeper learning curve
⚠️ GMAW / MIG  ⚠️ wire feed with shielding gas. ⚠️ Easiest to learn,
   fast, ⚠️ poor in wind (blows the shielding gas away)
   ⚠️ FCAW (flux-cored) works outdoors; more fume
⚠️ GTAW / TIG  ⚠️ non-consumable tungsten, separate filler.
   ⚠️ Most control and best quality; slowest and hardest.
   ⚠️ The only easy choice for thin material and aluminium
OXY-ACETYLENE  ⚠️ versatile (weld, braze, cut, heat), largely
   displaced for welding but still excellent for heating and bending
```
**⚠️ The fundamentals that determine weld quality**: ⚠️ **CLEAN metal (grind off mill scale,
rust, paint, oil — and NEVER weld galvanized without extraction); FIT-UP (a good weld
cannot fix a bad gap); joint preparation and bevelling on thicker material; correct
amperage; travel speed; and consistent arc length.**
**⚠️ Distortion is the thing beginners don't anticipate**: ⚠️ **welding shrinks as it cools
and pulls the work out of alignment.** **⚠️ Tack first, weld in a staggered sequence,
alternate sides, clamp, and expect movement.**
**⚠️ Common defects**: **porosity (contamination or lost shielding), undercut, lack of
fusion (⚠️ the dangerous one, because it can look fine), cracking, and excessive spatter.**
> **⚠️ GOTCHA — WELDING PPE IS NOT OPTIONAL AND THE HAZARDS EXTEND BEYOND THE WELDER.**
> ⚠️ **The arc emits intense UV: "arc eye" is a corneal burn whose symptoms appear hours
> later, and it burns EXPOSED SKIN like severe sunburn.** **⚠️ Bystanders need screening.**
> **⚠️ Correct shade lens, leathers, no synthetic clothing (it melts), boots, and
> ⚠️ RESPIRATORY PROTECTION AND EXTRACTION** (§27.2 → `shop-reference`).

## §20. Brazing and Soldering

**⚠️ Joining WITHOUT melting the parent metal** — ⚠️ **filler flows by CAPILLARY ACTION into
a close-fitting joint, so JOINT CLEARANCE is the critical variable and a sloppy gap makes
a weak joint.**
**⚠️ Brazing** (above ~450°C) — ⚠️ **strong, joins dissimilar metals, excellent for cast
iron and for thin tube (bicycle frames), and lower distortion than welding.**
**⚠️ Soldering** (below ~450°C) — **electronics and plumbing.**
**⚠️ Flux does the essential work**: ⚠️ **it removes oxide and prevents re-oxidation, and
the right flux for the metal and temperature is not optional.**
**⚠️ Heat the WORK, not the filler** — ⚠️ **the joint should melt the filler, which is how
you know it's hot enough for the alloy to flow.**

## §21. Forming and Forging

**⚠️ Cold forming**: **bending (⚠️ minimum bend radius, springback — see a manufacturing
reference), rolling, and ⚠️ the fact that work hardening limits how far you can go before
annealing** (§14 → `shop-metalwork-cutting-drilling-and-machining`, §22).
**⚠️ Sheet metal by hand**: **brake, stakes, hammer and dolly, planishing, shrinking and
stretching.**
**⚠️ Hot forming and forging**: ⚠️ **heat lowers the force required enormously and resets
work hardening.** **⚠️ Forging aligns grain flow, which is why forged parts outperform cast
ones in fatigue** (see a manufacturing reference §10).
**⚠️ Basic smithing operations**: **drawing out, upsetting, bending, punching, drifting,
and forge welding.**
**⚠️ Temperature by colour is the traditional gauge** — ⚠️ **and it requires dim ambient
light to read, which is why forges are traditionally dark.**

## §22. Heat Treatment

```
⚠️ ANNEALING  heat and cool SLOWLY → soft, machinable, stress-free
⚠️ NORMALIZING  heat and air cool → uniform grain
⚠️ HARDENING  ⚠️ heat above critical temperature, then QUENCH →
   very hard AND VERY BRITTLE. ⚠️ Unusable in this state
⚠️ TEMPERING  ⚠️ reheat the hardened part to a lower temperature →
   trades some hardness for toughness. ⚠️ NEVER skip this
⚠️ CASE HARDENING  hard surface, tough core
```
**⚠️ Only hardenable steels harden** — ⚠️ **mild steel will not, regardless of what you do
to it, because it lacks the carbon.**
**⚠️ Practical indicators**: ⚠️ **the magnet test (steel becomes non-magnetic at the
critical temperature — a genuinely useful shop trick), and TEMPER COLOURS on clean steel
(straw through to blue as temperature rises).**
**⚠️ Quench medium matters and is specified by the steel**: ⚠️ **water is fast and risks
cracking; oil is slower; air-hardening steels need neither.** **⚠️ Using the wrong quench
cracks the part.**
**⚠️ SAFETY**: ⚠️ **quench tanks flash and spit; oil quenching can ignite; never quench in a
sealed container.**

## §23. Metal Finishing

**⚠️ Mechanical**: **filing, draw filing (⚠️ produces a genuinely flat finish), abrasives
in sequence, wire brushing, polishing, ⚠️ and the fact that grinding and sanding marks must
be fully removed at each grit or they show through the polish.**
**⚠️ Chemical and coating**: **paint (⚠️ surface prep and primer determine adhesion),
powder coating, ⚠️ bluing and blackening, anodizing (aluminium — ⚠️ note it changes
dimensions slightly), plating, and passivation.**
**⚠️ Rust prevention** is a system: ⚠️ **oil films on tools, desiccants, dehumidification,
and paint on anything left outdoors.** **⚠️ Bare cast iron machine tables need active
protection.**

# PART III — SHARED PRACTICE

## §24. Fasteners and Fits

**⚠️ Thread standards**: **metric coarse and fine, UNC/UNF, ⚠️ and BSP/NPT pipe threads
(tapered, sealed by interference).** ⚠️ **Never force mismatched threads; identify with a
thread gauge.**
**⚠️ A bolted joint works by CLAMPING** (see a manufacturing reference) — ⚠️ **preload
creates friction that carries the load.** **⚠️ Torque specs exist for that reason.**
**⚠️ Fits**: **clearance, transition, interference.** ⚠️ **Press fits are assembled by force
or by THERMAL means — heat the outer part or chill the inner, which is elegant and
avoids damaging either.**
**⚠️ Thread repair**: **helical inserts, oversize tapping, and ⚠️ the observation that a
stripped thread in aluminium is common and preventable with correct torque.**

## §25. Measurement

**⚠️ Tools**: **rule, tape (⚠️ the hook is deliberately loose to account for its own
thickness — that's not a fault), calipers (⚠️ good to ~0.05 mm realistically), micrometers
(⚠️ 0.01 mm and better, with a ratchet for consistent feel), dial indicators, squares,
feeler gauges, and gauge blocks.**
**⚠️ Practical discipline**: ⚠️ **temperature matters at tight tolerances; measuring FORCE
matters with calipers (over-squeezing reads small); zero your instrument before use; and
⚠️ measure the same feature the same way each time.**
**⚠️ In wood, "measure twice cut once" is real; ⚠️ in metal, measure after every cut,
because you cannot put it back.**

## §26. Shop Setup, Dust and Fume

```
⚠️ DUST (wood)  ⚠️ the fine invisible fraction is the hazard (§2).
   ⚠️ A shop vac captures chips and NOT the dangerous fraction
   ⚠️ EXTRACTION AT SOURCE > ambient air cleaner > respirator
   ⚠️ HEPA filtration; ⚠️ and note ambient cleaners run AFTER the
   dust is airborne, which is already the failure
⚠️ FUME (metal)  ⚠️ LOCAL EXHAUST VENTILATION at the arc is the
   primary engineering control (§27.2). ⚠️ Positioning your head
   out of the plume is free and effective
⚠️ RESPIRATORS  ⚠️ a nuisance dust mask is NOT respiratory protection
   for fume or fine dust. ⚠️ P2/P3 (or N95/P100), fit-tested,
   and ⚠️ facial hair breaks the seal
⚠️ LIGHTING · power (⚠️ RCD/GFCI protection) · fire extinguisher ·
   first aid · ⚠️ clear floor and defined walkways
```
**⚠️ Small shop reality**: ⚠️ **you cannot do everything, and the honest sequence is to buy
tools as projects demand them rather than in advance.** **⚠️ Spend on the workbench,
workholding, sharpening and extraction before the third power tool.**

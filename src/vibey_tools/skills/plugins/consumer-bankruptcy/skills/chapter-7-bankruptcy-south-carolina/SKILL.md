# Chapter 7 Bankruptcy in South Carolina

> **Part 1 of 1** of the *Consumer Bankruptcy* reference (plugin `consumer-bankruptcy`).
>
> **Currency:** Procedure is stable; dollar figures are **as of October 2026**: the means-test medians for cases filed on or after April 1, 2026, the court fees in effect in 2026, and the South Carolina exemptions adjusted July 1, 2026. Confirm every figure against the numbers in effect on the filing date.

> **⚠️ Scope.** A research summary for orientation, **not legal advice**. Bankruptcy outcomes turn on facts this reference cannot see. Use it to prepare for a consultation with a licensed South Carolina bankruptcy attorney, and confirm everything with them.

## 0. When Chapter 7 is the chapter to ask about

Chapter 7 (liquidation) discharges most unsecured debt in a few months; a trustee may sell non-exempt property to pay creditors, though many South Carolina cases are **no-asset cases** with nothing to sell. Chapter 13 (a three-to-five-year repayment plan) is mainly for someone who wants to keep property they would otherwise lose and has regular income to fund a plan. A filer who intends to surrender secured property (a house or car) and has no income to fund a plan is usually looking at Chapter 7.

## 1. Do I qualify? The means test

- The means test compares household income to the **South Carolina median** for the household size. For cases filed on or after **April 1, 2026**, the SC median is **$64,808** for one person, **$83,761** for two, **$95,672** for three and **$116,314** for four.
- **At or below the median:** the filer presumptively qualifies. **Above it:** the test moves on to allowed expenses, and Chapter 7 may still be available.
- If the only household income is **SSI or SSDI**, the test is passed without the arithmetic.

**The catch: the look-back.** The test does not use today's income. It averages the **six full calendar months before filing**.

- That window can still include pay from a job that has ended, and any money paid out at separation.
- **Unemployment benefits count** as income for the test.
- If earlier pay was high, filing too soon can push the average over the median; filing a few months later moves the window past those months.
- Ask the attorney to run the test before choosing a filing date.

## 2. What it costs

| Item | Cost (2026) |
|---|---|
| Court filing fee | $338 (Chapter 7) |
| Credit counselling + debtor education | up to ~$100 in total (roughly $10–$50 each) |
| Attorney (South Carolina) | most charge $1,100–$3,000 |
| **Realistic total** | **~$1,500–$3,500** |

**Relief from the court fee:**

- **Fee waiver:** available when household income is below **150% of the federal poverty guideline** and the filer cannot pay in installments. For one person that is roughly $24,000 a year (an estimate; check the current guideline).
- **Installments:** up to four payments within 120 days of filing.
- Neither option covers attorney fees.

## 3. How bankruptcy attorneys get paid

Most charge a **flat fee**, generally due **in full before filing**: a Chapter 7 discharge would wipe out an unpaid fee, which is why attorneys do not file first and bill later.

Ways to manage it:

1. **A pre-filing payment plan.** Pay in installments; the attorney files once paid. Ask whether it is offered and on what schedule.
2. **Help from family.** Ask the attorney how to document it: money moved around before filing is scrutinised.
3. **Free or low-cost help.** South Carolina Legal Services serves eligible low-income filers; confirm the office and phone number for your county.
4. **Filing without an attorney (pro se).** Possible, but not advisable for anyone with a pending lawsuit, a business interest, or a house being surrendered.

## 4. Timeline

| Step | When |
|---|---|
| Credit counselling (approved agency) | within 180 days **before** filing |
| Gather documents | before filing |
| File the petition; the automatic stay starts | Day 1 |
| Statement of Intention (for property being surrendered) | within 30 days of filing |
| 341 meeting of creditors | ~30–45 days after filing |
| Debtor education course | after filing, before discharge |
| Discharge | ~60–90 days after the 341 meeting |
| **Total** | **roughly 4–6 months** |

**Documents to gather:** six months of pay stubs and bank statements; two years of federal and state tax returns; titles and deeds; every collection notice and statement; any unemployment award letter; records for any business the filer owns; paperwork for any lawsuit the filer is part of.

**The automatic stay** takes effect on filing. It stops collection calls, lawsuits, repossession attempts and foreclosure sales.

**The 341 meeting** is a short session under oath with the trustee. Creditors rarely attend.

## 5. Surrendering a house or car

- **Little or no equity means little for the trustee.** Compare what the property would net after selling costs with what is owed on it. If the loan is larger, the trustee has nothing to sell.
- **The lender still has to act.** The stay covers surrendered property, so the lender must ask the court for relief from the stay and then foreclose (in South Carolina, a judicial foreclosure in state court) or repossess. Some lenders wait until after discharge.
- **Until the foreclosure sale,** the owner can generally keep living in the home, and the lender cannot be made to move quickly.
- **The personal debt goes away; the lien does not.** A shortfall after foreclosure or repossession is unsecured debt and is discharged, but the lien survives, so the lender can still take the property after the case ends.
- **Costs keep running while the filer is owner of record.** HOA dues, insurance and utilities continue until title transfers, and **HOA charges that accrue after filing can survive discharge.** Ask the attorney how to handle them.
- **Plan housing and transport** around an uncertain move-out date.

## 6. What must be disclosed, or could be exposed

- **A lawsuit the filer is pursuing** is an asset the trustee can take an interest in, and exemptions for it are small. The litigation attorney and the bankruptcy attorney need to coordinate **before** filing.
- **A business the filer owns** (an LLC, for example): ownership, accounts and debts must all be listed.
- **Valuations:** informal estimates of a house or car are a starting point; the trustee will form their own.
- **Tax refunds** and any other money expected may count as assets.

## 7. Property the filer keeps: South Carolina exemptions

South Carolina has **opted out of the federal exemptions**, so the state's own exemptions apply. Sources disagree on the exact figures; roughly:

| Exemption | Approx. amount |
|---|---|
| Homestead equity | ~$80,000 |
| One vehicle (equity) | ~$8,000 |
| Wildcard | ~$8,000 |

- The amounts are adjusted on **July 1 of every even-numbered year**; the latest adjustment took effect July 1, 2026. ⚠️ Verify the current figures against the South Carolina notice.
- Unemployment compensation and Social Security benefits are exempt.
- Using South Carolina's exemptions generally requires about **two years of residence** in the state.

## 8. What is not discharged

- **Fines and penalties owed to a government**, traffic tickets included.
- Child support and alimony, and certain recent taxes.
- Student loans, except in rare cases that need a separate proceeding.

## 9. Before filing: what to avoid

- **Do not favour one creditor.** Large payments to a single creditor in roughly the 90 days before filing can be clawed back by the trustee; payments to relatives look worse, and the window is longer.
- **Do not run up new charges or take cash advances.** Charges in the last ~70–90 days are presumed fraudulent and can survive discharge.
- **Mind accounts at a bank you owe.** A bank can freeze or offset its customer's account against a debt to it; ask the attorney about moving accounts and when.
- **Do not transfer or sell assets** without asking first.
- **Ask when to stop mortgage and car payments** rather than deciding alone.
- **Do not start a hardship plan** without checking with the attorney: one can complicate the filing.

## 10. Credit and long-term effects

- A Chapter 7 stays on the credit report for **10 years**, but scores often recover meaningfully within a few years with clean new accounts.
- **Waiting periods for a future mortgage** (general guidance; verify with a lender): about 2 years after discharge for FHA and VA loans, about 4 years for conventional loans, and longer after a foreclosure.
- **No second Chapter 7 discharge for 8 years.**
- A private employer generally cannot fire someone solely for filing.

## 11. Alternatives

| Option | When it fits |
|---|---|
| Chapter 13 | To keep property the filer would otherwise lose, with regular income to fund a plan. |
| Hardship plans with creditors | To keep paying at a reduced rate while income is low. |
| Doing nothing | South Carolina generally does not allow wage garnishment for ordinary consumer debt, but lawsuits and judgments can still follow. |

## 12. Questions that decide the path

1. Do I pass the means test now, or should I wait to file?
2. What is your flat fee, and can I pay it in installments before filing?
3. Who pays HOA and carrying costs while a home is being surrendered, and how long do people typically stay?
4. How should a lawsuit I am pursuing, or a business I own, be handled?
5. When should I stop paying the mortgage and the car loan?
6. What foreclosure or repossession timeline should I expect from my lenders?

# Pipeline Thesis Development
## Content Engineering Pipeline — Phases 1, 2, and 3

This skill governs the first three phases of the Content Engineering
Pipeline. These three phases form
a single integrated system: Phase 1 produces the claim, Phase 2 maps
the territory the claim must navigate, and Phase 3 investigates every
argument that claim depends on.

---

## BEFORE PHASE 1 — STATE YOUR GOVERNING POSTULATES

Postulates operate as axioms across all work in this pipeline. They
apply to every phase and every decision within them. Before Phase 1
begins, the author states their own governing postulates explicitly,
in writing, as three numbered statements:

- **Your First Postulate — what you hold to be true.** The conviction
  about reality, or about the best way to live, that the work proceeds
  from and does not itself set out to prove.
- **Your Second Postulate — the ethic your work serves.** The practical
  rule for how the work, and its author, treat the people it reaches.
- **Your Third Postulate — your limits on means.** What you will not
  do to reach a result, however good the result.

Record them at the top of `00_phase1_claim.md`. From this point on —
in this skill and in its five sibling skills — "the postulates", "the
Three Postulates" and "the First / Second / Third Postulate" mean
**the postulates you stated**. The finished book's introduction states
them as the author's governing axioms (Phase 4).

**The method's limits on means stand regardless.** Wherever this
pipeline applies the Third Postulate — no predetermined conclusion, no
selective terrain map, no confirmation hunting, no fabricated or
approximated testimony, no narrative power in excess of the evidence,
no manufactured urgency, outrage amplification, false social proof or
misrepresented content — those rules are the general ethics of the
method, and they stand on their own. Your Third Postulate states your
limits on means in your own words; it may tighten these rules, never
loosen them. The rest of the pipeline calls them **the limits on
means**.

> **Example — one author's governing postulates:**
>
> **The First Postulate:** Christianity and the culture it produces is
> the best way of life available to human beings.
>
> **The Second Postulate:** The Golden Rule is the best practical
> expression of the gospel at both the individual and interpersonal
> levels.
>
> **The Third Postulate:** The ends never justify the means.

These are the three the methodology that follows was built on, kept
here verbatim as a worked example of the step, not as a requirement.
The first two are convictions held from inside the Christian
tradition — theological claims, not findings this pipeline sets out
to establish — and they show the First and Second Postulates doing
their job: naming what the author holds to be true and the ethic the
work serves, plainly enough that a reader can see the work's operating
constraints. The third is the limits on means in its shortest form.
State your own with the same explicitness; the methodology applies
equally to any author who brings their own principles with equal
seriousness.

## PHASE 1 — PROBLEM IDENTIFICATION

### What Phase 1 Actually Is

Phase 1 is not the beginning of writing. It is the beginning of
thinking — the systematic process of converting a frustration, a
question, an observation, or a gap into an intellectual claim precise
enough to be argued against, significant enough for someone to care
about, and surprising enough to earn a stranger's sustained attention.

Most books fail before the first word of prose is written because the
author started with a topic rather than a thesis. A topic is a
territory. A thesis is a position staked inside that territory — a
specific claim that could be wrong, that challenges what the reader
already believes, and that changes how they operate in the world if
it turns out to be true.

**The foundational test:** Can you write one sentence that someone
intelligent could argue against? If yes, you have a thesis. If the
sentence merely describes what you plan to cover, what you find
interesting, or what most people already believe, you have a topic.
No amount of subsequent prose rescues a book whose claim was never
sharp enough to cut.

### The Limits on Means Applied to Phase 1

The limits on means directly prohibit starting with a predetermined
conclusion and working backward through the evidence. Research that
begins with a predetermined answer is advocacy masquerading as inquiry
— a violation of the limits on means regardless of whether its
conclusion happens to be correct.

Phase 1 demands what the investigative journalism tradition calls
**hypothesis discipline**: the working claim must be genuinely
testable, which means it must be genuinely possible for the research
to disprove it. An author who cannot describe what evidence would
change their mind has not yet formed a thesis — they have formed a
preference.

### Topic vs. Thesis: The Core Distinction

- **Topic** = static territory; names what you are interested in;
  makes no claims. Example: "Spiritual formation." A book built
  around a topic produces a "report" — information without a
  governing claim.
- **Thesis** = position staked inside the territory; a specific
  claim that could be wrong; changes how the reader operates if
  proven true.

The failure mode: mistaking the topic for the thesis produces the
most common and most damaging sentence in book proposals — "This book
is about [topic]." A finished Phase 1 never produces that sentence.
It produces: "This book argues that [claim]."

### The Fundamental Progression: Topic → Question → Problem → Claim

**Stage One: Topic.** A static territory. Necessary starting point,
not an argument.

**Stage Two: Question.** Moving from topic to question gives research
a direction and purpose. A question names what you do not know. The
critical distinction: a question whose answer is settled fact, purely
speculative, or a dead end — where a perfect answer connects to
nothing larger — is not a research question. A genuine research
question is answerable through evidence and consequential in its
implications.

**Stage Three: Problem.** The most important and most frequently
skipped transition. A question names what you want to find out. A
problem names what is at stake if the question remains unanswered.
A question becomes a research problem only when not answering it
costs a community of readers something they value. Every problem
has two essential components: a **condition** (the state of not-
knowing) and a **cost** (what that gap prevents).

Apply the cascading "So what?" sequence: state your question, then
ask "So what? Why would anyone care if this question remains
unanswered?" Continue until the reader cannot reasonably ask "So
what?" one more time — until they are asking "What do we do about
this?" That is the problem.

**Stage Four: The Claim.** The answer to the problem. Not a preview
of topics to be covered, but a specific, falsifiable position the
book will defend through accumulated evidence, argument, and earned
personal testimony.

Robert McKee's "controlling idea" provides the most useful
formulation: a sentence containing a value and its cause. "Strategic
competence without character formation produces cultural failure,
because strategies are adopted by institutions but formation changes
people" is a controlling idea. Every chapter, finding, and piece of
testimony in the finished book either proves, develops, or applies
the controlling idea. If a chapter cannot be connected to the
controlling idea, it does not belong in the book.

### What Makes a Claim Load-Bearing vs. Ornamental

A **load-bearing claim**:
- Is falsifiable — a specific type of evidence could, in principle,
  disprove it
- Is significant — asks the reader to change something they actually
  believe, not merely add information
- Is surprising — a knowledgeable reader finds it genuinely
  unexpected; the reaction is "That can't be right — or can it?"
- Is implicative — if true, changes how the reader operates in the
  world on a specific Tuesday afternoon
- Is vantage-dependent — could not be claimed with equal authority
  by any writer with access to the same research

An **ornamental claim** confirms what a knowledgeable person already
suspects, summarizes what the book covers, or requires no proof
because no reasonable person would dispute it.

### The Thick Thesis Formula

A thin thesis states a position without reasons. A thick thesis states
a position, the reasons that support it, the qualifications that
limit it, and an acknowledgment of the strongest counterposition.

**Formula: Claim + because + Reasons + although + Acknowledgment**

To develop thin to thick: (1) State the thin version. (2) Ask "Why
is this true?" — write the two or three primary reasons. (3) Ask
"Under what conditions is this not true?" — write the most important
qualifying conditions. (4) Ask "What would someone who genuinely
disagrees say?" — write the strongest version of the counterposition,
not a strawman.

### The Five-Part Problem Statement

Phase 1 is complete only when all five components are answered in
writing. Incomplete answers are symptoms that the thinking is not
yet finished.

**Component 1: The Problem.** One sentence naming the gap, failure,
contradiction, or confusion that currently exists — the condition the
book addresses.

**Component 2: Who Has It.** A portrait of the specific person in
the specific situation who experiences this problem most acutely. Name
their current condition: what they already know, what they have tried,
what is not working. Name the condition they will be in when the book
is finished: what they will understand and do differently. Specific
enough to recognize on the street.

**Component 3: Why Existing Answers Fail.** One sentence per existing
answer — what each gets right and where it falls short. The most
interesting gaps are in books that are largely correct but missing
one specific, consequential thing. That missing thing is the
author's claim.

**Component 4: The Author's Vantage Point.** An argument — not a
credential list — for why this author's specific combination of
experience, formation, limitation, and perspective enables them to
see something that a full insider and a full outsider would each
miss. When the author's specific history is the only thing that
makes the claim credible, the vantage point is essential.

**Component 5: The Claim.** If this book succeeds, the reader will
believe this specific thing they did not believe before — in thick
thesis format.

### Social Currency Audit (Four Tests, Applied in Writing)

Social Currency — the quality that makes a reader say "I did not
know that and I cannot stop thinking about it" — is a property of
the underlying claim, not of polished prose.

- **Surprise Test:** Would a knowledgeable person find the claim
  genuinely unexpected? The reaction must shift from "Obviously" to
  "That can't be right — can it?"
- **Specificity Test:** Can the claim be stated in one or two
  sentences that a stranger could repeat to a friend at dinner?
- **Implication Test:** If true, does it change how the reader
  acts on a specific Tuesday afternoon in a specific role?
- **Verification Test:** Is the claim grounded in something beyond
  personal conviction — a study, a dataset, a documented case?

### The Awe Diagnostic

Awe — the genuine cognitive response to discovering that reality is
more coherently designed than previously assumed — is not a stylistic
goal but an argumentative one. Three primary sources of awe:

1. **Independent traditions converging on the same truth** —
   empirical research arriving at what a philosophical, theological,
   or cultural tradition already mapped, producing the awe of double
   confirmation. The convergence is most powerful when the two
   traditions developed independently.
2. **Overturning of assumed hierarchies** — the thing the reader
   assumed was powerful is actually weak; what they dismissed as
   secondary is actually central.
3. **The specific containing the universal** — a single concrete
   observation illuminates a structural principle governing a much
   larger domain.

### The Phase 1 Quality Gate

Phase 1 is complete only when all of the following are checked in
writing. Any unchecked item means the project must not proceed to
Phase 2.

- [ ] Falsifiable — revision conditions are written out
- [ ] Significant — the "So what?" cascade reaches genuine stakes
- [ ] Surprising — a knowledgeable reader's reaction is not
      "Obviously"
- [ ] Implicative — changes reader behavior on a specific Tuesday
- [ ] Vantage-dependent — could not be claimed by any writer
- [ ] Held provisionally — revision conditions are explicit
- [ ] Five-part problem statement complete in writing
- [ ] All four Social Currency tests pass in writing

**Output:** `00_phase1_claim.md` at the root of the project directory.

## PHASE 2 — TERRAIN MAPPING

### The Critical Distinction: Phase 2 Is NOT Research

This is the most consequential distinction in the pipeline. Research
— the kind that produces citations, develops evidence chains, and
builds the argumentative case — belongs in Phase 3.

What Phase 2 produces is a **map of the intellectual territory** the
Phase 1 claim occupies. The difference between a map and a research
report is the difference between knowing a mountain range exists and
having climbed it.

The Turabian-Booth-Colomb-Williams framework frames this as entering
a conversation that began more than five thousand years ago. You
cannot enter a conversation intelligently if you do not know what
has already been said.

Brant Houston's investigative journalism concept of "building the
wall" is the complementary principle: solid documented prior
understanding built BEFORE in-depth investigation, not during it.
Building them simultaneously produces unsupported sections that fail
under real scrutiny.

**Phase 2 produces breadth, not depth. Inventory, not argument.**
It asks "what exists?" not "what does it prove?"

**Phase 2 does NOT:**
- Produce developed arguments (that is Phase 3 work)
- Verify citations against primary sources (that is Phase 3 work)
- Assign arguments to chapters (that is Phase 4 work)
- Produce prose for the book (that is Phase 4 work)

### The Limits on Means Applied to Terrain Mapping

The temptation is to map selectively — to identify supporting
arguments thoroughly while giving cursory treatment to opposing ones.
This violates the limits on means applied to knowledge production.
A terrain map that produces twenty supporting arguments and two
opposing arguments is not an honest map. It is a document that has
predetermined its conclusions dressed in the form of research.

The gold standard: map the opposing landscape **more carefully** than
the supporting one, because the supporting arguments are already
partially known to the author while the opposing arguments are most
likely to have been underestimated.

### The Five Categories of Terrain

**Category One: Arguments For the Claim**
Organized by discipline — empirical and social science, theological
and biblical, historical and case study, philosophical and
argumentative, practical and operational. The convergence between
disciplines is itself a primary source of awe. Gaps in the supporting
literature are as important as the arguments themselves.

**Category Two: Arguments Against the Claim**
The most important category. Must be at least as extensive as
Category One. Three predictable objection families must be located:
(1) Alternative cause — the phenomenon has causes other than the
one the claim identifies; (2) Counterexample — cases that appear
to disprove the generalization; (3) Definitional — key terms mean
something different from what the author intends.

The steelmanning requirement applies at full force: the goal is
the strongest possible version of every opposing argument, in its
most compelling form, engaged on its own terms. Peter Elbow's
"believing game" is the required posture — temporarily inhabiting
the most intelligent, well-informed, serious opponent of your own
claim.

**Category Three: Adjacent Literature**
Work that addresses the same domain without directly arguing for
or against the Phase 1 claim. For each piece: central argument,
what it gets right, where it falls short, and the specific gap
between its accomplishment and what the Phase 1 claim addresses.
That gap is the author's contribution to the conversation.

**Category Four: Definitional Requirements**
Every term the Phase 1 claim depends on that must be established
before the argument can proceed. This is not dictionary work — it
is the identification of conceptual commitments whose ambiguity
will produce misreadings that no subsequent argument can correct.

**Category Five: Social Currency Candidates**
Findings that are both surprising to a knowledgeable reader AND
sufficiently grounded in evidence. Minimum five candidates required.
Phase 2 identifies; Phase 3 verifies and develops.

### The Citation Confidence System

Every piece of content in the Phase 2 document is tagged:

- **[VERIFIED]** — confirmed against a primary or reliably secondary
  source (rare in Phase 2)
- **[LIKELY — verify in Phase 3]** — plausible; most common tag
- **[UNCONFIRMED — high value if accurate]** — AI tool returned
  something potentially significant; handle with caution
- **[DIRECTIONAL — no specific source]** — general orientation
  of scholarship without a specific citable source

No Phase 3 argument development draws on [UNCONFIRMED] or
[DIRECTIONAL] content without first verifying the specific source.

### The Conversation Positioning Statement

Written at the end of Phase 2. Three components:

1. **Conversation summary** — two to three sentences describing
   where the field currently stands on the question. Recognizable
   to a well-informed reader as accurate.
2. **Entry point** — one sentence: "This book responds to the
   specific gap between [position A's correct insight] and [the
   question position A cannot answer], by arguing that [CLAIM]."
3. **Contribution statement** — one sentence: the specific thing
   this book adds to the existing conversation that was not there
   before it arrived.

### The Phase 2 Quality Gate

- [ ] All five terrain categories represented with substantive content
- [ ] Category Two is at least as extensive as Category One
- [ ] Steelmanning requirement applied to three strongest opposing
      arguments — full development, not summaries
- [ ] Adjacent literature map includes genuine gap analysis for
      every major work
- [ ] Definitional audit complete; Phase 1 revised if required
- [ ] Five Social Currency candidates pass all four tests
- [ ] Citation confidence system applied consistently throughout
- [ ] Conversation Positioning Statement complete with all three
      components

**Output:** `00_phase2_terrain.md` at the root of the project
directory, plus a Phase 3 Priority List organized by urgency.

## PHASE 3 — ARGUMENT INVESTIGATION

### What Phase 3 Actually Is

Phase 3 is where the map becomes the territory. Phase 3 enters the
mapped landscape on foot, argument by argument, and does the work
only depth investigation can do.

The unit of investigation: the **component argument** — a sub-claim
that, together with the other component arguments, builds the case
for the Phase 1 master claim. Phase 3 investigates each component
argument separately, systematically, and in sufficient depth to
produce a complete record of what can be said for it, what must
be conceded against it, and what it contributes to the larger
argument.

**Three things Phase 3 does that neither Phase 1 nor Phase 2 can:**

1. Finds specific evidence supporting each component argument
2. Builds the logical architecture connecting evidence to the master
   claim
3. Tests the Phase 1 working hypothesis against actual evidence —
   the most important function of the phase

A Phase 3 investigation that produces zero revisions to the Phase 1
claim is a sign that the investigation was conducted to confirm
rather than test — the precise failure mode the limits on means
prohibit.

### The Limits on Means in Phase 3: Their Most Demanding Application

The evidence-gathering process is inherently selective. This creates
the opening for confirmation bias. The limits on means require that
Phase 3 give the same investigative energy to evidence that
complicates the claim as to evidence that supports it.

Specific prohibited moves:
- Mischaracterizing the scope of a supporting study
- Noting a complicating finding without developing it sufficiently
  to understand its implications
- Treating an objection as addressed when it has only been noted
- Presenting a secular-tradition convergence as tighter than the
  evidence actually supports

### The Seven-Step Investigation Protocol

Every component argument is investigated through all seven steps.
The protocol is complete for a given component argument only when
all seven steps have produced substantive, verified output.

**Step One: Evidence Inventory**
Gather the strongest available evidence — not all evidence, but the
best, organized by type. BCW five-part quality standard: accurate,
precise, sufficient, representative, authoritative.

Evidence types: peer-reviewed empirical studies, longitudinal or
meta-analytic data, historical case studies, contemporary cases,
documented practitioner testimony. Every piece tagged with the
citation confidence system; no piece used in Phase 4 without
[VERIFIED] tag and documented verification record.

**Step Two: The Best Objection**
Identify and fully develop the strongest argument against the
component claim — not the first objection that comes to mind but
the specific argument a serious, well-informed, intellectually
honest opponent would make. The steelmanning requirement at the
component argument level: the opponent should recognize the
author's representation as a fair and complete statement of their
argument. Check all three objection families: alternative cause,
counterexample, definitional.

**Step Three: The Response Architecture**
Build the response using the BCW taxonomy:

- **Rebuttal** — when the component's evidence is stronger than the
  objection's, or the objection's logic contains an identifiable flaw
- **Concession** — when the objection raises a point that cannot be
  adequately rebutted; not intellectual defeat but the mechanism by
  which the claim develops honest limiting conditions
- **Qualification** — limits the scope, certainty, or conditions of
  the component claim without fully conceding
- **Concede-then-rebut** — the most intellectually trustworthy
  structure; acknowledging what the objection gets right before
  demonstrating the component argument survives the concession

**Step Four: Warrant Architecture**
Identify the principle connecting evidence to claim. Three warrant
types: generalization (what holds for every known instance probably
holds for all), analogy (X resembles Y in important respects), sign
(Y regularly accompanies X). Warrants must be stated explicitly when:
readers are outside the author's field, the reasoning principle is
new or controversial, or the claim is one readers will resist.

**Step Five: Worldview/Tradition Integration** *(for works with a
values or philosophical dimension)*
Develop the specific convergence point where secular research
independently arrives at territory the author's foundational framework
or tradition already mapped. The standard for a genuine convergence:
the secular researcher should not have been trying to confirm the
tradition; the tradition should not have been predicting the empirical
finding. The double independence is what produces awe.

The formula: "[Researcher] did not set out to confirm [framework or
tradition]. They arrived there anyway." This formula is a precise claim
about independence and direction. It appears in the record only when
those conditions are actually met. Omit entirely for works whose
argument does not have this dimension — a forced integration damages
credibility more than its absence.

**Step Six: Social Currency Assessment**
Apply all four tests to the fully developed component argument. The
critical distinction between the most surprising finding (belongs at
the chapter opening, where it arrests attention) and the most
important finding (belongs at the worldview integration step, where
it carries the argument's deepest weight).

Final status for each candidate: CONFIRMED / DISQUALIFIED /
SECONDARY.

**Step Seven: Personal Testimony Slot**
Identify whether the component argument requires personal testimony
to function fully. The three testimony categories:

- **Category A** — established biographical fact; documented,
  publicly available, broadly known
- **Category B** — general professional observation; patterns from
  the author's working life
- **Category C** — a specific verifiable scene with a particular
  time, place, and outcome

Category C is the most powerful kind and the most dangerous. It
cannot be fabricated, approximated, or invented. Every Category C
slot identified in Phase 3 is marked **[PLACEHOLDER — Author
Confirmation Required]** and added to the Placeholder Inventory.
It is not written until the author confirms the specific scene.

### Citation Verification Protocol

Every source with a tag other than [VERIFIED] from Phase 2 must be
verified before the investigation record is complete:

- **Peer-reviewed studies** — confirm author names, journal, year,
  sample size or scope, and headline finding against the abstract
  or full text
- **Statistics and data points** — trace the number to its original
  source; statistics migrate from context and acquire distorted
  meanings as they are re-reported
- **Quotations** — absolute standard: every direct quotation verified
  against the primary text; AI tools misquote and misattribute with
  a frequency that makes any unverified quotation a liability

The Citation Verification Log — the running record of every source
verified with verification date, method, and confirmed details — is
the authoritative record that follows through all subsequent phases.

### Three Failure Modes to Avoid

1. **Premature synthesis** — writing before the evidence is complete;
   the most common Phase 3 failure
2. **Confirmation hunting** — investigating only supporting evidence;
   a direct violation of the limits on means
3. **Unresolved citations** — carrying [UNCONFIRMED] tags forward
   into Phase 4; every such tag must be resolved in Phase 3

### The Phase 3 Quality Gate

- [ ] Every component argument from Phase 2 Priority List investigated
      through all seven steps
- [ ] Every source carries [VERIFIED] tag with verification record;
      no [LIKELY], [UNCONFIRMED], or [DIRECTIONAL] tags remain
      unresolved
- [ ] Three steelmanned opposing arguments have full Objections
      Resolution Matrix entries, including residual force
      acknowledgment
- [ ] All worldview integrations confirmed as genuine convergences
      rather than superficial parallels
- [ ] All Social Currency candidates confirmed or disqualified with
      documented reasoning
- [ ] All personal testimony slots identified and categorized (A, B,
      or C); all Category C items in Placeholder Inventory
- [ ] Working Hypothesis Log updated; Phase 1 revised if any
      third-tier responses were required
- [ ] Placeholder Inventory complete and formatted for Phase 5 handoff

**Output:** `00_phase3_investigation.md` at the root of the project
directory — the fully researched argument inventory ready for Phase 4
assembly.

## HOW THE THREE PHASES CONNECT

Phase 1 produces the claim. Phase 2 maps the territory the claim
occupies. Phase 3 enters that territory argument by argument and
builds the verified case for the claim.

Each phase is complete only when its quality gate passes in full.
Proceeding to Phase 2 without a cleared Phase 1 gate means the
research has no precise target. Proceeding to Phase 3 without a
cleared Phase 2 gate means the investigation is proceeding without
a map — producing work that cannot be assembled without the conversion
step that should have happened in Phase 2. Proceeding to Phase 4
without a cleared Phase 3 gate means building the book on a
foundation whose integrity has not been confirmed.

The three phases are not overhead. They are the difference between
a book that proves its claim and a book whose author merely believed
it.

## OPERATING PRINCIPLES FOR THIS SKILL

When a user invokes this skill, determine which phase or phases they
are working on. If Phase 1 has not begun and the user has not yet
stated their governing postulates, ask for them first (see *Before
Phase 1* above), offering the example only as an example. Then:

1. Confirm the current state of the relevant quality gate items
2. Identify which gate items are unchecked and why
3. Produce the specific output — claim, terrain map, or argument
   inventory — for the phase in question
4. Apply the citation confidence system to all sources referenced
5. Never proceed past a phase quality gate that has unchecked items
6. Hold your stated postulates and the method's limits on means as
   governing constraints throughout;
   never allow the ends to justify the means in claim formation,
   terrain mapping, or argument investigation

The Phase 1 claim document is the foundational source of truth for
the entire pipeline. Nothing in Phase 2 or beyond may contradict it
without a documented revision to that document. If the claim changes
during research, the change is recorded with the reason and the
evidence that prompted it.

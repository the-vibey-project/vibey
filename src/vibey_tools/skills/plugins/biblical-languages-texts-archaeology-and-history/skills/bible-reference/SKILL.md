## How to use this document

This is built as one connected system, not nine separate essays, because that is how the disciplines actually work. A translator cannot choose a word until a textual critic has decided which words are there; a textual critic weighs manuscripts that archaeologists dug up and palaeographers dated; historians use all of it and then argue about what it means. Part VII walks three real verses through the whole pipeline so you can see the gears mesh.

## §35 Learning paths

### §35.1 If you want to read the languages

1. **Greek first or Hebrew first?** Greek has more resources and a more familiar grammar for English speakers; Hebrew is smaller and pays off quickly in the Old Testament. Either works.
2. **Hebrew:** Pratico & Van Pelt, *Basics of Biblical Hebrew*; Futato, *Beginning Biblical Hebrew*; or immersion/living-language approaches (Biblical Language Center, Randall Buth). Then graded readers and the *Reader's Hebrew Bible*.
3. **Greek:** Mounce, *Basics of Biblical Greek*; Decker, *Reading Koine Greek*; Black, *Learn to Read New Testament Greek*; immersive options (Polis Institute). Then the *UBS Reader's Greek New Testament*.
4. **Aramaic:** Van Pelt, *Basics of Biblical Aramaic*.
5. **Latin:** Wheelock's *Latin*; for Church Latin specifically, Collins, *A Primer of Ecclesiastical Latin*. Then read the Vulgate Gospels alongside the Greek.
6. **Habit that matters most:** daily reading of real text, even a few verses, beats occasional intensive study.

### §35.2 If you want to understand textual criticism

- Bruce Metzger & Bart Ehrman, *The Text of the New Testament* (4th ed., 2005) — the standard introduction.
- Kurt & Barbara Aland, *The Text of the New Testament* (2nd ed.).
- Peter Gurry & Elijah Hixson (eds.), *Myths and Mistakes in New Testament Textual Criticism* (2019) — corrects common apologetic *and* skeptical errors.
- Tommy Wasserman & Peter Gurry, *A New Approach to Textual Criticism: An Introduction to the Coherence-Based Genealogical Method* (2017).
- David Parker, *An Introduction to the New Testament Manuscripts and Their Texts* (2008).
- Bruce Metzger, *A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament* (2nd ed., 1994) — explains the UBS committee's decisions verse by verse.
- Emanuel Tov, *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible* (4th ed., 2022).
- Ellis Brotzman & Eric Tully, *Old Testament Textual Criticism: A Practical Introduction*.
- Ronald Hendel, *Steps to a New Edition of the Hebrew Bible* (2016).

### §35.3 If you want to understand translation

- Eugene Nida & Charles Taber, *The Theory and Practice of Translation* (1969).
- Ernst-August Gutt, *Translation and Relevance* (1991).
- Dave Brunn, *One Bible, Many Versions* (2013) — excellent on why "literal" is complicated.
- Leland Ryken, *The Word of God in English* (2002) — the case for essentially literal translation.
- Gordon Fee & Mark Strauss, *How to Choose a Translation for All Its Worth* (2007).
- David Daniell, *The Bible in English* (2003).
- Adam Nicolson, *God's Secretaries* (2003) — the KJV translators.
- Robert Alter, *The Art of Bible Translation* (2019).
- The **NET Bible's translators' notes** (free online) — the best practical window into translation decisions.

### §35.4 If you want to understand archaeology

- Amihai Mazar, *Archaeology of the Land of the Bible, 10,000–586 B.C.E.* (1990) — still the standard synthesis.
- Israel Finkelstein & Neil Asher Silberman, *The Bible Unearthed* (2001) — the Low Chronology view for general readers.
- William Dever, *What Did the Biblical Writers Know and When Did They Know It?* (2001) and *Beyond the Texts* (2017).
- Jodi Magness, *The Archaeology of the Holy Land: From the Destruction of Solomon's Temple to the Muslim Conquest* (2012).
- Eric Cline, *Biblical Archaeology: A Very Short Introduction* (2009).
- Edward Harris, *Principles of Archaeological Stratigraphy* (2nd ed., 1989).
- Colin Renfrew & Paul Bahn, *Archaeology: Theories, Methods, and Practice* (general archaeology textbook).
- Journals and magazines: *Near Eastern Archaeology*, *BASOR*, *Israel Exploration Journal*, *Tel Aviv*, *Levant*; *Biblical Archaeology Review* for popular access.

## §37 Glossary

- **Apparatus (critical apparatus):** the footnote section of a critical edition listing variants and their witnesses.
- **Autograph:** the original document written by an author (none survive).
- **Back translation:** a literal rendering of a draft translation into a consultant's language for checking.
- **Balk (baulk):** an unexcavated strip between excavation squares, preserving a vertical section.
- **BHS / BHQ:** Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia / Quinta — critical editions of the Hebrew Bible.
- **Binyan:** a Hebrew verb stem pattern.
- **CBGM:** Coherence-Based Genealogical Method.
- **Codex:** a book with pages (vs a scroll). Christians adopted the codex early and enthusiastically.
- **Conflation:** combining two variant readings into one.
- **Diplomatic edition:** prints a single manuscript's text, putting variants in the apparatus.
- **Eclectic edition:** prints a reconstructed text drawn from many witnesses.
- **ECM:** Editio Critica Maior.
- **Formal / functional equivalence:** translation that prioritizes source form / target-language meaning and naturalness.
- **Gregory-Aland number:** the catalogue number of a Greek NT manuscript.
- **Harris Matrix:** diagram of stratigraphic relationships.
- **Initial text (Ausgangstext):** the reconstructed text from which the surviving tradition descends.
- **Ketiv/Qere:** "what is written / what is read" in the Masoretic tradition.
- **Koine:** the common Greek of the Hellenistic–Roman world.
- **Lectio difficilior potior:** "the harder reading is preferable."
- **Locus:** the basic excavation unit (a distinct deposit or feature).
- **LXX:** the Septuagint.
- **Masoretes / Masorah:** medieval Jewish scholars who vocalized and annotated the Hebrew text / their notes.
- **MT:** Masoretic Text.
- **NA28/NA29:** Nestle-Aland *Novum Testamentum Graece*, 28th/29th editions.
- **Palimpsest:** a manuscript whose original text was scraped off and overwritten.
- **Provenance:** documented ownership and find history of an object.
- **Recension:** a deliberate revision of a text.
- **Stratigraphy:** study of the sequence of deposits.
- **Tell:** a mound formed by successive settlements.
- **Textus Receptus (TR):** the printed Greek NT tradition from Erasmus to the Elzevirs, underlying the KJV New Testament.
- **Variation unit:** a place in the text where witnesses differ.
- **Vorlage:** the source text a translator worked from.
- **Vulgate:** the Latin Bible associated with Jerome.

## §38 What remains genuinely open (as of 2026)

1. Whether biblical texts can be dated linguistically (Hebrew diachrony debate).
2. How the Biblical Hebrew verb encodes tense, aspect, and mood.
3. The limits of verbal aspect in Greek.
4. Whether the "initial text" of the ECM can be equated with the authorial text — and whether "original text" is a coherent target.
5. The final shape of NA29 and ECM (target 2030) — which changes to translation footnotes will follow.
6. Diplomatic vs eclectic editing of the Hebrew Bible.
7. The Iron Age I–IIA chronology and the nature of the 10th-century Judahite polity.
8. The emergence of Israel and the historical memory in the Exodus traditions.
9. The role of AI in field Bible translation — quality, ownership, and community acceptance.
10. The replacement for the criteria of authenticity in historical Jesus research.
11. The significance of new palaeographic dating tools like Enoch.

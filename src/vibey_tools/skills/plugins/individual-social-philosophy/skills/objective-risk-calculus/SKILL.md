# Objective Social Risk Calculus

## 1. The Social Risk Index ($R_s$)

To evaluate interpersonal exposure objectively, social risk is formalized as:

$$R_s = \frac{E \cdot H \cdot C}{I \cdot A \cdot S}$$

Each variable is rated along an integer scale from 1 to 10:

### Numerator (Threat and Vulnerability Exposure)
* **$E \in [1, 10]$ (Exposure Level)**: The depth and sensitivity of access granted to the counterpart (confidential information, financial access, physical vulnerability, strategic plans).
* **$H \in [1, 10]$ (Hazard Severity)**: The maximum potential damage that could occur if the counterpart defects (reputational destruction, career ruin, legal exposure, financial ruin, physical harm).
* **$C \in [1, 10]$ (Adversarial Capacity)**: The counterpart's socio-political power, intelligence, connections, vengefulness, and capacity to inflict harm upon becoming hostile.

### Denominator (Trust and Structural Constraints)
* **$I \in [1, 10]$ (Demonstrated Integrity)**: The counterpart's historically verified record of honesty, debt payment, and discretion under pressure across prolonged observation.
* **$A \in [1, 10]$ (Incentive Alignment)**: The degree to which the counterpart's utility function and self-interest benefit from your flourishing.
* **$S \in [1, 10]$ (Structural Accountability)**: External checks that deter defection (written contracts, independent witnesses, ecclesiastical oversight, mutual reputational exposure).

### Risk Score Interpretation Thresholds
* **$R_s \ge 3.0$**: **Critical Danger Zone**. Immediate defensive posture; zero private disclosure; establish legal, institutional, and spatial buffers.
* **$1.0 \le R_s < 3.0$**: **Guarded Engagement**. Standard peripheral engagement; maintain polite affability, strict boundaries, and ongoing verification.
* **$R_s < 1.0$**: **Secure Relational Space**. Progressive testing and mutual vulnerability remain appropriate.

## 2. The Six Empirical Observation Vectors

Evaluate counterparts across six objective behavioral vectors:

### Vector 1: Speech Governance (The Gossip and Slander Metric)
* *The Principle*: How an individual speaks of third parties in their absence indicates how they will speak of you in your absence ([Proverbs 16:28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+16%3A28&version=ESV); [Sirach 19:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Sirach+19%3A8&version=RSVCE); *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q. 74).
* *Observation*: Sharing other people's confidential struggles, mocking former friends, or conducting whisper campaigns (*susurratio*) caps integrity at $I \le 3$.

### Vector 2: Pressure Response (The Adversity Metric)
* *The Principle*: True character reveals itself under scarcity, fatigue, and status threat ([Sirach 6:8-10](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Sirach+6%3A8-10&version=RSVCE)).
* *Observation*: When resources become constrained or reputations are challenged, observe whether they accept responsibility or scapegoat others to preserve their standing.

### Vector 3: Inner Ring Susceptibility (The Status and Sycophancy Metric)
* *The Principle*: The craving for insider status corrupts moral integrity (C.S. Lewis, *The Inner Ring*).
* *Observation*: Flattering authority figures, abandoning lower-status associates when elites enter the room, or altering convictions to gain admission to elite cliques indicates high susceptibility.

### Vector 4: Cross-Context Consistency (The Character Metric)
* *The Principle*: Integrity is wholeness and consistency across time and space.
* *Observation*: High integrity displays the same person in church, at work, at home, on social media, and when under the influence of alcohol or stress. Radical shifts indicate an opportunistic persona.

### Vector 5: Asymmetric Leverage Geometry (The Incentive Metric)
* *The Principle*: Understand the counterpart's underlying interest before relying on their counsel ([Sirach 37:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Sirach+37%3A8&version=RSVCE)).
* *Observation*: Determine whether this person gains financially, socially, or professionally from your degradation. When their gain correlates with your loss, incentive alignment sits at $A \le 2$.

### Vector 6: Reaction to Boundaries (The Entitlement Metric)
* *The Principle*: Moral persons respect personal sovereignty; exploiters view boundaries as aggression.
* *Observation*: When presented with an unequivocal, polite "No," observe whether they respond with composure and respect, or with passive-aggressive guilt, anger, and boundary-pushing. Entitlement reactions cap $I \le 3$.

## 3. Threat Typology

Interpersonal harm manifests across five distinct vectors:
1. **Physical**: Bodily harm, assault, sexual boundary violations, physical intimidation.
2. **Financial and Material**: Embezzlement, unpaid debts, theft, sabotage of livelihood.
3. **Reputational**: Slander (*detractio*), gossip (*susurratio*), character assassination, public humiliation.
4. **Relational**: Poisoning alliances, alienating children or family, turning mutual friends into adversaries.
5. **Spiritual and Moral**: Enticement into unethical behavior, destruction of faith, exploitation through religious guilt.

# Foundations and Methodology: Individual Social Philosophy

## 1. Scope: Micro-Sociology and Macro-Governance

Political philosophy analyzes collective statecraft, legal statutes, and resource distribution. Cultural philosophy studies collective meaning, ancestral traditions, and institutional structures. 

Individual Social Philosophy addresses the single actor:
* How a human person navigates interpersonal life.
* How trust is established, calibrated, expanded, and revoked.
* Cognitive filters required to evaluate interpersonal risk.
* The operational necessity of guardedness and vulnerability.

## 2. Anthropological Baseline: Universal Fallenness

A functional social philosophy begins with an empirical diagnosis of the social field:

1. **The Fall and the Deceitful Heart**: Jeremiah 17:9 affirms that the human heart is deceitful above all things. In the absence of sanctifying grace and external constraints, individuals follow self-preservation, self-aggrandizement, and betrayal under pressure.
2. ***Homo Incurvatus in Se***: The human person curves inward upon the self, as formulated by Augustine, Luther, and Calvin. Social counterparts prioritize their own status, comfort, and security.
3. **The Empirical Reality of Malice**: People lie, break confidence, hold grudges, envy, and inflict reputational, financial, and physical harm. Prudence assumes that such behaviors remain active possibilities. Proverbs 14:15 notes: "The simple believes everything, but the prudent gives thought to his steps."

## 3. The Christological Precedent: John 2:23-25

The definitive New Testament standard for interpersonal engagement appears in Christ's conduct:
> *"Now when he was in Jerusalem at the Passover Feast, many believed in his name when they saw the signs that he was doing. But Jesus on his part did not entrust himself to them, because he knew all people and needed no one to bear witness about man, for he himself knew what was in man."* (John 2:23-25)

The Greek text employs the identical root (*pisteuō*): the crowds *believed* (episteusan), while Jesus *withheld personal entrustment* (ouk episteuen auton). 

**The Theological Rule:** Christian charity commands unconditional benevolence (*agape*) toward all people. Trust (*fides*) and vulnerability require proven character.

## 4. Epistemic Method: Triangulation and Verification

Applying the comparative framework from historical methodology:
* **Distinguish Categorical Spheres**: Keep separate what a tradition formally teaches (doctrine), what people did over centuries (history), shared patterns of meaning (culture), and concrete interpersonal mechanics (social life).
* **Assess Direct Evidence**: Evaluate individual actors based on observable behaviors, verified integrity, and structural incentives.
* **Verification Serves Charity**: Auditing claims, demanding milestones, and testing loyalty represent basic moral obligations of prudence.

# Operational Playbook: Individual Social Philosophy

## 1. Phase 1: Entry and Peripheral Engagement

When entering a new workplace, neighborhood, church, or community:

1. **Practice Thomistic Affability (*Amicitia*)**: Greet all individuals with warmth, politeness, and dignity. Fulfill the basic requirements of social justice by remaining pleasant and respectful.
2. **Enforce Asymmetric Information Flow**:
   * Listen attentively. Ask questions about their work, interests, and public ideas.
   * Maintain strict silence regarding personal liabilities, marital tensions, deep financial details, or confidential ambitions.
3. **Control Emotional Reactivity**: Follow James 1:19: "quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger." Commitments and private disclosures belong to calm, reflective deliberation.

## 2. Phase 2: Micro-Probing and Probation (The St. Aelred Protocol)

When an associate shows promise for deeper connection, execute systematic, low-stakes micro-probes before advancing them toward Tier 2 or Tier 3:

### Test 1: The Discretion Micro-Probe (*Discretio*)
* **Action**: Share a minor, harmless piece of private information accompanied by an explicit boundary request: *"I am considering a new side project, and I ask that you keep this strictly between us for now."*
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Pass*: The information remains completely confidential.
  * *Fail*: The information leaks back through a third party. Halt all disclosures and keep the relationship at Tier 1.

### Test 2: The Reliability Micro-Probe (*Fides*)
* **Action**: Establish a low-stakes commitment, such as loaning a book or agreeing on a specific meeting time or small joint deliverable.
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Pass*: They honor their word promptly and handle borrowed property with care.
  * *Fail*: Chronic tardiness, excuses, or careless handling of borrowed property indicates low reliability.

### Test 3: The Constructive Friction Micro-Probe (*Patientia*)
* **Action**: Offer a gentle, constructive dissent or set a minor boundary, such as politely declining an invitation or offering an alternative perspective.
* **Evaluation**:
  * *Pass*: They accept the boundary or disagreement with composure and respect.
  * *Fail*: They react with passive-aggressive withdrawal, guilt-tripping, sulking, or retaliatory gossip.

## 3. Phase 3: The Immediate Revocation Protocol (Matthew 18)

When an individual breaches trust through malicious deception, slander, or disclosure of secrets:

```
[Breach Detected] ──► [Immediate Access Severance] (Informational and Spatial Buffer)
                              │
                              ▼
                      [Private Confrontation] (Matthew 18:15: Facts, Zero Affect)
                              │
             ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
             ▼                                 ▼
   [Repentance & Restitution]        [Denial, Hostility, Gaslighting]
   (Long-term re-probation)                    │
                                               ▼
                                     [Escalate with Witnesses] (Matt 18:16)
                                               │
                                               ▼
                                     [Reclassification to Tier 0 or 1]
                                     ("Gentile and Tax Collector", Matt 18:17)
```

### Immediate Access Severance
* Instantly cut off access to confidential data, private keys, financial information, and vulnerable spaces.
* Move the individual from Tier 3 or 2 to Tier 0 or 1 immediately. Secure all boundaries as the first operational step.

### The Matthew 18 Process
1. **Private Confrontation (Matthew 18:15)**: State the objective fact of the breach directly, privately, and with calm composure.
2. **Escalation to Objective Witnesses (Matthew 18:16)**: If they deny, gaslight, or deflect, bring one or two objective witnesses to establish the facts.
3. **The Boundary Protocol (Matthew 18:17)**: If they refuse to hear the church or community, treat them "as a Gentile and a tax collector."
   * Treat them with civil dignity from a distance.
   * Discontinue personal intimacy and partnership.

## 4. The Distinction Between Forgiveness and Trust

Popular culture often conflates forgiveness with trust:

| Dimension | Christian Forgiveness | Interpersonal Trust |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biblical Mandate** | Commanded for all believers ([Matthew 6:14-15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+6%3A14-15&version=ESV)) | Evaluated through testing ([Sirach 6:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Sirach+6%3A7&version=RSVCE); [1 John 4:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+John+4%3A1&version=ESV)) |
| **Nature** | Unilateral release of debt and surrender of vengeance | Bilateral confidence in character and competence |
| **Cost** | Absorbed by the forgiver through the cross of Christ | Earned through proven fidelity and restitution |
| **Timeframe** | Immediate, decisional act | Slow, arithmetic accumulation over time |
| **Requirement** | Offered freely from the heart | Requires verified repentance, structural change, and probation |

**The Operational Standard**: Forgive an enemy seventy times seven to guard your own soul against bitterness. Grant keys to your home and your secrets only when character has been rigorously rebuilt through verified action.
